# S16_Planning_Techniques_and_Task_1_Evidence - Lesson: Planning techniques and evidencing Task 1

## Goal
The learner uses mood boards, mind maps, concept sketches and visualisation diagrams to develop ideas, and knows how to evidence a suitable design concept, a justification and detailed planning documentation for Task 1.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R094-2.3 - Techniques to plan visual identity and digital graphics: use mood boards, mind maps, concept sketches and visualisation diagrams to generate and develop ideas
- R094-T1a - Task 1 strand 1a: producing a suitable design concept: know how to present a fully suitable visual identity design concept for the client and audience
- R094-T1b - Task 1 strand 1b: justifying the visual identity: know how to write a justification of how and why the identity is fit for purpose for both client and audience
- R094-T1c - Task 1 strand 1c: planning documentation and asset table: know how to evidence detailed layout planning and a permissions-aware asset table

## How to teach this
Say clearly: 'The teacher can teach you the techniques, but in the assessment the ideas and decisions must be yours. So in practice we will use a practice brief and you will show me your own process.' Ask: 'How would you turn a client brief into a logo idea without just copying something?' Then teach the tools and the evidence.

#### R094-2.3 Techniques to plan visual identity and digital graphics
Four documents generate and develop ideas. A mood board (physical on a large sheet or digital on slides) gathers pictures, text and colours to set the look and feel. A mind map (paper or software) expands ideas and identifies details from the brief (audience, values, colours, imagery). Concept sketches develop design ideas by trying several logo and layout options quickly. Visualisation diagrams show design ideas and possible layouts as annotated sketches or concept art created in software, labelling positions, fonts, colours and sizes. Project-management planning such as workplans and Gantt charts is not part of this unit. Method: start with the brief (client, audience, purpose), mind map keywords, collect mood board images that fit, sketch several concepts, choose one and refine, then draw a detailed visualisation diagram. Sourced images on a mood board should be identified as inspiration and not copied.

#### R094-T1a Task 1 strand 1a: producing a suitable design concept
Strand 1a (6 marks) marks the design concept for the visual identity: MB1 limited in suitability for the client, MB2 adequate, MB3 fully suitable. OCR says a simple logo could be credited in MB1; for higher bands the identity needs to be more complex and include suitable component features (name, logo, slogan, colour palette, typography) clearly appropriate to the intended audience and the nature of the client's product or service. Evidence of developing ideas (sketches, iterations, mood boards) can contribute, but the final concept must be clearly shown for MB3. Coaching prompts allowed: 'What does the brief say about the audience?' Not allowed: telling a learner what their logo should be. Good evidence: the final concept clearly labelled with each component, alongside the developmental work that shows why it was chosen.

#### R094-T1b Task 1 strand 1b: justifying the visual identity
Strand 1b (8 marks) marks understanding of how far the identity is fit for purpose. MB1: statements that it meets the client's needs. MB2: sound understanding. MB3: comprehensive understanding covering both the client and the target audience, giving how and why. This is analytical and evaluative, not descriptive. A strong justification takes each element (name, logo shape, colours, typography, layout) and says what it communicates, how that links to the client's values, business type and positioning, and why it will appeal to the intended audience. It may also note limits ('the thin font may be hard to read at small sizes'). Structure a paragraph as: element, what was chosen, why it suits the client, why it suits the audience. Avoid long generic descriptions of what visual identity is.

#### R094-T1c Task 1 strand 1c: planning documentation and asset table
Strand 1c (6 marks) marks planning documentation for the digital graphic product. Two evidence items are expected: what the intended graphic will look like (a layout diagram or visualisation diagram) and the details of any assets to be used (an asset table). A simple layout diagram is MB1; for the upper bands it must be clear and detailed enough for the client or another graphic designer to use, showing position, sizes, fonts, colours and content. The asset table can be in the learner's own format or the OCR template from Teach Cambridge, and at higher bands needs detail including source, format and size and licence or permission information. Learners should not be told which planning tasks to complete, but can be reminded of the teaching content. Check before submitting: could a stranger build the graphic from these?

## Explicitly not here
Creating the graphics (S17 onwards) and Task 2 evidence (S19). Do not write the learner's concept or justification for them.
