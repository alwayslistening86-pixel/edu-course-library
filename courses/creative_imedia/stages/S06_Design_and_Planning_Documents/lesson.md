# S06_Design_and_Planning_Documents - Lesson: Documents used to design and plan media products

## Goal
The learner knows the purpose, components, tools, users and effectiveness of storyboards, wireframe layouts, visualisation diagrams, asset logs, flow charts and scripts, and picks the right one for a situation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-3.3a - Storyboards, wireframe layouts and visualisation diagrams: know the purpose, components, tools, users and effectiveness of the three visual design documents
- R093-3.3b - Asset logs, flow charts and scripts: know the purpose, components, tools, users and effectiveness of the three planning documents

## How to teach this
Give the learner six blank cards labelled storyboard, wireframe, visualisation diagram, asset log, flow chart and script. Read out six planning needs ('list every image and its source', 'show the order of screens', 'write what actors say') and have them match each need to a card before you teach the detail.

#### R093-3.3a Storyboards, wireframe layouts and visualisation diagrams
A storyboard is a sequence of drawn frames showing what happens shot by shot in video, animation or an interactive sequence. Components: numbered frames, a picture of each shot, camera angle and shot type, action, dialogue or audio, transitions and duration. Users: director, camera operator, animator, client. A wireframe layout is a simple screen or page plan showing where elements go (header, navigation, images, text blocks, buttons) without final colours or images; users are web designers, developers and clients; it suits websites, apps and interfaces. A visualisation diagram is a labelled sketch or mock-up of a single finished item such as a poster or a magazine cover, with annotations for colours, fonts, images, positions and dimensions; users are designers and clients. Effective versions are clear enough for a third party to build from: labelled, to scale or with sizes, consistent notation. Tools: pen and paper, or software such as presentation, drawing or specialist wireframing tools. Improvements: add missing labels, timings, colour notes or screen sizes.

#### R093-3.3b Asset logs, flow charts and scripts
An asset log (asset table) lists every asset needed: file name, type, source, format, size or resolution, creator or owner, and the copyright or permission status. It supports legal compliance and checks that everything needed is available. Users: producer, designer, client. A flow chart shows the order of steps or the paths a user can take, using standard shapes (start/end ovals, process rectangles, decision diamonds) and arrows; it suits explaining how a game, quiz or website flows and the choices offered. A script sets out words and instructions: for video, scene headings, character names, dialogue and action; for audio, narration and cues; for interactive products, text and prompts. Users: actors, presenters, directors, sound and video teams. Software: word processors and spreadsheets, or flowchart and scriptwriting tools. Effective documents have a consistent layout, cover everything needed and can be followed by someone who did not write them.
