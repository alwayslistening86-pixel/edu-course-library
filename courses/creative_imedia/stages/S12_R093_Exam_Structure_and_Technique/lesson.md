# S12_R093_Exam_Structure_and_Technique - Lesson: R093 exam structure, command words and extended responses

## Goal
The learner understands the R093 paper, answers Section A recall questions accurately and writes analysis, evaluation and application responses to the Section B scenario in the way the mark scheme rewards.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-EX1 - R093 exam structure, marks and Section A technique: know the 1 hour 30 minute, 70-mark paper, its two sections and how to answer Section A recall questions
- R093-EX2 - Command words used in the exam: respond correctly to state, identify, describe, explain, analyse, discuss, evaluate, justify and the other command words
- R093-EX3 - Section B scenario and extended response technique: plan and write the three extended responses (two analysis/evaluation, one recall and application) tied to the scenario

## How to teach this
Show the learner the structure of the exam before any practice: 'This paper is worth 40% of the whole qualification and is the only unit that can be sat once and must be sat in your final series. What does that tell you about how to prepare?' Then teach the command words as a set of instructions before letting them attempt scenarios.

#### R093-EX1 R093 exam structure, marks and Section A technique
R093 is a written exam of 1 hour 30 minutes worth 70 marks (80 UMS), set and marked by OCR, with no calculator. Section A has 10 marks and 7 to 10 closed, multiple choice and short answer questions that test recall; every topic area appears at least once. Section B has 60 marks of context-based questions on one scenario that develops through the paper, with closed, short answer and three extended response questions; content is sampled from all topic areas. Under the terminal assessment rule the exam must be taken in the final assessment series of the course: an earlier attempt counts only as practice. The assessed skills are PO1 (recall and understanding, mainly Section A), PO2 (apply) and PO3 (analyse and evaluate). Section A technique: read carefully, answer every question, use the marks as a guide to the number of points, never leave a multiple choice blank, and use precise vocabulary. Time plan: about 10 minutes for Section A, then roughly one minute per mark in Section B, leaving time for the extended answers.

#### R093-EX2 Command words used in the exam
Learn what each word asks for, using OCR's own definitions: State (a short factual answer), Identify (recognise, name or select), Describe (give an account including relevant characteristics), Outline (short account), Explain (give reasons using because, therefore, this means that), Analyse (break down into parts and explain the impacts or pros and cons with a chain of reasoning), Discuss (present, analyse and evaluate points such as for and against), Evaluate (make a reasoned qualitative judgement considering different factors), Justify (give good reasons for an opinion or conclusion), Compare and contrast (similarities and differences), Create (produce a visual solution such as a mind map or flow chart), Annotate/Label/Complete/Fill in (add the needed parts), Draw, Calculate, Choose, Circle. Teaching move: give the same content with three different command words (State, Explain, Evaluate) and show how the answer grows. Common error: describing when the question says explain or evaluate.

#### R093-EX3 Section B scenario and extended response technique
Two of the three extended responses test analysis and evaluation (PO3) and one tests recall and application of knowledge (PO1/PO2). Use a simple structure: point (a relevant fact or choice), evidence from the scenario (use the client name, audience, product), explanation (because...), consequence or impact, and a judgement or conclusion for evaluate and discuss questions that weighs at least one advantage and one disadvantage or alternatives. Plan in the margin with three points; develop two or three well rather than list six. Read the scenario twice and underline the client, product, audience, purpose and constraints, because Section B answers that are generic, however accurate, lose marks. Include technical vocabulary such as ppi, lossy, segmentation, contingency. For evaluation end with a reasoned judgement such as 'overall the app is the better choice because...'.
