# S23_R097_Planning_Documents_and_Task1_Interpretation - Lesson: Planning documents for interactive media, and interpreting a client brief

## Goal
The learner produces wireframes, storyboards, asset tables and navigation diagrams, and interprets a client brief to explain how a product will meet it and appeal to its audience.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R097-1.4a - Wireframes and storyboards for interface planning: plan screens, colour scheme, text, layout, navigation, GUI and media interaction in wireframes and storyboards
- R097-1.4b - Master pages, asset tables and planning assets: plan templates and assets with position, purpose, age appropriateness, quality, size, file size, type and resolution
- R097-1.4c - Navigation and hierarchy diagrams, interactive features and controls: plan movement between screens, user interaction and responses (feedback and closure)
- R097-T1a - Task 1 strand 1a: interpreting the brief and explaining audience appeal: know how to interpret a client brief and explain how the product meets it and appeals to the audience

## How to teach this
Give the learner a one-paragraph client brief and ask them to plan a screen for it using only words first, then ask them to draw it. 'What did drawing it show you that writing didn't?' Use this to motivate why planning documents exist: they force decisions to be made and checked before time is spent building.

#### R097-1.4a Wireframes and storyboards for interface planning
A wireframe is a labelled, low-fidelity sketch of a single screen's layout - colour scheme, text placement, navigation elements, GUI features and where media/interaction sit - used to plan structure before visual polish. A storyboard shows a sequence of screens and the user's path between them, useful for planning flow through a product (for example a checkout process). Both should be detailed enough that someone else could build from them: vague sketches without labels or notes do not evidence planning to Level 2 standard. Activity: wireframe one screen of the leisure-centre booking product from S20, labelling at least five features.

#### R097-1.4b Master pages, asset tables and planning assets
A master page (or template) plans elements repeated across screens - header, footer, navigation bar - so they stay consistent. An asset table lists every planned asset with its position on the screen, purpose, required age-appropriateness, quality, size, file size, type and resolution, so the creation stage has a clear specification to build to rather than vague intentions. A thorough asset table is one of the most heavily rewarded pieces of planning evidence because it demonstrates that every decision has been thought through in advance. Activity: draft three rows of an asset table for the booking product (for example a logo, a class photo, a "book now" button).

#### R097-1.4c Navigation and hierarchy diagrams, interactive features and controls
A navigation or hierarchy diagram maps every screen in the product and the links between them, showing structure (linear, hierarchical or non-linear/web) at a glance and catching missing or dead-end links before building starts. It should also note interactive features and controls - buttons, menus, forms - and their intended feedback (what happens when a user clicks) and closure (how the user knows a task is finished). Skipping this step risks building screens that do not connect properly. Activity: draw a simple hierarchy diagram for the booking product showing home, class list, class detail and booking confirmation screens.

#### R097-T1a Task 1 strand 1a: interpreting the brief and explaining audience appeal
Strand 1a (8 marks) asks the learner to interpret a client brief and explain, in writing, how their planned product will meet its purpose and appeal to its stated audience. MB3 evidence identifies the brief's explicit and implicit requirements accurately, and explains - not just asserts - why specific planned features, content and design choices will appeal to the named audience, using audience characteristics (age, interests, needs) rather than generic claims like "people will like it". Weak (MB1) evidence restates the brief without analysis. Activity: write a short interpretation of the leisure-centre brief, explaining two ways the planned product will appeal to visitors aged 16-60.

## Explicitly not here
Sourcing and creating assets (S24 onwards), testing and review (S27-S28).
