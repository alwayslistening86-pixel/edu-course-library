# S20_R097_Overview_Formats_and_Content - Lesson: R097 overview and the formats and content of interactive digital media

## Goal
The learner knows how R097 is assessed and can match a format of interactive digital media, and the content types within it, to a given purpose and audience.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R097-A - R097 assessment overview: three tasks, strands and mark bands: know the three tasks, strands, 70 marks, mark bands and independence rules
- R097-1.1a - Format types of interactive digital media: know websites, information points, mobile apps, e-learning, digital maps and games and how format links to purpose and audience
- R097-1.1b - Content used in interactive digital media: explain how images, audio, video, animation, text, tables, lists, forms, buttons, maps, quizzes and layers are used
- R097-1.1c - Hardware devices used to access interactive media: link computers, consoles, kiosks, phones, smart TVs and tablets to audience and purpose and adapt content

## How to teach this
Open with a real website, a mobile app and a digital map on the same subject (for example a museum): 'Why would the museum build all three instead of just one?' Draw out that format follows audience, purpose and how people will actually access the product. R097 is the third and final unit built here: unlike R093's exam, it is another OCR-set, practical assignment like R094, but larger and in three tasks instead of two.

#### R097-A R097 assessment overview: three tasks, strands and mark bands
R097 is a 40 GLH, OCR-set assignment worth 70 marks (70 UMS), completed over roughly 15-17 hours of supervised, independent time and moderated by OCR. Task 1 (Topic Area 1: Plan) has three strands - 1a interpreting the brief and audience appeal (8 marks), 1b pre-production and planning documentation (16), 1c understanding how assets contribute (6). Task 2 (Topic Area 2: Create) has three strands - 2a creating and preparing component parts (10), 2b creating the final product (16), 2c appropriate formats and properties (6). Task 3 (Topic Area 3: Review) has two strands - 3a testing and review of effectiveness (10), 3b recommendations for improvement (4). Every strand uses the same three mark bands as R094 (MB1 limited, MB2 adequate/sound, MB3 effective/comprehensive); no MB1 evidence in a task scores zero for that task. As with R094, the practice work here is original and never the live OCR set assignment.

#### R097-1.1a Format types of interactive digital media
The specification names six format types: websites, information points (kiosks), mobile apps, e-learning materials, digital quizzes/maps and games. Each format links to a purpose (inform, educate, entertain, persuade) and an audience, and constrains what content and interaction can be used - a kiosk is fixed to a single physical location and short sessions, while an app supports touch and location data on the move. A given client brief may allow more than one plausible format, so the learner must justify the choice against the brief, not simply pick the most familiar option. Teaching activity: give three one-line briefs (a museum trail, a homework help tool, a shop-floor product finder) and have the learner match and justify a format for each.

#### R097-1.1b Content used in interactive digital media
Content types include images (still), audio, video, animation, text, tables, lists, interactive forms, buttons, maps, quizzes and layers (for example expandable panels or hotspots). Good products combine several content types purposefully rather than for decoration: a quiz needs feedback content, a map needs location data layered on an image, a form needs validation and confirmation content. The learner should be able to say, for a given piece of content, what it contributes to purpose and audience engagement, and note where combining too many types risks clutter or slow loading. Activity: for the museum trail brief, list five content types it would need and state what each contributes.

#### R097-1.1c Hardware devices used to access interactive media
Devices include desktop and laptop computers, games consoles, information kiosks, smartphones, smart TVs and tablets. Device choice affects screen size, available input methods, connectivity, processing power and where and how the audience will use the product (in short attentive bursts on a phone versus a longer session at a desktop). Content and interface design must adapt to the device: touch targets are larger on mobile, kiosk interfaces must be simple for one-off public use, and a smart TV interface is controlled at a distance with a remote rather than a pointer. Teaching activity: for the same museum trail, compare how the interface would change between a kiosk in the lobby and a visitor's own phone.
