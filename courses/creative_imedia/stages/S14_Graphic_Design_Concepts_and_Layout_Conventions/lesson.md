# S14_Graphic_Design_Concepts_and_Layout_Conventions - Lesson: Graphic design concepts and layout conventions

## Goal
The learner applies alignment, typography, colour systems and white space, keeps the visual identity consistent, and knows typical layouts for common graphic products.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R094-2.1a - Concepts of graphic design: use visual identity, alignment, typography, colour systems and white space to make clear, consistent graphics
- R094-2.1b - Layout conventions for graphic products: know typical layouts for adverts, covers, games, leaflets, magazines, multimedia, packaging, posters and web graphics

## How to teach this
Show a badly laid out poster (mixed fonts, text touching the edges, clashing colours) and ask the learner to say everything that is wrong before you name any concept. Then rebuild it step by step using each concept.

#### R094-2.1a Concepts of graphic design
The specification lists five concepts. Application of visual identity: graphics for the client must incorporate the logo, colours and fonts consistently, forming a house style, so the audience recognises the brand. Alignment: elements line up on a grid or guides (left, right, centre or justified) so the layout looks ordered and professional. Typography: fonts, sizes and styles chosen for a clear message: headline larger and bolder, body copy readable, no more than two or three font families, good contrast with the background. Use of colour and colour systems: colour conveys meaning and mood, and systems such as Pantone and NCS give consistent, reproducible colours across print runs; colours trend over time. White space (negative space) is empty area that gives elements room, improves readability and directs the eye. Teach a critique checklist: Is the identity applied? Are things aligned? Is text legible? Do colours match meaning? Is there breathing room? Common error: filling every gap because it feels empty.

#### R094-2.1b Layout conventions for graphic products
Different products have typical arrangements. Advertisements: strong image or headline, short copy, logo and contact or call to action. CD/DVD/Blu-ray covers: title, artwork or key image, credits, age rating logos and barcode. Games: title and key art, platform and rating symbols, strap line and back-cover screenshots. Leaflets: cover panel, folded panels with headings, body copy, images and contact details. Magazine and book covers: masthead or title at top, cover lines, main image, price and barcode. Multimedia products: title screens and menus. Packaging: front panel with brand and product name, information panels, nutrition or legal information and barcodes. Posters: dominant image, headline, key details and logo readable from a distance. Web images and graphics: banners and buttons at standard sizes. The layout ideas listed are headlines and copy, image content, titles and mastheads and additional information. Billboards are not included, though they may be discussed as advertisements.
