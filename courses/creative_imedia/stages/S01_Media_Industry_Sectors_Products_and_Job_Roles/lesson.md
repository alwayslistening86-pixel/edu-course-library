# S01_Media_Industry_Sectors_Products_and_Job_Roles - Lesson: The media industry: sectors, products and job roles

## Goal
The learner can name the sectors and products of the media industry, say which sectors use a product, and explain what each creative, technical and senior job role contributes and in which production phase.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-1.1 - Media industry sectors and products: know the traditional and new media sectors, the products of the industry and that one product can serve several sectors
- R093-1.2 - Job roles in the media industry: know the creative, technical and senior roles, their responsibilities and phases, and why one person may do several roles

## How to teach this
Open with a familiar product: 'Think of a video game trailer you saw this month. Which sectors of the media industry might have produced it, and how many different people worked on it?' Let the learner build a list before you show the specification wording. This exam unit rewards knowing the lists AND explaining how they apply to a scenario, so every fact should be tried out on a client situation.

#### R093-1.1 Media industry sectors and products
The specification splits the industry into traditional media (film, television, radio, print publishing) and new media (computer games, interactive media, the internet, digital publishing). Learners must know the sectors, know that they are evolving (print now publishes digitally, television streams on demand), and know the products each sector makes and uses: video, audio, music, animation, special effects (SFX and VFX), digital imaging and graphics, social media platforms and apps, digital games, comics and graphic novels, websites, multimedia, eBooks and AR/VR. The key idea is that one product can serve several sectors: a video appears in film, on television and on the internet; a comic can be printed or an eBook. Activity: give five products and ask the learner to place each in as many sectors as they can and justify it. Common error: treating a product as belonging to one sector only.

#### R093-1.2 Job roles in the media industry
Roles are grouped as creative (animator, content creator, copywriter, graphic designer, illustrator/graphic artist, photographer, script writer, web designer), technical (camera operator, games programmer/developer, sound editor, audio technician, video editor, web developer) and senior (campaign manager, creative director, director, editor, production manager). For each role the learner must know the main responsibilities and how it contributes to a product, for example a script writer produces the words and structure that the director and actors realise. Some roles belong to one phase (a location scout in pre-production, a video editor mostly in post-production) while others span phases (a director). On a small project one person may do several roles, because the size and scale of the production limits the budget and team. The specification does not ask for the specific skills a role needs. Exam habit: link the role to the scenario ('the web designer plans the site layout so users find the menu easily') and never stop at a definition.

## Explicitly not here
Audience, purpose and client work (next stages), pre-production documents (S05, S06) and file formats (S10 onwards).
