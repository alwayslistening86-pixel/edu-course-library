# S13_R094_Overview_and_Visual_Identity - Lesson: R094 overview: what visual identity is and how it is designed

## Goal
The learner knows how R094 is assessed, explains the purpose and components of a visual identity and applies graphics, typography, colour and layout to a brand's business type, values and market position.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R094-A - R094 assessment overview: two tasks, strands and mark bands: know the two tasks, the strands, the 50 marks, the three mark bands and how independence is required
- R094-1.1a - Purpose of visual identity and its component features: explain recognition, brand establishment, loyalty and visual communication, and the name, logo and slogan components
- R094-1.1b - Elements of visual identity and design style: apply graphics, typography, colour palette and layout to business type, brand values and economy, mid-range or high-end positioning

## How to teach this
Open with three logos the learner knows well: 'What does each one tell you about the company before you read a single word?' Then explain that R094 is a practical unit: they will design and justify a visual identity, plan and create digital graphics, and evidence every step themselves. Everything taught here is practised on original practice briefs, never the live OCR set assignment, which the school gives out only when the summative assessment begins.

#### R094-A R094 assessment overview: two tasks, strands and mark bands
Unit R094 is an OCR-set assignment of two practical tasks taking about 10-12 hours (30 GLH unit), worth 50 marks (50 UMS), marked by the teacher and moderated by OCR. Task 1 covers Topic Areas 1 and 2: strand 1a design concept for the visual identity (6 marks), 1b justification of fitness for purpose (8), 1c planning documentation for the digital graphic (6). Task 2 covers Topic Areas 2 and 3: 2a create and save the visual identity (6), 2b prepare assets (6), 2c create the digital graphic (12), 2d save and export the graphics in the required formats (6). Each strand is marked in mark bands (MB1 limited/basic, MB2 adequate/sound, MB3 effective/fully suitable/comprehensive). If work meets no MB1 criteria for a task it scores zero for that task. Students must make their own decisions and must not be led through a process, told which software or tools to use, or given a bank of assets. Learners should hold a copy of the marking criteria and the specification so they know what evidence to produce.

#### R094-1.1a Purpose of visual identity and its component features
Visual identity is how a brand or business looks and communicates: the visible part of the wider brand identity. Purposes: recognition and familiarity (people recognise it instantly), establishing a brand, developing brand loyalty, and visual communication with audiences and consumers. Its component features are the name, the logo and the slogan or strap line. A visual identity communicates the nature of the business and its products or services, so a children's toy shop and a law firm need very different identities. If the impression the identity creates does not match the desired brand identity, it is not fit for purpose. Teaching activity: cover the names on three familiar logos and ask the learner to say what business they think each belongs to and why; then discuss what would go wrong if a funeral director used a bubbly cartoon logo.

#### R094-1.1b Elements of visual identity and design style
The elements are graphics (a shape or symbol), typography, colour palette and its meaning, and layout or complexity. Each combines to shape perception and emotional response: rounded shapes feel friendly, sharp angular shapes feel technical; serif type can feel traditional and trustworthy, rounded sans-serif casual; blue can suggest trust and calm, green nature and health, red energy and urgency, black luxury; a simple layout suggests modern, minimal quality while a busy one suggests fun or value. Design style must reflect business type, brand values and brand positioning: economy (clear, bold, simple, value-focused), mid-range (balanced, friendly, reliable) and high-end (refined, minimal, premium materials and restrained palette). The visual identity must also be relevant and appropriate to the audience and type of market. Activity: give the learner the same fictional business and have them design a rough concept sketch for each of economy and high-end positioning and explain what changed.

## Explicitly not here
Graphic design conventions (S14), graphic properties and licences (S15), planning techniques and evidencing Task 1 (S16), and all creating skills (S17 onwards).
