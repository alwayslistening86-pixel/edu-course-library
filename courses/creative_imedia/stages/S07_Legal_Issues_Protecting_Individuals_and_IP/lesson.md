# S07_Legal_Issues_Protecting_Individuals_and_IP - Lesson: Law and media: protecting individuals and intellectual property

## Goal
The learner explains privacy and permissions, defamation, data protection and intellectual property in media production and what producers must do to comply.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-3.4.1 - Legal considerations that protect individuals: explain privacy and permissions, defamation and data protection and what producers must do to comply
- R093-3.4.2 - Intellectual property rights and using copyrighted material: explain copyright, ideas, patents, trademarks, licences, fair dealing and the implications of unlicensed use

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A YouTuber films a busy high street and uploads it with music from their favourite band. What might go wrong?' Collect the learner's ideas, then sort them into privacy, defamation, data protection and copyright. The specification does not require specific Acts of Parliament, so focus on purposes, duties and consequences.

#### R093-3.4.1 Legal considerations to protect individuals
Privacy and permissions: in a public place a producer can usually photograph or record, but must not harass people or invade their privacy; on private property permission is needed from the owner; publishing and especially commercial use of images of identifiable people normally needs their permission (a release form). Harassment and invasion of privacy are misuse of recording. Defamation is a false statement that harms someone's reputation: libel is written or published (an article, a caption, a post) and slander is spoken (a broadcast comment). Truth is the key protection; producers must check facts and avoid inaccurate claims about real people. Data protection covers the rights of data subjects in the collection, use and storage of personal data: people should know why their data is collected, it should be used only for that purpose, kept securely, kept accurate and not held longer than needed; individuals can ask to see or correct their data. Impacts of inaccurate personal information: reputational damage, distress and legal action against the producer and lost trust.

#### R093-3.4.2 Intellectual property rights and using copyrighted materials
Intellectual property (IP) is creations of the mind that belong to their owner. Copyright protects original creative work such as text, images, music, video and software automatically when it is created; ideas alone are not protected in that way but a written or recorded idea may be (and confidential ideas can be protected by agreements); patents protect inventions; trademarks protect brand names, logos and slogans. Legislation exists to reward creators and prevent theft. Producers must respect IP: get permission, pay fees or licences, use assets with suitable licences such as Creative Commons (with its conditions, for example attribution) or create original work. Fair dealing allows limited use without permission for purposes such as criticism, review, reporting news, education and parody, but it is narrow and does not cover using a whole song in an advert. Watermarks and the copyright symbol signal ownership. Using copyrighted material without permission can lead to takedown, fines, damages, legal action and reputational damage.

## Explicitly not here
Regulation, classification and health and safety (S08). Do not teach or require names of specific Acts.
