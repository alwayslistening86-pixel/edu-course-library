# S02_Purpose_Style_and_Client_Briefs - Lesson: Purpose, style, content and layout, and client requirements

## Goal
The learner explains how style, content and layout are adapted to the purpose of a product, and interprets a client brief to pick out the requirements that shape planning and production.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-2.1 - How style, content and layout link to purpose: explain how colour, genre, language, positioning and style are adapted to advertise, educate, entertain, inform or influence
- R093-2.2 - Client requirements and client brief formats: pick out requirements in a brief, explain how they constrain planning and know the ways briefs are communicated

## How to teach this
Show the learner two products that do the same job differently (for example a cereal advert aimed at children and a bank advert). Ask: 'What is different about the colours, language and layout, and why?' Then hand them a two-line client brief and ask them to underline the keywords that would control a design.

#### R093-2.1 How style, content and layout are linked to the purpose
The five purposes are advertise/promote, educate, entertain, inform and influence. A product may have more than one. The style, content and layout are the tools a producer adapts to meet the purpose: colour, conventions of genre, formal or informal language, positioning of elements, style of audio representation, style of visual representation and tone of language. Examples: an advert for a sports drink uses bright high-energy colour, short punchy informal language and a call to action placed prominently; an educational science video uses a calm tone, clear labelled visuals and a logical order; an influencing product such as a charity poster uses emotive imagery and persuasive language. Teach the exam pattern: name the purpose, name the design choice, then say why it works for that purpose ('the informal tone makes teenagers feel the brand is for them, so they are more likely to engage'). Common error: describing the product without linking to purpose.

#### R093-2.2 Client requirements and client brief formats
A client brief tells the producer what the client wants. Requirements include type of product, purpose, audience, client ethos, content, genre, style, theme and timescales. Learners must recognise keywords in a brief, know which requirements inform planning, and explain how requirements constrain planning and production (a short deadline limits how much can be produced; a strict style stops the designer using a favourite look). They must also interpret requirements to generate ideas: 'eco-friendly' suggests green and natural colours, recycled-look textures and honest, simple language. Briefs arrive in different formats: commission, formal, informal, meeting/discussion, negotiated and written. A negotiated brief is agreed between client and producer, an informal one might be a chat or message, a formal one is a written document. Activity: give a short brief and have the learner produce a table of requirement, evidence in the brief and design idea.

## Explicitly not here
