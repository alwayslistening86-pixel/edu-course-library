# S08_Regulation_Classification_and_Health_and_Safety - Lesson: Regulation, classification, health and safety

## Goal
The learner explains the roles of ASA, Ofcom, BBFC and PEGI, why regulation and classification exist, and how risks are identified and reduced in media production through risk assessments and location recces.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-3.4.3 - Regulation, certification and classification: know ASA, Ofcom, BBFC and PEGI and explain the impact of regulation on production
- R093-3.4.4 - Health and safety, risk assessments and location recces: know common hazards, what producers must do to reduce risk and the purpose of risk assessments and recces

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Why is there a 15 on some films and a 12 on some games, and who decides?' Then ask: 'You are filming on a cliff path. What could go wrong before the camera even rolls?'

#### R093-3.4.3 Regulation, certification and classification
Regulation means official bodies set and enforce rules. The Advertising Standards Authority (ASA) regulates adverts across media, making sure they are legal, decent, honest and truthful and not misleading or harmful. Ofcom, the Office of Communications, regulates broadcasting (television and radio) and telecoms, including standards on harm, offence, fairness and privacy. Classification is done by the British Board of Film Classification (BBFC), which certifies films and video (U, PG, 12A/12, 15, 18 and the restricted R18), and PEGI (Pan European Game Information), which rates games by age (3, 7, 12, 16, 18) with content descriptors for things like violence. Reasons: protect audiences, especially children, from unsuitable content and help parents and viewers choose. Impact on production: producers must design content for the age rating they want (cutting a scene to get a lower certificate widens the audience and sales), follow advert rules, and may face sanctions, bans or lost revenue. The specification asks learners to know the roles and areas of responsibility, not the detailed codes.

#### R093-3.4.4 Health and safety, risk assessments and location recces
Common hazards in media production: trailing cables, unstable lighting stands, heavy equipment, working at height, electrical faults, hot lights, crowded or public locations, traffic, poor weather, long screen hours and repetitive strain, and stunts or special effects. Producers must reduce risks by taking sensible actions: tidy or tape cables, secure stands, check equipment, brief the team, plan breaks, wear suitable clothing and have first aid available. A risk assessment lists each hazard, who could be harmed, how likely and how serious it is, and the controls that reduce the risk; it is completed before work starts and reviewed. A location recce is a visit to a filming or photography location beforehand to check access, power, space, light, noise, hazards, permissions and safe places for equipment. Health and safety applies in all phases of production, including post-production where display screen use can cause eye strain. Learners do not need to create a risk assessment or recce in the exam, or name specific Acts.
