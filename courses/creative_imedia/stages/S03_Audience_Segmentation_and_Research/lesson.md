# S03_Audience_Segmentation_and_Research - Lesson: Audience segmentation, research methods and data

## Goal
The learner segments an audience, explains how audience characteristics shape a product, and compares research methods, sources and types of data with advantages and disadvantages.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-2.3 - Audience demographics and segmentation: know the segmentation categories and explain how audience characteristics shape design and production
- R093-2.4 - Research methods, sources and types of data: compare primary and secondary research and qualitative and quantitative data, with advantages and disadvantages

## How to teach this
Ask: 'You are making a game for 7-year-olds and another for 40-year-old commuters. What would you need to know about each group before you started, and how would you find out?' That single question introduces segmentation and research together.

#### R093-2.3 Audience demographics and segmentation
Segmentation divides an audience into groups with shared characteristics so a product can be designed for them. The categories are age, gender, occupation, income, education, location, interests and lifestyle. Learners need an example for each: age 8-12; students; income under or over a threshold; a rural or urban location; interests such as football; lifestyle such as busy commuters. The reasons and benefits: a product aimed at a defined group is more likely to appeal, marketing can be targeted, and money is not wasted on the wrong people. Characteristics influence design and production: young children need bright colours, large simple text and short sequences; low-income audiences may value free content; a location-based audience may need local references. Exam habit: never say 'teenagers like it', say which characteristic and how the design changes because of it.

#### R093-2.4 Research methods, sources and types of data
Primary research is collected first-hand: focus groups, interviews, online surveys and questionnaires. Secondary research uses existing sources: books and journals, internet sites, magazines and newspapers, television. Reasons for research: to understand audience needs, find out what competitors do and reduce risk. Primary advantages: specific to your needs and up to date; disadvantages: takes time, costs money, sample may be small or biased. Secondary advantages: quick and cheap; disadvantages: may be out of date, not specific, or unreliable. Each method has its own points: focus groups give rich opinions but can be influenced by strong voices; online surveys reach many people quickly but responses may be shallow; interviews give depth but are slow. Data is qualitative (opinions, descriptions, why) or quantitative (numbers, counts, percentages). Activity: give the learner five research findings and ask them to label qualitative or quantitative and primary or secondary.
