# S04_Media_Codes - Lesson: Media codes: how meaning, impact and engagement are created

## Goal
The learner recognises technical, symbolic and written codes and explains how a combination of codes works for a given audience, purpose and context.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R093-2.5a - Technical, symbolic and written media codes: know the three families of media code and the techniques that belong to each
- R093-2.5b - How media codes convey meaning, create impact and engage audiences: explain how animation, audio, camera, colour, graphics, interactivity, lighting, mise-en-scene, movement, transitions and typography work together for a given audience, purpose and context

## How to teach this
Play or describe the first ten seconds of a horror-film trailer and ask the learner to list everything they can see and hear. Then ask which of those details were chosen on purpose and what each was meant to make the audience feel.

#### R093-2.5a Technical, symbolic and written media codes
The specification names three families. Technical codes are created by equipment and editing: camera techniques (angles such as high, low and eye level; shots such as long, mid and close-up; movement such as pan, tilt, tracking and zoom), lighting (intensity or levels and position), audio (dialogue, music genre, silence, sound effects, vocal intonation), animation, movement and transitions. Symbolic codes carry meaning through what is shown: colour, mise-en-scene (everything placed within the frame such as setting, props, costume and positioning) and graphics. Written codes use language: typography (font types, font size, emphasis), headlines, captions and the tone of language. OCR lists the techniques but does not publish a definitive mapping to the three families, so accept a sensible classification with a reason. The learner must know the vocabulary, for example that a low angle makes a subject look powerful and that silence can build tension.

#### R093-2.5b How codes convey meaning, create impact and engage audiences
The exam asks how, not just what. Meaning is what the audience understands (red means danger). Impact is the strength of the effect (a sudden loud sound effect). Engagement is holding attention (interactivity that makes the user choose). The strongest answers combine content and codes: a low-angle close-up, lit from below with a low drone in the score, makes a villain feel threatening. Codes must relate to audience, purpose and context: bold sans-serif type and fast transitions suit a teen sports advert, while a serif font, slow fades and soft piano suit a luxury perfume. Interactivity is also a code: buttons, choices and feedback engage a user. Teach the chain: code, effect on the audience, reason it suits this audience or purpose. Common error: listing codes without saying what they achieve, or applying codes that do not fit the audience.
