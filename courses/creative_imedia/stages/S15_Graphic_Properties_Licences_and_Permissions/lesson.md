# S15_Graphic_Properties_Licences_and_Permissions - Lesson: Technical properties of graphics, licences and permissions

## Goal
The learner chooses between bitmap and vector for a purpose, explains colour depth, colour mode, compression, transparency, scalability and compatibility, and works out the licence and permission position of client, internet, logo, photograph and stock assets.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R094-2.2a - Technical properties of bitmap and vector graphics: choose bitmap or vector using colour depth, colour mode, compression, transparency, scalability, file size and compatibility
- R094-2.2b - Licences and permissions for assets: judge the rights and permissions for client, internet, logo, photograph and stock assets and record them in an asset table

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Your logo looks perfect on screen. The printer says it is too blurry for a banner. What happened, and how do you prevent it?' Then ask: 'You found a great photo online. Can you use it in a client's poster?'

#### R094-2.2a Technical properties of bitmap and vector graphics
Bitmap/raster properties: colour depth (bits per pixel: more bits means more colours and larger files), colour mode (RGB for screens, CMYK for print, greyscale), compression settings (lossy JPEG or lossless PNG), overall quality and transparency (whether a background can be transparent, as in PNG but not JPEG). Limitations: raster files support only so many colours (GIF 256), do not scale well (enlarging blurs them) and only some formats hold transparency. Vector properties: compatibility (which software opens the file), file size (usually small for simple shapes), scalability (resize to any size without loss) and software support. Benefits: vector logos scale for large print use. Learners should use both types sensibly, but the specification does not require creating graphics only in vector. Teaching task: give three uses (a favicon, a van livery, a product photograph) and have the learner choose type, colour mode and format and justify.

#### R094-2.2b Licences and permissions to use assets
Assets can come from client images, the internet, logos, photographs and stock libraries. Each carries rights. Client images and logos: the client owns them, and using them requires permission, usually given in the brief; third-party logos need the owner's permission. Internet images: most are copyright protected; use search engine filters (size, type, licence) to find images marked for reuse, and read the licence conditions. Stock libraries have terms and conditions: free or paid, with limits on commercial use, editing and redistribution. Social media content has limits on reuse: it belongs to the poster. Students may permit use of their own photographs and graphics in a client product. Record licence and copyright information in an asset table (source, owner, licence, permission, date). The unit does not require contacting owners or paying fees, only understanding and recording. Common error: saying 'I found it on Google' as the source.
