## Test items
1. Explain how SSRIs are thought to reduce the symptoms of OCD. [3 marks]
2. Outline evidence from twin studies for a genetic explanation of OCD, and explain one limitation of this evidence. [4 marks]
3. [8-mark style] Discuss the neural explanation for OCD. [8 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 SSRIs block the reuptake (reabsorption) of serotonin into the presynaptic neuron after release; B1 this leaves more serotonin available in the synapse to bind to postsynaptic receptors; B1 which is thought to help normalise the abnormal brain functioning (e.g. in serotonin-related circuits) associated with OCD symptoms.
2. [4] B1 concordance rates for OCD are consistently higher in monozygotic (identical) than dizygotic (non-identical) twins; B1 supporting a genetic contribution to OCD; B2 limitation: concordance is well below 100% even in identical twins, showing environmental factors also play a substantial role, so genes alone cannot fully explain OCD (aetiological heterogeneity).
3. [8] Mark out of 8 using AQA's levels of response (AO1 knowledge + AO3 discussion/evaluation): Level 4 (7-8) knowledge is accurate and reasonably detailed; discussion/evaluation is effective; answer is clear and coherent. Level 3 (5-6) knowledge is evident, generally accurate; discussion/evaluation is presented, if not fully developed; answer is mostly clear. Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is limited/mainly descriptive; discussion/evaluation is limited or superficial. Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is very limited; discussion/evaluation is barely discernible. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate knowledge of the role of serotonin, the basal ganglia and the orbitofrontal cortex, with discussion of supporting evidence (e.g. brain-imaging comparisons of OCD patients and controls, and the effectiveness of serotonin-based drug treatment as indirect support), and limitations (e.g. difficulty establishing whether neural abnormalities are a cause or a consequence of OCD, and that OCD is unlikely to be explained by a single neural pathway alone).
