## Test items
1. Explain the difference between covert and overt observation, including one strength and one limitation of each. [6 marks]
2. Outline what a case study is, and evaluate its use in psychological research. [6 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 covert observation: participants are unaware they are being observed; B1 strength: reduces demand characteristics/participants behave more naturally; B1 limitation: raises ethical/informed consent concerns; B1 overt observation: participants know they are being observed; B1 strength: addresses ethical/consent concerns; B1 limitation: risks demand characteristics/participants altering their natural behaviour.
2. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit an accurate definition (detailed, in-depth investigation of a single individual/small group/event, drawing on multiple sources of data); evaluation should include the value of studying rare phenomena and gaining rich, holistic insight, versus the limitation of generalising from a single case, and concerns about subjectivity/researcher interpretation and reliance on incomplete or unreliable retrospective information.
