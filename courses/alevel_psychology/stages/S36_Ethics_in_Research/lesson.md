# S36_Ethics_in_Research - Lesson: Ethics in research

## Goal
The learner explains key ethical issues in psychological research and how they are addressed, using the British Psychological Society's Code of Ethics and Conduct.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RM7 - Ethical issues in research and the BPS Code of Ethics and Conduct

## How to teach this
Ask: if deceiving participants about the true purpose of a study would make the results far more valid -- but the participants would be upset to learn they'd been misled -- how should a researcher decide what to do? Every named study, theorist and theory in this course (e.g. Asch, Milgram, Zimbardo, Adorno, Moscovici, Atkinson and Shiffrin, Baddeley and Hitch, Loftus and Palmer, Johnson and Scott, Yuille and Cutshall, Geiselman, Schaffer and Emerson, Lorenz, Harlow, Bowlby, Ainsworth, Van Ijzendoorn and Kroonenberg, Rutter, Hazan and Shaver, Beck, Ellis, Wiles, Wundt, Pavlov, Skinner, Bandura, Freud, Maslow, Rogers, Maguire, Sperry, Siffre, Gottesman, Bateson, Meehl, Buss, Walster, Kerckhoff and Davis, Thibaut and Kelley, Rusbult, Duck, McCutcheon, Dollard, Tinbergen, Wilson and Daly, Irwin and Cressey, Sykes) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of psychology, checked against real psychological knowledge and AQA's own published subject content when this course was built -- present it as real research, never as illustrative fiction, and correct the learner explicitly if they misattribute a study or misstate a finding. Research Methods (4.2.3, at least 25% of assessment across all three papers, plus a minimum 10% mathematical-skills requirement) is examined throughout, not only in its own stages -- sampling, experimental design and inferential statistics can appear applied to any topic area. Every numerical worked example (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, the sign test) was computed and verified with Python when this course was built, and the learner should always show full working for a calculation, since AQA credits correct method as well as a correct final answer. Extended-writing ('Discuss...'/'Evaluate...') questions are marked holistically against AQA's levels-of-response scheme (see `rubric.json`), not by a checklist of individual points -- teach the learner to build a structured AO1 (accurate knowledge) + AO3 (effective discussion/evaluation), and where the question is scenario-based, AO2 (explicit application to the scenario) response, not a list of disconnected facts.

#### RM7 Ethical issues in research and the BPS Code of Ethics and Conduct
**Ethical issues in research.** The **British Psychological Society (BPS)** publishes a Code of Ethics and Conduct that UK psychological researchers are expected to follow, built around core ethical principles. **Informed consent**: participants should be given enough information about the study's procedures (though not always its full aim, if deception is used, see below) to make a genuinely voluntary, informed decision about whether to take part, usually confirmed by signing a consent form; when full informed consent is not possible (e.g. with deception, or with very young children), alternatives include **presumptive consent** (asking a similar group of people, not participants themselves, whether they would find the study acceptable) or, for children, consent from a parent/guardian. **Deception**: deliberately misleading or withholding information from participants about the true nature/purpose of a study; sometimes necessary to avoid demand characteristics undermining a study's validity, but must be justified against the potential harm it may cause, and is followed by a full **debrief**. **Protection from harm**: participants should not be exposed to risks greater than those encountered in everyday life, covering physical and psychological harm (e.g. embarrassment, distress); researchers must stop a study if harm becomes apparent, and offer support/counselling if needed. **Confidentiality**: participants' personal data and individual results should be protected, usually by using anonymous data (e.g. participant numbers rather than names) unless a participant explicitly consents otherwise. **The right to withdraw**: participants must be told, and reminded as necessary, that they can leave the study at any point, and withdraw their data (including retrospectively, after taking part), without needing to give a reason and without penalty. **Debriefing**: after the study, participants should be told its true aim and procedure in full (especially important if deception was used), given the opportunity to ask questions, have any distress addressed, and be reminded of their right to withdraw their data even at this late stage; debriefing is not itself a way to justify otherwise unethical treatment during the study, but a way to address issues (e.g. from deception) after the fact. Evaluation: ethical guidelines provide important, generally clear and actionable protection for participants and help maintain public trust in psychological research, but some ethical dilemmas (e.g. the trade-off between a study's scientific/social value, which may require some deception to achieve valid results, and full transparency) cannot be fully resolved by guidelines alone, requiring judgement, and some historically important studies (e.g. Milgram's, see SI4-SI5, or Zimbardo's, see SI3) would very likely not be approved under today's stricter standards, raising ongoing debate about how to weigh scientific/social value against participant welfare.
