## Test items
1. A researcher wants to study genuine helping behaviour, but believes that telling participants in advance that helping behaviour is being measured would make them behave unnaturally. Discuss the ethical issues this raises and how the researcher could address them. [6 marks]
2. Outline the BPS guidelines on protection from harm and confidentiality. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit identification of deception (withholding the true aim) and the consequent difficulty of full informed consent, and discussion of how these could be addressed: e.g. presumptive consent from a similar group beforehand, minimising unnecessary deception, ensuring no undue distress/protection from harm during the study, and a full debrief afterward explaining the true aim, offering the right to withdraw data even at that late stage, and addressing any concerns raised.
2. [4] B1 protection from harm: participants should not be exposed to risks greater than in everyday life, covering physical and psychological harm; B1 researchers must stop a study and offer support if harm becomes apparent; B1 confidentiality: participants' personal data/individual results should be protected; B1 usually through anonymising data (e.g. participant numbers rather than names) unless the participant explicitly agrees otherwise.
