# S50_Neural_Hormonal_and_Evolutionary_Explanations_of_Aggression - Practice: Neural/hormonal mechanisms and ethological/evolutionary explanations of aggression

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Explain how low levels of serotonin are linked to increased aggression. [2 marks]
2. Outline Tinbergen's research with stickleback fish and what it demonstrated about innate releasing mechanisms. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 serotonin normally has a calming, inhibitory effect on neural activity linked to impulsive/aggressive behaviour; B1 lower serotonin levels are associated with reduced control/inhibition over aggressive impulses.
2. [2] B1 the sight of the colour red (resembling a rival male's underbelly) reliably triggered a fixed, stereotyped aggressive display in male sticklebacks, even toward a crude, non-fish-shaped red model; B1 demonstrating a specific, simple sign stimulus can trigger a fixed action pattern via an innate releasing mechanism.
