# S05_Minority_Influence_and_Social_Change - Practice: Minority influence and social change

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Give two factors that make a minority more influential. [4 marks]
2. Outline what Moscovici's blue-green slides study found. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 B2 any two of consistency (diachronic/synchronic agreement over time and between members), commitment (e.g. via the augmentation principle -- sacrifice suggesting genuine belief), flexibility (willingness to adapt/negotiate rather than being rigid), each briefly explained.
2. [2] B1 a consistent minority of two confederates saying 'green' to blue slides led some genuine participants to also say 'green' on at least one trial (about 8.42% of all trials, 32% at least once); B1 correctly attributed to Moscovici (1969) and shown as evidence minorities can produce genuine influence.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
