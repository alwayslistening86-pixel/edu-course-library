## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 the matching hypothesis proposes people tend to select partners who are a reasonably close match to their own level of physical attractiveness, rather than simply seeking the most attractive partner possible; B1 partly to avoid the risk of rejection from pursuing a much more attractive partner; B1 Walster et al.'s original 'computer dance' study found only limited direct support; B1 but later research using established couples has found stronger evidence that partners' attractiveness levels correlate.
2. [6] B1 social demography: proximity/social class/religion/education restrict the realistic 'field of availables', an early-stage filter; B1 similarity in attitudes: important in relatively short-duration relationships, since major disagreement can end a developing relationship; B1 complementarity of needs: becomes more important in longer-term relationships, as partners value how each meets the other's (sometimes different) needs; B1 correctly explains social demography operates before people even meet many potential partners; B1 correctly explains similarity matters earlier, as attitudes are assessed once a relationship begins; B1 correctly explains complementarity becomes more relevant as a relationship matures and deepens.
