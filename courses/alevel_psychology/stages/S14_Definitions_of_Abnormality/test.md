## Test items
1. Outline Jahoda's deviation from ideal mental health definition of abnormality, and give one limitation of this definition. [4 marks]
2. Compare statistical infrequency and failure to function adequately as definitions of abnormality. [6 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 Jahoda proposed a set of positive criteria for good mental health (e.g. positive self-view, resistance to stress, self-actualisation, autonomy, accurate perception of reality, environmental mastery); B1 abnormality is judged by the absence/degree of absence of these criteria; B2 limitation: e.g. an unrealistically demanding standard almost no one fully meets, or the criteria (e.g. self-actualisation) are vague/reflect a particular Western, individualistic cultural ideal.
2. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate description of both definitions (statistical infrequency: rarity relative to the population/normal distribution; failure to function adequately: inability to cope with ordinary demands of daily life, e.g. Rosenhan and Seligman's criteria) and effective evaluation, e.g. statistical infrequency's failure to distinguish desirable from undesirable rare traits, and common-but-serious conditions being missed; the subjectivity of judging 'coping' in failure to function, and cases where apparently non-functional behaviour is adaptive.
