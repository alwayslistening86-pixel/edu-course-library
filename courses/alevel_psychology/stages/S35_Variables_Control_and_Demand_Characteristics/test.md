## Test items
1. Explain what demand characteristics are, and describe one way researchers can try to reduce their effect. [4 marks]
2. A researcher testing a new memory-training technique interacts personally with participants in the experimental group, but not with those in the control group. Explain how this could produce an investigator effect, and how the researcher could address this using a double-blind design. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 demand characteristics are cues in a study that reveal its aim/hypothesis to participants; B1 who may then consciously or unconsciously alter their natural behaviour as a result (e.g. the 'please-U' effect); B1 a valid method to reduce them, e.g. using a single- or double-blind design so the participant does not know the study's true aim/which condition they are in; B1 correctly explained why this reduces demand characteristics.
2. [4] B1 the researcher's own behaviour (e.g. warmth, enthusiasm, expectation) toward the experimental group could unintentionally influence participants' performance/behaviour, independently of the actual training technique; B1 this is an investigator effect, confounding the comparison between groups; B1 in a double-blind design, neither the participant nor the researcher directly running the session would know which condition/group a participant is in; B1 reducing the chance the researcher's own behaviour or recording is biased by knowing the expected outcome.
