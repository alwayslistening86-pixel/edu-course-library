## Test items
1. Distinguish between a directional and a non-directional hypothesis, and explain when each would typically be used. [4 marks]
2. Explain one strength and one limitation of using a correlation rather than an experiment to investigate a relationship between two variables. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 directional hypothesis: predicts the specific direction of a difference/relationship; B1 typically used when previous research/theory gives a clear basis for a particular direction; B1 non-directional hypothesis: predicts only that a difference/relationship will exist, without specifying direction; B1 typically used when there is no clear existing basis for predicting a specific direction.
2. [4] B2 strength: e.g. can investigate a relationship where a true experiment would be unethical/impractical (e.g. cannot manipulate the variable), explained with an example; B2 limitation: cannot establish cause and effect, since a third variable could be responsible for the observed relationship, explained.
