# S33_Correlations_Aims_and_Hypotheses - Lesson: Correlations, aims and hypotheses

## Goal

## How to teach this
Ask: if ice cream sales and drowning deaths both rise in summer, does that mean ice cream causes drowning -- or is something else going on? Every named study, theorist and theory in this course (e.g. Asch, Milgram, Zimbardo, Adorno, Moscovici, Atkinson and Shiffrin, Baddeley and Hitch, Loftus and Palmer, Johnson and Scott, Yuille and Cutshall, Geiselman, Schaffer and Emerson, Lorenz, Harlow, Bowlby, Ainsworth, Van Ijzendoorn and Kroonenberg, Rutter, Hazan and Shaver, Beck, Ellis, Wiles, Wundt, Pavlov, Skinner, Bandura, Freud, Maslow, Rogers, Maguire, Sperry, Siffre, Gottesman, Bateson, Meehl, Buss, Walster, Kerckhoff and Davis, Thibaut and Kelley, Rusbult, Duck, McCutcheon, Dollard, Tinbergen, Wilson and Daly, Irwin and Cressey, Sykes) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of psychology, checked against real psychological knowledge and AQA's own published subject content when this course was built -- present it as real research, never as illustrative fiction, and correct the learner explicitly if they misattribute a study or misstate a finding. Research Methods (4.2.3, at least 25% of assessment across all three papers, plus a minimum 10% mathematical-skills requirement) is examined throughout, not only in its own stages -- sampling, experimental design and inferential statistics can appear applied to any topic area. Every numerical worked example (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, the sign test) was computed and verified with Python when this course was built, and the learner should always show full working for a calculation, since AQA credits correct method as well as a correct final answer. Extended-writing ('Discuss...'/'Evaluate...') questions are marked holistically against AQA's levels-of-response scheme (see `rubric.json`), not by a checklist of individual points -- teach the learner to build a structured AO1 (accurate knowledge) + AO3 (effective discussion/evaluation), and where the question is scenario-based, AO2 (explicit application to the scenario) response, not a list of disconnected facts.

#### RM4 Correlations; aims and hypotheses (directional/non-directional)
**Correlations.** A correlation is a technique (not itself an experiment) for measuring the strength and direction of the relationship between two co-variables, neither of which is manipulated by the researcher (unlike an experiment's manipulated IV). A **positive correlation** is when both co-variables increase together (e.g. more revision time associated with higher exam grades); a **negative correlation** is when one co-variable increases as the other decreases (e.g. more stress associated with poorer sleep quality); a correlation close to zero indicates no meaningful linear relationship between the variables. Crucially, a correlation on its own **cannot establish cause and effect**: even a strong correlation between two co-variables does not show that one directly causes the other, since a third, unmeasured variable might independently be causing both (and the direction of causality, if any exists, also cannot be determined from a correlation alone). Strength: correlations can be a useful, efficient way to investigate a relationship where a true experiment would be unethical or impractical to conduct (e.g. investigating a link between early attachment type and later relationship quality, where attachment type cannot ethically be manipulated), and can suggest hypotheses worth testing experimentally later; limitation: the inability to establish causation, and the risk of variables that only appear related in a straight line (linear) being missed if the true relationship is actually curved (curvilinear).

**Aims and hypotheses.** An **aim** is a general statement of the purpose of an investigation -- what the researcher intends to study and why. A **hypothesis** is a testable, precise, operationalised statement predicting the outcome of the study, derived from the aim. The **null hypothesis** predicts there will be no difference/relationship (or that any difference/relationship found is due to chance alone); the **alternative (experimental) hypothesis** predicts there will be a genuine difference/relationship. An alternative hypothesis can be **directional (one-tailed)**, predicting the specific direction of a difference/relationship (e.g. 'participants who revise using flashcards will score significantly higher than participants who revise by re-reading notes'), typically used when previous research/theory gives a clear basis for predicting a particular direction; or **non-directional (two-tailed)**, predicting only that a difference/relationship will exist, without specifying its direction (e.g. 'there will be a significant difference in exam scores between participants using flashcards and participants re-reading notes'), typically used when there is no clear existing basis for predicting a specific direction. Both variables in a hypothesis must be clearly **operationalised** -- defined in a way that makes them precisely measurable (see RM5).
