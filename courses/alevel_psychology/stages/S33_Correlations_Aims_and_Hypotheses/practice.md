# S33_Correlations_Aims_and_Hypotheses - Practice: Correlations, aims and hypotheses

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Explain why a correlation cannot establish cause and effect. [2 marks]
2. Write a suitable directional (one-tailed) alternative hypothesis for a study comparing recall scores between a group who sleep 8 hours and a group who sleep 4 hours before a memory test. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 a correlation shows only that two co-variables are related/vary together, not that one causes the other; B1 a third, unmeasured variable might be independently causing both co-variables (or the direction of any causal effect cannot be determined).
2. [2] B1 correctly predicts a specific direction of difference (e.g. 'participants who sleep 8 hours will recall significantly more words than participants who sleep 4 hours'); B1 both variables are operationalised/clearly measurable.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
