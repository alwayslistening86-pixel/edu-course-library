#### SI1 Types and explanations of conformity (internalisation, identification, compliance; ISI/NSI)
**Types of conformity.** Conformity is a change in a person's behaviour or opinions as a result of real or imagined group pressure. AQA distinguishes three types, differing in how deep and lasting the change is. **Compliance**: the shallowest type -- publicly going along with the majority view while privately disagreeing; the change lasts only as long as the group is present and is usually motivated by a wish to be accepted or to avoid disapproval. **Identification**: conforming to the opinions/behaviour of a group because membership of that group is valued, even if the person does not fully agree in private; the change persists as long as the person values membership of that group (e.g. adopting the views of a new social circle). **Internalisation**: the deepest and most permanent type -- both publicly and privately accepting a group's view as correct, so that the change persists even when the group is no longer present, typically because the group's view has become genuinely integrated into the person's own belief system.

**Explanations for conformity.** **Informational social influence (ISI)**: people conform because they want to be right, and look to others -- assumed to have more knowledge or a better grasp of a situation, especially one that is ambiguous, novel or a crisis -- for information about how to behave; this is a cognitive process (about what is correct) and typically leads to internalisation, since the person genuinely comes to believe the group's view. **Normative social influence (NSI)**: people conform because they want to be liked, accepted and approved of, and to avoid the social rejection/ridicule of standing out; this is an emotional rather than a cognitive process and typically leads only to compliance, since the underlying private belief does not change. NSI is more likely when a group is important to the individual (e.g. friends rather than strangers) or when the person is anxious about rejection; ISI is more likely in situations of uncertainty.
