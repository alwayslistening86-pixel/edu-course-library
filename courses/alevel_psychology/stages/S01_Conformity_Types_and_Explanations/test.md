## Test items
1. Explain the difference between informational social influence and normative social influence, and state which type of conformity (internalisation or compliance) each is more likely to produce. [6 marks]
2. A new employee starts agreeing with their team's working practices even though they privately still prefer their old methods, purely because they want the team to like them. Identify the type of conformity shown and the explanation that best accounts for it, justifying your answer. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 informational social influence: conforming because we want to be right, looking to others for information, especially in an ambiguous/novel situation; B1 this typically produces internalisation, since the group's view is genuinely accepted as correct; B1 normative social influence: conforming because we want to be liked/accepted and to avoid social rejection; B1 this typically produces compliance only, since the underlying private view does not change; B1 correct example or situational factor for ISI (e.g. ambiguity/crisis); B1 correct example or situational factor for NSI (e.g. importance of the group, fear of rejection).
2. [4] B1 identifies compliance (public change only, underlying private view unchanged); B1 identifies normative social influence as the explanation; B1 justifies via the desire to be liked/accepted by the team, not a genuine belief the new practices are correct; B1 correctly distinguishes this from informational social influence (which would involve genuinely believing the new practices are better).
