# S21_Biological_Approach - Lesson: The biological approach

## Goal
The learner describes and evaluates the biological approach, including the influence of genes, biological structures and neurochemistry, the genotype/phenotype distinction, the genetic basis of behaviour, and the role of evolution in explaining behaviour.

## Syllabus items taught here
- APP4 - The biological approach (genes, biological structures, neurochemistry, genotype/phenotype, evolution)

## How to teach this
Ask: if identical twins share 100% of their genes, why do they still sometimes differ noticeably in personality or mental health? Every named study, theorist and theory in this course (e.g. Asch, Milgram, Zimbardo, Adorno, Moscovici, Atkinson and Shiffrin, Baddeley and Hitch, Loftus and Palmer, Johnson and Scott, Yuille and Cutshall, Geiselman, Schaffer and Emerson, Lorenz, Harlow, Bowlby, Ainsworth, Van Ijzendoorn and Kroonenberg, Rutter, Hazan and Shaver, Beck, Ellis, Wiles, Wundt, Pavlov, Skinner, Bandura, Freud, Maslow, Rogers, Maguire, Sperry, Siffre, Gottesman, Bateson, Meehl, Buss, Walster, Kerckhoff and Davis, Thibaut and Kelley, Rusbult, Duck, McCutcheon, Dollard, Tinbergen, Wilson and Daly, Irwin and Cressey, Sykes) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of psychology, checked against real psychological knowledge and AQA's own published subject content when this course was built -- present it as real research, never as illustrative fiction, and correct the learner explicitly if they misattribute a study or misstate a finding. Research Methods (4.2.3, at least 25% of assessment across all three papers, plus a minimum 10% mathematical-skills requirement) is examined throughout, not only in its own stages -- sampling, experimental design and inferential statistics can appear applied to any topic area. Every numerical worked example (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation, the sign test) was computed and verified with Python when this course was built, and the learner should always show full working for a calculation, since AQA credits correct method as well as a correct final answer. Extended-writing ('Discuss...'/'Evaluate...') questions are marked holistically against AQA's levels-of-response scheme (see `rubric.json`), not by a checklist of individual points -- teach the learner to build a structured AO1 (accurate knowledge) + AO3 (effective discussion/evaluation), and where the question is scenario-based, AO2 (explicit application to the scenario) response, not a list of disconnected facts.

#### APP4 The biological approach (genes, biological structures, neurochemistry, genotype/phenotype, evolution)
**The biological approach.** This approach explains thoughts, feelings and behaviour in terms of biological/physical processes in the body, particularly the nervous system, genes and neurochemistry (see BIO1-BIO3). **Biological structures**: differences in brain structure/function (e.g. localisation of function, see BIO4) can influence behaviour, and this can be investigated using brain-scanning techniques (see BIO6). **Neurochemistry**: levels and functioning of neurotransmitters/hormones influence psychological functioning and behaviour (e.g. serotonin's link to OCD and mood, see PSY4; dopamine's link to schizophrenia, see SCZ2); abnormal levels are associated with atypical/disordered behaviour, and drugs that alter neurochemical levels are used therapeutically. **Genetic basis of behaviour**: the biological approach argues behaviour, and psychological disorders, can be at least partly inherited; this is investigated using **twin studies** (comparing concordance rates -- the probability that both twins in a pair share a characteristic/disorder -- between monozygotic (MZ, genetically identical) and dizygotic (DZ, sharing on average 50% of genes, like ordinary siblings) twins: a higher MZ than DZ concordance rate suggests a genetic contribution, since a purely environmental cause should produce similar concordance regardless of genetic relatedness) and **family/adoption studies** (comparing biological versus adoptive relatives to separate genetic from shared-environment influences). The **genotype** is an individual's actual genetic makeup (their specific genetic code); the **phenotype** is the observable characteristics of an individual, resulting from the interaction between their genotype and environmental/experiential factors -- the same genotype can therefore produce different phenotypes in different environments, meaning behaviour is rarely purely genetically determined. **The role of evolution**: any genetically determined behaviour that enhances an individual's chances of survival and reproduction will be naturally selected and passed on to future generations (Darwin's theory of natural selection); the biological approach therefore explains some human behaviours (e.g. attachment, see ATT5; aggression, see AGG2; mate preferences, see REL1) as adaptive, having evolved because they increased ancestral humans' reproductive success, even where the behaviour is not necessarily adaptive in modern environments. Evaluation: the biological approach uses relatively objective, scientific methods (e.g. brain scanning, genetic analysis) giving it strong scientific credibility, and has led to effective biological treatments (e.g. drug therapy); however, it is criticised for biological determinism (underplaying the role of free will, cognition and environmental/social influence, see ID2/ID3), and twin studies are limited by the difficulty of fully separating genetic from shared-environment effects (e.g. MZ twins may also be treated more similarly by others than DZ twins, confounding pure genetic explanations).
