# S47_Classification_and_Diagnosis_of_Schizophrenia - Practice: Classification and diagnosis of schizophrenia

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Distinguish between a positive symptom and a negative symptom of schizophrenia, with an example of each. [4 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by co-morbidity, and why it is a problem for the reliability of schizophrenia diagnosis. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 positive symptom: an atypical symptom experienced in addition to normal experience, e.g. hallucinations or delusions; B2 negative symptom: an atypical symptom reflecting the loss/reduction of normal functioning, e.g. speech poverty or avolition.
2. [2] B1 co-morbidity: schizophrenia is frequently diagnosed alongside other conditions (e.g. depression, substance-use disorders); B1 this complicates diagnosis and can lead different clinicians to reasonably disagree about the primary diagnosis, reducing reliability.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
