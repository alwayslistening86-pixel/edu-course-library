## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 encoding specificity principle: a retrieval cue only helps recall if it was present at both encoding and retrieval; B1 context-dependent forgetting: external/environmental cues present at learning are absent at recall, e.g. Godden and Baddeley's diving study; B1 state-dependent forgetting: internal/physiological cues (e.g. mood) present at learning are absent at recall, e.g. Carter and Cahill's mood study; B1 in both cases, the mismatch between encoding and retrieval conditions makes the memory temporarily inaccessible, not lost.
2. [4] B1 participants learned a word list, then a second list; recall of the first list was tested; B1 the more similar the second list was to the first (e.g. synonyms), the worse recall of the original list was; B1 unrelated words produced the least disruption; B1 showed interference is greater the more similar the two sets of material are.
