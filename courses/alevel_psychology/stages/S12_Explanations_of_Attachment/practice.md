# S12_Explanations_of_Attachment - Practice: Learning theory and Bowlby's monotropic theory

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Explain how attachment would form through operant conditioning, according to learning theory. [3 marks]
2. Give two features of Bowlby's monotropic theory. [4 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [3] B1 food acts as a primary reinforcer, reducing the discomfort of hunger; B1 the caregiver, as the source of food, becomes a secondary reinforcer through this association; B1 proximity to the caregiver becomes rewarding, encouraging attachment behaviour.
2. [4] B2 B2 any two of: monotropy (one particularly important primary attachment), social releasers (innate infant behaviours eliciting caregiving), a critical/sensitive period for attachment formation, the internal working model (a template for future relationships), each briefly explained.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
