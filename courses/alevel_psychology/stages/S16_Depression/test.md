## Test items
1. Outline Ellis's ABC model of depression. [4 marks]
2. Explain how CBT, based on Ellis's model, is used to treat depression. [4 marks]
3. [16-mark style] Discuss the cognitive approach to explaining and/or treating depression. [16 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 an Activating event occurs; B1 followed by a Belief, which may be rational or irrational; B1 producing a Consequence -- rational beliefs lead to healthy emotional responses, irrational beliefs to unhealthy, disproportionate ones such as depression; B1 accurate example of an irrational belief (e.g. musturbatory thinking or utopianism).
2. [4] B1 identifies and challenges the client's irrational beliefs; B1 in REBT, a Dispute stage is added to the ABC model, where the therapist actively challenges the irrational belief (e.g. logically, empirically or pragmatically); B1 aims to replace irrational beliefs with more rational ones, producing healthier emotional consequences; B1 behavioural activation (engagement in rewarding activities) is often also used alongside the cognitive work.
3. [16] Mark out of 16 using AQA's levels of response for a 16-mark 'Discuss ...' essay (AO1 knowledge + AO3 discussion/evaluation, no fixed AO1/AO3 split): Level 4 (13-16) knowledge is accurate and generally well detailed; discussion/evaluation is thorough and effective; the answer is clear, coherent, focused and well structured, using appropriate psychological terminology throughout. Level 3 (9-12) knowledge is evident but there are some inaccuracies/a lack of detail; discussion/evaluation is mostly effective and shows some depth; the answer is mostly clear and organised, with generally appropriate terminology. Level 2 (5-8) knowledge is limited, and mostly focused on description; discussion/evaluation is limited, superficial or lacks focus; the answer lacks a logical structure, with some use of psychological terminology. Level 1 (1-4) knowledge is very limited or muddled; evaluation/discussion, if present at all, is barely discernible or inappropriate; the answer lacks any clear structure; terminology is used inaccurately or not at all. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate knowledge of Beck's negative triad/cognitive vulnerability and/or Ellis's ABC model, and CBT/REBT treatment based on these; discussion should include research support such as Wiles et al.'s RCT finding CBT plus usual care outperformed usual care alone, CBT's cost-effectiveness and brevity relative to some alternatives, reduced effectiveness for very severe depression where engagement with demanding cognitive work is harder, and the risk that a purely cognitive explanation underplays biological or situational causes.
