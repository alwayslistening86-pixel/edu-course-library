## Test items
1. Explain why a quasi-experiment does not allow researchers to draw firm conclusions about cause and effect. [3 marks]
2. A researcher wants to compare memory performance with and without background music, using the same 20 participants in both conditions. Explain how counterbalancing could be used to control for order effects in this study, and why this is necessary. [4 marks]
3. A study comparing memory performance between naturally occurring groups of people with high versus low anxiety levels (not manipulated by the researcher) is best described as a: Choose every correct option.
   A. quasi-experiment
   B. laboratory experiment
   C. field experiment
   D. natural experiment

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 the 'independent variable' is a pre-existing characteristic/difference between people (e.g. age, diagnosed condition), not one the researcher manipulates; B1 participants cannot be randomly allocated to conditions; B1 so any difference found between groups could be due to other, confounding participant variables rather than the variable of interest.
2. [4] B1 order effects (e.g. practice or fatigue) could occur because participants do both conditions, potentially confounding the results; B1 counterbalancing could be used, e.g. an ABBA design; B1 half the participants complete the 'with music' condition first then 'without music', and the other half complete 'without music' first then 'with music'; B1 this balances order effects out across the whole sample rather than letting them systematically favour one condition.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
