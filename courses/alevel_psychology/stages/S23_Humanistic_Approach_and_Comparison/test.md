## Test items
1. Explain how incongruence develops, according to Rogers, and how client-centred therapy aims to address it. [4 marks]
2. Compare the humanistic approach with the behaviourist approach on the free will versus determinism debate. [6 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 incongruence is a mismatch between a person's self-concept and their ideal self; B1 often arising from conditions of worth in childhood rather than unconditional positive regard; B1 client-centred therapy provides unconditional positive regard, empathy and genuineness in the therapeutic relationship; B1 aiming to reduce incongruence and move the client toward congruence/self-actualisation.
2. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate description of humanistic psychology's emphasis on free will (humans as active agents, largely in control of their own behaviour) versus behaviourism's environmental determinism (behaviour shaped by learned associations/reinforcement history); evaluation should note the practical/therapeutic value each position brings, and the difficulty of empirically testing 'free will' as a concept versus behaviourism's more directly testable, observable claims.
