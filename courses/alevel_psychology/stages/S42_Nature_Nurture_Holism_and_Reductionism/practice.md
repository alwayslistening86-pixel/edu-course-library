# S42_Nature_Nurture_Holism_and_Reductionism - Practice: The nature-nurture debate, and holism versus reductionism

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline the diathesis-stress model as an example of interactionism. [2 marks]
2. Give one strength and one limitation of biological reductionism. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 a person may have a genetic/biological vulnerability (diathesis) to a disorder; B1 but the disorder typically develops only if the person also experiences a sufficient environmental trigger/stressor -- nature and nurture interacting, not acting alone.
2. [2] B1 strength: allows precise, testable, scientifically credible explanations, and has led to effective biological treatments; B1 limitation: risks oversimplifying complex behaviour and ignoring important psychological/social/contextual influences.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
