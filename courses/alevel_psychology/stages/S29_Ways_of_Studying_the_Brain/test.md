## Test items
1. Compare fMRI and EEG in terms of their spatial and temporal resolution. [4 marks]
2. Evaluate post-mortem examinations as a way of studying the brain. [6 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 fMRI has good spatial resolution (can fairly precisely locate active brain areas); B1 but poor temporal resolution (several-second delay before the blood-flow change it measures occurs); B1 EEG has excellent temporal resolution (millisecond-level, real-time detection); B1 but poor spatial resolution (cannot precisely locate the source of a signal within the brain).
2. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit strengths such as detailed cellular-level examination not possible with live scanning, and historical foundational value for localisation of function; limitations should include the difficulty of establishing causation (the observed abnormality may not be the actual cause of the condition, and confounding factors such as medication use before death), and ethical issues around consent for use of brain tissue.
