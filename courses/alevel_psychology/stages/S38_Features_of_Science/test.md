## Test items
1. Explain Kuhn's concept of a paradigm shift, and why Kuhn argued psychology may not yet have achieved a single paradigm. [4 marks]
2. [8-mark style] Discuss the role of peer review in psychological research. [8 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 a paradigm is a shared set of basic assumptions and ways of working agreed on within a mature scientific community; B1 a paradigm shift is a scientific revolution in which accumulating unexplained evidence causes the old paradigm to be replaced by a new one; B1 Kuhn argued psychology has a range of competing/co-existing approaches (e.g. biological, behaviourist, cognitive, psychodynamic, humanistic) rather than one broadly agreed paradigm; B1 raising debate over how far psychology fully qualifies as a mature science on Kuhn's definition.
2. [8] Mark out of 8 using AQA's levels of response (AO1 knowledge + AO3 discussion/evaluation): Level 4 (7-8) knowledge is accurate and reasonably detailed; discussion/evaluation is effective; answer is clear and coherent. Level 3 (5-6) knowledge is evident, generally accurate; discussion/evaluation is presented, if not fully developed; answer is mostly clear. Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is limited/mainly descriptive; discussion/evaluation is limited or superficial. Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is very limited; discussion/evaluation is barely discernible. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate description of the peer review process (independent experts scrutinising a submission's quality, methodology and validity before publication) and its aim of maintaining research quality/credibility; discussion should include criticisms such as reviewer bias favouring mainstream findings, publication/file-drawer bias toward positive results, and the inability of peer review to reliably detect deliberate fraud.
