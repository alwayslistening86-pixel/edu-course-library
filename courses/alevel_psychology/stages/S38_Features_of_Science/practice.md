# S38_Features_of_Science - Practice: Features of science, report writing, peer review and economic implications

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Explain what Popper meant by 'falsifiability'. [2 marks]
2. Name the six sections of a conventional scientific report, in order. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 a genuinely scientific theory must be capable, in principle, of being proven false/refuted by evidence; B1 a theory that cannot be falsified by any conceivable observation is not properly scientific, however plausible it seems.
2. [3] B1 abstract and introduction (in order); B1 method and results (in order); B1 discussion and referencing (in order).

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
