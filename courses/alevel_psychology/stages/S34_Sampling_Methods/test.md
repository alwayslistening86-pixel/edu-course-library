## Test items
1. Explain why opportunity sampling and volunteer sampling can both produce unrepresentative samples, using a different reason for each. [4 marks]
2. [6-mark style] A researcher wants to study attitudes to a new school policy among the whole student body of a large secondary school. Discuss which sampling method would be most appropriate, and why. [6 marks]
3. Selecting every 15th name from an alphabetical list of a school's students is an example of: Choose every correct option.
   A. systematic sampling
   B. stratified sampling
   C. volunteer sampling
   D. random sampling

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 opportunity sampling: the sample is drawn only from whoever happens to be conveniently available at a particular time/place, which is unlikely to reflect the full diversity of the target population; B1 developed with an example (e.g. sampling only people in one specific location); B1 volunteer sampling: people who choose to volunteer may systematically differ (e.g. in motivation, curiosity or personality) from those who do not, known as volunteer bias; B1 developed with an example or consequence for generalisability.
2. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit a clearly justified choice (most defensibly stratified sampling by year group, or random sampling from a full student list, or a reasoned defence of a simpler method acknowledging its limitations); discussion should compare at least two methods' suitability for this specific population and practical context, and reach a reasoned recommendation.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
