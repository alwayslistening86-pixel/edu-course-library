## Test items
1. Describe the process of synaptic transmission. [6 marks]
2. Explain the difference between sensory, relay and motor neurons, referring to their location and function. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 an electrical impulse (action potential) reaches the presynaptic terminal; B1 triggering release of neurotransmitters, stored in synaptic vesicles, into the synaptic gap; B1 neurotransmitters diffuse across the gap and bind to specific receptors on the postsynaptic neuron; B1 converting the signal back into an electrical impulse in the postsynaptic neuron; B1 correctly distinguishes excitatory (increases likelihood of firing, e.g. adrenaline) from inhibitory (decreases likelihood, e.g. serotonin/GABA) neurotransmitter effects; B1 explains summation -- whether the neuron fires depends on the net balance of excitatory and inhibitory inputs reaching a threshold.
2. [3] B1 sensory neurons: carry impulses from sensory receptors to the CNS; B1 relay neurons: connect sensory to motor (or other relay) neurons, located mainly within the CNS, and make up the large majority of all neurons; B1 motor neurons: carry impulses from the CNS to effectors (muscles/glands).
