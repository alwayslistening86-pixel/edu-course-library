## Test items
1. Evaluate the nomothetic approach to psychological investigation. [6 marks]
2. Explain, using an example, why a researcher planning a study should consider its wider ethical implications beyond the direct treatment of participants during the study itself. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] Mark out of 6 using AQA's levels of response (typically an 'outline and evaluate' question, AO1 + AO3): Level 3 (5-6) accurate and organised knowledge, with effective, coherent evaluation; Level 2 (3-4) knowledge is evident but not always accurate/organised, evaluation is present but not fully effective; Level 1 (1-2) knowledge is basic/muddled, evaluation is weak or missing. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit strengths such as allowing scientific, statistically testable, generalisable conclusions consistent with an objective, empirical science, and named example research (e.g. Asch, Milgram); limitations should include the risk of losing sight of the unique individual, reducing rich human experience to broad statistical trends that may not accurately describe any single person.
2. [4] B1 findings, or even the research question itself, can have real-world social consequences for the group being studied, beyond any harm/benefit to the specific individuals who took part; B1 accurate, relevant example (e.g. research comparing rates of a disorder or behaviour across social/ethnic groups risking misuse to justify prejudice or stigma); B1 Sieber and Stanley identify this as extending to the research question's framing, the institutional context, and how findings are interpreted/applied once published; B1 correctly notes such implications can arise even from scientifically sound, well-intentioned research.
