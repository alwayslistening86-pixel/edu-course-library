# S43_Idiographic_Nomothetic_and_Ethical_Implications - Practice: Idiographic/nomothetic approaches and ethical implications of research

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Distinguish between the idiographic and the nomothetic approach. [2 marks]
2. Define socially sensitive research, and name the psychologists who proposed the term. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 idiographic approach: focuses on the unique, subjective experience of a particular individual, often using qualitative methods, without necessarily seeking to generalise; B1 nomothetic approach: aims to establish general laws/principles applying broadly across people, typically using quantitative methods and larger samples.
2. [2] B1 research in which the findings, or the research question itself, may have direct, real-world social consequences for participants or the group they represent; B1 correctly attributed to Sieber and Stanley (1988).

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
