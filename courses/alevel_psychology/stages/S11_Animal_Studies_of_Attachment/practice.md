# S11_Animal_Studies_of_Attachment - Practice: Animal studies of attachment (Lorenz, Harlow)

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Define 'imprinting' as demonstrated by Lorenz's research. [2 marks]
2. Outline what Harlow found when infant monkeys were reared with a wire feeding mother and a soft cloth non-feeding mother. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 a rapid, innate form of attachment occurring within a strict, biologically-determined critical period after hatching/birth; B1 correctly linked to Lorenz's goslings following the first moving object they saw.
2. [2] B1 infant monkeys spent far more time clinging to/seeking comfort from the cloth mother regardless of which surrogate fed them, especially when frightened; B1 suggesting attachment is based on contact comfort, not simply on feeding.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
