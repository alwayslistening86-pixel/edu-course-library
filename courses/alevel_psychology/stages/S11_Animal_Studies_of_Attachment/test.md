## Test items
1. Describe Harlow's procedure and outline the long-term effects he found in monkeys deprived of adequate contact comfort. [6 marks]
2. Evaluate the use of animal studies such as Lorenz's and Harlow's research for understanding human attachment. [9 marks]
3. In Lorenz's research, the process by which birds later direct courtship behaviour toward the species/object they imprinted on as chicks is called: Choose every correct option.
   A. sexual imprinting
   B. interactional synchrony
   C. the critical period
   D. contact comfort

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 infant rhesus monkeys reared with a wire feeding surrogate and/or a cloth non-feeding surrogate, or in isolation; B1 monkeys strongly preferred the cloth surrogate for comfort/as a safe base, regardless of feeding; B1 a critical period (about 90 days) identified for healthy attachment; B1 monkeys deprived beyond this period showed severe, often irreversible social/emotional abnormalities in adulthood; B1 including abnormal/neglectful parenting of their own offspring; B1 and abnormal social/sexual behaviour.

Credit accurate reference to both studies and effective discussion of generalisability (birds' attachment is more rigid/innate than mammals'; rhesus monkeys are more closely related to humans, strengthening relevance but still limited), ethical issues (particularly Harlow's severe, lasting harm to the monkeys, which would not be permitted today), and the real-world value of the findings (e.g. informing childcare/institutional care practice) despite these limitations.
