# S08_Eyewitness_Testimony - Practice: Eyewitness testimony: misleading information and anxiety

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline what Loftus and Palmer found when they varied the verb used in the critical question ('contacted' vs 'smashed'). [3 marks]
2. Outline the weapon focus effect and the study that demonstrated it. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [3] B1 estimated speed of the cars increased with the intensity of the verb used; B1 e.g. average estimate around 31.8mph for 'contacted' versus around 40.5mph for 'smashed'; B1 a week later, participants given 'smashed' were more than twice as likely to falsely recall seeing broken glass that was not actually there.
2. [2] B1 attention narrows onto a weapon/threatening object at the cost of encoding other details, e.g. the perpetrator's face, reducing identification accuracy; B1 correctly attributed to Johnson and Scott (1976), using a pen vs blood-covered knife condition.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
