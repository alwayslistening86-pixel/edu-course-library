## Test items
1. Explain the difference between the response-bias explanation and the substitution explanation of how leading questions affect EWT. [4 marks]
2. Discuss why research into the effect of anxiety on EWT accuracy has produced conflicting findings, referring to the Yerkes-Dodson law. [9 marks]
3. In Gabbert et al.'s (2003) study, witnesses who discussed a witnessed event with another 'witness' who had seen a different version: Choose every correct option.
   A. incorporated details from the other person's account into their own recollection
   B. became significantly more accurate than witnesses who did not discuss the event
   C. showed no change in their own memory report
   D. only mentioned the discussion effect when asked directly

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 response-bias explanation: the wording of the question simply influences how a participant chooses to answer, without actually altering the underlying memory; B1 substitution explanation: the memory itself is altered/replaced to become consistent with the information suggested by the question; B1 Loftus and Palmer's finding that participants given 'smashed' later falsely recalled broken glass is evidence for the substitution explanation, since it involved a genuinely new false memory rather than just a biased answer; B1 correctly links this back to Loftus and Palmer's (1974) study.
2. [9] Mark out of 9 using AQA's levels of response (AO1 knowledge + AO3 discussion/evaluation): Level 3 (7-9) accurate, coherent, effective discussion, showing sound knowledge and understanding; Level 2 (4-6) evident knowledge and understanding, discussion is less thorough/effective or slightly muddled in places; Level 1 (1-3) knowledge is limited, discussion is barely evident or absent, answer may just be a list of points. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate knowledge of both Johnson and Scott (anxiety impairs accuracy, weapon focus) and Yuille and Cutshall (real witnesses, higher stress linked to greater accuracy), and effective discussion of the Yerkes-Dodson law's inverted-U relationship between arousal and performance as a reconciling explanation, plus points on the ecological validity of staged laboratory versus real-crime research.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
