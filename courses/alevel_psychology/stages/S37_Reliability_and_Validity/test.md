## Test items
1. Explain how inter-observer reliability would be assessed and improved in an observational study. [4 marks]
2. Distinguish between ecological validity and temporal validity, each with an example. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 assessed by having two or more observers independently observe/score the same behaviour; B1 then correlating their two sets of scores, with a strong positive correlation (e.g. at least +0.80) indicating good reliability; B1 improved by ensuring behavioural categories are clearly, precisely and unambiguously operationalised; B1 and by ensuring observers are adequately trained/practised beforehand.
2. [4] B2 ecological validity: the extent to which findings generalise to real-world settings beyond the specific research situation, e.g. a lab-based memory study may lack ecological validity for everyday remembering; B2 temporal validity: the extent to which findings remain true/applicable over time rather than being specific to the era of the original research, e.g. a 1950s conformity study may not fully apply to a very different, present-day social context.
