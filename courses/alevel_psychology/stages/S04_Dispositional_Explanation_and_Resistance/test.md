## Test items
1. Outline Adorno's Authoritarian Personality explanation of obedience, and give one limitation of this explanation. [6 marks]
2. Explain the difference between an internal and an external locus of control, and how this relates to resisting social influence. [4 marks]
3. In Adorno's theory, the process by which resentment toward a harsh parent is redirected onto a weaker, safer target is called: Choose every correct option.
   A. scapegoating
   B. agentic shift
   C. informational social influence
   D. deindividuation

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 authoritarian personality: extreme respect for/submissiveness to authority, contempt for those seen as inferior, rigid/conventional outlook; B1 measured using the F-scale; B1 originates in a harsh childhood with conditional love and strict discipline, producing displaced resentment (scapegoating); B2 limitation: e.g. the theory is correlational, not causal (both authoritarianism and obedience could share a third cause), or the F-scale suffers response bias/acquiescence, or the theory struggles to explain widespread obedience across whole populations; B1 limitation clearly and accurately explained.
2. [4] B1 internal locus of control: believing you are largely responsible for/in control of what happens to you; B1 external locus of control: believing outcomes are due to luck, fate or powerful others; B1 people with a stronger internal locus of control have been found to be more resistant to social influence (more self-confident, less concerned with approval); B1 people with an external locus of control are more likely to conform/obey.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
