# S04_Dispositional_Explanation_and_Resistance - Practice: The dispositional explanation and resistance to social influence

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. According to Adorno, what kind of childhood produces an authoritarian personality? [2 marks]
2. Explain why social support helps a person resist obedience, using Milgram's variation studies as evidence. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 a harsh, strict childhood involving conditional love (affection dependent on strict obedience) and severe discipline; B1 producing resentment the child displaces onto safer targets (scapegoating) rather than expressing toward parents.
2. [2] B1 the presence of others refusing to obey/conform gives the individual confidence to resist too, breaking the appearance of unanimous agreement; B1 correctly cites Milgram's finding that obedience fell (to 10%) when the participant was joined by two disobedient confederates.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
