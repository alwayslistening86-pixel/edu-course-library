## Test items
1. Explain what is meant by an 'imposed etic' in cross-cultural psychological research, using an example. [3 marks]
2. Distinguish between biological determinism, environmental determinism and psychic determinism, naming the approach most associated with each. [6 marks]
3. [16-mark style] Discuss the free will versus determinism debate in psychology. [16 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 using a technique/theory/assumption developed within one culture and assuming, without adequate justification, that it applies with the same meaning in a different culture; B1 correctly named as an imposed etic; B1 accurate example (e.g. the Strange Situation being applied cross-culturally with an assumed universal meaning).
2. [6] B2 biological determinism: behaviour caused by biological factors (genes, brain, hormones) beyond conscious control, associated with the biological approach; B2 environmental determinism: behaviour caused entirely by conditioning/learning experiences, associated with the behaviourist approach; B2 psychic determinism: behaviour driven by unconscious conflicts/drives, associated with the psychodynamic approach.
3. [16] Mark out of 16 using AQA's levels of response for a 16-mark 'Discuss ...' essay (AO1 knowledge + AO3 discussion/evaluation, no fixed AO1/AO3 split): Level 4 (13-16) knowledge is accurate and generally well detailed; discussion/evaluation is thorough and effective; the answer is clear, coherent, focused and well structured, using appropriate psychological terminology throughout. Level 3 (9-12) knowledge is evident but there are some inaccuracies/a lack of detail; discussion/evaluation is mostly effective and shows some depth; the answer is mostly clear and organised, with generally appropriate terminology. Level 2 (5-8) knowledge is limited, and mostly focused on description; discussion/evaluation is limited, superficial or lacks focus; the answer lacks a logical structure, with some use of psychological terminology. Level 1 (1-4) knowledge is very limited or muddled; evaluation/discussion, if present at all, is barely discernible or inappropriate; the answer lacks any clear structure; terminology is used inaccurately or not at all. 0 marks: no relevant content. Credit accurate description of free will, hard determinism, soft determinism, and biological/environmental/psychic determinism, with named approaches associated with each; discussion should include the tension between hard determinism and everyday notions of moral/legal responsibility, science's general preference for causal, deterministic explanation, the practical value of deterministic approaches (e.g. drug therapy), and evidence (e.g. resistance to social influence) suggesting meaningful choice is possible even under situational pressure.
