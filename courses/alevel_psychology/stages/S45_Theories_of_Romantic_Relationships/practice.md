# S45_Theories_of_Romantic_Relationships - Practice: Theories of romantic relationships

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Distinguish between the comparison level and the comparison level for alternatives in social exchange theory. [2 marks]
2. Explain why equity theory predicts that an over-benefiting partner, as well as an under-benefiting partner, may be dissatisfied in an inequitable relationship. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 comparison level: what a person believes they reasonably deserve/expect from a relationship, based on past experience/general expectations; B1 comparison level for alternatives: whether a person believes they could get a better outcome in an alternative relationship (or being single).
2. [2] B1 equity theory predicts satisfaction depends on the perceived fairness of the reward-to-input ratio between both partners, not simply on maximising one's own rewards; B1 an over-benefiting partner may experience guilt or discomfort at the perceived unfairness, even while receiving more.
