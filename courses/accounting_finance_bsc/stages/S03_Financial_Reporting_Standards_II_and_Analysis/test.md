## Test items
1. A company faces a legal claim. Lawyers assess a 30% chance of losing, with damages of £80,000 if it loses. State how this should be treated under IAS 37, and why. [3 marks]
2. A contract bundles hardware (standalone selling price £30,000) and one year of maintenance (standalone selling price £10,000) for a combined contract price of £36,000. Allocate the transaction price to each performance obligation. [3 marks]
3. A lease liability of £100,000 carries an implicit interest rate of 5%. Calculate the year 1 finance cost and the right-of-use asset's annual straight-line depreciation charge if the lease term is 5 years with no residual value assumption. [3 marks]
4. Which statement about the indirect-method operating section of the statement of cash flows is correct? Choose every correct option.
   A. It starts from profit before tax and adds back non-cash expenses like depreciation
   B. An increase in inventory is added to profit
   C. An increase in trade receivables is deducted from profit
   D. It is unaffected by accrual-basis accounting judgements
5. A company's profit has grown for three years running, but its operating cash flow has fallen each year over the same period. Explain a plausible accounting reason for this divergence, and why a user of the accounts should be concerned. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 no provision is recognised, because an outflow is not judged probable (30% is less than 'more likely than not'); B1 it is instead disclosed as a contingent liability in the notes (nature of the claim and an estimate of the financial effect); B1 if the assessed probability later rose above 50%, a provision would then be recognised at the best estimate of the outflow.
2. [3] M1 total standalone selling price = 30,000+10,000 = £40,000; M1 hardware allocation = 30,000/40,000 x 36,000 = £27,000; A1 maintenance allocation = 10,000/40,000 x 36,000 = £9,000.
3. [3] M1 year 1 finance cost = 100,000 x 5% = £5,000; A1 annual depreciation = 100,000/5 = £20,000; B1 both a depreciation charge (in operating expenses) and a finance cost (in finance costs) now appear, unlike the old operating-lease treatment which showed only a single rental expense.
4. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
5. [4] B2 a plausible cause: receivables and/or inventory are growing faster than sales (e.g. looser credit terms, slow-moving stock), so profit is being recognised on an accruals basis but the cash has not yet been collected/tied up in stock, so operating cash flow lags reported profit; B2 this matters because a business needs cash, not just accounting profit, to pay suppliers, wages and debt -- persistent divergence can signal a liquidity or working-capital-management problem even while the business looks profitable on paper.
