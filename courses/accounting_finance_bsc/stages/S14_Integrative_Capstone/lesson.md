# S14_Integrative_Capstone - Lesson: Integrative capstone

## Goal
The learner synthesises financial reporting, audit/governance, financial management, management accounting and taxation within a single integrated business scenario, exercising the professional judgement the QAA Accounting benchmark expects of a graduate: applying technical material correctly to specific facts, connecting topics across the whole degree, and communicating a clear, well-reasoned professional recommendation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- CAP-1 - Integrating financial reporting, audit and governance
- CAP-2 - Integrating financial management and management accounting
- CAP-3 - Tax implications in a business scenario
- CAP-4 - Writing an integrated professional-style report

## How to teach this
Present a single integrated case (a company facing a real decision -- e.g. whether to acquire a target, how to finance an expansion, or how to respond to an audit finding) and ask the learner to identify, unprompted, which parts of the degree are relevant before working through any calculation. Teach each topic by building on what A-level Accounting and A-level Mathematics already gave the learner: name the A-level idea it extends (e.g. A-level Accounting's basic financial statements, before IAS 2/IAS 16/IFRS 15/IFRS 16 formalise specific standards), then show the IFRS-specific technical treatment or quantitative technique degree-level accounting/finance adds. Work every numerical example with the learner predicting a step before it is shown; every figure in this course was computed with Python when the course was built. UK tax figures (S13) are the 2026/27 tax year; always check HMRC's current published rates before relying on a real-world tax calculation, since UK tax rates/thresholds/allowances change at least annually.

#### CAP-1 Integrating financial reporting, audit and governance
**Integrating financial reporting, audit and governance**: a realistic scenario might ask the learner to identify a financial reporting issue (e.g. is a provision required under IAS 37, or has revenue been recognised correctly under IFRS 15?), consider what audit evidence and procedures would be relevant to testing management's judgement on it, and assess whether the issue and management's handling of it raise any governance concerns (e.g. does it suggest a control weakness, or a need for audit committee escalation?). This connects S02-S03 (standards), S10 (audit/governance) and the professional judgement the QAA benchmark expects: not just 'what does the standard say', but 'what would a professional accountant, exercising scepticism, actually do here'.

#### CAP-2 Integrating financial management and management accounting
**Integrating financial management and management accounting in a strategic decision**: a realistic scenario might present a company deciding between two investment projects (or a make-or-buy/outsourcing decision, or a proposed acquisition), requiring NPV/IRR appraisal (S11), an appropriate discount rate reflecting project risk, a check on how the decision would affect divisional performance measures if delegated to a division (S12, ROI vs RI goal congruence), and the financing implications (S05, sources of finance, gearing, WACC) of however the investment would be funded. This connects S05 (foundations), S11-S12 (advanced) into one coherent strategic recommendation, not four separate, disconnected calculations.

#### CAP-3 Tax implications in a business scenario
**Tax implications in a business scenario**: a realistic scenario might ask the learner to identify how a business decision (e.g. selling an asset, restructuring, changing how directors are remunerated between salary and dividends) affects the company's corporation tax position, any individual's income tax/CGT position, and VAT, connecting S13's technical tax computations to the wider commercial decision -- tax is rarely the only consideration, but a professionally competent recommendation always accounts for its effect on the numbers.

#### CAP-4 Writing an integrated professional-style report
**Writing an integrated professional-style report**: the capstone's extended task asks the learner to produce a short professional report (as if advising a board or client) that identifies the relevant issues across financial reporting, audit/governance, financial management/management accounting and tax; applies the correct technical treatment to the specific facts given (not a generic textbook recitation); reaches a clear, reasoned recommendation; and is written in the structured, evidence-based, appropriately qualified style the QAA Accounting benchmark expects of a graduate exercising real professional judgement -- explicitly flagging assumptions made and their sensitivity, rather than presenting a single number as certain.

## Explicitly not here
This is the final taught stage; the cumulative exam follows once every stage test is passed.
