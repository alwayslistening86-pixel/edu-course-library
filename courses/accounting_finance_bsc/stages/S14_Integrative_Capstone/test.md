# S14_Integrative_Capstone - Test: Integrative capstone

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of this stage's real OU module: calculation questions with full working shown (M/A/B mark tags), short explain/apply questions marked by points, and for the capstone stage an extended professional-report question marked by levels. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A company is deciding whether to finance a £500,000 expansion with new debt at an after-tax cost of 5% or a new share issue with a cost of equity of 11%. Explain which source is cheaper on a simple cost basis, and name one non-cost factor that could still justify choosing the more expensive source. [4 marks]
2. A company's director proposes recognising £60,000 of revenue this year for a service contract that will not be delivered until next year, to hit this year's profit target. Identify the IFRS 15 issue and the governance/ethical concern this raises. [4 marks]
3. [Integrated case, extended answer] A private UK trading company is considering acquiring a smaller competitor for £2,400,000 (a premium of £300,000 over the target's standalone value of £2,100,000). The acquisition would be financed by £1,500,000 of new bank debt at 7% and the rest from cash reserves. The company's finance director estimates post-acquisition annual synergy savings (before tax) of £180,000 in perpetuity, and the company's corporation tax rate is 25%.
Write a short report: (a) calculate whether the acquisition creates value, using an appropriate perpetuity valuation of the after-tax synergy at the company's 9% cost of capital; (b) explain what would happen to the company's gearing and identify one financial risk this raises; (c) state one audit/governance consideration the board should raise about the finance director's synergy estimate before relying on it. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 debt (5% after-tax cost) is cheaper than equity (11% cost of equity) on a simple cost basis; B1 correct reasoning: debt is cheaper partly because interest is tax-deductible and debt holders rank ahead of shareholders on a winding-up, so require a lower return; B2 any valid non-cost factor: taking on more debt raises gearing and financial risk (fixed interest commitment, covenant risk), which could justify equity despite its higher cost if the company is already highly geared or wants to preserve financial flexibility/borrowing headroom for future needs.
2. [4] B2 under IFRS 15, revenue is recognised when (or as) the performance obligation is satisfied -- since the service has not yet been delivered, recognising the revenue this year would breach the standard (it is not yet earned); B2 this also raises a governance/ethical concern: a deliberate attempt to manipulate reported profit to hit a target threatens the fundamental principles of integrity and objectivity, and is exactly the kind of management bias/override risk external audit and a strong audit committee exist to catch.
3. [20] Mark out of 20 using this course's undergraduate accounting/finance-report levels (adapted from the QAA Accounting benchmark's description of graduate analytical and professional-judgement skill): Level 1 (1-5) largely descriptive, states relevant rules/figures but does not apply them to the specific facts; Level 2 (6-10) applies the relevant technical material with some development, but limited integration across topics or limited critical judgement; Level 3 (11-15) a well-structured response that correctly applies technical material to the specific facts, integrates more than one topic area, and reaches a supported conclusion or recommendation; Level 4 (16-20) a sophisticated, professional-standard response that applies technical material accurately and completely, exercises real professional judgement on ambiguous or contestable points, and communicates a clear, well-reasoned recommendation as a qualified accountant/finance professional would to a client or board. Indicative technical content: (a) after-tax synergy = 180,000 x (1-0.25) = £135,000 a year; present value as a perpetuity at 9% = 135,000/0.09 = £1,500,000; net value created = PV of synergy - premium paid = 1,500,000 - 300,000 = £1,200,000 -- a positive figure, so the acquisition appears to create value for the acquirer's shareholders on these assumptions, provided the synergy estimate is realistic. (b) taking on £1,500,000 of new debt raises gearing, increasing the fixed interest burden (£105,000 a year before tax) relative to earnings and the risk that a downturn leaves insufficient profit to cover interest, i.e. higher financial risk. (c) the synergy estimate comes from the FD who has an interest in the deal proceeding, so the board should seek independent corroboration/challenge of the assumption (e.g. an independent review of the estimate, sensitivity testing at lower synergy levels, or audit committee scrutiny) before relying on a single, potentially optimistic internal estimate -- a governance point about management bias and the need for independent challenge, not merely a technical calculation check.
