## Test items
1. A company's quality-inspection cost pool is £50,000, driven by 250 inspections. Product X requires 8 inspections for a batch of 200 units. Calculate the ABC overhead cost per unit of Product X for this cost pool. [3 marks]
2. Which characterises target costing, as distinct from traditional cost-plus pricing? Choose every correct option.
   A. It starts from a market-determined selling price and derives an allowable cost
   B. It adds a standard profit margin on top of whatever the product costs to make
   C. It drives cost reduction during the design stage before production begins
   D. It ignores the selling price entirely
3. Explain what is meant by 'equivalent units' in process costing, and why they are needed. [3 marks]
4. A department manager, knowing the budget will be used to judge their performance, deliberately understates expected sales and overstates expected costs when submitting their budget proposal. Name this behaviour and explain one negative consequence for the organisation. [3 marks]
5. Which is a genuine advantage of zero-based budgeting (ZBB) over incremental budgeting? Choose every correct option.
   A. Every cost must be justified afresh each period, rather than assumed to continue
   B. It is generally quicker and less resource-intensive to prepare
   C. It automatically eliminates all budgetary slack
   D. It removes the need for any budgetary control at all

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 cost driver rate = 50,000/250 = £200 per inspection; M1 cost to Product X's batch = 8 x 200 = £1,600; A1 cost per unit = 1,600/200 = £8.00.
2. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
3. [3] B1 equivalent units express partially completed work-in-progress as an equivalent number of fully completed units (e.g. 100 units 60% complete = 60 equivalent units); B1 they are needed because process costing must split one continuous process's total cost between units fully completed and units still in progress at the period end; B1 without equivalent units, work-in-progress (only partially finished) could not be fairly compared with or costed alongside fully completed units.
4. [3] B1 this is budgetary slack (building deliberate room into a budget to make the target easier to achieve); B1 one consequence: resources may be allocated inefficiently across the organisation, since the department appears to need more (or contribute less) than it genuinely does, at the expense of other departments or projects with a more honest budget; B1 an acceptable alternative consequence: reported 'favourable' variances become meaningless as a genuine measure of managerial performance, undermining the value of budgetary control.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
