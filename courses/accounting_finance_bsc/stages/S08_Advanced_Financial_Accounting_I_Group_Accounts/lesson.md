# S08_Advanced_Financial_Accounting_I_Group_Accounts - Lesson: Advanced financial accounting I: group accounts

## Goal
The learner calculates goodwill on acquisition and non-controlling interest (NCI) using the proportionate method, prepares a consolidated statement of financial position including post-acquisition retained earnings, eliminates unrealised profit on intra-group trading (downstream and upstream), and prepares a consolidated statement of profit or loss -- matching Advanced Financial Accounting (B350), Part I: group accounts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- B350-1 - Consolidated SFP: goodwill and NCI at acquisition (proportionate method)
- B350-2 - Post-acquisition retained earnings and NCI in the consolidated SFP
- B350-3 - Intra-group trading and unrealised profit (downstream and upstream)
- B350-4 - Consolidated statement of profit or loss

## How to teach this
Ask: if a parent company owns 75% of a subsidiary, why do the group's published financial statements show 100% of the subsidiary's assets and liabilities, not just 75% of them? Teach each topic by building on what A-level Accounting and A-level Mathematics already gave the learner: name the A-level idea it extends (e.g. A-level Accounting's basic financial statements, before IAS 2/IAS 16/IFRS 15/IFRS 16 formalise specific standards), then show the IFRS-specific technical treatment or quantitative technique degree-level accounting/finance adds. Work every numerical example with the learner predicting a step before it is shown; every figure in this course was computed with Python when the course was built. UK tax figures (S13) are the 2026/27 tax year; always check HMRC's current published rates before relying on a real-world tax calculation, since UK tax rates/thresholds/allowances change at least annually.

#### B350-1 Consolidated SFP: goodwill and NCI at acquisition (proportionate method)
A **subsidiary** (control, normally >50% of voting rights, per IFRS 10) is **fully consolidated**: 100% of its assets, liabilities, income and expenses are added, line by line, to the parent's own, with the portion not owned by the parent shown separately as **non-controlling interest (NCI)** -- consolidation reflects that the parent controls (and so the group's financial statements report) the whole of the subsidiary's resources, not merely its ownership share. **Goodwill on acquisition** (IFRS 3) = consideration transferred + NCI at acquisition (fair value, or, under the **proportionate method**, NCI's share of the subsidiary's identifiable net assets at fair value) - fair value of the subsidiary's identifiable net assets acquired. *Worked example (proportionate NCI method)*: Parent acquires 75% of Subsidiary for £480,000; at acquisition Subsidiary's identifiable net assets (share capital £100,000 + retained earnings £220,000) are £320,000: NCI at acquisition = 25% x 320,000 = £80,000; goodwill = 480,000 + 80,000 - 320,000 = £240,000.

#### B350-2 Post-acquisition retained earnings and NCI in the consolidated SFP
**Consolidated retained earnings** = parent's own retained earnings + parent's share of the subsidiary's **post-acquisition** retained earnings movement only (pre-acquisition retained earnings are already captured within goodwill/NCI at acquisition, not added again). **NCI in the consolidated statement of financial position** = NCI at acquisition + NCI's share of the subsidiary's post-acquisition reserves movement. *Worked example, continuing S08's acquisition (75% owned, subsidiary's retained earnings £220,000 at acquisition)*: subsidiary's retained earnings are now £300,000 at the reporting date, a post-acquisition increase of £80,000; parent's share added to consolidated retained earnings = 75% x 80,000 = £60,000; NCI's share added to NCI's acquisition-date value = 25% x 80,000 = £20,000.

#### B350-3 Intra-group trading and unrealised profit (downstream and upstream)
**Intra-group trading**: sales between group companies are eliminated on consolidation (they are not sales to an external customer from the group's perspective), and any profit still unrealised (goods bought from another group company still held in inventory at the year end, not yet sold outside the group) must also be eliminated, since the group has not yet earned that profit by selling to an outside party. *Worked example, downstream sale (parent sells to subsidiary)*: Parent sells goods to Subsidiary for £80,000 that cost Parent £60,000 (profit £20,000); 40% of these goods remain in Subsidiary's closing inventory: unrealised profit = 40% x 20,000 = £8,000. This is eliminated by reducing group inventory by £8,000 and reducing consolidated retained earnings by the same amount -- and because this was a **downstream** sale (parent as seller), the whole adjustment falls on the parent's own retained earnings, with **none** charged against NCI, since the unrealised profit arose in the parent's own books, not the subsidiary's. An **upstream** sale (subsidiary sells to parent) instead has the unrealised profit adjustment split between group retained earnings and NCI in the ownership ratio, since that profit arose in the subsidiary's own books and NCI shares in the subsidiary's results.

#### B350-4 Consolidated statement of profit or loss
The **consolidated statement of profit or loss** adds the parent's and subsidiary's revenue, cost of sales and other income/expenses line by line (subsidiary's results included in full for the post-acquisition period only, if acquired partway through the year), eliminates intra-group revenue and the matching intra-group cost of sales (and any unrealised profit adjustment, as in B350-3), then splits the resulting total profit for the year between the parent's shareholders and NCI, based on NCI's ownership share of the subsidiary's profit for the period (not the group's consolidated profit as a whole -- NCI has no claim on the parent's own results). *Worked example*: after eliminations, group profit for the year is £500,000, of which the subsidiary (75% owned) contributed £120,000: profit attributable to NCI = 25% x 120,000 = £30,000; profit attributable to the parent's owners = 500,000 - 30,000 = £470,000.
