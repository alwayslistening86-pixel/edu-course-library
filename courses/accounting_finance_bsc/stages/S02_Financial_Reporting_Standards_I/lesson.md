# S02_Financial_Reporting_Standards_I - Lesson: Financial reporting standards I (inventories, PPE, intangibles, impairment)

## Goal
The learner extends A-level Accounting's basic financial statements with the IASB Conceptual Framework and IAS 1 presentation requirements, and applies specific IFRS Accounting Standards -- IAS 2 (inventories), IAS 16 (property, plant and equipment) and IAS 38/IAS 36 (intangible assets and impairment) -- to measure and present items correctly under IFRS, matching Intermediate Financial Accounting (B250).

## Syllabus items taught here
- B250-1 - The Conceptual Framework and IAS 1 presentation of financial statements
- B250-2 - IAS 2 Inventories: cost formulas (FIFO/AVCO) and net realisable value
- B250-3 - IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment: measurement, depreciation, revaluation
- B250-4 - IAS 38 Intangible assets and IAS 36 Impairment of assets

## How to teach this
Ask: A-level Accounting already showed how to prepare a statement of financial position -- so what does a real, IFRS-compliant set of published financial statements need that a school exam scenario usually skips? Teach each topic by building on what A-level Accounting and A-level Mathematics already gave the learner: name the A-level idea it extends (e.g. A-level Accounting's basic financial statements, before IAS 2/IAS 16/IFRS 15/IFRS 16 formalise specific standards), then show the IFRS-specific technical treatment or quantitative technique degree-level accounting/finance adds. Work every numerical example with the learner predicting a step before it is shown; every figure in this course was computed with Python when the course was built. UK tax figures (S13) are the 2026/27 tax year; always check HMRC's current published rates before relying on a real-world tax calculation, since UK tax rates/thresholds/allowances change at least annually.

#### B250-1 The Conceptual Framework and IAS 1 presentation of financial statements
A-level Accounting introduced the accounting equation and basic financial statements; degree-level financial reporting formalises this under **IFRS Accounting Standards**, issued by the IASB. The **Conceptual Framework** defines the elements (asset, liability, equity, income, expense) and the qualitative characteristics (relevance, faithful representation, comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability). **IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements** sets the minimum required line items and structure for a complete set of financial statements (statement of financial position, statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income, statement of changes in equity, statement of cash flows, notes), and requires assets/liabilities to be classified as **current** (expected to be realised/settled within 12 months or the normal operating cycle) or **non-current**.

#### B250-2 IAS 2 Inventories: cost formulas (FIFO/AVCO) and net realisable value
**IAS 2 Inventories**: inventory is measured at the **lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV)**. Cost includes purchase price plus costs of conversion and other costs to bring inventory to its present location and condition (not selling costs). Where identical units are bought at different prices, **FIFO** (first-in-first-out: oldest cost assumed sold first) or **weighted average cost (AVCO)** allocates cost between cost of sales and closing inventory (LIFO is not permitted under IFRS). *Worked example, FIFO*: opening inventory 100 units @ £5.00; buys 150 units @ £6.00; sells 180 units. Under FIFO the 180 units sold are the 100 opening units (£500) plus 80 of the new batch (£480) = £980 cost of sales; closing inventory = 70 units @ £6.00 = £420.

#### B250-3 IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment: measurement, depreciation, revaluation
**IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment**: initially measured at cost (purchase price plus directly attributable costs of bringing the asset to working condition, e.g. delivery, installation); subsequently measured under the **cost model** (cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment) or the **revaluation model** (fair value less subsequent depreciation, with gains normally taken to a revaluation surplus in other comprehensive income, not profit or loss, unless reversing a previous downward revaluation charged to profit or loss). Depreciation allocates the depreciable amount (cost/valuation less residual value) systematically over useful life; a **componentised** asset with parts of materially different useful lives (e.g. an aircraft's engines versus its frame) is depreciated part by part. *Worked example, revaluation*: an asset with carrying amount £80,000 is revalued to £95,000: a revaluation surplus of £15,000 is recognised in other comprehensive income (not profit or loss), increasing equity.

#### B250-4 IAS 38 Intangible assets and IAS 36 Impairment of assets
**IAS 38 Intangible Assets**: recognised only if identifiable, controlled, and probable future economic benefits will flow (internally generated **goodwill** and, generally, internally generated brands are never recognised as assets; internally generated **development** costs are capitalised only once six strict criteria are met -- technical feasibility, intention and ability to complete/use or sell, probable future benefits, availability of resources, and ability to measure the expenditure reliably -- **research** costs are always expensed). **IAS 36 Impairment of Assets**: an asset (or cash-generating unit) is impaired when its carrying amount exceeds its **recoverable amount** (the higher of fair value less costs of disposal and value in use, the discounted present value of future cash flows the asset generates); the impairment loss (carrying amount less recoverable amount) is charged to profit or loss (or first against any revaluation surplus for a revalued asset). *Worked example*: an asset's carrying amount is £60,000; recoverable amount is assessed at £48,000 (the higher of fair value less costs of disposal £45,000 and value in use £48,000): impairment loss = £12,000.
