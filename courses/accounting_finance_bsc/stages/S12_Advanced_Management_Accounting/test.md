# S12_Advanced_Management_Accounting - Test: Advanced management accounting

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of this stage's real OU module: calculation questions with full working shown (M/A/B mark tags), short explain/apply questions marked by points, and for the capstone stage an extended professional-report question marked by levels. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A selling division's variable cost is £40.00/unit; it is operating at full capacity and would have to give up an external sale earning a contribution of £18.00/unit to supply the other division instead. Calculate the minimum transfer price and explain why it includes the lost contribution. [3 marks]
2. Division Y has profit of £240,000 and investment of £1,500,000; the company's cost of capital is 10%. Calculate ROI and residual income. [3 marks]
3. Division Y (from the previous question, current ROI 16.0%, company cost of capital 10%) is considering a new project earning a 13% return on the investment required. Explain whether a manager evaluated on ROI alone might reject this project even though the company should want it accepted, and why residual income avoids that problem. [4 marks]
4. Which are recognised limitations of using ROI (rather than residual income) to judge divisional managers? Choose every correct option.
   A. A manager may reject a project earning above the cost of capital if it is below the division's current average ROI
   B. ROI cannot be compared meaningfully across divisions of different sizes
   C. ROI can encourage dysfunctional, short-term decisions that protect an already-high average return
   D. ROI is always a worse measure than RI in every possible circumstance
5. Explain what the value chain framework is used for in strategic management accounting, and give one example of a support activity (as distinct from a primary activity). [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 minimum transfer price = 40.00 + 18.00; A1 = £58.00/unit; B1 because the division is at full capacity, supplying the other division means genuinely giving up an external sale, so the opportunity cost of that lost external contribution must be recovered as well as variable cost, or the selling division (and the group, if the internal use is less valuable than the lost external sale) is worse off.
2. [3] M1 ROI = 240,000/1,500,000; A1 = 16.0%; A1 RI = 240,000 - (10% x 1,500,000) = £90,000.
3. [4] B2 a manager judged on ROI alone might reject the project: 13% is below Division Y's current 16.0% average ROI, so accepting it would pull the division's average ROI down, even though 13% comfortably exceeds the company's 10% cost of capital and so genuinely adds value from the company's perspective; B2 residual income avoids this because any project earning more than the imputed cost-of-capital charge (10% here) adds a positive amount to RI regardless of the division's existing average return, so RI-based evaluation is goal-congruent with the company's own investment criterion (accept anything earning more than the cost of capital).
4. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B1 the value chain breaks a business down into the discrete activities that create value for the customer, to identify where genuine cost advantage or differentiation is being created or lost, relative to competitors; B1 primary activities are those directly involved in creating/delivering/supporting the product (e.g. inbound logistics, operations, marketing and sales); B1 a support activity example: procurement, technology development, human resource management, or firm infrastructure (any one correctly identified as supporting, not directly creating, the product/service).
