## Test items
1. Explain the concept of separate legal personality and one practical consequence it has for a shareholder's exposure to the company's debts. [3 marks]
2. A company issues 40,000 £0.50 ordinary shares at £1.20 each. Calculate the amount credited to share capital (nominal value) and to share premium. [3 marks]
3. Which of the following are among a director's statutory duties under the Companies Act 2006? Choose every correct option.
   A. To promote the success of the company for the benefit of members as a whole
   B. To avoid conflicts of interest
   C. To act only in the director's own personal financial interest
   D. To exercise reasonable care, skill and diligence
4. Explain the difference between wrongful trading and fraudulent trading, and state one consequence a director found liable for either might face. [4 marks]
5. A company voluntary arrangement (CVA) and administration are both possible responses to financial difficulty. Explain how a CVA differs from administration. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 a company incorporated under the Companies Act 2006 is a distinct legal person from its shareholders/owners, able to own property, sue and be sued, and enter contracts in its own name; B1 the company, not the shareholders personally, is liable for its own debts; B1 consequence: a shareholder's liability for the company's debts is normally limited to the amount unpaid on their shares (limited liability), so personal assets beyond that are protected if the company fails.
2. [3] M1 share capital = 40,000 x £0.50 = £20,000; M1 total proceeds = 40,000 x £1.20 = £48,000; A1 share premium = 48,000 - 20,000 = £28,000.
3. Correct: A, B, D (exactly these options, no others)
4. [4] B1 wrongful trading: continuing to trade after a director knew or ought reasonably to have concluded insolvent liquidation could not be avoided, without taking every reasonable step to minimise creditor loss -- it does not require dishonest intent; B1 fraudulent trading: carrying on business with actual intent to defraud creditors (or for any other fraudulent purpose) -- it does require dishonest intent, and is also a criminal offence; B1 a director found liable for either can be ordered to personally contribute to the company's assets available to creditors on liquidation; B1 a director may additionally be disqualified from acting as a company director for a period.
5. [3] B1 a CVA is a formal agreement between the company and its creditors to pay debts (often at a reduced amount or over an extended period), typically allowing the directors to remain in control and the company to keep trading normally; B1 administration instead places an insolvency practitioner (the administrator) in control of the company, displacing the directors' authority, with a statutory objective of rescuing the company, achieving a better outcome for creditors than immediate liquidation, or realising assets for secured/preferential creditors; B1 a CVA is generally used where the company's core business remains viable and creditors are willing to accept a repayment plan, while administration is used where more direct intervention/protection (e.g. a moratorium on creditor action) is needed.
