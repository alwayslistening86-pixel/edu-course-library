# S10_Governance_Audit_and_Assurance - Test: Governance, audit and assurance

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of this stage's real OU module: calculation questions with full working shown (M/A/B mark tags), short explain/apply questions marked by points, and for the capstone stage an extended professional-report question marked by levels. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Which best describes the 'comply or explain' basis of the UK Corporate Governance Code? Choose every correct option.
   A. A company must comply with every provision with no exceptions permitted
   B. A company either complies with a provision or publicly explains why it has not
   C. The Code has no practical effect on listed companies
   D. Compliance is assessed and enforced only by the external auditor
2. Explain the difference between a test of control and a substantive procedure, giving one example of each. [4 marks]
3. Total assets are £5,000,000; materiality is set at 2% of total assets. A misstatement of £60,000 is identified during the audit. Calculate materiality, and state whether this misstatement, on its own, appears material. [3 marks]
4. Which audit opinion is appropriate where the auditor is unable to obtain sufficient appropriate audit evidence about a matter that is both material and pervasive? Choose every correct option.
   A. Unmodified (unqualified) opinion
   B. Qualified ('except for') opinion
   C. Adverse opinion
   D. Disclaimer of opinion
5. Name three of the five fundamental ethical principles in the auditor's Code of Ethics, and explain why an audit firm providing extensive non-audit consultancy services to the same client can threaten objectivity. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [4] B2 a test of control checks whether a client's internal control is operating effectively, e.g. re-performing/checking evidence that purchase invoices over a set limit were properly authorised before payment; B2 a substantive procedure directly tests whether an account balance/transaction is free of material misstatement, e.g. sending receivables confirmation requests to a sample of customers, or attending the year-end inventory count.
3. [3] M1 materiality = 2% x 5,000,000 = £100,000; A1 correct figure; B1 £60,000 is below the £100,000 materiality threshold, so on its own it would not normally be considered material -- though the auditor should still consider it in aggregate with any other uncorrected misstatements, and its nature (e.g. whether it indicates fraud) before concluding.
4. Correct: D (exactly these options, no others)
5. [4] B3 any three of: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour; B1 a large non-audit fee relationship can create a self-interest threat (reluctance to challenge a lucrative client) and/or a self-review threat (auditing figures or systems the firm itself helped design/implement as a consultant), both of which can compromise the auditor's objectivity and independence, whether real or merely perceived.
