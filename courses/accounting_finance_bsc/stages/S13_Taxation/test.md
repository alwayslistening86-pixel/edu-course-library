# S13_Taxation - Test: Taxation

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of this stage's real OU module: calculation questions with full working shown (M/A/B mark tags), short explain/apply questions marked by points, and for the capstone stage an extended professional-report question marked by levels. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. An individual has total income of £80,000 for 2026/27. Calculate their income tax liability. [4 marks]
2. A company's taxable total profits are £200,000 for 2026/27. Calculate its corporation tax liability, showing the marginal relief calculation. [4 marks]
3. An individual has a chargeable gain of £40,000 on a disposal in 2026/27, with only £10,000 of unused basic rate band remaining. Calculate the capital gains tax due. [5 marks]
4. A VAT-registered business makes standard-rated sales of £120,000 (net) and standard-rated purchases of £70,000 (net) in a period. Calculate the VAT payable to (or reclaimable from) HMRC. [3 marks]
5. Which statement about UK VAT is correct? Choose every correct option.
   A. A zero-rated supply is still a taxable supply, but at 0%
   B. A business must register once its taxable turnover exceeds the registration threshold in a rolling 12-month period
   C. Input tax on the business's own taxable purchases can normally be reclaimed against output tax
   D. Exempt supplies are treated identically to zero-rated supplies for VAT purposes

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 taxable income = 80,000-12,570 = £67,430; M1 basic rate band (20%) covers the first 37,700: tax = 7,540; M1 remaining taxable income at 40% = 29,730, tax = £11,892; A1 total = £19,432.
2. [4] M1 tax before relief = 200,000 x 25% = £50,000; M1 marginal relief = 3/200 x (250,000-200,000) = £750; A1 corporation tax payable = 50,000 - 750 = £49,250; B1 correct reasoning that this falls between the small profits rate (19%) and main rate (25%) because profits are between the two thresholds.
3. [5] M1 taxable gain = 40,000-3,000 (annual exempt amount) = £37,000; M1 of this, £10,000 falls within the remaining basic rate band, taxed at 18% = £1,800; M1 the remainder, 27,000, is taxed at 24% = £6,480; A1 total CGT = £8,280; B1 correct reasoning that the taxable gain is split across the two rates because it exceeds the remaining basic rate band.
4. [3] M1 output tax = 120,000 x 20% = £24,000; M1 input tax = 70,000 x 20% = £14,000; A1 VAT payable = 24,000 - 14,000 = £10,000.
5. Correct: A, B, C (exactly these options, no others)
