# S03_Complex_Roots_and_Argand_Diagrams - Test: Complex roots of polynomials and Argand diagrams

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of OCR's Further Mathematics papers: structured questions with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows full working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. The equation z^4 - 2z^3 + 6z^2 - 8z + 8 = 0 has a root 2i. Find all the roots. [6 marks]
2. z = 4(cos(5π/6) + i sin(5π/6)) and w = 2(cos(π/3) + i sin(π/3)). Find zw and z/w in modulus-argument form with principal arguments. [4 marks]
3. On an Argand diagram, the point A represents z = 3 + i. Describe the geometrical transformation that maps A to the point representing iz, and find iz. [2 marks]
4. Explain why a cubic equation with real coefficients cannot have roots 1, i and 2i. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 -2i is a root; M1 factor z^2 + 4; M1 divides: z^2 - 2z + 2; A1 correct quotient; M1 solves; A1 1 ± i (roots ±2i, 1 ± i).
2. [4] M1 multiplies moduli and adds arguments; A1 8(cos(-5π/6) + i sin(-5π/6)); M1 divides and subtracts; A1 2(cos(π/2) + i sin(π/2)).
3. [2] B1 rotation by π/2 anticlockwise about O; B1 -1 + 3i.
4. [2] B1 non-real roots of a real polynomial come in conjugate pairs; B1 so -i and -2i would also be roots, giving at least five roots for a cubic, impossible.
