# S16_Limited_Company_Accounts_Extended - Test: Limited company accounts: revaluation, share issues and reporting

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Which type of share issue raises new cash for the company? Choose every correct option.
   A. A bonus issue
   B. A rights issue
   C. Neither raises cash
   D. Both raise the same amount of cash
2. A company with 80,000 £1 ordinary shares makes a 1-for-4 rights issue at £1.50. Calculate the number of new shares issued, the cash raised, and the amount credited to share premium. [5 marks]
3. A company with 200,000 £1 ordinary shares makes a 1-for-10 bonus issue. Calculate the number of new shares issued and state the effect on total equity. [3 marks]
4. Which best explains why comparability of published accounts matters to an outside investor? Choose every correct option.
   A. It lets the investor avoid paying corporation tax
   B. It allows the investor to compare companies on a like-for-like basis when deciding where to invest
   C. It guarantees the company will be profitable
   D. It removes the need for an audit
5. Explain why an increase on revaluation of a non-current asset is credited to a revaluation reserve rather than to the statement of profit or loss. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [5] M1 new shares = 80,000/4 = 20,000; A1 correct; M1 cash raised = 20,000 x 1.50 = £30,000; A1 correct; B1 share premium credited = 20,000 x (1.50-1) = £10,000 (the excess over nominal value).
3. [3] M1 new shares = 200,000/10 = 20,000; A1 correct; B1 total equity is unchanged - the bonus issue only transfers an amount between reserves and share capital, no cash is raised, and no new resources enter the company.
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. [2] B1 the gain is unrealised (no sale has taken place, so no cash or receivable has actually arisen) - recognising it as profit would overstate distributable profit; B1 the prudence concept means an unrealised gain is recognised in equity (a reserve) rather than treated the same as a realised trading profit.
