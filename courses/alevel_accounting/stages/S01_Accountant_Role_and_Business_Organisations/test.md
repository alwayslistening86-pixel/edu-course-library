# S01_Accountant_Role_and_Business_Organisations - Test: The accountant's role and types of business organisation

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Which business type has unlimited liability for all its owners? Choose every correct option.
   A. Public limited company
   B. Private limited company
   C. Partnership (ordinary)
   D. None of these
2. Explain why a bank overdraft might be a riskier source of short-term finance for a business than a fixed-term bank loan. [2 marks]
3. Which is a key advantage of a plc over a Ltd company? Choose every correct option.
   A. No requirement to publish accounts
   B. Can offer shares for sale to the general public
   C. Owners have unlimited liability
   D. No corporation tax is payable
4. A company issues £80,000 of 5% debentures. Calculate the annual interest and state one reason this differs from a dividend on ordinary shares. [3 marks]
5. Identify the accounting role most concerned with preparing next year's departmental budgets, and state whether this output follows a legally required format. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
2. [2] B1 an overdraft is technically repayable on demand by the bank, so the business could be asked to repay it at short notice; B1 a fixed-term loan has a set repayment schedule the business can plan and budget around.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] M1 interest = 80,000 x 0.05; A1 = £4,000; B1 debenture interest must be paid regardless of profit (and is tax-deductible), whereas a dividend is only paid if the company chooses to and after tax.
5. [2] B1 management accounting; B1 no - management accounting reports have no legally required format, unlike statutory financial statements.
