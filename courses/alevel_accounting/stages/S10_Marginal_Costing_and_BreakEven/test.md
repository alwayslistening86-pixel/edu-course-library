# S10_Marginal_Costing_and_BreakEven - Test: Marginal costing and break-even analysis

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Selling price is £60/unit, variable cost is £36/unit and fixed costs are £96,000. Calculate the contribution per unit, the break-even point in units, and the break-even revenue. [5 marks]
2. Which is a variable cost? Choose every correct option.
   A. Factory rent
   B. Direct materials
   C. A supervisor's annual salary
   D. Insurance on the factory building
3. Break-even output is 3,000 units and budgeted sales are 3,800 units. Calculate the margin of safety in units and as a percentage of budgeted sales. [4 marks]
4. A factory has spare capacity and is offered a one-off order for 800 units at £22 each. Variable cost per unit is £15 and fixed costs are unaffected by the order. Calculate the contribution from the order and advise whether it should be accepted on financial grounds alone. [4 marks]
5. On a break-even chart, the margin of safety is shown as: Choose every correct option.
   A. The gap between the fixed cost line and the variable cost line
   B. The gap between the break-even point and budgeted/actual output
   C. The point where total revenue crosses the vertical axis
   D. The gap between total cost and total revenue at zero output

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] M1 contribution = 60-36 = £24/unit; A1 break-even units = 96,000/24 = 4,000 units; M1 break-even revenue = 4,000 x 60; A1 = £240,000; B1 correct method throughout (contribution before break-even units, break-even units before break-even revenue).
2. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
3. [4] M1 margin of safety = 3,800 - 3,000 = 800 units; A1 = 800 units; M1 percentage = 800/3,800 x 100; A1 = 21%.
4. [4] M1 contribution per unit = 22-15 = £7; A1 total contribution = 7 x 800 = £5,600; B1 accept, because contribution is positive and fixed costs are unaffected, so profit rises by £5,600; B1 correct reasoning that this ignores non-financial factors (e.g. reaction of existing customers, whether capacity is really spare).
5. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
