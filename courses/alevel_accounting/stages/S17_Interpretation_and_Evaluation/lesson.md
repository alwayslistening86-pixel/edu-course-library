# S17_Interpretation_and_Evaluation - Lesson: Interpreting and evaluating performance

## Goal
The learner uses accounting techniques, measures and ratios to interpret financial and management information and evaluate performance, both internally (against budgets/targets) and externally (against competitors, prior years or industry norms).

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.17a - Internal evaluation of performance
- 3.17b - External evaluation of performance

## How to teach this
Ask: a manager sees an adverse material usage variance this month. What further, non-financial questions would you want answered before concluding the production team was to blame? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.17a Internal evaluation of performance
**Internal evaluation**: performance is judged against internal benchmarks - the budget (via variance analysis, S09/S11), targets set for a department or product, or trend over recent periods - to hold managers accountable for what they control and to guide corrective action. Internal evaluation can draw on detailed management information not available to outsiders (e.g. per-product, per-department figures, standard cost variances), giving a richer, more immediate picture than external analysis alone.

#### 3.17b External evaluation of performance
**External evaluation**: performance is judged against outside benchmarks - a competitor's published accounts, an industry-average ratio, or what investors/lenders expect - using the financial statements and the ratios covered in 3.8 (profitability, liquidity, efficiency, capital structure). External evaluation is inherently more limited than internal evaluation: it relies only on what is published (annual, often summarised, accounts), cannot see the causes behind a number the way an internal manager can, and must be interpreted alongside the general limitations of ratio analysis (3.8d) - the need for a like-for-like benchmark, differing accounting policies between businesses, and the historical nature of the figures.

## Explicitly not here
The ratio calculations themselves are S08a/S08b; the ethical dimension of how conclusions are reported is S19/S20.
