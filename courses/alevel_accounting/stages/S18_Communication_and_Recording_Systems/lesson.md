# S18_Communication_and_Recording_Systems - Lesson: Stakeholders, communication and recording systems

## Goal
The learner identifies the interests of different stakeholders in accounting information, communicates findings and recommendations effectively, and evaluates the impact of different systems of recording financial data.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.17c - Stakeholder interests and effective communication
- 3.17d - Impact of different systems of recording data

## How to teach this
Ask: a shareholder, a supplier and an employee all read the same set of published accounts. Why might each be looking for something quite different in the same figures? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.17c Stakeholder interests and effective communication
**Stakeholder interests and effective communication**: **shareholders/owners** want to know about profitability, dividends and the security of their investment; **lenders/banks** care most about liquidity and the ability to service debt (interest cover, gearing); **suppliers** want reassurance the business can pay on time before extending credit; **employees** care about job security and the business's ability to pay wages/pensions; **management** needs both financial and non-financial information to run the business; **HMRC** needs figures to assess tax; and **the public/community** may be interested in a business's wider impact. Because these audiences differ, communication must be tailored: a technical ratio table alone may serve an analyst, but a recommendation to non-specialist managers or owners needs to be expressed clearly, in context, with the "so what" (what it means and what should be done) made explicit, not just the number.

#### 3.17d Impact of different systems of recording data
**Impact of different systems of recording data**: a manual, paper-based system is cheap to set up but slow, more error-prone (no automatic cross-checks) and hard to scale as a business grows; a computerised accounting system automates posting and can enforce double entry (rejecting an unbalanced entry), produce reports instantly and support far larger volumes, but requires investment, staff training, and carries risks around data security, system failure and the cost/disruption of switching systems. The choice affects not just cost, but the speed, accuracy and level of detail available for both financial reporting and the internal evaluation covered in 3.17a.
