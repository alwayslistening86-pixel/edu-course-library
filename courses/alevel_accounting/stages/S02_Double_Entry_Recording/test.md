# S02_Double_Entry_Recording - Test: Recording transactions by double entry

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A business receives £2,500 cash from a credit customer. State the debit and credit entries. [2 marks]
2. Which book of prime entry records credit purchases before they are posted to the ledger? Choose every correct option.
   A. The sales day book
   B. The purchases day book
   C. The cash book
   D. The general journal
3. A business pays £9,000 for a new delivery van, plus £400 to have the company logo painted on it and £150 for its first tank of fuel. State how much is capital expenditure and how much is revenue expenditure, with a reason for each. [4 marks]
4. Which of these is capital income? Choose every correct option.
   A. Cash sales
   B. Proceeds of issuing new ordinary shares
   C. Rent received from a tenant
   D. Interest received on a bank deposit
5. After posting all transactions for the month, total debits in the trial balance are £48,600 and total credits are £48,600. Does this prove there are no errors in the ledger? Explain. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 Debit Cash/Bank £2,500 (asset increases); A1 Credit Receivables £2,500 (asset decreases, the debt is settled).
2. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
3. [4] M1 capital expenditure = 9,000 + 400 = £9,400 (the van and the livery painting are both needed to bring the asset into use for the business, so both are added to the asset's cost); A1 correct figure; B1 revenue expenditure = £150 (the fuel is a running cost, not an improvement to the asset); B1 correct reasoning that fuel is consumed immediately rather than providing lasting benefit.
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. [2] B1 no; B1 the trial balance only proves debits equal credits in total - errors that affect both sides equally (e.g. an error of omission, a compensating error, or posting to the wrong account of the correct type) would still leave it balancing.
