# S20_Ethics_Regulatory_Frameworks_and_Action - Lesson: Regulatory frameworks, professional bodies and appropriate action

## Goal
The learner explains the legal and regulatory frameworks relevant to accounting, the role of professional bodies, and the appropriate courses of action when an unethical or illegal act is suspected.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.18c - Legal and regulatory frameworks
- 3.18d - Role of professional bodies
- 3.18e - Appropriate action on suspected unethical/illegal acts

## How to teach this
Ask: an accountant suspects, but is not yet certain, that a colleague has been falsifying expense claims. What should they do first, and why does 'nothing, until I am completely sure' fail the test of appropriate action? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.18c Legal and regulatory frameworks
**Legal and regulatory frameworks**: accounting in the UK sits within a framework including company law (e.g. the Companies Act's requirements to keep proper accounting records and file accounts), tax law (enforced by HMRC), and anti-money-laundering legislation (requiring accountants to report knowledge or suspicion of money laundering, overriding client confidentiality in that specific, legally defined circumstance). For companies following IFRS Accounting Standards, the IASB's standards (3.16d) sit alongside this legal framework, giving detailed technical rules for how transactions must be recognised, measured and disclosed.

#### 3.18d Role of professional bodies
**The role of professional bodies**: bodies such as ICAEW, ACCA and CIMA set entry requirements and qualifications for their members, issue and enforce codes of ethics (embodying the fundamental principles in 3.18a-3.18b), provide continuing professional development, and operate disciplinary processes - a member found to have acted unethically can face sanctions up to being excluded from membership (which, for a practising accountant, can end their ability to work in the profession). This self-regulation by professional bodies, alongside statute law, is intended to maintain public trust in the accounting profession as a whole, not just protect any one client.

#### 3.18e Appropriate action on suspected unethical/illegal acts
**Appropriate courses of action on suspected unethical or illegal acts**: an accountant who suspects (not necessarily is certain of) an unethical or illegal act should generally: gather and document the facts objectively rather than act on rumour; raise the concern through the appropriate internal channel first where one exists (e.g. a manager, an ethics/compliance function, or a formal whistleblowing policy), since this is usually proportionate and preserves working relationships where the concern can be resolved internally; escalate further (e.g. to a professional body, or - for money laundering - to the National Crime Agency, as legally required) if the internal response is inadequate, if the matter is sufficiently serious, or if raising it internally is not appropriate (e.g. suspicion falls on senior management); and, throughout, maintain confidentiality except where disclosure is itself legally required (as with money laundering) - "doing nothing until completely certain" fails the fundamental principles because a reasonable, professionally sceptical response to a genuine suspicion, acted on in good time, is what the principles require, not proof beyond doubt before any action is taken.
