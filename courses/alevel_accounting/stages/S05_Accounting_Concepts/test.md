# S05_Accounting_Concepts - Test: The accounting concepts

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Which concept means goodwill built up through good service is not recorded as an asset? Choose every correct option.
   A. Going concern
   B. Money measurement
   C. Prudence
   D. Realisation
2. A business receives a customer order in December for goods to be delivered and invoiced in January. Explain, using the realisation concept, when the revenue should be recognised. [2 marks]
3. A business buys a £12 pack of pens, expected to last several years, and expenses the whole cost immediately rather than treating it as a non-current asset. Which concept justifies this? Choose every correct option.
   A. Consistency
   B. Materiality
   C. Going concern
   D. Duality
4. Explain the difference between the cost concept and the prudence concept, each with a brief example. [4 marks]
5. Which concept requires the same depreciation method to be used from one year to the next unless there is good reason to change? Choose every correct option.
   A. Consistency
   B. Prudence
   C. Accruals
   D. Realisation

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [2] B1 revenue is recognised only when it is earned - typically when the goods are delivered and the right to payment arises; B1 so no revenue is recognised in December from the order alone; it is recognised in January when the goods are delivered/invoiced.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. [4] B2 the cost concept records assets at their historical (original) cost, an objective, verifiable figure - e.g. a machine bought for £10,000 is recorded at £10,000, not at management's estimate of its current worth; B2 the prudence concept applies caution where there is genuine uncertainty, without deliberately understating assets/income or overstating liabilities/expenses - e.g. creating an allowance for doubtful debts against receivables that experience suggests will not all be collected.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
