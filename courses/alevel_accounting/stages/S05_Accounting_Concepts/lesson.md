# S05_Accounting_Concepts - Lesson: The accounting concepts

## Goal
The learner names and applies the accounting concepts used in preparing financial statements: money measurement, duality, cost, going concern, accruals, consistency, prudence, materiality, realisation and business entity.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.5a - Accounting concepts I (money measurement, duality, cost, going concern, accruals)
- 3.5b - Accounting concepts II (consistency, prudence, materiality, realisation, business entity)

## How to teach this
Ask: a business owner is confident her firm's reputation and loyal customer base are worth far more than anything on the statement of financial position. Why does the money measurement concept mean this is never recorded as an asset? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.5a Accounting concepts I (money measurement, duality, cost, going concern, accruals)
**The accounting concepts**: **money measurement** (only items that can be expressed in monetary terms are recorded - staff morale or brand reputation are not, however valuable); **duality** (every transaction has two effects, the basis of double entry: Assets = Liabilities + Equity); **cost** (assets are normally recorded at their historical cost, an objective and verifiable figure, rather than a more subjective current value); **going concern** (financial statements assume the business will continue operating for the foreseeable future, so assets are valued for continuing use, not forced sale, unless there is intention or necessity to liquidate); **accruals** (income and expenses are recognised in the period they relate to, not when cash moves - the basis for accruals/prepayments, depreciation and inventory adjustments).

#### 3.5b Accounting concepts II (consistency, prudence, materiality, realisation, business entity)
**Further concepts**: **consistency** (the same accounting policies - e.g. the same depreciation method - are applied from one period to the next, changed only for good reason, so that results are comparable over time); **prudence** (a degree of caution is applied where there is uncertainty - e.g. an allowance for doubtful debts, or recognising a loss as soon as it is anticipated - without deliberately understating assets/income or overstating liabilities/expenses, which would be as misleading as the opposite); **materiality** (an item is only worth separate disclosure or precise treatment if it is significant enough to influence a user's decisions - a business may, for example, expense a £15 stapler immediately rather than depreciate it as a non-current asset, even though strictly it will be used for years); **realisation** (revenue is recognised only when it is earned - typically when goods/services are delivered and the right to payment arises - not merely when an order is placed or cash is received in advance); **business entity** (the business is accounted for separately from its owner(s), so an owner's private spending is drawings, not a business expense).
