# S19_Ethics_Fundamental_Principles - Lesson: Fundamental ethical principles

## Goal
The learner explains the fundamental principles of ethical behaviour expected of accounting professionals and how they shape professional conduct.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.18a - Fundamental ethical principles
- 3.18b - How ethical principles shape professional conduct

## How to teach this
Ask: an accountant is asked by a director to value inventory more generously than the evidence supports, to help the company secure a loan. Which ethical principle is most directly under threat? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.18a Fundamental ethical principles
**The fundamental principles of ethical behaviour** (as set out by professional accountancy bodies such as ICAEW/ACCA, reflecting a widely used international ethical framework): **integrity** (being straightforward and honest in all professional and business relationships); **objectivity** (not allowing bias, conflict of interest, or undue influence from others to override professional judgement); **professional competence and due care** (maintaining the knowledge and skill needed to provide a competent service, and acting diligently, in line with applicable technical and professional standards); **confidentiality** (not disclosing information acquired professionally without proper authority, nor using it for personal advantage); and **professional behaviour** (complying with relevant laws and regulations and avoiding any conduct that discredits the profession).

#### 3.18b How ethical principles shape professional conduct
**How the principles shape professional conduct**: these principles guide an accountant through everyday pressures - e.g. **objectivity** requires resisting a director's pressure to overstate inventory value or delay recognising a known bad debt in order to flatter reported profit; **integrity** requires disclosing a material error found in previous accounts rather than staying silent; **confidentiality** requires not passing a client's or employer's sensitive financial information to a third party (e.g. a family member trading shares) even informally; and **professional competence and due care** requires declining, or seeking help with, a task genuinely beyond the accountant's current expertise (e.g. an unfamiliar area of tax law) rather than guessing.

## Explicitly not here
The legal/regulatory frameworks and professional bodies that enforce these principles are S20; the specific actions to take when facing a genuine ethical dilemma are also S20.
