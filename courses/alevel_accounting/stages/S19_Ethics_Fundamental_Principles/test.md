# S19_Ethics_Fundamental_Principles - Test: Fundamental ethical principles

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Which fundamental principle is most directly concerned with not disclosing a client's information without proper authority? Choose every correct option.
   A. Integrity
   B. Objectivity
   C. Confidentiality
   D. Professional competence and due care
2. A finance director asks the company accountant to delay writing off a known irrecoverable debt until after the year end, so this year's profit looks better. Identify the principle most directly threatened and explain why. [3 marks]
3. An accountant is asked to advise on a highly specialised area of tax law outside their experience. Which principle is most relevant to how they should respond? Choose every correct option.
   A. Confidentiality
   B. Professional competence and due care
   C. Professional behaviour
   D. Integrity
4. Explain, with reference to one named ethical principle, why an accountant should not pass on confidential information learned at work to a family member, even informally and with no direct payment involved. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
2. [3] B1 objectivity (or integrity); B2 the accountant is being pressured to let the director's preference for a better-looking result override the professional judgement that the evidence (the debt is known to be irrecoverable) actually supports, which would also misrepresent the financial statements.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 confidentiality; B1 the principle applies regardless of whether the accountant personally benefits financially - using or disclosing information acquired professionally for anyone's advantage without proper authority is a breach, informal or not.
