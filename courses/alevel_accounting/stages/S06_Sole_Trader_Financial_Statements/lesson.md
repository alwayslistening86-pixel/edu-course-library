# S06_Sole_Trader_Financial_Statements - Lesson: Sole trader financial statements

## Goal
The learner prepares a sole trader's statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position from a trial balance, incorporating period-end adjustments.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.6a - Sole trader statement of profit or loss
- 3.6b - Sole trader statement of financial position

## How to teach this
Ask: two figures in a trial balance both relate to inventory - the opening figure and the closing figure. Why does only one of them appear in the cost of sales calculation, and where does the other one go? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.6a Sole trader statement of profit or loss
**Statement of profit or loss structure**: revenue less **cost of sales** (opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory) gives **gross profit**; gross profit less operating expenses (wages, rent, depreciation, irrecoverable debts, etc.) gives **profit for the year**. *Worked example*: opening inventory £8,000, purchases £95,000, closing inventory £11,000: cost of sales = 8,000 + 95,000 - 11,000 = £92,000. Revenue £140,000: gross profit = 140,000 - 92,000 = £48,000 (gross profit margin = 34.3%). Operating expenses £35,000: profit for the year = £13,000.

#### 3.6b Sole trader statement of financial position
**Statement of financial position structure**: non-current assets (at carrying amount, i.e. cost less accumulated depreciation) plus current assets (closing inventory, receivables net of any allowance for doubtful debts, prepayments, cash/bank) less current liabilities (payables, accruals, a bank overdraft) less non-current liabilities (a long-term loan) equals **capital employed**, which must equal **capital** (opening capital + profit for the year - drawings). *Worked example*: opening capital £40,000, profit for the year £28,000 (from 3.6a's cost of sales example with lower expenses), drawings £15,000: closing capital = 40,000 + 28,000 - 15,000 = £53,000, and this figure must equal total assets less total liabilities in the statement of financial position.

## Explicitly not here
Limited company statements (with share capital, reserves and a statement of changes in equity) are S07; ratio-based evaluation of these statements is S08.
