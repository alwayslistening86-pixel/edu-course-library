# S07_Limited_Company_Accounts - Lesson: Limited company accounts

## Goal
The learner prepares a limited company's internal statement of profit or loss (through to profit after tax), statement of changes in equity, and statement of financial position with the required subheadings.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.7a - Company statement of profit or loss
- 3.7b - Statement of changes in equity
- 3.7c - Company statement of financial position with subheadings

## How to teach this
Ask: a company's statement of profit or loss ends with 'profit for the year after tax' - so where does the dividend the shareholders actually receive get accounted for, if not as an expense on that statement? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.7a Company statement of profit or loss
**Company statement of profit or loss**: builds on the sole trader format but continues further: gross profit less operating expenses gives **profit from operations**; less finance costs (e.g. debenture interest) gives **profit for the year before tax**; less corporation tax gives **profit for the year after tax** - this is the company's equivalent of a sole trader's "profit for the year", and it belongs to the shareholders, whether or not it is paid out as a dividend. *Worked example*: profit from operations £60,000, finance costs £5,000: profit before tax = 60,000 - 5,000 = £55,000; tax at 19% = 10,450 (rounded): profit after tax = £44,550.

#### 3.7b Statement of changes in equity
**Statement of changes in equity**: reconciles the movement in each component of equity (share capital, share premium, retained earnings) over the period: opening balance + new shares issued (at nominal value to share capital, any premium to share premium) + profit for the year (to retained earnings) - dividends paid (from retained earnings) = closing balance. Only a dividend actually **paid** (or declared and approved) in the period is deducted here - it is never treated as an expense in the statement of profit or loss. *Worked example*: retained earnings brought forward £80,000, profit for the year £45,000, dividend paid £20,000: retained earnings carried forward = 80,000 + 45,000 - 20,000 = £105,000.

#### 3.7c Company statement of financial position with subheadings
**Statement of financial position, with required subheadings**: **non-current assets**; **current assets** (inventory, receivables, prepayments, cash); **current liabilities** (payables, accruals, tax payable, an overdraft, any loan due within a year); **non-current liabilities** (a bank loan/debentures not due within a year); and **equity** (share capital, share premium, retained earnings - drawn from the closing figures in the statement of changes in equity). As for a sole trader, total assets less total liabilities must equal total equity.

## Explicitly not here
Revaluations of non-current assets, share issues (including rights and bonus issues) and published accounts/IAS are S16; interpreting these statements with ratios is S08.
