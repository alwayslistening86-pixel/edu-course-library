# S14_Incomplete_Records - Test: Accounting for organisations with incomplete records

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Opening assets are £44,000 and opening liabilities are £9,000. Closing assets are £61,000 and closing liabilities are £13,000. Drawings during the year were £14,000 and the owner introduced £5,000 of additional capital. Calculate profit for the year. [5 marks]
2. In the net assets method, drawings during the year are: Choose every correct option.
   A. Added when calculating profit, because they reduced net assets for a reason other than a loss
   B. Deducted when calculating profit
   C. Ignored entirely
   D. Treated as a liability at the year end
3. Opening receivables are £7,000, cash received from credit customers during the year is £95,000, and closing receivables are £8,500. Calculate credit sales for the year. [3 marks]
4. State one limitation of relying on incomplete (single-entry) records rather than full double-entry bookkeeping. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] M1 opening net assets = 44,000-9,000 = £35,000; M1 closing net assets = 61,000-13,000 = £48,000; M1 increase in net assets = 48,000 - 35,000 = £13,000; A1 profit = 13,000 + 14,000 - 5,000 = £22,000; B1 correct treatment of drawings (added back) and capital introduced (deducted).
2. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
3. [3] M1 credit sales = closing receivables - opening receivables + cash received; A1 = 8,500 - 7,000 + 95,000; A1 = £96,500.
4. [2] B2 with no double entry there is no built-in check that debits equal credits, so errors and omissions are much harder to detect; or: producing a full, detailed statement of profit or loss requires extra reconstruction work (e.g. rebuilding total accounts for sales, purchases and expenses) rather than being a direct output of the records (either point, fully explained, for 2 marks).
