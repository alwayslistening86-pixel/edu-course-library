# S15_Partnership_Accounts - Lesson: Partnership accounts

## Goal
The learner prepares a partnership appropriation account and partners' capital and current accounts, and accounts for a change in partnership (admission or retirement of a partner, including goodwill).

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.15a - Partnership appropriation account
- 3.15b - Partners' capital and current accounts
- 3.15c - Admission/retirement of a partner and goodwill

## How to teach this
Ask: two partners put in very different amounts of capital and one works full-time in the business while the other is a silent investor. Why would splitting the profit simply 50:50 likely be unfair to both? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### 3.15a Partnership appropriation account
**The appropriation account**: divides a partnership's profit for the year among the partners, in this order: **interest on capital** (rewarding partners for the capital they have invested, at an agreed rate), **partners' salaries** (rewarding a partner's work in the business, distinct from a limited company director's salary, which is a statement-of-profit-or-loss expense - a partner's "salary" is an appropriation of profit, not an expense), then the **residual profit** shared in the agreed **profit-sharing ratio**. *Worked example*: profit for the year £30,000; Partner A's capital £40,000 and Partner B's capital £20,000, both earning interest on capital at 5%; Partner B also receives a salary of £8,000; remaining profit shared 3:2 (A:B). Interest on capital: A = 40,000 x 0.05 = £2,000, B = 20,000 x 0.05 = £1,000. Remaining profit after interest and salary = 30,000 - 2,000 - 1,000 - 8,000 = £19,000; A's share = 19,000 x 3/5 = £11,400; B's share = 19,000 x 2/5 = £7,600. Total to A = 2,000 + 11,400 = £13,400; total to B = 1,000 + 8,000 + 7,600 = £16,600 (the two totals sum back to the £30,000 profit).

#### 3.15b Partners' capital and current accounts
**Capital and current accounts**: each partner has a fixed **capital account** (the capital they have introduced, only changed by a further injection or permanent withdrawal of capital), and a separate **current account** (accumulating each year's share of profit per the appropriation account, less drawings, so it fluctuates year to year) - keeping the "permanent stake" and "running balance of undrawn profit" clearly apart. A current account can go overdrawn (a debit balance) if a partner draws out more than their accumulated profit share.

#### 3.15c Admission/retirement of a partner and goodwill
**Changes in partnership - admission or retirement, and goodwill**: when a partner joins or leaves, the profit-sharing ratio changes, and **goodwill** (the value of the business's reputation, customer base and other unrecorded advantages, over and above its net assets) built up under the old ratio must be fairly credited to the existing partners before the change, since the new/continuing partner(s) should not gain a share of value they did not help create. This is normally done **without** permanently recording goodwill as an asset: goodwill is valued, credited to the old partners in their **old** profit-sharing ratio (via their capital accounts), then immediately debited to all partners (including any new one) in the **new** ratio - so goodwill nets to zero in the accounts but each partner's capital account correctly reflects the adjustment. *Worked example*: goodwill is valued at £40,000 when a new partner joins; the two existing partners shared profit 3:2 and will now share (with the new partner) 2:2:1. Credit old partners in old ratio: partner 1 = 40,000 x 3/5 = £24,000, partner 2 = 40,000 x 2/5 = £16,000; then debit all three partners in the new ratio: partner 1 = 40,000 x 2/5 = £16,000, partner 2 = 40,000 x 2/5 = £16,000, new partner = 40,000 x 1/5 = £8,000.
