# S21_Quantitative_and_Exam_Skills - Lesson: Quantitative and exam skills: written, numerical and evaluative

## Goal
The learner draws together quantitative and qualitative accounting information to reach and support a decision or judgement, and practises the exam skills needed across Section A (multiple choice/short answer), Section B (structured, calculation-based) and Section C (25-mark extended-response evaluation) on both papers.

## Syllabus items taught here
- QS8 - Written, graphical and numerical information
- QS9 - Quantitative and non-quantitative information for decisions

## How to teach this
Ask: a set of accounts shows strong ROCE but a very poor current ratio, and the business's biggest customer has just gone into administration. How would you weigh the numbers against that one piece of qualitative news in reaching an overall judgement? Teach each topic with a worked numerical example wherever the content is calculation-based (ledger entries, ratios, variances, investment appraisal): have the learner attempt the calculation before seeing the worked answer. For evaluative content (S17-S21), always pair a calculated figure with the qualitative/contextual factors that should be weighed against it - AQA's Section C marking specifically rewards this. All numerical worked examples were computed with Python when the course was built.

#### QS8 Written, graphical and numerical information
QS8: **interpret, apply and analyse information in written, graphical and numerical forms** - every AQA Accounting exam question draws on some mix of a scenario (written), figures in a table or trial balance (numerical), and sometimes a chart (graphical, e.g. a break-even chart); the skill is reading all three together accurately before answering, not treating the numbers in isolation from the context given.

#### QS9 Quantitative and non-quantitative information for decisions
QS9: **use and interpret quantitative and non-quantitative information in order to make decisions** - the culmination of the course: a real business decision (accept an order, invest in a project, choose a costing method, admit a partner) is rarely settled by a single calculated figure alone. AQA's 25-mark Section C questions (worth 5 AO2 application marks and 20 AO3 analysis/evaluation marks, marked by a five-level response scheme: Level 1, 1-5 marks, an asserted but unsupported judgement; Level 2, 6-10, a basic judgement with limited evaluation of evidence; Level 3, 11-15, an underdeveloped judgement supported by evaluation of a range of evidence; Level 4, 16-20, a reasoned but in places unbalanced judgement supported by evaluation of a wide range of evidence; Level 5, 21-25, a clear and balanced response supported by an astute consideration of a wide range of evidence) reward weighing calculated figures **against** qualitative, contextual factors (risk, stakeholder impact, reliability of estimates, non-financial consequences) to reach a genuinely supported judgement, not just stating "the NPV is positive so accept" without any consideration of the evidence on the other side.

## Explicitly not here
Nothing new is taught here; this stage practises applying everything from S01-S20 together, in AQA's own question styles.
