# S21_Quantitative_and_Exam_Skills - Test: Quantitative and exam skills: written, numerical and evaluative

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A project has a positive NPV of £8,000 but relies on a sales forecast the finance director admits is 'optimistic', and the discount rate used has not been reviewed for three years. Using both the quantitative and non-quantitative evidence, write a short evaluation (Level 3 standard) of whether the project should be accepted. [9 marks]
2. Which piece of information below is qualitative (non-quantitative) rather than quantitative? Choose every correct option.
   A. Net present value of £8,000
   B. A payback period of 3.2 years
   C. The finance director's admission that the sales forecast is optimistic
   D. A discount rate of 10%
3. Explain why a 25-mark Section C answer that only states 'the NPV is positive, so the project should be accepted' would not reach the top mark band, even if the NPV calculation itself is correct. [3 marks]
4. Two competitor businesses have identical ROCE, but Business X operates in a stable, established market while Business Y operates in a market undergoing rapid technological change. Explain why this non-quantitative context matters when comparing the two ratios. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [9] Level 3 (5-9 marks, underdeveloped judgement supported by evaluation of a range of evidence): identifies the positive NPV as a reason favouring acceptance (AO2, restating the calculated evidence); sets this against the non-financial evidence given - an admittedly optimistic sales forecast (inflating the cash inflows used) and a discount rate not reviewed in three years (potentially understating the true cost of capital, both of which would reduce or eliminate the £8,000 margin if corrected) - and reaches a judgement (e.g. accept only once the forecast and discount rate are independently reviewed, or reject as currently unreliable) that is explicitly supported by weighing this evidence, rather than asserted. Level 1-2 (1-4 marks): asserts a decision with little or no use of the evidence given. Level 4-5 (marks above this band are not available for a 9-mark item at this course's standard) would require a fuller range of evidence than a 9-mark question allows; the full five-level, 25-mark version of this skill is assessed in the exam's Section C.
2. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
3. [3] B3 the top level (21-25) requires a clear and balanced response supported by an astute consideration of a wide range of evidence; a bare assertion from one calculated figure alone shows no evaluation of the evidence at all (no weighing of risk, reliability of the forecasts/assumptions used, or other qualitative factors), so it would sit only in the lowest level even though the underlying calculation is correct - AO3 (analysis/evaluation) marks reward the reasoning, not just a correct number.
4. [3] B3 an identical ROCE does not mean identical risk or future prospects - Business Y's return may be harder to sustain if rapid technological change threatens to make its products or processes obsolete, whereas Business X's return, in a stable market, may be more reliably repeatable; comparing the ratio alone, without this qualitative context, could mislead a decision-maker choosing between the two.
