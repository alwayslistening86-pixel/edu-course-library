# S11_Standard_Costing_and_Variances - Test: Standard costing and variance analysis

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AQA's style: short calculations with mark schemes (M = method, A = accuracy, B = independent/bookwork mark), multiple-choice items, and short written/evaluative questions. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Standard material cost is 3kg at £4/kg = £12/unit. Actual output of 800 units used 2,600kg costing £10,140. Calculate the material price variance and the material usage variance, stating favourable or adverse for each. [6 marks]
2. A favourable labour rate variance means: Choose every correct option.
   A. More hours were worked than standard allowed
   B. The actual rate paid per hour was less than the standard rate
   C. Fewer hours were worked than standard allowed
   D. The actual rate paid per hour was more than the standard rate
3. Standard labour cost is 3 hours at £12/hour = £36/unit. Actual output of 600 units took 1,850 hours costing £23,310. Calculate the labour rate variance and the labour efficiency variance, stating favourable or adverse for each. [6 marks]
4. State one advantage and one disadvantage of a standard costing system. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] M1 standard cost of actual quantity = 4 x 2,600 = £10,400; A1 price variance = 10,400 - 10,140 = £260 favourable; M1 standard quantity for actual output = 800 x 3 = 2,400kg; A1 usage variance = (2,400 - 2,600) x 4 = £800 adverse; B1 correct sign/interpretation for the price variance (actual cost of the material used was below standard, so favourable); B1 correct sign/interpretation for the usage variance (more material was used than standard allowed, so adverse).
2. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
3. [6] M1 standard cost of actual hours = 12 x 1,850 = £22,200; A1 rate variance = 22,200 - 23,310 = £1,110 adverse; M1 standard hours for actual output = 600 x 3 = 1,800 hours; A1 efficiency variance = (1,800 - 1,850) x 12 = £600 adverse; B1 correct interpretation of the rate variance (actual rate paid exceeded standard); B1 correct interpretation of the efficiency variance (more hours were taken than standard allowed).
4. [2] B1 advantage: it supports management by exception, letting managers focus attention on significant variances rather than checking every cost individually (or: supports budgeting/pricing); B1 disadvantage: standards can become outdated and need ongoing revision, or may not suit highly variable/bespoke production (any valid advantage and disadvantage, one mark each).
