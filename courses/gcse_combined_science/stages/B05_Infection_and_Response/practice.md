# B05_Infection_and_Response - Practice: Communicable disease, defence, vaccination and drugs

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Make a table of measles, HIV, tobacco mosaic virus, salmonella, gonorrhoea, rose black spot and malaria: pathogen type, symptoms, how spread and how controlled.
2. Explain the roles of skin, mucus, stomach acid and white blood cells (phagocytosis, antibodies, antitoxins) in defence.
3. Explain how vaccination protects an individual and gives herd immunity, and why the second exposure produces a faster response.
4. Explain why antibiotics do not work on viruses, and describe the stages of testing a new drug including placebo and double-blind trials. Higher: explain how antibiotic resistance arises.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
