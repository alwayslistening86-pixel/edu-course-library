# B06_Bioenergetics - Lesson: Photosynthesis, respiration and metabolism

## Goal
The learner explains the photosynthesis reaction and its limiting factors, aerobic and anaerobic respiration, the response to exercise and metabolism, and carries out the photosynthesis investigation.

## How to teach this
Start with: 'Where does the mass of a tree come from?' Let the learner argue before revealing carbon dioxide and water.

#### 4.4.1.1 Photosynthetic reaction
Photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts using chlorophyll to absorb light. Word equation: carbon dioxide + water gives glucose + oxygen, using light. Symbol equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O gives C6H12O6 + 6O2. It is an endothermic reaction because energy is transferred from the environment to the chloroplasts.

#### 4.4.1.2 Rate of photosynthesis
Rate can be limited by light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature or amount of chlorophyll. On a graph the rate rises then levels off when another factor becomes limiting. At high temperatures enzymes denature and the rate falls. Higher: light intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the source: doubling the distance cuts intensity to one quarter. Greenhouse growers control light, CO2 and temperature to raise yields, balancing costs and profit.

#### 4.4.1.3 Uses of glucose from photosynthesis
Plants use glucose for respiration, convert it to starch for storage, to fats or oils for storage in seeds, to cellulose for cell walls, and with nitrate ions from soil to make amino acids and then proteins. So plants need minerals as well as light and CO2.

#### 4.4.2.1 Aerobic and anaerobic respiration
Respiration transfers energy from glucose and is exothermic. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen and takes place in mitochondria: glucose + oxygen gives carbon dioxide + water (C6H12O6 + 6O2 gives 6CO2 + 6H2O). Anaerobic respiration in muscles gives glucose to lactic acid; in plants and yeast it gives ethanol + carbon dioxide (fermentation, used in brewing and baking). Anaerobic releases much less energy per glucose molecule.

#### 4.4.2.2 Response to exercise
During exercise the muscles need more energy, so heart rate, breathing rate and breath volume increase to supply more oxygen and remove more carbon dioxide. If oxygen supply is not enough, anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid, causing muscle fatigue. After exercise, extra oxygen is needed to react with the lactic acid: this is the oxygen debt. Blood transports lactic acid to the liver to be converted back to glucose.

#### 4.4.2.3 Metabolism
Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions in a cell or the body. It includes conversion of glucose to starch, glycogen and cellulose, formation of lipid molecules from a molecule of glycerol and three fatty acids, use of glucose and nitrate ions to make amino acids and then proteins, respiration, and breakdown of excess protein to form urea for excretion.

#### B-RP5 Required practical: light intensity and the rate of photosynthesis
Investigate the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis using an aquatic plant such as pondweed. Place pondweed in a beaker of water with sodium hydrogencarbonate, position a lamp at measured distances, and count bubbles of oxygen per minute (or measure the volume of gas). Control temperature with a water bath or heat shield and repeat to obtain a mean. Plot rate against distance or 1 divided by distance squared.
