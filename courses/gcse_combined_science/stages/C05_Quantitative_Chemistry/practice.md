# C05_Quantitative_Chemistry - Practice: Conservation of mass, moles and concentration

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Balance: Mg + O2 -> MgO; Al + O2 -> Al2O3; and calculate the relative formula mass of CaCO3 and Mg(OH)2.
2. Explain why the mass increases when magnesium burns in an open crucible and why it decreases when calcium carbonate is heated.
3. 5 g of solute is dissolved in 250 cm3 of solution; calculate the concentration in g/dm3. Six repeat readings give a range of 0.4; state the uncertainty as half the range.
4. Higher: calculate the moles in 4.4 g of carbon dioxide and the mass of magnesium oxide made from 12 g of magnesium in 2Mg + O2 -> 2MgO.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
