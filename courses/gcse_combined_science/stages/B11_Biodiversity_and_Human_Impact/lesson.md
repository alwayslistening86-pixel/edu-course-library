# B11_Biodiversity_and_Human_Impact - Lesson: Biodiversity, land use and global warming

## Goal
The learner explains biodiversity, waste management, land use, deforestation, global warming and the maintenance of biodiversity, and evaluates human impacts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.7.3.1 - Biodiversity: learn and apply: biodiversity
- 4.7.3.2 - Waste management: learn and apply: waste management
- 4.7.3.3 - Land use: learn and apply: land use
- 4.7.3.4 - Deforestation: learn and apply: deforestation
- 4.7.3.5 - Global warming: learn and apply: global warming
- 4.7.3.6 - Maintaining biodiversity: learn and apply: maintaining biodiversity

## How to teach this
Start with: 'What would be lost if all the hedgerows on a farm were cleared?' Lead to biodiversity and the trade-offs people face.

#### 4.7.3.1 Biodiversity
Biodiversity is the variety of all living species on Earth or in an ecosystem. High biodiversity gives ecosystems stability because species depend on each other. Humans have reduced biodiversity by using land, generating waste, deforestation and adding greenhouse gases; there are now positive efforts to reduce the harm.

#### 4.7.3.2 Waste management
As the population and standard of living rise, more resources are used and more waste is produced. Water can be polluted by sewage, fertiliser and toxic chemicals; air by smoke and acidic gases from burning fuel; land by landfill and toxic chemicals, including pesticides. Unless carefully managed, pollution kills plants and animals and reduces biodiversity.

#### 4.7.3.3 Land use
Humans reduce land available for other animals and plants by building, quarrying, farming and dumping waste. Peat bogs are destroyed to provide compost and fuel; this releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere and destroys habitats for specialised species.

#### 4.7.3.4 Deforestation
Large-scale deforestation in tropical areas provides land for cattle and rice fields, and for growing crops for biofuels. Consequences include reduced biodiversity, reduced photosynthesis removing less CO2 from the air, and CO2 released by burning and decay of the trees.

#### 4.7.3.5 Global warming
Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and methane absorb thermal energy, maintaining Earth's temperature; increasing levels contribute to global warming. Consequences include rising sea levels from melting ice, changes in the distribution of species, loss of habitat and extreme weather, and less biodiversity.

#### 4.7.3.6 Maintaining biodiversity
Actions include breeding programmes for endangered species, protection and regeneration of rare habitats, reintroduction of field margins and hedgerows in agricultural areas where farmers grow only one crop, reducing deforestation and CO2 emissions by some governments, and recycling resources rather than dumping waste. Conservation may conflict with economic needs of local people, so evaluations weigh costs and benefits.
