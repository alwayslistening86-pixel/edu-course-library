# P07_Motion - Lesson: Distance, speed, velocity and acceleration

## Goal
The learner distinguishes distance and displacement, speed and velocity, uses distance-time and velocity-time graphs, and calculates acceleration.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 6.5.4.1.1 - Distance and displacement: learn and apply: distance and displacement
- 6.5.4.1.2 - Speed: learn and apply: speed
- 6.5.4.1.3 - Velocity: learn and apply: velocity
- 6.5.4.1.4 - The distance-time relationship: learn and apply: the distance-time relationship
- 6.5.4.1.5 - Acceleration: learn and apply: acceleration

## How to teach this
Start with: 'A runner completes one lap of a 400 m track. What is her distance and her displacement?'

#### 6.5.4.1.1 Distance and displacement
Distance is how far an object moves and is a scalar; displacement includes both the distance and direction in a straight line from the start to the finish, and is a vector. A runner who completes a 400 m lap has travelled 400 m but has displacement zero.

#### 6.5.4.1.2 Speed
Speed does not need to have direction. Distance travelled = speed x time (s = v t). Typical speeds: walking about 1.5 m/s, running 3 m/s, cycling 6 m/s, cars 25 m/s, sound in air 330 m/s. A car at 20 m/s covers 1200 m in 60 s. Speed is often not constant, so calculations use an average speed.

#### 6.5.4.1.3 Velocity
Velocity is speed in a given direction and is a vector. An object moving in a circle at constant speed has a changing velocity because its direction changes continuously. So it accelerates even when its speed is constant, and this needs a resultant force towards the centre.

#### 6.5.4.1.4 The distance-time relationship
On a distance-time graph a horizontal line means stationary and a straight sloping line means constant speed; the gradient is the speed. A curved line means changing speed, and the speed at a point is the gradient of the tangent. Steeper means faster. A curve getting steeper shows acceleration.

#### 6.5.4.1.5 Acceleration
Acceleration = change in velocity / time taken (a = change in v / t), in m/s2. A car going from 0 to 24 m/s in 8 s has acceleration 3 m/s2. Deceleration is negative acceleration. For uniform acceleration, v squared minus u squared = 2 a s. On a velocity-time graph the gradient is the acceleration and the area under the line is the distance travelled. Objects falling through the air reach terminal velocity when air resistance equals weight, so the resultant force is zero.
