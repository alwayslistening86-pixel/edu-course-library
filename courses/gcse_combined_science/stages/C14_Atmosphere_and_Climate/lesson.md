# C14_Atmosphere_and_Climate - Lesson: The atmosphere and climate change

## Goal
The learner describes the composition and evolution of the atmosphere, greenhouse gases, human activity, climate change and carbon footprints.

## How to teach this
Start with: 'What would the air have been like 3 billion years ago, and how do we know?' Build from evidence.

#### 5.9.1.1 The proportions of different gases in the atmosphere
For about the last 200 million years the atmosphere has been about 80 per cent nitrogen, about 20 per cent oxygen, with small proportions of carbon dioxide (about 0.04 per cent), water vapour and noble gases such as argon.

#### 5.9.1.2 The Earth's early atmosphere
The early atmosphere, billions of years ago, came from volcanic activity and was mainly carbon dioxide with little or no oxygen, plus water vapour, nitrogen, and small amounts of methane and ammonia. As the Earth cooled, water vapour condensed to form the oceans. Theories are uncertain because the evidence is billions of years old.

#### 5.9.1.3 How oxygen increased
Algae and later plants evolved and produced oxygen by photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water gives glucose + oxygen. Over about a billion years the oxygen level rose, enabling evolution of animals.

#### 5.9.1.4 How carbon dioxide decreased
Carbon dioxide decreased because it was absorbed by algae and plants for photosynthesis, dissolved into the oceans, and locked away in sedimentary rocks such as limestone (calcium carbonate from shells and skeletons) and in fossil fuels formed from dead organisms.

#### 5.9.2.1 Greenhouse gases
Greenhouse gases such as water vapour, carbon dioxide and methane absorb long-wavelength infrared radiation from the Earth and re-radiate some back, keeping the Earth warm enough for life. Without them, the average temperature would be much colder.

#### 5.9.2.2 Human activities which contribute to an increase in greenhouse gases in the atmosphere
Burning fossil fuels and deforestation increase carbon dioxide; livestock farming and rice paddies produce methane; landfill decay also releases methane. Estimating the effects has uncertainties, and peer review and models must be evaluated critically.

#### 5.9.2.3 Global climate change
Increased greenhouse gases are linked to a rise in average global temperature. Possible effects include rising sea levels, more frequent extreme weather, changes in the amount and distribution of rainfall, and changes to habitats, crop yields and the distribution of species. Predictions use computer models, which are complex, so there is uncertainty; evidence should be judged for bias and correlation versus causation.

#### 5.9.2.4 The carbon footprint and its reduction
The carbon footprint is the total amount of carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases emitted over the full life cycle of a product, service or event. Reduction methods include using renewable energy, improving energy efficiency, using alternative fuels, and carbon capture and storage; economic and social factors, international disagreement and individual lifestyles make reduction challenging.
