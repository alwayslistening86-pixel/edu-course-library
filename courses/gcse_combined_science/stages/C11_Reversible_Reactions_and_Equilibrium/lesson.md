## How to teach this
Start with: 'Is it possible for a reaction to go backwards? What would you see?'

#### 5.6.2.1 Reversible reactions
In a reversible reaction the products can react to produce the original reactants: A + B (double arrow) C + D. Example: heating hydrated copper sulfate gives anhydrous copper sulfate and water, and adding water reverses it. Ammonium chloride decomposes on heating to ammonia and hydrogen chloride, which recombine on cooling.

#### 5.6.2.2 Energy changes and reversible reactions
If a reaction is exothermic in one direction, it is endothermic in the opposite direction, and the same amount of energy is transferred in each. Heating hydrated copper sulfate takes in energy (endothermic); adding water to the anhydrous form releases the same energy (exothermic).

#### 5.6.2.3 Equilibrium
When a reversible reaction occurs in a closed system, equilibrium is reached when the forward and reverse reactions occur at exactly the same rate. Concentrations of reactants and products stay constant, though reactions continue: this is a dynamic equilibrium. The relative amounts of products and reactants depend on the conditions.

#### 5.6.2.4 The effect of changing conditions on equilibrium (Higher tier)
Le Chatelier's principle: if a system is at equilibrium and a condition is changed, the system responds to counteract the change. Use it to predict the effect of changes in concentration, temperature and pressure.

#### 5.6.2.5 The effect of changing concentration (Higher tier)
If the concentration of a reactant is increased, more products form until equilibrium is reached again. If the concentration of a product is decreased, more reactants react to form product to restore equilibrium. Removing a product as it forms therefore pushes the reaction forward.

#### 5.6.2.6 The effect of temperature changes on equilibrium (Higher tier)
If the temperature is raised, the equilibrium shifts in the endothermic direction to absorb the energy, so more of the products of the endothermic direction form. If the temperature is lowered, the equilibrium shifts in the exothermic direction. For an exothermic forward reaction, raising temperature decreases the yield of products.

#### 5.6.2.7 The effect of pressure changes on equilibrium (Higher tier)
Pressure changes affect only reactions involving gases. Increasing pressure shifts the equilibrium to the side with fewer gas molecules; decreasing pressure shifts it to the side with more. N2 + 3H2 (double arrow) 2NH3 has 4 molecules on the left and 2 on the right, so high pressure favours ammonia.
