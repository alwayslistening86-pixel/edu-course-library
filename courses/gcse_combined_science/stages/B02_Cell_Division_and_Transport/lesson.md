# B02_Cell_Division_and_Transport - Lesson: Cell division, stem cells and transport in cells

## Goal

## How to teach this
Start with: 'A cut on your hand heals. Where do the new cells come from, and how do substances get into them?'

#### 4.1.2.1 Chromosomes
The nucleus contains chromosomes made of long DNA molecules; each chromosome carries many genes and each gene codes for a particular protein. Body cells are diploid: humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes, 46 in total, one of each pair from each parent. Gametes (sperm and egg) have only 23 single chromosomes.

#### 4.1.2.2 Mitosis and the cell cycle
Cells divide by mitosis as part of the cell cycle. First the cell grows, increasing the number of sub-cellular structures such as ribosomes and mitochondria, and the DNA replicates so each chromosome is copied. In mitosis the chromosome copies are pulled to opposite ends of the cell and the nucleus divides. Finally the cytoplasm and membranes divide (cytokinesis), giving two genetically identical diploid daughter cells. Mitosis is used for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

#### 4.1.2.3 Stem cells
Stem cells are undifferentiated cells that can divide and become specialised. Embryonic stem cells can become any cell type; adult stem cells, for example in bone marrow, can form only some types; plant meristem cells can become any plant cell throughout life. Uses: treating diseases such as diabetes or paralysis, and therapeutic cloning, which makes an embryo with the patient's genes so tissue is not rejected. Risks include viral infection transferred with the cells, and ethical objections to using embryos.

#### 4.1.3.1 Diffusion
Diffusion is the spreading out of particles from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration, so needs no energy. Gases and dissolved substances such as oxygen, carbon dioxide and urea cross membranes this way. Rate rises with a steeper concentration gradient, higher temperature and larger surface area. Exchange surfaces such as alveoli, villi, gills and root hairs are adapted with a large surface area, thin membranes, a good blood supply and ventilation. Small organisms have a large surface-area-to-volume ratio; large ones need specialised exchange systems.

#### 4.1.3.2 Osmosis
Osmosis is the diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane from a dilute solution (high water concentration) to a more concentrated solution. If plant tissue gains water it gains mass; if it loses water it loses mass. Percentage change in mass = (final mass minus initial mass) divided by initial mass, times 100. A potato cylinder going from 2.5 g to 2.2 g has changed by minus 12 per cent.

#### 4.1.3.3 Active transport
Active transport moves substances from a more dilute solution to a more concentrated one, against the concentration gradient, using energy from respiration. Root hair cells absorb mineral ions this way from soil that is more dilute than the cell. In the gut, glucose is absorbed into the blood by active transport when its concentration in the gut is lower than in the blood. Unlike diffusion, it needs energy and carrier proteins.

#### B-RP2 Required practical: osmosis in plant tissue
Investigate the effect of a range of concentrations of salt or sugar solutions on the mass of plant tissue. Cut equal-sized potato cylinders, dry and weigh them, place each in a different concentration for a fixed time, blot and reweigh. Plot percentage change in mass against concentration; the point where the line crosses zero shows the concentration inside the potato cells. Control the size, time, temperature and volume of solution.
