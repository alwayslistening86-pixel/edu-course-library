# C02_The_Periodic_Table - Lesson: The periodic table and groups

## Goal
The learner explains how the periodic table is arranged and developed, and describes the properties and trends of metals, non-metals, Group 0, Group 1 and Group 7.

## How to teach this
Start with: 'How did Mendeleev predict elements nobody had found?' Let the learner sort element cards before naming the rules.

#### 5.1.2.1 The periodic table
Elements are arranged in order of atomic number, in rows called periods and columns called groups. Elements in a group have the same number of outer electrons, so similar chemical properties; the group number is the outer shell electron number. The table shows metals on the left and non-metals on the right.

#### 5.1.2.2 Development of the periodic table
Early attempts arranged elements by atomic weight. Newlands' law of octaves grouped similar elements but broke down for heavier ones. Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic weight but also by properties, switching some order and leaving gaps for undiscovered elements, then predicting their properties; the discovery of gallium and germanium matching those predictions gained acceptance. Knowledge of protons and electronic structure later explained the order by atomic number and the presence of isotopes.

#### 5.1.2.3 Metals and non-metals
Metals react to form positive ions by losing outer electrons. Non-metals do not form positive ions; they gain electrons or share them. Metals are typically shiny, malleable, ductile and good conductors; non-metals are generally poor conductors with lower melting points. Metal oxides are basic, non-metal oxides are acidic.

#### 5.1.2.4 Group 0
The noble gases (helium, neon, argon and so on) are unreactive, non-flammable, monatomic gases because they have full outer shells, and they do not easily form molecules. Boiling points increase down the group as relative atomic mass increases.

#### 5.1.2.5 Group 1
The alkali metals have one electron in the outer shell. They are soft, with low melting points and density; reactivity increases down the group because the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more easily lost. They react with water to produce a metal hydroxide (alkaline solution) and hydrogen: 2Na + 2H2O gives 2NaOH + H2. They react with non-metals to form ionic compounds containing a 1+ ion, for example with chlorine forming white crystalline chlorides.

#### 5.1.2.6 Group 7
The halogens are non-metals with seven outer electrons that exist as diatomic molecules (F2, Cl2, Br2, I2). Chlorine is a pale green gas, bromine an orange-brown liquid, iodine a grey solid giving a purple vapour. Melting and boiling points increase down the group. Reactivity decreases down the group because the outer shell is further from the nucleus, so it attracts an extra electron less strongly. A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from a solution of its salt: chlorine displaces bromine from potassium bromide. Halogens form ionic salts with metals (1- ions) and covalent compounds with non-metals.
