# S19_Water_Resources_and_Life_Cycle_Assessment - Lesson: Sustainable development, potable water, waste water, alternative extraction, life cycle assessment and recycling (required practical 8)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Why can't we simply drink seawater or river water, and what has to be done to make it safe?' Then extend to what happens to waste water.

#### 4.10.1.1 Using the Earth's resources and sustainable development
Humans use natural resources for warmth, shelter, food and transport. Finite resources (crude oil, metal ores) are not replaced; renewable resources can be replaced. Sustainable development meets the needs of current generations without compromising future ones. Chemistry improves agricultural and industrial processes and provides new synthetic products.

#### 4.10.1.2 Potable water
Potable water is safe to drink; it has low levels of dissolved substances and microbes. In the UK, fresh water from rain collects in lakes, rivers and groundwater. Treatment: choose a suitable source, filter to remove solids, and sterilise with chlorine, ozone or ultraviolet light. If fresh water is scarce, desalination of salt water by distillation or reverse osmosis is used, but they need large amounts of energy.

#### 4.10.1.3 Waste water treatment
Sewage and agricultural and industrial waste water need treatment before release. Sewage treatment: screening and grit removal, sedimentation to produce sewage sludge and effluent, anaerobic digestion of the sludge, and aerobic biological treatment of the effluent. Industrial waste water may need extra treatment to remove organic matter and harmful chemicals.

#### 4.10.1.4 Alternative methods of extracting metals
Low-grade ores can be treated by phytomining, in which plants absorb metal compounds from soil, are harvested and burned, and the ash contains metal compounds. In bioleaching, bacteria produce a leachate solution containing metal compounds. The metals are extracted from these solutions by displacement using scrap iron or by electrolysis. These methods reduce the environmental impact of mining but are slower.

#### 4.10.2.1 Life cycle assessment
A life cycle assessment (LCA) assesses the environmental impact of a product at each stage: extracting and processing raw materials, manufacturing and packaging, use and operation during its lifetime, and disposal, including transport. It considers use of energy, water and resources and the production of waste and pollutants. Some values can be quantified; others need value judgements, so LCAs can be biased or selective.

#### 4.10.2.2 Ways of reducing the use of resources
Reduce, reuse and recycle. Recycling metals, glass, building materials and plastics reduces the amount of new raw materials extracted, the use of energy and the amount of waste going to landfill. Recycling metals requires less energy than extraction, though separation and collection have costs.

#### RP8 Required practical 8: analysis and purification of water samples
Analyse water samples from different sources, including seawater and rainwater: test pH with a pH meter or universal indicator, find the mass of dissolved solids by evaporating a known volume, and purify by distillation, collecting the condensed distillate in a condenser. Be careful heating flasks and use a water bath or heating mantle.
