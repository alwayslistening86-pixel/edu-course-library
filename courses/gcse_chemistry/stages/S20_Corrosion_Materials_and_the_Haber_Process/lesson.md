#### 4.10.3.1 Corrosion and its prevention
Corrosion is the destruction of materials by chemical reactions with substances in the environment. Rusting needs both air (oxygen) and water: iron + oxygen + water gives hydrated iron(III) oxide. Prevention: barrier methods (painting, oiling, coating with plastic, electroplating) and sacrificial protection with a more reactive metal such as zinc or magnesium (galvanising, blocks on ships). Aluminium resists corrosion because of its oxide layer.

#### 4.10.3.2 Alloys as useful materials
Alloys are mixtures of a metal with other elements. Bronze is copper and tin; brass is copper and zinc; gold is alloyed with copper and other metals, with carat showing gold content (24 carat is pure); steels are alloys of iron with carbon and other metals: low-carbon steel is soft and easily shaped, high-carbon steel is hard and brittle, stainless steel (chromium and nickel) resists corrosion. Aluminium alloys are low density and used in aircraft.

#### 4.10.3.3 Ceramics, polymers and composites
Ceramics such as soda-lime glass (sand, sodium carbonate, limestone), borosilicate glass (sand and boron trioxide, higher melting point) and clay ceramics (pottery, bricks) are hard and brittle. Polymers: low-density polyethene (LDPE) and high-density polyethene (HDPE) differ by reaction conditions and catalysts; thermosoftening polymers soften on heating and can be remoulded, thermosetting polymers do not melt. Composites consist of a matrix (binder) surrounding fibres or fragments (reinforcement): fibreglass, carbon fibre, concrete and wood.

#### 4.10.4.1 The Haber process
The Haber process makes ammonia from nitrogen (from air) and hydrogen (from natural gas): N2 + 3H2 (double arrow) 2NH3, a reversible exothermic reaction. Conditions: an iron catalyst, 450 degrees Celsius and 200 atmospheres. Ammonia is cooled and liquefied and removed, and unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen are recycled. Higher tier: high pressure favours ammonia (fewer molecules) but is costly and needs strong equipment; low temperature raises yield but slows the rate, so 450 degrees Celsius is a compromise.

#### 4.10.4.2 Production and uses of NPK fertilisers
Compounds of nitrogen, phosphorus and potassium are essential for plant growth. NPK fertilisers contain these three elements as salts. Ammonia reacts with nitric acid to make ammonium nitrate. Phosphate rock reacts with nitric or sulfuric acid to make soluble phosphate salts. Potassium chloride and potassium sulfate are mined and used. In the lab, small amounts are made by neutralisation; industry uses continuous large-scale production with a continuous flow to raise output.
