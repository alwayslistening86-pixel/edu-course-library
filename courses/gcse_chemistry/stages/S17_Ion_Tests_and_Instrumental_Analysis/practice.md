# S17_Ion_Tests_and_Instrumental_Analysis - Practice: Flame tests, precipitation tests, instrumental methods (required practical 7)

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. A compound gives a lilac flame, and a white precipitate with barium chloride after adding hydrochloric acid. Name the compound.
2. Describe what you would see when sodium hydroxide is added to solutions of copper(II), iron(III) and aluminium ions.
3. Write an ionic equation for the formation of silver chloride from silver nitrate and sodium chloride.
4. Compare flame tests and flame emission spectroscopy, giving two advantages of the instrument.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
