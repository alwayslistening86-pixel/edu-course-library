# S23_Mathematical_Skills_for_Chemistry - Lesson: Mathematical requirements for GCSE Chemistry

## How to teach this
Use chemistry contexts (moles, concentration, rate graphs, percentage yield) so that maths is a tool for the chemistry, not a separate topic.

#### M1a Maths: decimal form
Recognise and use numbers in decimal form: 0.05 dm3 is 50 cm3; 25.0 cm3 is 0.0250 dm3. Compare decimals by aligning place values and keep trailing zeros when they show the precision of a measurement.

#### M1b Maths: standard form
Write large and small numbers as a x 10 to the power n with a between 1 and 10: the Avogadro constant is 6.02 x 10 to the power 23; an atom's radius is 1 x 10 to the power -10 m. To multiply, multiply the numbers and add the powers; to divide, subtract the powers.

#### M1c Maths: ratios, fractions and percentages
Use ratio and percentage in chemistry: reacting ratios from equations (2:1), percentage yield = actual / theoretical x 100, percentage by mass of an element, and atom economy. Simplify a ratio by dividing both parts by the same number.

#### M1d Maths: estimating results
Estimate answers by rounding to one significant figure: Mr of a compound roughly, or a yield near 50 per cent, so that a calculator result of 5000 per cent would be spotted as wrong. Use estimates to check calculations.

#### M2a Maths: significant figures
Give final answers to a sensible number of significant figures, usually the same as the least precise data value. Count from the first non-zero digit. For example 0.0413 mol/dm3 has 3 significant figures; 24 dm3 x 0.05 mol = 1.2 dm3.

#### M2b Maths: arithmetic means
The arithmetic mean is the sum of values divided by the number of values. In titrations, average only concordant titres; for 22.30, 22.35 and 22.25 the mean is 22.30 cm3. Exclude anomalies before averaging and say so.

#### M2c Maths: frequency tables, bar charts and histograms
A frequency table records how many times each value or class occurs. Bar charts have gaps for categories; histograms have no gaps for continuous data. Read and construct them with labelled axes and scales.

#### M2h Maths: order of magnitude
Order of magnitude compares sizes to the nearest power of ten. A nanoparticle of 10 nm and a dust particle of 10 000 nm differ by three orders of magnitude. An atom (10 to the power -10 m) and its nucleus (10 to the power -14 m) differ by four orders of magnitude.

#### M3a Maths: symbols
Understand and use =, <, <<, >>, >, proportional to, and approximately equal to. For example, the mass of an electron << the mass of a proton, and rate is proportional to concentration for some reactions.

#### M3b Maths: changing the subject of an equation
Rearrange by doing the same to both sides. From concentration = mass / volume, mass = concentration x volume, and volume = mass / concentration. From moles = mass / Mr, mass = moles x Mr.

#### M3c Maths: substituting values with units
Substitute numbers into equations with the correct units and check units are consistent. For moles = concentration x volume, use dm3 for volume: 25 cm3 must be converted to 0.025 dm3 first. Write the answer with its unit.

#### M4a Maths: translating information between graphical and numeric form
Read values from a graph to fill in a table, and plot table values as a graph. For a graph of volume of gas against time, read the volume at 30 s, and record it with units in the table.

#### M4b Maths: y = mx + c
The equation y = mx + c describes a straight-line relationship: m is the gradient and c the intercept on the y axis. If y is directly proportional to x, then c = 0 and the line passes through the origin.

#### M4c Maths: plotting two variables
Plot the independent variable on the x axis and the dependent variable on the y axis, choose scales that fill the grid, label axes with units, plot points with small crosses and draw a smooth curve or straight line of best fit through the trend, not joining the dots.

#### M4d Maths: slope and intercept
Gradient = change in y divided by change in x, using two points far apart on the line. On a graph of mass of product against time (for a straight section), the gradient is the rate. The intercept is where the line meets the y axis.

#### M4e Maths: tangent to a curve as rate of change (Higher tier)
On a curved graph, draw a tangent at the point of interest, a straight line touching the curve there, then find the gradient of the tangent by choosing two points on it. This gives the rate of reaction at that instant. Choose a large triangle to reduce error.

#### M5b Maths: visualising 2D and 3D forms
Visualise and represent 2D and 3D shapes, including 2D representations of 3D objects: draw ball-and-stick and lattice models of molecules and giant structures, and describe the 3D shape of diamond, graphite layers and fullerenes from a diagram.

#### M5c Maths: areas and volumes of basic shapes
Area of rectangle = length x width; volume of cuboid = length x width x height; area of circle = pi x r squared; volume of sphere = 4/3 x pi x r cubed. Use in surface area to volume ratios for nanoparticles: a cube of side 2 nm has surface area 24 and volume 8, ratio 3:1.
