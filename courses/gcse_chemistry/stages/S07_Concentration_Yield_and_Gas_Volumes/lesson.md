# S07_Concentration_Yield_and_Gas_Volumes - Lesson: Concentration, percentage yield, atom economy and gas volumes

## Goal
The learner calculates concentrations in g/dm3 and mol/dm3, percentage yield, atom economy and gas volumes, and evaluates sustainability of processes.

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A recipe says to make 2 litres of squash with 100 g sugar. What is the concentration, and how would you halve it?'

#### 4.3.2.5 Concentration of solutions
Concentration = mass of solute (g) divided by volume of solution (dm3). Convert cm3 to dm3 by dividing by 1000. 5 g in 250 cm3 (0.25 dm3) is 20 g/dm3. A more concentrated solution has more solute in the same volume.

#### 4.3.3.1 Percentage yield
Percentage yield = actual mass of product divided by theoretical maximum mass x 100. The yield is less than 100 per cent because the reaction may be reversible, some product may be lost in separation, or side reactions may occur. If the theoretical yield is 12 g and 9 g is obtained, the yield is 75 per cent.

#### 4.3.3.2 Atom economy
Atom economy = relative formula mass of desired product from the equation divided by sum of relative formula masses of all reactants x 100. A high atom economy means less waste and is more sustainable and economic. Using a different reaction can raise atom economy, and useful by-products increase it further.

#### 4.3.4.1 Using concentrations of solutions in mol/dm3
Moles of solute = concentration (mol/dm3) x volume (dm3). 25 cm3 of 0.10 mol/dm3 acid contains 0.0025 mol. To convert between g/dm3 and mol/dm3, divide or multiply by the Mr. Use in titration calculations to find an unknown concentration from the volume of one solution needed to neutralise another, using the equation ratio.

#### 4.3.5.1 Use of amount of substance in relation to volumes of gases
Equal volumes of gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm3 at room temperature and pressure (20 degrees Celsius and 1 atmosphere). Volume of gas = moles x 24. In a reaction, gas volumes react in the same ratio as the coefficients: 2H2 + O2 gives 2H2O means 100 cm3 of hydrogen reacts with 50 cm3 of oxygen.
