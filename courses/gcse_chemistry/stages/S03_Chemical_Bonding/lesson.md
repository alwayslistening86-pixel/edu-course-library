# S03_Chemical_Bonding - Lesson: Ionic, covalent and metallic bonding

## Goal
The learner describes how atoms form ionic, covalent and metallic bonds, draws dot-and-cross diagrams and works out the formulae of ionic compounds.

- 4.2.1.1 - Chemical bonds: learn and apply: chemical bonds

- 4.2.1.5 - Metallic bonding: learn and apply: metallic bonding

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Why is salt a hard crystal but oxygen a gas? What are the atoms in each doing with their outer electrons?'

#### 4.2.1.1 Chemical bonds
There are three types of strong chemical bond: ionic (between a metal and a non-metal, electrons transferred), covalent (between non-metal atoms, electrons shared) and metallic (in metals and alloys, delocalised electrons). Bonds form so atoms reach a full outer shell.

#### 4.2.1.2 Ionic bonding
When a metal reacts with a non-metal, the metal atom loses electrons to become a positively charged ion and the non-metal atom gains electrons to become a negatively charged ion, giving both a full outer shell. The oppositely charged ions attract strongly (electrostatic force). Group 1 forms 1+ ions, Group 2 forms 2+, Group 6 forms 2- and Group 7 forms 1-. In dot-and-cross diagrams, show electrons transferred and the charges: sodium chloride has Na+ and Cl-, magnesium oxide has Mg2+ and O2-.

#### 4.2.1.3 Ionic compounds
Ionic compounds are giant structures of ions, arranged in a regular lattice held by strong electrostatic forces acting in all directions. Work out the formula so the charges balance: magnesium chloride is Mg2+ and two Cl-, so MgCl2; aluminium oxide is 2 Al3+ and 3 O2-, so Al2O3. Ionic diagrams can be dot-and-cross, 3D models, or lattice diagrams, each with limitations.

#### 4.2.1.4 Covalent bonding
Atoms share pairs of electrons to make covalent bonds, giving each atom a full outer shell. Examples: small molecules such as hydrogen (H2), chlorine (Cl2), oxygen (O2), nitrogen (N2), hydrogen chloride (HCl), water (H2O), ammonia (NH3) and methane (CH4); polymers; and giant structures such as diamond and silicon dioxide. Draw with dot-and-cross diagrams, ball-and-stick models, displayed formulae and 3D models, and know the limitations of each.

#### 4.2.1.5 Metallic bonding
Metals consist of giant structures of atoms arranged in layers. The outer electrons are delocalised, forming a sea of free electrons. The strong electrostatic attraction between the positive metal ions and the delocalised electrons is the metallic bond, which explains conductivity and malleability.
