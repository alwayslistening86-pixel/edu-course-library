## How to teach this
Ask: 'Bromine water turns colourless with some hydrocarbons but not others. What does that tell us about their bonds?'

#### 4.7.2.1 Structure and formulae of alkenes
Alkenes are hydrocarbons with a carbon-carbon double bond, general formula CnH2n: ethene C2H4, propene C3H6, butene C4H8, pentene C5H10. They are unsaturated, since they have fewer hydrogens than the alkane with the same number of carbons, and more reactive. Bromine water turns from orange to colourless when shaken with an alkene, a test for a double bond.

#### 4.7.2.2 Reactions of alkenes
Alkenes burn with a smoky flame in air (incomplete combustion). They react with hydrogen (nickel catalyst, 60 degrees Celsius) to form alkanes, with water (steam and a catalyst) to form alcohols, and with halogens to form haloalkanes. Ethene + bromine gives dibromoethane. These are addition reactions because the double bond opens.

#### 4.7.2.3 Alcohols
Alcohols contain the functional group -OH: methanol, ethanol, propanol and butanol. They dissolve in water to give neutral solutions, react with sodium to give hydrogen, burn in air, and can be oxidised to carboxylic acids either by oxidising agents or by microbial action (wine turning to vinegar). Ethanol is made by fermentation of sugars using yeast in anaerobic conditions at 30 to 40 degrees Celsius; it is used as a fuel and solvent, and in alcoholic drinks.

#### 4.7.2.4 Carboxylic acids
Carboxylic acids have the functional group -COOH: methanoic, ethanoic, propanoic and butanoic acids. They dissolve in water to give acidic solutions (weak acids, pH about 3 to 5), react with carbonates to give a salt, water and carbon dioxide, and react with alcohols in the presence of an acid catalyst to make esters, such as ethanoic acid + ethanol gives ethyl ethanoate + water. Esters are sweet smelling, used in perfumes and flavourings.

#### 4.7.3.1 Addition polymerisation
Many small alkene molecules (monomers) join, opening their double bonds, to form a very long-chain molecule (polymer). Ethene forms poly(ethene). In an addition polymer the repeat unit has the same atoms as the monomer, and only one product forms. Draw the repeat unit with the two open bonds through brackets.

#### 4.7.3.2 Condensation polymerisation (Higher tier)
Condensation polymerisation involves monomers with two functional groups, for example a diol (two -OH) and a dicarboxylic acid (two -COOH). They react to form a polyester, and a small molecule, such as water, is released at each link. Polyesters such as PET (in drinks bottles) are made this way. The repeat unit is drawn with the ester link.

#### 4.7.3.3 Amino acids (Higher tier)
Amino acids have an amine group (-NH2) and a carboxyl group (-COOH). Different amino acids can join by condensation polymerisation to form polypeptides and proteins, releasing water. Twenty different amino acids occur naturally in proteins.

#### 4.7.3.4 DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) and other naturally occurring polymers
DNA is a polymer of four different monomers called nucleotides, forming two chains in a double helix. Other naturally occurring polymers include proteins, starch and cellulose (polymers of sugar units), and are essential for life.
