## How to teach this
Ask: 'What is the difference between salt water and salt (sodium chloride)? Which could you separate with a filter or by boiling, and which not?' It leads to mixtures and compounds.

#### 4.1.1.1 Atoms, elements and compounds
Atoms are the smallest particles of an element, and there are about 100 different elements, each with its own symbol on the periodic table. A compound is formed when two or more elements are chemically combined in fixed proportions, for example water is always H2O. Compounds can only be separated into elements by chemical reactions, and their properties are different from those of the elements in them. Word and symbol equations represent reactions.

#### 4.1.1.2 Mixtures
A mixture consists of two or more elements or compounds not chemically combined, so each keeps its own properties. Mixtures can be separated by physical processes with no chemical reaction: filtration (insoluble solid from liquid), crystallisation (dissolved solid from solution), simple distillation (solvent from solution), fractional distillation (liquids with different boiling points) and chromatography.

#### 4.1.1.3 The development of the model of the atom
Dalton thought of atoms as tiny solid spheres. J.J. Thomson discovered the electron and proposed the plum pudding model of a ball of positive charge with electrons embedded. Rutherford's alpha scattering experiment showed most alpha particles passed straight through but a few were deflected strongly, so he proposed a small, dense, positive nucleus surrounded by empty space. Bohr showed electrons orbit the nucleus at specific distances (shells). Later Chadwick provided evidence for neutrons in the nucleus.

#### 4.1.1.4 Relative electrical charges of subatomic particles
A proton has relative charge +1 and relative mass 1; a neutron has charge 0 and mass 1; an electron has charge -1 and a very small mass (about 1/1836, taken as almost zero). Atoms have no overall charge because the number of protons equals the number of electrons. The atomic number is the number of protons; the mass number is protons plus neutrons. Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same protons) with different numbers of neutrons, such as carbon-12 and carbon-14.

#### 4.1.1.5 Size and mass of atoms
Atoms have a radius of about 0.1 nanometres (1 x 10 to the power -10 m). The nucleus has a radius of less than one ten-thousandth of the atom's radius, yet contains almost all of the atom's mass, since electrons have almost no mass. Ions and atoms are so tiny that comparisons use standard form and orders of magnitude.

#### 4.1.1.6 Relative atomic mass
The relative atomic mass (Ar) of an element takes account of the abundance of its isotopes: it is the weighted average mass. Ar = sum of (isotope mass x abundance) divided by 100. Chlorine has 75 per cent chlorine-35 and 25 per cent chlorine-37: (35 x 75 + 37 x 25) / 100 = 35.5.

#### 4.1.1.7 Electronic structure
Electrons occupy shells (energy levels) from the lowest, holding 2, then 8, then 8 (for the first 20 elements). Write electronic structures as numbers, such as sodium 2,8,1 and chlorine 2,8,7, and draw them as dots or crosses on concentric circles. The number of electrons in the outer shell equals the group number and controls chemical behaviour.
