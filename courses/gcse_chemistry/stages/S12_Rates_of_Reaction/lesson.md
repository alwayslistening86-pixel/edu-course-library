## How to teach this
Ask: 'Why does a sugar lump dissolve slowly but powdered sugar quickly? And why does milk go off faster in a warm kitchen?'

#### 4.6.1.1 Calculating rates of reactions
Mean rate = quantity of reactant used or product formed divided by time taken. Units are g/s, cm3/s or (Higher) mol/s. On a graph of product against time, the steepest line shows the fastest rate and the line becomes flat when the reaction stops. Higher tier: draw a tangent to the curve at a point, then gradient = change in y divided by change in x to find the rate at that time.

#### 4.6.1.2 Factors which affect the rates of chemical reactions
The rate increases with increasing temperature, increasing concentration of dissolved reactants, increasing pressure of gases, increasing surface area of solid reactants (smaller pieces), and the presence of a catalyst. Powdered marble reacts with acid faster than a lump of the same mass.

#### 4.6.1.3 Collision theory and activation energy
Particles must collide with sufficient energy, the activation energy, for a reaction to occur. Increasing temperature makes particles move faster, so collisions are more frequent and a greater proportion has enough energy. Increasing concentration, pressure or surface area increases the frequency of collisions, so more successful collisions per second.

#### 4.6.1.4 Catalysts
A catalyst speeds up a reaction without being used up, by providing an alternative reaction pathway with a lower activation energy. Enzymes are biological catalysts. Catalysts do not appear in the overall equation, and are used in industrial processes to reduce energy costs. Reaction profiles show a lower hump with a catalyst.

#### RP5 Required practical 5: concentration and rate of reaction
Investigate how changing concentration affects reaction rate using two methods. (1) Measure gas volume: magnesium ribbon with different concentrations of hydrochloric acid and collect hydrogen in a gas syringe against time. (2) Change in turbidity: mix sodium thiosulfate with hydrochloric acid and time how long for a cross under the flask to disappear; rate is proportional to 1 divided by time. Control temperature, volumes and mass.
