# Mathematics (BSc Hons) — Open University module-framework structure, QAA MSOR Benchmark graded - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
One paper covering every stage. Section A (S01-S09, foundations, groups, linear algebra, analysis): structured questions and proofs. Section B (S10-S15, applied methods and Stage-3 options): further structured questions and proofs. Questions are original; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests. The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest.

## 8 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. [Section A] Find all fourth roots of -16, giving your answers in the form a + bi. [5 marks]
2. [Section A] Let G = (Z_12, +). List the elements of the subgroup generated by 8, and state its order. Verify this order divides |G|, consistent with Lagrange's theorem. [4 marks]
3. [Section A] Let T: R^3 -> R^3 be given by T(x,y,z) = (x+y, y+z, x+2y+z). Find a basis for the kernel of T and state the rank of T using the Rank-Nullity theorem. [6 marks]
4. [Section A] Use the epsilon-N definition of a limit to prove that the sequence a_n = (3n+1)/(n+2) converges to 3. [5 marks]
5. [Section B] Solve the second-order linear ODE y'' - 4y' + 4y = e^{2x}, giving the general solution. [6 marks]
6. [Section B] Let R = Z[i] (Gaussian integers). Show that 2 is not irreducible in R by exhibiting a factorisation into non-units. [4 marks]
7. [Section B] Use the Residue Theorem to evaluate the contour integral of f(z) = 1/(z^2+1) around the circle |z|=2 traversed anticlockwise. [5 marks]
8. [Section B] A random sample of size n=25 from a normal population with unknown mean mu and known variance sigma^2=9 has sample mean 41.2. Find a 95% confidence interval for mu. [4 marks]

## Answer key for the ready-made items (tutor only)
1. [5] M1 writes -16 = 16 cis(pi); M1 fourth roots have modulus 2; A1 arguments (pi + 2k pi)/4 for k=0,1,2,3; A2 the four roots 2cos(pi/4)+2isin(pi/4)=1.414+1.414i and their reflections/negatives (roots root2 + i root2, -root2 + i root2, -root2 - i root2, root2 - i root2).
2. [4] M1 computes multiples of 8 mod 12: 8,4,0; A1 subgroup {0,4,8}, order 3; B1 3 divides 12; A1 correct Lagrange statement.
3. [6] M1 sets up T(v)=0 as a linear system; M1 row-reduces; A1 kernel is span{(1,-1,1)}, nullity 1; M1 applies Rank-Nullity (dim domain 3); A1 rank 2; B1 basis stated explicitly.
4. [5] M1 computes |a_n - 3| = 5/(n+2); M1 states the epsilon-N target: given epsilon>0 want 5/(n+2) < epsilon; A1 solves for N: N > 5/epsilon - 2; A1 for n > N, |a_n-3|<epsilon rigorously justified; B1 concluding statement.
5. [6] M1 auxiliary equation m^2-4m+4=0 has repeated root m=2; A1 complementary function (A+Bx)e^{2x}; M1 since e^{2x} is already in the CF (twice), tries particular integral Cx^2e^{2x}; A1 substitutes and solves for C; A1 C = 1/2; A1 general solution y = (A+Bx)e^{2x} + (x^2/2)e^{2x}.
6. [4] M1 recalls the norm N(a+bi)=a^2+b^2 is multiplicative; A1 factorisation 2 = (1+i)(1-i); M1 checks N(1+i)=N(1-i)=2, neither a unit (units have norm 1); A1 conclusion: 2 is reducible in Z[i].
7. [5] M1 identifies simple poles at z=i and z=-i, both inside |z|=2; M1 residue at z=i is -I/2; A1 residue at z=-i is I/2; M1 sums residues (0) and multiplies by 2 pi i; A1 integral = 0.
8. [4] M1 uses X-bar +/- 1.96 sigma/root(n); A1 standard error = 0.600; M1 margin = 1.176; A1 interval (40.024, 42.376).
