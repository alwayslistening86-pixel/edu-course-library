## Test items
1. Explain why the October 1917 seizure of power sits awkwardly with a purely structural theory of revolution. [6 marks]
2. Comparing at least two revolutions you have studied, assess whether structural conditions or the decisions of specific individuals better explain why revolutions succeed. [30 marks]
3. Which of these correctly pairs a historian with their main argument about revolution? Choose every correct option.
   A. C.L.R. James - the Haitian Revolution deserves a central place in Atlantic revolutionary history
   B. Leopold von Ranke - historical narrative is shaped by literary emplotment
   C. Theda Skocpol - the French Revolution was purely a matter of contingent political decisions
   D. E.H. Carr - history can recover the past exactly 'as it actually was'

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 October 1917 was a swift, small-scale, well-organised Bolshevik action in Petrograd, not a mass uprising; B1 its success depended heavily on Bolshevik leadership, timing and organisation (Lenin's April Theses, the party's decision to act in October) - contingent, agency-driven factors; B1 structural conditions (war strain, agrarian crisis, weak Provisional Government) explain why revolution became possible/likely, per Skocpol's model, but not why the Bolsheviks specifically succeeded that week; B1 the February Revolution, by contrast, was more genuinely mass-based and spontaneous, fitting a structural account better; B1 this suggests revolutions may need both a structural explanation of the opportunity and a contingent explanation of the specific outcome; B1 credit reference to Skocpol by name and/or the general structure-versus-agency debate.
