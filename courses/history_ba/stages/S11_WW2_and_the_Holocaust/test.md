## Test items
1. Explain the difference between intentionalist and functionalist explanations of the Holocaust. [6 marks]
2. 'The Holocaust was the product of long-planned Nazi ideology, not of wartime circumstance and cumulative radicalisation.' Assess the validity of this view. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 intentionalists (e.g. Dawidowicz) argue Hitler held a genocidal intention from early in his career, with war as opportunity not cause; B1 functionalists (e.g. Mommsen, Broszat) argue the Final Solution emerged cumulatively from wartime radicalisation and bureaucratic competition, not a single long-held plan; B1 Kershaw's 'working towards the Führer' offers a synthesis, with officials radicalising policy anticipating Hitler's wishes; B1 credit accurate reference to the Wannsee Conference (1942) as the administrative coordination point, whichever side an answer favours; B1 credit reference to Browning's Ordinary Men or Goldhagen's Hitler's Willing Executioners as related but distinct debates about perpetrator motivation rather than overall planning; B1 credit any other accurate, well-explained distinction.

[30] Mark out of 30 using this course's undergraduate-history generic levels, adapting the alevel_history-style levels convention to honours standard and to the QAA benchmark's description of graduate historical skill: Level 1 (1-6) largely descriptive/narrative, little independent analysis, no real engagement with historiography; Level 2 (7-12) some analysis and awareness that historians disagree, but underdeveloped, uneven use of evidence; Level 3 (13-18) a structured argument that explains a range of relevant factors, engages with named historians or schools of interpretation, and reaches a judgement, though depth or balance may be uneven; Level 4 (19-24) a well-organised, evidenced argument that weighs competing historiographical positions explicitly and reaches a supported judgement, showing real synthesis of complex material; Level 5 (25-30) a sophisticated, wide-ranging argument that formulates its own sharp historical question, synthesises extensive and sometimes conflicting evidence, engages critically and precisely with historiography, and sustains a fully substantiated, original judgement throughout. Credit: the intentionalist case - Hitler's early and consistent antisemitic rhetoric, the ideological continuity from Mein Kampf onward; the functionalist case - the step-by-step escalation (emigration policy, ghettoisation, mass shootings by the Einsatzgruppen after Barbarossa, only then industrialised extermination), bureaucratic improvisation and inter-agency competition, Kershaw's synthesis; a strong answer names specific historians on both sides and reaches a genuine, substantiated judgement rather than simply describing the debate.
