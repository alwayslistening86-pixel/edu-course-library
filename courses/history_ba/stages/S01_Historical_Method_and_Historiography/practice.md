# S01_Historical_Method_and_Historiography - Practice: What is history; degree-level source criticism; historiographical schools

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Give two differences between Ranke's view of history and Carr's. [4 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by an 'archival silence' and why it matters to historians. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B1 Ranke believed rigorous archival research could recover objective truth ('as it actually was'); B1 Carr argued facts are always selected/interpreted by the historian, so objectivity in that pure sense is unattainable; B1 (or) Carr saw history as a dialogue between past and present rather than a fixed, recoverable record; B1 credit any two developed, accurate contrasts.
2. [3] B1 evidence about a group or event that was never recorded, or was lost/destroyed, leaving a gap in the historical record; B1 disproportionately affects marginalised groups (the poor, enslaved people, women, the illiterate); B1 means the absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and historians must account for what the archive cannot tell them.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish an attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
