## Test items
1. Explain why the Brezhnev Doctrine's effective abandonment by Gorbachev was significant for the events of 1989. [6 marks]
2. Drawing on the whole of your specialist study (1914-89), assess how far the collapse of Communism in 1989 should be understood as the final consequence of the First World War's destruction of the old European order, rather than as a distinct, later turning point. [30 marks]
3. Which of these pairs a Cold War crisis with the correct year? Choose every correct option.
   A. The Hungarian Uprising - 1956
   B. The Prague Spring - 1956
   C. The Berlin Blockade - 1968
   D. The fall of the Berlin Wall - 1968

## Answer key (for the tutor only)

[6] B1 the Brezhnev Doctrine (following Prague 1968) had asserted the USSR's right to intervene militarily to preserve socialism in any Warsaw Pact state; B1 Gorbachev's renunciation removed the coercive threat that had underpinned Soviet control of Eastern Europe since the late 1940s; B1 without the credible threat of Soviet military intervention, Eastern-bloc governments could not suppress 1989's protests the way Hungary (1956) and Czechoslovakia (1968) had been suppressed; B1 this directly enabled the rapid, largely peaceful collapse of Communist rule across the bloc in 1989; B1 credit specific reference to glasnost/perestroika as the wider context for Gorbachev's change of policy; B1 credit contrast with the violent exception, Romania, showing the pattern was not absolute.
