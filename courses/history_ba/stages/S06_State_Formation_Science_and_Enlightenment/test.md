## Test items
1. Explain why the Scientific Revolution is often seen as a precondition for the Enlightenment. [6 marks]
2. 'Louis XIV's absolutism was more image than reality.' Assess the validity of this view. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 shifted authority in knowledge-claims from tradition/scripture-based authority to observation and mathematical proof (Copernicus, Galileo, Newton); B1 Newton's Principia (1687) modelled the universe as governed by discoverable natural laws, suggesting society/politics might be understood the same way; B1 Galileo's trial (1633) showed the tension between new science and religious authority, a tension Enlightenment thinkers later engaged with directly; B1 gave later thinkers confidence that reason and empirical method, not just tradition, could reliably produce knowledge; B1 credit reference to specific figures/dates; B1 credit noting this is a widely accepted historians' claim about intellectual causation, not a strict logical necessity.

Credit: the case for image over reality - practical limits of pre-modern communication and administration, continuing local/regional power despite intendants, the propagandistic function of Versailles itself; the case for genuine centralisation - the real and lasting weakening of independent noble power after the Fronde, sustained absence of the Estates-General, the real religious uniformity imposed by revoking the Edict of Nantes and its real consequences (the Huguenot exodus); a strong answer reaches a judgement rather than simply listing both sides.
