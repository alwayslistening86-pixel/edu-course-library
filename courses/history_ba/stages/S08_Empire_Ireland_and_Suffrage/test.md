## Test items
1. Explain why the British government's response to the Great Irish Famine is heavily criticised by historians. [6 marks]
2. 'Women won the vote in 1918 because of their contribution to the First World War, not because of the pre-war suffrage campaigns.' Assess the validity of this view. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 adherence to laissez-faire doctrine limited direct state relief on principle; B1 officials such as Trevelyan saw the famine in providentialist terms, worsening the policy response; B1 grain continued to be exported from Ireland during the famine under legal protection, a lasting grievance; B1 the scale of death (c.1 million) and emigration (c.1-2 million) was worsened, in historians' view, by policy choices rather than being purely a natural disaster; B1 the response accelerated Irish nationalism (Young Ireland, later Home Rule), itself evidence of the political damage done; B1 credit any other accurate, developed criticism.
