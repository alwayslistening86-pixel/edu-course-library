## Test items
1. Distinguish between a sole trader and a partnership, giving one advantage a partnership has over a sole trader. [3 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by limited liability, and identify one form of business ownership that has it. [3 marks]
3. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to a business of converting from a private limited company (ltd) to a public limited company (plc). [4 marks]
4. Explain why a not-for-profit organisation is different from a typical business in its main objective. [2 marks]
5. Which form of business ownership has unlimited liability and is owned and run by just one person? Choose every correct option.
   A. sole trader
   B. partnership
   C. private limited company
   D. public limited company

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 a sole trader is owned/run by one person; B1 a partnership is owned by two or more people who share capital, work and profit; B1 advantage, e.g. more capital available/shared workload and skills.
2. [3] B1 limited liability means owners/shareholders can only lose the amount they invested in the business, not their personal assets; B1 correctly contrasted with unlimited liability (where personal assets are at risk); B1 a private limited company (ltd) or a public limited company (plc).
3. [4] B1 advantage, e.g. able to raise much larger amounts of capital by selling shares to the general public on a stock exchange; B1 correctly explained; B1 disadvantage, e.g. risk of an unwanted takeover as anyone can buy shares, or far greater regulation/public scrutiny of its accounts; B1 correctly explained.
4. [2] B1 a not-for-profit exists mainly to achieve a social, charitable or environmental purpose rather than to maximise profit for owners; B1 any surplus made is normally reinvested in that purpose rather than paid out to owners.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
