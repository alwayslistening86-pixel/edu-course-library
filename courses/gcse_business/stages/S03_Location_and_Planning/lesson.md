# S03_Location_and_Planning - Lesson: Business location and business planning

## Goal
The learner explains the factors that influence where a business locates, and the purpose, main sections and basic financial content of a business plan.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.1.5 - Business location: factors influencing where a business locates (proximity to market, raw materials, labour, competitors, costs)
- 3.1.6 - Business planning: the purpose of a business plan, its main sections, and basic financial terms and calculations used in it

## How to teach this
Ask the learner why a coffee shop opens on a busy high street rather than a quiet back street, but a large warehouse opens on a cheap out-of-town industrial estate -- location decisions trade off different factors for different businesses. GCSE Business has real quantitative content throughout (at least 10% of all marks are quantitative skills): break-even (identifying break-even output and margin of safety from a chart -- AQA does not require the break-even formula or chart-drawing, but understanding the underlying contribution logic is how a learner reads a chart correctly), cash-flow forecasts (net cash flow, opening/closing balance), gross and net profit margins, and average rate of return on an investment. AQA does not give formulae in the exam for gross/net profit margin or average rate of return, so the learner must know these by heart, not just how to plug numbers in. Every numerical worked example in this course was computed and checked when this course was built, and the learner should always be made to show full working. AQA GCSE Business explicitly does NOT examine named motivational theories (e.g. Maslow, Herzberg, Taylor) by name -- only financial and non-financial motivation methods in general terms -- so this course does not teach or test theorist names either.

#### 3.1.5 Business location: factors influencing where a business locates (proximity to market, raw materials, labour, competitors, costs)
A business's **location** decision weighs several factors: **proximity to market** (being close to customers cuts delivery time/cost and can boost footfall, especially important for retail); **availability of raw materials** (important for a manufacturer that needs to minimise transport costs of heavy/perishable materials); **availability of labour** (a suitable local workforce with the right skills, at an affordable wage); **proximity to competitors** (some businesses benefit from clustering near similar businesses, e.g. a 'restaurant quarter' that draws in more customers overall; others prefer to avoid direct competition); and **costs** (rent, business rates and other overheads vary greatly by location -- a prime high-street location costs far more than an out-of-town retail park or industrial estate). *Example:* a high-end fashion retailer likely locates on an expensive but busy city-centre high street (proximity to market matters more than cost); a large online-only warehouse likely locates on a cheap out-of-town site with good road access (cost and transport links matter more than footfall).

#### 3.1.6 Business planning: the purpose of a business plan, its main sections, and basic financial terms and calculations used in it
A **business plan** is a written document setting out a new (or expanding) business's objectives and how it intends to achieve them. Its **purpose** includes: helping the entrepreneur think through and clarify their idea before committing money; and, crucially, persuading potential lenders or investors (e.g. a bank considering a loan) that the business is viable, since it is usually required to raise external finance. Main sections typically include: a description of the **business idea/product**, the **target market** and how it will be reached, the **management team's** relevant skills/experience, and **financial forecasts** -- basic financial terms and calculations used here include forecast **revenue** (price × expected sales), **costs**, and expected **profit**, plus how much finance is needed and from where. *Example:* a bank asked for a £20,000 loan will want to see a business plan showing forecast monthly revenue and costs for at least the first year, to judge whether the business can realistically repay the loan.
