# S03_Location_and_Planning - Test: Business location and business planning

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, data-response/case-study and extended-response questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain two factors, other than cost, that might influence where a new coffee shop chooses to locate. [4 marks]
2. Explain why cost is often the most important location factor for a large distribution warehouse. [3 marks]
3. Explain two main sections a business plan is likely to contain, and why a bank considering a loan would want to see it. [4 marks]
4. A new business forecasts it will sell 2,000 units a month at £8 each. Calculate its forecast monthly revenue, showing your working. [2 marks]
5. Which of these is most likely to be a main section of a business plan? Choose every correct option.
   A. financial forecasts
   B. the CEO's holiday plans
   C. a history of a rival firm's advertising
   D. the weather forecast for next month

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 proximity to its target market/customer footfall, e.g. a busy high street or shopping centre; B1 correctly explained; B1 availability of suitable labour locally; B1 correctly explained (accept proximity to competitors -- clustering can draw more overall footfall, or avoiding direct competition).
2. [3] B1 warehouses need a large amount of land/floor space; B1 out-of-town or industrial-estate sites are far cheaper (lower rent/business rates) than city-centre sites of the same size; B1 footfall/proximity to individual customers matters far less for a warehouse than for a shop, so cost dominates the decision.
3. [4] B1 section 1, e.g. the business idea/target market or financial forecasts; B1 correctly explained; B1 section 2, e.g. the management team's skills/experience; B1 the bank wants evidence the business is viable/can realistically repay the loan before it lends money.
4. [2] M1 2,000 x 8; A1 = £16,000.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
