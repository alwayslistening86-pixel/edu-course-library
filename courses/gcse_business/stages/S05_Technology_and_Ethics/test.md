# S05_Technology_and_Ethics - Test: Technology, ethics and the environment

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, data-response/case-study and extended-response questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain two ways digital communication has changed how a business operates. [4 marks]
2. Explain the difference between a legal requirement and an ethical consideration for a business, with an example of each. [4 marks]
3. Explain what is meant by sustainability, and give one example of a sustainability concern facing businesses. [3 marks]
4. A haulage firm is criticised for its delivery lorries causing heavy congestion and noise in a residential area near its depot. Discuss whether the firm should invest in quieter, lower-emission vehicles despite the extra cost. [6 marks]
5. Which of these best describes an ethical business decision? Choose every correct option.
   A. choosing to pay overseas suppliers a fair price even though it is not legally required and raises costs
   B. choosing the cheapest possible supplier regardless of how workers are treated
   C. advertising a product using only true, legally required information
   D. paying staff exactly the legal minimum wage and no more

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 way 1, e.g. faster/cheaper communication with customers, suppliers or staff (email, video calls, messaging); B1 correctly explained; B1 way 2, e.g. enables flexible/remote working, or lets negative reviews/comments spread quickly online; B1 correctly explained.
2. [4] B1 a legal requirement is something the law compels the business to do, e.g. paying at least the minimum wage; B1 correct example; B1 an ethical consideration is doing what is morally 'right' even where not legally required, e.g. paying overseas suppliers a genuinely fair price rather than the legal minimum; B1 correct example.
3. [3] B1 sustainability means meeting today's needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs; B1 correctly explained; B1 example, e.g. contributing to global warming through carbon emissions, or using up scarce/non-renewable resources.
4. [6] Level 2 (4-6): balanced discussion, e.g. the investment raises costs and could reduce short-term profit, but reduces the environmental/ethical harm to the local community (a stakeholder), can improve the firm's reputation and could avoid future regulation/fines, with a reasoned overall view. Level 1 (1-3): one-sided or limited discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
