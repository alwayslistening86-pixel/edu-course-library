# S07_Legislation_and_Competition - Lesson: Legislation and the competitive environment

## Goal
The learner explains the impact of employment, health and safety, and consumer law on business, and the impact of operating in a competitive market including the uncertainty and risks businesses face.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.5 - Legislation: employment law, health and safety law, and consumer law
- 3.2.6 - Competitive environment: the impact of operating in competitive markets, and the uncertainty and risks businesses face

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: what stops an employer paying whatever wage it likes, or a shop selling a faulty product with no comeback for the buyer? The law sets minimum standards businesses must meet. GCSE Business has real quantitative content throughout (at least 10% of all marks are quantitative skills): break-even (identifying break-even output and margin of safety from a chart -- AQA does not require the break-even formula or chart-drawing, but understanding the underlying contribution logic is how a learner reads a chart correctly), cash-flow forecasts (net cash flow, opening/closing balance), gross and net profit margins, and average rate of return on an investment. AQA does not give formulae in the exam for gross/net profit margin or average rate of return, so the learner must know these by heart, not just how to plug numbers in. Every numerical worked example in this course was computed and checked when this course was built, and the learner should always be made to show full working. AQA GCSE Business explicitly does NOT examine named motivational theories (e.g. Maslow, Herzberg, Taylor) by name -- only financial and non-financial motivation methods in general terms -- so this course does not teach or test theorist names either.

#### 3.2.5 Legislation: employment law, health and safety law, and consumer law
Businesses must comply with **legislation** (the law), which raises costs but protects employees, customers and the public. **Employment law** sets minimum standards for how staff are treated, e.g. the National Minimum/Living Wage, protection from unfair dismissal, and rules against discrimination -- compliance raises a business's wage/administrative costs but protects staff and can improve motivation/reputation. **Health and safety law** requires businesses to provide a safe working environment (e.g. safe equipment, adequate training, risk assessments) -- compliance costs money (equipment, training) but reduces accidents, absence and the risk of costly lawsuits/fines. **Consumer law** protects buyers, e.g. requiring goods to be of satisfactory quality, as described, and fit for purpose, with a right to a refund/repair/replacement if not -- compliance can mean higher returns-handling costs, but protects the business's reputation and builds customer trust; breaking any of these laws risks fines, lawsuits, and serious reputational damage.

#### 3.2.6 Competitive environment: the impact of operating in competitive markets, and the uncertainty and risks businesses face
A **competitive environment** -- one with many rival firms -- affects a business significantly: it must work harder to attract and retain customers (e.g. through better prices, quality, service or marketing), which can **squeeze prices and profit margins**, but also drives businesses to **innovate and improve** to avoid losing customers to rivals. All businesses face genuine **uncertainty and risk**: demand can be hard to predict, competitors can launch a better/cheaper product without warning, costs (e.g. raw materials, energy) can rise unexpectedly, and wider events (economic downturns, new laws, new technology) can change a market quickly -- a business must constantly adapt, and even a well-run business can fail due to factors partly or wholly outside its control.
