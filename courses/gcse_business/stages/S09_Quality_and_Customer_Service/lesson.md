# S09_Quality_and_Customer_Service - Lesson: Quality and good customer service

## Goal
The learner explains the consequences of poor quality, total quality management, and the costs and benefits of quality, plus what makes good customer service and why it matters.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.3 - The concept of quality: consequences of poor quality, total quality management (TQM), and the costs and benefits of quality
- 3.3.4 - Good customer service: product knowledge, after-sales service, customer satisfaction/loyalty, reputation risk, and the impact of ICT

## How to teach this
Ask the learner about the last time they received genuinely excellent (or terrible) customer service -- did it change whether they went back? That's the commercial value of quality and service in one story. GCSE Business has real quantitative content throughout (at least 10% of all marks are quantitative skills): break-even (identifying break-even output and margin of safety from a chart -- AQA does not require the break-even formula or chart-drawing, but understanding the underlying contribution logic is how a learner reads a chart correctly), cash-flow forecasts (net cash flow, opening/closing balance), gross and net profit margins, and average rate of return on an investment. AQA does not give formulae in the exam for gross/net profit margin or average rate of return, so the learner must know these by heart, not just how to plug numbers in. Every numerical worked example in this course was computed and checked when this course was built, and the learner should always be made to show full working. AQA GCSE Business explicitly does NOT examine named motivational theories (e.g. Maslow, Herzberg, Taylor) by name -- only financial and non-financial motivation methods in general terms -- so this course does not teach or test theorist names either.

#### 3.3.3 The concept of quality: consequences of poor quality, total quality management (TQM), and the costs and benefits of quality
**Quality** means a product or service consistently meeting a defined standard/customers' expectations. Poor quality has serious **consequences**: lost sales/customers, costly product recalls, wasted materials/rework, and lasting reputational damage that can be far more expensive than the original cost of getting it right. **Total quality management (TQM)** is an approach where quality is treated as **everyone's responsibility** throughout the whole organisation (not just a separate inspection department at the end of production) -- aiming to identify and fix quality problems as early as possible, ideally preventing them before they happen. Maintaining quality has **costs**: inspection (checking output meets standard), staff training, and the cost of any recalls that still occur -- but also clear **benefits**: additional sales (a reputation for quality attracts and retains customers), a stronger reputation, and the ability to charge a **higher price** for a product/service customers trust to be reliably good.

#### 3.3.4 Good customer service: product knowledge, after-sales service, customer satisfaction/loyalty, reputation risk, and the impact of ICT
**Good customer service** means giving customers a positive experience before, during and after a purchase. It depends on staff having strong **product knowledge** (able to answer questions and give good advice) and engaging well with customers (friendly, helpful, responsive); it also includes **post-sales service** such as staff training on how to use a product, help lines, and servicing/repairs after purchase. Good customer service builds **customer satisfaction and loyalty** -- satisfied customers are more likely to return and to recommend the business to others (positive word of mouth) -- while poor service creates a **reputation risk**: dissatisfied customers can switch to a competitor and share negative reviews, especially given how far and fast reviews and complaints can now spread via **ICT** (websites, e-commerce platforms and social media), which has made customer service both more visible and more important than ever.
