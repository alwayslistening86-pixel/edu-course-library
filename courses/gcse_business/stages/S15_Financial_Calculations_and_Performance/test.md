# S15_Financial_Calculations_and_Performance - Test: Financial calculations and analysing financial performance

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, data-response/case-study and extended-response questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Distinguish between a fixed cost and a variable cost, giving one example of each, and state the formula linking them to total cost. [3 marks]
2. A business has fixed costs of £3,000, sells its product at £20 and has a variable cost of £14 per unit. It plans to make 700 units. Calculate the break-even output and the margin of safety at this planned output, showing your working. [4 marks]
3. A business invests £25,000 in a new vehicle, expecting total profit of £9,000 over 3 years. Calculate the average rate of return, showing your working. [3 marks]
4. Distinguish between the income statement and the statement of financial position, and give one example of an asset and one example of a liability. [4 marks]
5. A business's revenue is £200,000, cost of sales is £130,000 and expenses are £40,000. Calculate its gross profit margin and net profit margin, showing your working. [5 marks]
6. Which of these best describes the margin of safety on a break-even chart? Choose every correct option.
   A. the gap between actual/planned output and the break-even output
   B. the total revenue earned at the break-even point
   C. the total fixed costs of the business
   D. the average rate of return on an investment

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 fixed cost does not change with output, e.g. rent; B1 variable cost changes directly with output, e.g. raw materials; B1 total cost = fixed costs + variable costs.
2. [4] M1 contribution per unit = 20-14 = £6; M1 break-even output = 3,000/6 = 500 units; A1 margin of safety = 700-500; A1 = 200 units.
3. [3] M1 average annual profit = 9,000/3 = £3,000; A1 ARR = 3,000/25,000 x 100 = 12%; B1 correct method (average annual profit / initial cost x 100) clearly shown.
4. [4] B1 the income statement shows revenue, costs and profit/loss over a period; B1 the statement of financial position shows what a business owns and owes at a single point in time; B1 asset example, e.g. cash, stock, equipment or buildings; B1 liability example, e.g. a loan, overdraft or money owed to suppliers.
5. [5] M1 gross profit = 200,000-130,000 = £70,000; A1 gross profit margin = 70,000/200,000 x 100 = 35%; M1 net profit = 70,000-40,000 = £30,000; A1 net profit margin = 30,000/200,000 x 100 = 15%; B1 correct method for both margins clearly shown.
6. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
