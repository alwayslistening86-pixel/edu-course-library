# S12_Customers_and_Segmentation - Lesson: Understanding customers and market segmentation

## Goal
The learner explains why businesses need to understand their customers, and how markets are segmented by gender, age, location and income.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.5.1 - Identifying and understanding customers: why businesses need to know their customers (product fit, sales, marketing mix, avoiding costly mistakes, competitiveness)
- 3.5.2 - Segmentation: dividing a market by gender, age, location and income

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: would a business selling luxury sports cars advertise the same way, in the same place, as a business selling budget school shoes? Different customer groups need different approaches -- that's segmentation. GCSE Business has real quantitative content throughout (at least 10% of all marks are quantitative skills): break-even (identifying break-even output and margin of safety from a chart -- AQA does not require the break-even formula or chart-drawing, but understanding the underlying contribution logic is how a learner reads a chart correctly), cash-flow forecasts (net cash flow, opening/closing balance), gross and net profit margins, and average rate of return on an investment. AQA does not give formulae in the exam for gross/net profit margin or average rate of return, so the learner must know these by heart, not just how to plug numbers in. Every numerical worked example in this course was computed and checked when this course was built, and the learner should always be made to show full working. AQA GCSE Business explicitly does NOT examine named motivational theories (e.g. Maslow, Herzberg, Taylor) by name -- only financial and non-financial motivation methods in general terms -- so this course does not teach or test theorist names either.

#### 3.5.1 Identifying and understanding customers: why businesses need to know their customers (product fit, sales, marketing mix, avoiding costly mistakes, competitiveness)
Businesses need to identify and understand their **customers** for several reasons: to **provide a product or service customers will actually buy** (rather than guessing what might sell), to **increase sales** by targeting the right people with the right offer, to **select the correct marketing mix** (the right price, product features, promotion and place for that specific customer group), to **avoid costly mistakes** (e.g. launching a product nobody wants, or advertising in the wrong place), and to **remain competitive** against rivals who do understand their customers well. Without this understanding, a business risks wasting money on a product or campaign that misses its intended audience entirely.

#### 3.5.2 Segmentation: dividing a market by gender, age, location and income
**Segmentation** is dividing a whole market into smaller groups of customers with similar characteristics, so a business can target its marketing more precisely rather than treating all customers the same. Common ways to segment a market include: **gender**, **age** (e.g. children, teenagers, young adults, older customers, each with different needs/preferences/spending patterns), **location** (e.g. urban vs. rural, or by region -- needs/tastes and even prices can vary by area), and **income** (e.g. targeting a budget/value segment versus a premium/luxury segment with a higher price point and different marketing). *Example:* a clothing retailer might segment by age and income, selling a budget, trend-led range aimed at younger customers with less disposable income, and a premium, classic range aimed at older, higher-income customers -- each segment then gets its own marketing mix.
