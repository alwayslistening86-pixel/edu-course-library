# S25_Legal_Research - Test: Conducting legal research: sources, citation, analysis and ethical use

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in CILEX's style: short knowledge items (some multiple-select, mirroring the MCQ element of F1-F4) plus at least one scenario/problem question requiring issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC) reasoning -- identify the legal issue(s) a set of facts raises, state the applicable rule with its authority (statute section or case), apply it precisely to the facts, and reach a supported conclusion or piece of client advice. Give the whole test at once, with no hints. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. What does a citator tool (e.g. within Westlaw or Lexis+) primarily show? Choose every correct option.
   A. The court fees payable for issuing a claim based on that case
   B. Whether a case has subsequently been followed, applied, distinguished, doubted or overruled by later authority
   C. The full biography of the judges who decided the case
   D. An automatically generated summary written by artificial intelligence with no human verification
2. Write the correctly formatted neutral citation style for a fictional 2021 Court of Appeal (Civil Division) case between Green and Patel, case number 45, and explain what each element of the citation tells the reader. [6 marks]
3. A client asks whether a 2016 High Court decision supporting their position is still reliable. A trainee finds the case via a free online search, sees it appears to support the client, and drafts advice confirming the point is settled law in the client's favour, without checking a citator or looking for any later case law. Explain what is wrong with this approach and what the trainee should have done instead. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [6] B2 correct format: Green v Patel [2021] EWCA Civ 45 (party names conventionally italicised); B1 [2021] is the year the case was decided/reported under the neutral citation scheme; B1 EWCA Civ identifies the court (England and Wales Court of Appeal, Civil Division); B1 45 is the sequential case number assigned by the court that year, allowing the exact judgment to be located regardless of which law report series (if any) later reports it; B1 credit any accurate explanation of the elements even if the exact wording differs.
3. [10] B2 the trainee has not checked whether the 2016 decision remains good law -- it could since have been overruled, doubted, or distinguished by a later, potentially higher-court, decision, which a citator check (or at least a search for later citing cases) would reveal; B2 relying on an uncorroborated single search result without verifying currency risks giving the client materially wrong advice, since presenting an unconfirmed or since-overturned position as 'settled law' is both a competence failure and an ethical one -- overstating certainty to support what looks like a favourable answer for the client; B2 the trainee should have used a citator (or checked Find Case Law/a database's citing-references function) to confirm the case's current status, checked for later authority on the same point, and honestly reported any uncertainty or conflicting authority found, rather than presenting the point as definitively settled; B2 if genuine doubt remains after proper research, the trainee should flag this clearly to the client/supervisor rather than giving falsely confident advice, and escalate if the research is beyond their competence to resolve; B2 credit for identifying this as consistent with the course's IRAC standard -- a conclusion must be properly supported by verified, current authority, not merely by the first favourable-looking result found.
