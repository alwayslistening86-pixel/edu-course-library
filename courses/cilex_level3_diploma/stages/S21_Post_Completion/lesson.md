# S21_Post_Completion - Lesson: Post-completion: sale-side and purchase-side steps, tax and registration

## Goal
The learner explains the steps taken after completion on both the sale and purchase sides, including payment of SDLT/LTT and applications to HM Land Registry.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PPC.17 - Post-completion: sale-side and purchase-side steps, SDLT/LTT, HM Land Registry applications

## How to teach this
Ask: completion has just happened and the buyer has the keys. Is the legal work actually finished at this point? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### PPC.17 Post-completion: sale-side and purchase-side steps, SDLT/LTT, HM Land Registry applications
**Post-completion.** On the **purchase side**, the buyer's conveyancer must: submit a **Stamp Duty Land Tax (SDLT)** return to HMRC (for land in England) or a **Land Transaction Tax (LTT)** return to the Welsh Revenue Authority (for land in Wales) and pay any tax due -- generally within **14 days** of completion for SDLT (check the current statutory filing deadline and rates/thresholds against a live source, as these change by Budget/Welsh Government announcement and can also depend on buyer circumstances, e.g. first-time buyer relief or additional-property surcharges); apply to **HM Land Registry** to register the transfer (and any new mortgage/charge) in the buyer's name, using the priority period obtained from the pre-completion OS1/OS2 search to protect the application's priority; and, once registration is complete, check the **official copies** showing the buyer as registered proprietor and send/report these (with a final report and account) to the client and, where relevant, the lender. Registration formally completes/perfects the buyer's legal title (for registered land, legal title does not pass until registration, even though completion has occurred as between the parties). On the **sale side**, the seller's conveyancer must: **redeem the seller's mortgage** using the sale proceeds and the redemption statement, obtaining confirmation of discharge from the lender (historically a paper **DS1**, now commonly an **electronic discharge (ED)** notified directly to HM Land Registry by larger lenders); account to the seller for the **net proceeds of sale** (sale price, less redemption, less the conveyancer's costs and disbursements, less any apportionments due to the buyer); and deal with any post-completion undertakings given (e.g. confirming discharge of the mortgage to the buyer's conveyancer, who relied on that undertaking to release completion funds). Throughout, the conveyancer should retain file notes/documents in line with the firm's file retention policy and professional record-keeping obligations, in case of a later query or claim.
