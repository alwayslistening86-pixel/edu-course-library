## Test items
1. Why does the buyer's conveyancer rely on the priority period from the pre-completion OS1/OS2 search when submitting the application to register the transfer? Choose every correct option.
   A. It extends the time available to pay SDLT/LTT
   B. It protects the buyer's registration application against adverse third-party entries made on the register between the search and the application being submitted
   C. It removes the need to redeem the seller's mortgage
   D. It guarantees the buyer's application will be processed the same day
2. Explain why land in England attracts SDLT while land in Wales attracts LTT, and why a conveyancer should always check current rates/thresholds before advising a client. [6 marks]
3. A buyer's purchase of a house in Wales completes on a Monday. The buyer's conveyancer is dealing with a heavy caseload and does not submit the LTT return and payment, or the HM Land Registry application, until six weeks later, by which point the OS2 priority period has expired and a third party has registered a conflicting interest in the interim. Using IRAC, advise on the significance of the delay. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [6] B2 Land Transaction Tax was devolved to the Welsh Government/Welsh Revenue Authority from April 2018, replacing SDLT for land transactions in Wales, while SDLT (administered by HMRC) continues to apply to land in England; B2 the two taxes have separate, independently set rates, bands and reliefs, which can and do diverge between the two nations; B2 rates, thresholds and reliefs (e.g. first-time buyer relief, additional-property surcharges) change periodically via Budget or Welsh Government announcement, so a conveyancer must check the current position against a live source (HMRC/Welsh Revenue Authority) rather than relying on a previously learned figure, to correctly advise the client and file an accurate return.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = the consequences of failing to act promptly post-completion on tax filing and registration, including the effect of the priority period expiring; rule = LTT (for Welsh land) must be filed and paid within the applicable statutory deadline, with penalties/interest for late filing; the priority period conferred by a pre-completion OS2 search protects the buyer's registration application only if the application is made within that period, after which the protection lapses and a third party's intervening registered entry can take priority; application = the six-week delay is very likely to have missed the LTT filing deadline, exposing the buyer to penalties and interest, and, more seriously, by delaying the registration application past expiry of the priority period, the buyer's application no longer has protected priority, meaning the third party's conflicting interest, now registered first, may take precedence and prejudice the buyer's title; conclusion = the delay is a serious failure exposing the conveyancer/firm to a potential negligence claim for any loss caused (e.g. LTT penalties, and any loss flowing from the third-party interest taking priority), and the buyer should be advised urgently on the registration position and any steps available to protect or rectify their title.
