# S09_Tort_Defences_Causation_and_Remedies - Lesson: Defences, causation and remedies in tort

## Goal
The learner explains the general defences of volenti non fit injuria, ex turpi causa and contributory negligence, and the rules on remoteness of damage and the remedies available in tort, including damages and injunctions.

## Syllabus items taught here
- TORT.7 - Defences: volenti non fit injuria, ex turpi causa, contributory negligence, rescuers
- TORT.8 - Causation and remedies in tort: remoteness, types of damages, injunctions

## How to teach this
Ask: a spectator at a cricket match is hit by a ball hit for six. Can they sue the batsman or the club? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### TORT.7 Defences: volenti non fit injuria, ex turpi causa, contributory negligence, rescuers
**Defences.** **Volenti non fit injuria** ("to a willing person, no injury is done") is a complete defence where the claimant had actual **knowledge** of the specific risk, **notice** of it, and **freely accepted** it (expressly or by conduct) -- commonly relevant to **participants in or spectators at** sporting/recreational activities and events, who are taken to accept risks inherent in the activity conducted within its normal rules. **Ex turpi causa non oritur actio** ("no action arises from a base cause") bars a claim where it arises directly out of the claimant's own **participation in a criminal offence**, where allowing the claim would offend the public conscience/undermine the criminal law's coherence. The **Law Reform (Contributory Negligence) Act 1945** provides for **contributory negligence** as a partial defence: where the claimant's own carelessness contributed to their accident or increased the extent of their injury (e.g. not wearing a seatbelt), the court apportions responsibility and reduces damages by a "just and equitable" percentage reflecting the claimant's share of the blame. Special rules and caution apply to **volunteers, rescuers and those acting in an emergency/imperilled situation** -- their intervention is generally treated as foreseeable (not volenti, not a novus actus, and not normally contributorily negligent merely for intervening), reflecting policy support for rescue.

#### TORT.8 Causation and remedies in tort: remoteness, types of damages, injunctions
**Causation and remedies in tort.** **Remoteness of damage** limits recovery to loss of a **reasonably foreseeable type** (the **egg-shell skull rule** still applies to the *extent* of a foreseeable type of injury); the leading foreseeability authority is ***The Wagon Mound*** (damage of an unforeseeable type is too remote, even if directly caused). **Intervening acts** (novus actus interveniens) can break the chain between breach and loss. **Pure economic loss** (financial loss not consequential on physical damage to the claimant's person or property) is recoverable in negligence only in limited, established categories (e.g. negligent misstatement relationships); **physical damage** and consequential financial loss are more readily recoverable, as is loss flowing from **defective goods or property** in appropriate circumstances. **Damages in personal injury claims**: **general damages** compensate non-quantifiable loss (pain, suffering, loss of amenity, future loss), assessed using guidelines/comparable awards; **special damages** compensate precisely quantifiable loss to the date of trial (medical expenses, lost earnings, quantifiable future loss). **Non-compensatory damages** are exceptional: **aggravated damages** (for injury to feelings from the manner of the wrong) and **exemplary/punitive damages** (rare, to punish outrageous conduct, only in defined categories of case). **Injunctions** can be **mandatory** (compel an act, e.g. remove an obstruction) or **prohibitory** (restrain future conduct, e.g. stop a continuing nuisance). **Contributory negligence** under the 1945 Act reduces damages proportionately; special considerations apply to **children**, whose contributory fault is judged against the standard of a reasonable child of that age, not an adult. **Mitigation** requires a claimant to take reasonable steps to limit their loss (a failure to mitigate reduces recoverable damages), and **betterment** (where a repair leaves the claimant better off than before, e.g. a new part replacing a worn one) may be reflected by a deduction from damages.
