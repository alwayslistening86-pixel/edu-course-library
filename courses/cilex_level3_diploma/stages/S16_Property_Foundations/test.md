## Test items
1. What is required for a purchaser to take registered land free of the beneficiaries' equitable interests under a trust of land, by overreaching? Choose every correct option.
   A. Payment of the purchase price to the sole legal owner
   B. Payment of the purchase price to at least two trustees (or a trust corporation)
   C. A statutory declaration signed by all beneficiaries
   D. Registration of a restriction against the title before completion
2. Explain a conveyancer's obligations under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 when taking on a new conveyancing client, and what a trainee must do if they suspect money laundering. [8 marks]
3. Two unmarried partners buy a house, contributing £150,000 and £50,000 respectively of the £200,000 price, and complete without saying anything to their conveyancer about how they want to hold the beneficial interest. One dies years later without a will, survived by the other and an adult child from a previous relationship. Using IRAC, advise on the practical importance of the conveyancer having addressed co-ownership at the outset, and the likely position now. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [8] B1 conveyancing is recognised as higher-risk for money laundering; B2 the firm must carry out client due diligence, verifying the client's identity (and beneficial ownership for corporate/trust clients) before establishing the business relationship; B1 enhanced due diligence applies in higher-risk situations, e.g. politically exposed persons or unusual/complex transactions; B1 records must be kept; B2 a trainee who suspects money laundering has a personal duty to report internally to the firm's Money Laundering Reporting Officer, who decides whether to submit a Suspicious Activity Report to the National Crime Agency; B1 the trainee must not 'tip off' the client that a report has been made or considered.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = the consequences of not addressing joint tenancy versus tenancy in common at the point of purchase, given unequal contributions; rule = co-owners hold the legal estate as joint tenants on a trust of land, but the beneficial interest can be held as joint tenants (survivorship, no distinct share) or tenants in common (distinct shares, passing under will/intestacy); absent an express declaration of trust, unequal contributions can point towards a tenancy in common in shares reflecting contribution, but this can be contested and is far less certain than an express declaration; a competent conveyancer should identify unequal contributions as a conflict/advice point and recommend an express declaration of trust; application = because no declaration was made, whether the couple held as beneficial joint tenants (in which case survivorship gives the whole interest to the survivor, and the child has no claim on the house) or tenants in common in unequal shares (in which case the deceased's share would pass under the intestacy rules, potentially benefiting the child) is now uncertain and could require evidence/litigation to resolve; conclusion = the conveyancer should have raised the co-ownership question and recommended an express declaration of trust recording the unequal contributions at the outset; its absence now creates real uncertainty and risk of dispute between the survivor and the child.
