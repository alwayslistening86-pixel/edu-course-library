## Test items
1. Explain the 'close connection' approach to vicarious liability and why an employer can be liable even for an employee's dishonest or unauthorised act. [6 marks]
2. A visitor to a warehouse ignores a clear, prominently displayed warning sign about a dangerous unfenced drop and is injured. A trespasser who climbed the perimeter fence at night, unknown to the occupier, is also injured on the same site. Using IRAC, advise the occupier on liability to each. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 vicarious liability makes an employer strictly liable for torts by an employee committed in the course of employment, without the employer itself being at fault; B2 modern case law asks whether the wrongful act is so closely connected with what the employee was authorised to do that it is fair and just to hold the employer liable; B2 this can extend to dishonest, disobedient or even criminal acts if sufficiently connected to the employee's role (e.g. a role giving the employee the opportunity/authority to commit the wrong), rather than being confined to acts the employer expressly sanctioned.
2. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = liability to a lawful visitor who ignored a clear warning, and to an unknown trespasser; rule = OLA 1957 duty to visitors can be discharged by an adequate warning, and no duty typically arises for obvious risks; OLA 1984 duty to trespassers arises only where the occupier knew/had reasonable grounds to believe both of the danger and of the trespasser's likely presence; application = the clear, prominent warning likely discharges the 1957 duty to the visitor (and the risk may also be treated as obvious); the occupier had no reason to believe an unknown night-time trespasser would be present, so the 1984 duty likely does not arise; conclusion = the occupier has a strong defence to both claims, though should ensure the warning met the standard required and consider whether repeated known trespass would change the 1984 analysis.
