## Test items
1. Explain why equity developed as a separate system from the common law, and what the Judicature Acts 1873-75 changed. [6 marks]
2. A defendant is prosecuted for causing criminal damage to a neighbour's fence and separately sued by the neighbour for the cost of repair. Using IRAC, explain why these are two different cases and what each must prove. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 common law's rigid writ system and single remedy (damages) left some claimants without an adequate remedy; B1 claimants petitioned the King/Lord Chancellor, who developed equity based on conscience/fairness; B1 equity introduced remedies common law lacked (injunction, specific performance, rescission, rectification) and the trust; B1 equity and common law were administered in separate courts (Chancery vs common law courts) until 1873-75; B1 the Judicature Acts 1873-75 fused their *administration* so every court can apply both; B1 the two bodies of principle remain conceptually distinct, and equity prevails where they conflict.
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether one act can give rise to both a criminal prosecution and a civil claim; rule = criminal and civil law are separate systems with separate objectives, burdens/standards of proof and remedies; application = the prosecution (state v defendant) must prove the offence beyond reasonable doubt to punish the conduct, while the neighbour's civil claim (in tort, e.g. trespass to land or negligence) need only be proved on the balance of probabilities to compensate the repair cost; conclusion = both cases can proceed independently and a result in one does not automatically determine the other, though the criminal standard is harder to meet.
