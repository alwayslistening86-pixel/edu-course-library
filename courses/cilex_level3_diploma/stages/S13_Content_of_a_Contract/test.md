## Test items
1. Under the Consumer Rights Act 2015, which implied term applies to a contract for a trader to service a consumer's car? Choose every correct option.
   A. Goods must be of satisfactory quality
   B. Services must be performed with reasonable care and skill
   C. Goods must be fit for a particular purpose made known to the trader
   D. Digital content must be of satisfactory quality
2. Explain the three requirements for an exemption clause to be effective at common law (incorporation, construction and statutory control), using one example of each. [9 marks]
3. A consumer buys a secondhand car from a trader's forecourt. The written contract, in small print on the reverse, states 'sold as seen; all conditions, warranties and liability excluded to the fullest extent'. The car turns out to have a dangerous steering fault, unknown to the consumer, that caused a crash and personal injury. Using IRAC, advise the consumer. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [9] B3 incorporation: the clause must be validly brought into the contract, e.g. by signature (L'Estrange v Graucob) or reasonable notice before/at the time of contracting (Interfoto v Stiletto for unusually onerous terms); B3 construction: the clause must, on its wording, actually cover the breach/loss that occurred, with ambiguity construed contra proferentem against the party relying on it; B3 statutory control: even a validly incorporated, applicable clause may be void (e.g. excluding negligence liability for death/personal injury under UCTA 1977) or subject to a reasonableness/fairness test under UCTA 1977 or the Consumer Rights Act 2015.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = whether the exclusion clause is effective against the consumer's claims for a defective car and personal injury; rule = for goods supplied to a consumer, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 implies terms as to satisfactory quality (s9); under the Act, a term excluding/limiting liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, or excluding the core statutory quality rights against a consumer, is treated as unfair/not binding, and more generally a term must be transparent and not cause a significant imbalance contrary to good faith; application = the dangerous steering fault breaches the satisfactory quality implied term, and the blanket exclusion clause purporting to exclude 'all liability' including for the personal injury caused would not be effective against the consumer, regardless of the 'as seen' wording or its location in small print; conclusion = the consumer can claim against the trader for breach of the satisfactory quality term and for the personal injury, and the exclusion clause does not protect the trader.
