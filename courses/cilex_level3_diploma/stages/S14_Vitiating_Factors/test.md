## Test items
1. A seller carelessly, but honestly and without reasonable grounds, tells a buyer that a business has an annual turnover of £500,000 when it does not. The buyer relies on this and buys the business. What category of misrepresentation is this? Choose every correct option.
   A. Fraudulent misrepresentation
   B. Negligent misrepresentation under s2(1) Misrepresentation Act 1967
   C. Innocent misrepresentation
   D. Not misrepresentation, as it was an honest belief
2. Explain the difference between rescission and damages as remedies for misrepresentation, and how the availability of each differs across fraudulent, negligent and innocent misrepresentation. [8 marks]
3. A long-standing junior business partner is told by the dominant senior partner, on whom she has always relied for financial advice and who manages all the firm's paperwork, to sign a personal guarantee 'or the partnership is over'. She signs without independent advice. Using IRAC, advise on whether the guarantee can be set aside. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [8] B2 rescission sets the contract aside and aims to restore the parties to their pre-contract position, available in principle for all three categories subject to the usual bars (affirmation, lapse of time, third-party rights, restitution being impossible); B2 damages in the tort of deceit are available for fraudulent misrepresentation with wide remoteness; B2 damages under s2(1) Misrepresentation Act 1967 are available for negligent misrepresentation once inducement is shown, unless the representor proves reasonable grounds for belief, and are assessed generously (as if fraudulent); B2 for innocent misrepresentation, rescission is the primary remedy, with damages in lieu available only at the court's discretion under s2(2) instead of (not in addition to) rescission.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = whether the guarantee is voidable for undue influence (and/or economic duress); rule = presumed undue influence arises where a relationship of trust and confidence is shown and the transaction calls for explanation, shifting the burden to the defendant to show it was freely entered, e.g. via independent advice; economic duress requires illegitimate pressure leaving no reasonable alternative; application = the relationship (reliance on the senior partner for financial matters) is capable of founding presumed undue influence, and a personal guarantee signed under an implicit threat to end the partnership, without independent advice, calls for explanation; the threat could also support an economic duress argument if sufficiently illegitimate; conclusion = the junior partner has a good argument to rescind the guarantee for undue influence (the presumption not having been rebutted, since no independent advice was taken), and possibly duress, subject to no bar to rescission such as affirmation or third-party rights having arisen.
