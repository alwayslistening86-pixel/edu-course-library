## Test items
1. Explain the 'but for' test and how a novus actus interveniens can affect it. [6 marks]
2. A surgeon operates without properly explaining a risk, which then materialises, causing the patient injury that a person with an unusually thin skull would suffer more severely than most. Using IRAC, advise on a negligence claim. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 the 'but for' test asks whether the claimant's harm would have occurred but for the defendant's breach -- if the harm would have happened anyway, factual causation is not established; B2 a novus actus interveniens is an intervening act (by the claimant, a third party, or a natural event) that is sufficiently independent and unforeseeable to break the chain of causation; B2 if established, the defendant is not liable for harm occurring after the intervening act, because it is no longer caused by their breach.
2. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = whether the surgeon owed and breached a duty of care, and the extent of liability given the claimant's vulnerability; rule = a professional is judged against the standard of a reasonably competent member of that profession; breach may arise from inadequate risk disclosure/consent; the egg-shell skull rule means the defendant takes the claimant as found and is liable for the full extent of a foreseeable type of injury even if unusually severe due to the claimant's own vulnerability; application = failing to properly explain a material risk is capable of being a breach of the professional standard of care, and if the injury (of a foreseeable type) is more severe due to the patient's thin skull, that does not reduce liability; conclusion = the claim is likely to succeed in negligence for the full extent of the injury, subject to establishing factual causation (that proper disclosure would have led to a different decision) and no novus actus.
