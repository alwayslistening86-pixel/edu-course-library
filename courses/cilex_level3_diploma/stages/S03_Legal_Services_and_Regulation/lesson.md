# S03_Legal_Services_and_Regulation - Lesson: The provision and regulation of legal services

## Goal
The learner identifies the different legal professions and levels of judge, the regulators of legal services, and the regulatory safeguards and professional ethics that govern client engagement.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LS.3 - The provision and regulation of legal services: the professions, the judiciary, regulators, professional ethics obligations

## How to teach this
Ask: a CILEX trainee spots that a large payment from a client hasn't been properly recorded. Who regulates them, and what should they do? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### LS.3 The provision and regulation of legal services: the professions, the judiciary, regulators, professional ethics obligations
**The legal professions.** A **Chartered Legal Executive** is a qualified lawyer specialising in a chosen area of law, able (once fully qualified/Fellow) to undertake work equivalent to a solicitor in that specialism, regulated by CILEX Regulation. A **solicitor** typically has broader initial training across areas of law before specialising, regulated by the SRA. A **barrister** typically specialises in advocacy and specialist advisory work, traditionally instructed by solicitors/CILEX lawyers though direct access is increasingly available, regulated by the BSB.

**The judiciary.** The **Lord Chancellor/Secretary of State for Justice** has constitutional responsibility for the judiciary and justice system, though judicial independence is protected by statute. Judicial levels, broadly senior to junior: **Justices of the Supreme Court** (final appellate court); **Lords Justices of Appeal** (Court of Appeal); **High Court Judges**; **Circuit Judges** (Crown Court and higher County Court work); **Recorders** (part-time judges, mainly Crown Court); **District Judges** (County Court and Magistrates' Court case management and trials).

**Regulatory bodies.** The **Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA)** regulates solicitors and SRA-regulated firms; **CILEX** (through CILEX Regulation) regulates Chartered Legal Executives and CILEX Lawyers; the **Bar Standards Board (BSB)** regulates barristers; the **Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)** regulates financial services activity that can overlap with legal practice (e.g. some claims-handling, insurance-related work).

**Regulatory safeguards and professional ethics.** Trainees must comply with: **data protection** -- the collection, storage and use of client data under the **Data Protection Act 2018** and **UK GDPR**, plus basic cybersecurity awareness; **financial crime/anti-money laundering** -- compliance with AML guidance under the **Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017** (as amended, including the 2019 amendment regulations), including client due diligence and reporting suspicious activity; **client money** -- handling in line with CILEX Regulation's accounts outcomes and (where relevant) the SRA Accounts Rules, keeping client money separate from the firm's own money; and the **SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors'** core principles (which CILEX-regulated staff working in SRA-regulated firms must also respect in practice), particularly maintaining trust, acting with honesty and integrity, competence, avoiding conflicts of interest, and confidentiality/disclosure. Every trainee bears **personal responsibility** for compliance with these regulatory requirements, and must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor.
