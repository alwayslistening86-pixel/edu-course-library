## Test items
1. Under the Law Society's Code for Completion by Post, in what capacity does the buyer's conveyancer complete the transaction? Choose every correct option.
   A. As principal, acting entirely for the buyer with no obligations to the seller's side
   B. As agent for the seller's conveyancer, subject to that conveyancer's instructions given under the Code
   C. As a neutral stakeholder appointed by both parties
   D. As agent for HM Land Registry
2. Explain the purpose of a redemption statement and why its timing matters for completion. [6 marks]
3. Exchange of contracts takes place by telephone under the Law Society's Formula B, with completion fixed for a date four weeks later. Two days before completion, the seller's conveyancer requests an up-to-date redemption statement, and on the completion date itself, the buyer's funds are received by the seller's conveyancer at 4:30pm, after the contractual 2pm deadline under the Standard Conditions. Using IRAC, advise the seller on their position. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [6] B2 a redemption statement is obtained from the seller's existing mortgage lender, confirming the exact sum needed to discharge/redeem the mortgage; B2 it is needed so the seller's conveyancer (and, via the completion statement, the transaction generally) knows how much of the sale proceeds must be paid to the lender to release the charge before/on completion; B2 timing matters because the figure typically includes interest calculated to a specific date and may change daily, so a stale redemption statement could leave a shortfall, delaying discharge of the mortgage and registration of the buyer's title free of that charge.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = the effect of completing after the contractual completion time, and the redemption statement's role in enabling completion; rule = the Standard Conditions fix a time for completion (commonly 2pm) and provide for interest to become payable by a party at fault for late completion, with a notice to complete procedure available if completion is delayed beyond the contractual date itself (not merely the same-day deadline); the redemption statement must be current so the correct sum is available to discharge the seller's mortgage; application = funds arriving at 4:30pm rather than 2pm on the correct completion date is a same-day delay (not a failure to complete on the correct date), for which the Standard Conditions typically provide a compensation/interest remedy rather than the more drastic step of serving notice to complete or treating the contract as at an end; the seller should ensure the redemption statement obtained shortly before completion is used so the mortgage is discharged for the correct amount; conclusion = advise the seller that completion has still occurred (on the correct date, albeit late in the day), and their remedy for the delay is typically to claim contractual interest for the period of lateness under the Standard Conditions, not to treat the contract as at an end or serve notice to complete, provided completion did in fact occur that day and the mortgage was properly redeemed using a current redemption figure.
