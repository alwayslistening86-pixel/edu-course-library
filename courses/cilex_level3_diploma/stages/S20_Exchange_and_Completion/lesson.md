# S20_Exchange_and_Completion - Lesson: Reporting and exchange, preparing for completion, and completion itself

## Goal
The learner explains the conveyancer's duty to report to the client and the mechanics of exchange of contracts, the steps taken in preparing for completion, and the methods and effect of completion itself.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PPC.14 - Reporting to client and exchange of contracts
- PPC.15 - Preparing for completion: redemption, TR1, completion statements, pre-completion searches
- PPC.16 - Completion: timing, methods, the Law Society's Code for Completion by Post

## How to teach this
Ask: on the day of completion, the buyer's money has been sent but the seller's conveyancer says it hasn't arrived yet. What actually needs to happen for completion to be treated as having occurred? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### PPC.14 Reporting to client and exchange of contracts
**Reporting to client and exchange of contracts.** Before exchange, the conveyancer must report fully to the client, covering: the terms of the draft contract (price, deposit, completion date, any special conditions); the results of title investigation and all searches/enquiries, flagging any adverse matters and how they have been (or should be) resolved; the mortgage offer, if any, and its conditions; and confirmation the client understands that, from exchange, the transaction becomes **legally binding** and (typically) **risk passes**, with a fixed completion date the client must be able to meet. Exchange itself is usually carried out by **telephone** between the two conveyancers, following the **Law Society's Formulae for Exchanging Contracts by Telephone, Telex or Fax** (Formula A, B or C, depending on whether one or both parts are already held by/released to the other side and whether a related chain transaction is involved), each conveyancer confirming their client's signed part is now held to the other's order and undertaking to send it immediately. On exchange: the **deposit** is paid (commonly 10%, though sometimes reduced, particularly with lender/broker involvement, subject to the seller's agreement); the **completion date** becomes fixed and binding; and each party is contractually committed, with damages (and, ultimately, specific performance or rescission and forfeiture of deposit) available for a failure to complete without lawful excuse.

#### PPC.15 Preparing for completion: redemption, TR1, completion statements, pre-completion searches
**Preparing for completion.** Between exchange and completion, the seller's conveyancer obtains a **redemption statement** from the seller's existing mortgage lender, confirming the sum needed to discharge (redeem) the mortgage on the completion date (this figure typically includes interest to the exact completion date and may include an early-repayment charge). The **TR1** (Land Registry transfer form) is prepared, approved and, once approved, executed as a deed by the seller (and, where relevant, the buyer, e.g. for a purchase subject to obligations) ahead of completion, ready for dating and release on the day. The buyer's conveyancer prepares a **completion statement**, itemising the final balance due from the buyer (purchase price, less deposit already paid, plus/less apportioned outgoings such as ground rent, service charge or council tax, plus disbursements such as search fees, Land Registry fee and the conveyancer's own costs). Immediately before completion, the buyer's conveyancer carries out final **pre-completion searches**: an official search of the register with priority (**OS1** for the whole title, **OS2** for part) at HM Land Registry, which confers a priority period during which the buyer's application to register can be protected against any adverse entry made by a third party in the interim, and a bankruptcy search (**K16**, essential wherever a mortgage is involved, to protect the lender) against each buyer (and, if relevant, seller) to check for any bankruptcy entries that could affect the transaction. Funds (the balance due, and any mortgage advance) are requested from the buyer/lender to arrive with the buyer's conveyancer in good time for completion.

#### PPC.16 Completion: timing, methods, the Law Society's Code for Completion by Post
**Completion.** Completion is the point at which the **purchase money is transferred**, the **transaction is finalised**, and (in practice) the buyer becomes entitled to possession/the keys are released, with the legal estate passing (registration at HM Land Registry then perfects/completes the buyer's legal title, but as between the parties completion is treated as the operative moment). **Timing**: the Standard Conditions provide for completion by a specified time on the completion date (commonly by 2pm, or as varied), with interest becoming payable by a defaulting party for late completion, and a **notice to complete** procedure allowing an innocent party, once the contractual date has passed, to serve notice requiring completion within a further fixed period (commonly ten working days), making time of the essence and, if still not met, triggering remedies including rescission and forfeiture of the deposit. **Methods of completion**: in person (the parties' conveyancers physically meet and exchange documents/money, now rare); through an agent (a local agent instructed to complete on the seller's conveyancer's behalf); or, most commonly today, by **post**, under the **Law Society's Code for Completion by Post**, whereby the buyer's conveyancer undertakes to complete as agent for the seller's conveyancer, subject to the seller's conveyancer's instructions given under the Code (covering matters such as what documents to date and release, how to deal with the mortgage redemption, and accounting for the balance), removing the need for physical attendance while imposing detailed undertakings and duties on the buyer's conveyancer acting on the seller's behalf for that limited purpose.
