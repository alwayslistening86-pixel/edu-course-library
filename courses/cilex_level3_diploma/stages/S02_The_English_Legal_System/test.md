## Test items
1. Explain the Full Code Test the CPS applies when deciding whether to prosecute. [4 marks]
2. A small manufacturing company wants to resolve a £15,000 dispute with a supplier without going to court, and separately faces a criminal prosecution for a breach of health and safety law arising from the same incident. Using IRAC, advise on (a) an appropriate ADR route for the civil dispute and (b) the criminal court and appeal route. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B2 the evidential stage: is there sufficient evidence to provide a realistic prospect of conviction; B2 the public interest stage: is prosecution in the public interest, considering factors such as seriousness, the offender's culpability and circumstances (both stages must be satisfied, evidential stage first).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = choice of ADR mechanism for a £15,000 commercial dispute, and identification of the criminal court/appeal route; rule = mediation or arbitration are suited to ongoing commercial relationships and confidentiality, arbitration giving a binding outcome without court proceedings; a health and safety offence against a company is typically either-way/indictable and would be tried in the Crown Court given likely seriousness, with appeal to the Court of Appeal (Criminal Division); application = £15,000 exceeds small claims track and the parties may want to preserve the trading relationship, favouring mediation first, with arbitration as a binding fallback if a supply contract has an arbitration clause; conclusion = recommend mediation (or arbitration if a clause exists) for the civil dispute, and confirm the criminal matter proceeds in the Crown Court with any appeal to the Court of Appeal.
