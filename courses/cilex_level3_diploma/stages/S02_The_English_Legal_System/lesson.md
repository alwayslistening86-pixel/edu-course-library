# S02_The_English_Legal_System - Lesson: The English legal system: courts, appeals, legal personality, ADR

## Goal
The learner describes the criminal and civil court structures and their appeal routes, the concept of legal personality, and the range of alternative dispute resolution (ADR/ODR) and out-of-court criminal disposals.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LS.2 - The English legal system: criminal and civil courts, appeal routes, legal personality, ADR/ODR, out-of-court criminal disposals

## How to teach this
Ask: a small business is owed £8,000 by a client who won't pay. Which court, and what happens if they lose and want to appeal? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### LS.2 The English legal system: criminal and civil courts, appeal routes, legal personality, ADR/ODR, out-of-court criminal disposals
**The criminal legal system.** Offences are classified as **summary** (minor, tried only in the Magistrates' Court, e.g. most motoring offences), **either-way** (can be tried in either court depending on seriousness, e.g. theft, ABH) or **indictable-only** (serious, tried only in the Crown Court, e.g. murder, rape). The **Magistrates' Court** (lay magistrates or a District Judge) tries summary and some either-way cases and holds first appearances for all criminal cases; the **Crown Court** (judge and jury) tries indictable and sent either-way cases and sentences; the **Court of Appeal (Criminal Division)** hears appeals against conviction/sentence from the Crown Court; the **Supreme Court** hears further appeals on points of law of general public importance. The **Criminal Procedure Rules 2015** govern criminal case management; their **overriding objective** is dealing with cases justly, including acquitting the innocent, convicting the guilty, dealing with the prosecution and defence fairly, and dealing with the case efficiently and expeditiously. The **Crown Prosecution Service** decides whether to prosecute using the **Full Code Test**: (1) is there sufficient evidence for a realistic prospect of conviction, and (2) is prosecution in the public interest; a private individual may also bring a **private prosecution** in limited circumstances.

**The civil legal system.** The **County Court** and **High Court** (Chancery, King's Bench and Family Divisions) hear civil claims at first instance, with jurisdiction/allocation depending on value and complexity; the **Civil Procedure Rules 1998** govern civil litigation, with an **overriding objective** of dealing with cases justly and at proportionate cost. Cases are allocated to the **three-track system**: small claims track (broadly claims up to £10,000, informal, limited costs recovery), fast track (broadly £10,000-£25,000, tighter case management), and multi-track (higher value/complex claims); **Practice Direction 26** governs allocation. The **Practice Direction on Pre-Action Conduct and Protocols** requires parties to exchange information and attempt to resolve disputes before issuing proceedings, with costs sanctions for non-compliance. Appeals go from the County Court/High Court to the **Civil Division of the Court of Appeal**, and from there (with permission, on a point of law of general public importance) to the **Supreme Court**.

**Criminal appeal routes.** From the Magistrates' Court: appeal against conviction and/or sentence to the Crown Court (a full rehearing), or appeal to the **Divisional Court of the King's Bench Division** of the High Court **by way of case stated** on a point of law. From the Crown Court: appeal against conviction and/or sentence to the **Court of Appeal (Criminal Division)**. The **Criminal Cases Review Commission** can refer a suspected miscarriage of justice back to the Court of Appeal after normal appeal routes are exhausted.

**Legal personality.** The law recognises **natural persons** (individuals) and **juristic (legal) persons** (entities the law treats as having their own legal identity separate from their members, notably **incorporated companies** and **limited liability partnerships**), which can sue, be sued, own property and contract in their own name. Courts may exceptionally **"pierce the corporate veil"** to hold those behind a company personally liable, e.g. where the company structure was used as a device to evade an existing legal obligation.

**ADR, ODR and out-of-court criminal resolution.** Alternative Dispute Resolution avoids full litigation: **negotiation** (parties/representatives resolve directly), **mediation** (a neutral third party facilitates agreement, non-binding unless a settlement is reached), **conciliation** (similar, with the conciliator more actively suggesting solutions), **arbitration** (a neutral arbitrator makes a binding decision, common in commercial/consumer contracts with an arbitration clause). **Online Dispute Resolution (ODR)** delivers these processes digitally, increasingly used for lower-value consumer and civil claims. Alternatives to prosecuting minor crime include **penalty notices**, **warnings**, **cautions**, and **conditional cautions/diversion** schemes, used where formal prosecution is disproportionate.
