## Test items
1. Explain the doctrine of privity of contract and why Parliament intervened with the Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999. [6 marks]
2. A wedding photographer is engaged by a bride's father to photograph the wedding, with the contract expressly stating that the bride may enforce the photographer's obligations. The photographer fails to attend. Who can sue the photographer? Choose every correct option.
   A. Only the father, because he alone paid and is a party to the contract
   B. Only the bride, because privity prevents the father (who suffered no financial loss) from suing
   C. Both the father (as a party) and the bride (under s1(1)(a) of the Contracts (Rights of Third Parties) Act 1999, being expressly identified with an express right to enforce)
   D. Neither, because a contract for services cannot be enforced by anyone other than the supplier
3. A business contract for the supply of goods is performed by illegally under-declaring the goods' value to evade import duty. One party later refuses to pay the agreed price. Using IRAC, advise on whether the price can be recovered. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 privity means only a party to a contract can sue or be sued on it, even where the contract was made to benefit someone else; B1 this caused unfairness where a third party who was clearly intended to benefit had no means of enforcing the term; B1 the 1999 Act allows a third party to enforce a term in their own right where s1(1)(a) the contract expressly says so, or B1 s1(1)(b) the term purports to confer a benefit on them and the parties did not intend otherwise; B1 the third party must be expressly identified (by name, class or description); B1 the Act preserves any independent right the third party has outside it (e.g. trust or agency).
2. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
3. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = whether illegality in the performance (not formation) of an otherwise lawful contract prevents enforcement/recovery; rule = a contract illegally performed, even if lawful in its formation, may be unenforceable by the party responsible for or knowingly party to the illegal performance, reflecting ex turpi causa, though modern authority weighs the purpose of the prohibition, other public policy considerations and proportionality rather than an automatic bar; application = under-declaring value to evade duty is illegal performance; if both parties knowingly participated, the claimant seeking to enforce may be denied a remedy for the tainted performance, though an innocent party's position may differ; conclusion = the claimant is at real risk of being unable to enforce payment via the courts because of their own participation in the illegality, subject to the proportionality-based modern approach.
