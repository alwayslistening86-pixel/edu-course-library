# S22_Personal_Impact_and_Teamwork - Lesson: Personal impact, working in a team, and setting professional standards

## Goal
The learner explains how to manage personal impact, equality and time effectively, how to work well within a team including using technology, and CILEX's professional standards, appraisal and CPD requirements.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PLS.1 - Personal impact: interpersonal relationships, equality/diversity/respect, time management, networking
- PLS.2 - Working in a team: role in the team, managing workload, impact of technology on teams
- PLS.3 - Setting standards: CILEX professional standards, appraisal and CPD

## How to teach this
Ask: a trainee is juggling three fee earners' work with conflicting deadlines, and one colleague keeps making comments that make a teammate uncomfortable. What should the trainee actually do about each? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### PLS.1 Personal impact: interpersonal relationships, equality/diversity/respect, time management, networking
**Personal impact.** How a trainee presents themselves -- professionally, in writing, and in person -- directly affects clients' and colleagues' confidence in them and in the firm. Key elements: **interpersonal relationships** -- building rapport and trust with colleagues, supervisors and clients through active listening, reliability and appropriate assertiveness (as opposed to passivity or aggression); **equality, diversity and respect** -- treating colleagues and clients fairly and without discrimination, being alert to the protected characteristics under the **Equality Act 2010** (age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage/civil partnership, pregnancy/maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, sexual orientation), understanding both **direct** and **indirect discrimination**, and actively fostering an inclusive working environment rather than merely avoiding overt discrimination; **time management** -- prioritising competing demands using tools such as urgency/importance matrices, realistic deadline-setting, and proactively flagging to a supervisor when a workload genuinely cannot be delivered on time, rather than silently missing deadlines; and **networking** -- building professional relationships within and outside the firm (colleagues, clients, other professionals, CILEX's own membership/CPD events) to support career development, referrals and a wider professional support network, conducted with the same standards of professionalism as any other client-facing activity.

#### PLS.2 Working in a team: role in the team, managing workload, impact of technology on teams
**Working in a team.** Effective teamworking requires understanding one's own **role within the team** (e.g. a trainee's role typically supports fee earners under supervision, with defined limits of authority) and contributing constructively -- sharing workload information, flagging problems early, and supporting colleagues under pressure. **Managing workload within a team context** involves realistic self-assessment of capacity, clear communication with supervisors and colleagues about competing priorities, and escalating conflicts (e.g. two fee earners both requiring urgent work at the same time) rather than silently choosing one over the other. The **impact of technology on teams** is significant: case/practice management systems, shared document/matter management, and remote/hybrid working tools change how teams collaborate, requiring digital competence, awareness of version control and document security, and adaptation of traditional team communication (meetings, handovers) to include digital channels -- while also introducing risks such as miscommunication over less rich channels (e.g. instant messaging) and the need for continued human oversight of automated processes.

#### PLS.3 Setting standards: CILEX professional standards, appraisal and CPD
**Setting standards.** CILEX sets **professional standards** for its members and trainees, reflecting the regulatory principles common across the legal professions -- acting with honesty and integrity, maintaining public trust, competence, and complying with the applicable Code of Conduct (via CILEX Regulation). A trainee is expected to understand and operate within these standards from the outset of training, not only once fully qualified. **Appraisal** is the structured process by which a trainee's performance, development needs and progress are formally reviewed (typically periodically, e.g. annually or more frequently during training), providing an opportunity to receive constructive feedback, agree development goals, and raise any support needs. **Continuing Professional Development (CPD)** is the ongoing, structured maintenance and development of professional knowledge and skills throughout a legal career, required in some form by CILEX (and other legal regulators) to ensure practitioners remain competent and up to date as the law, procedure and best practice evolve; CPD activities can include formal training courses, structured reading, webinars, and reflective learning from practice experience, and are typically expected to be recorded. Both appraisal and CPD reinforce the wider professional obligation -- explicit across the F2, F3 and F5 syllabus -- to recognise the limits of one's own competence and authority and know when to seek supervision.
