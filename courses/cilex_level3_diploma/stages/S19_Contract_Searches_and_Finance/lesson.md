# S19_Contract_Searches_and_Finance - Lesson: Drafting the contract, pre-contract searches and financing a purchase

## Goal
The learner explains how the contract for sale is drafted (including under the Standard Conditions of Sale), the pre-contract searches and enquiries typically made, and how a purchase is commonly financed.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PPC.11 - Sale: drafting the contract and pre-contract package, Standard Conditions of Sale
- PPC.12 - Purchase: pre-contract searches and enquiries
- PPC.13 - Financing a purchase: cash, mortgage, government-backed schemes

## How to teach this
Ask: a buyer's conveyancer wants to know if there are any planned road schemes near the property, and whether it's ever flooded. Which searches answer these questions? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### PPC.11 Sale: drafting the contract and pre-contract package, Standard Conditions of Sale
**Drafting the contract.** The seller's conveyancer normally drafts the contract, typically incorporating the **Law Society's Standard Conditions of Sale** (or, for higher-value/commercial property, the Standard Commercial Property Conditions) by reference, supplemented by **special conditions** addressing matters specific to the transaction (e.g. the completion date, any items included/excluded beyond the standard fittings list, retentions for defects, or agreed apportionments). The Standard Conditions cover matters such as: the **deposit** (traditionally 10% of the price, held by the seller's conveyancer as **stakeholder** -- unable to release it to the seller before completion -- unless the contract specifies the seller's conveyancer holds it as **agent** for the seller, which carries more risk for the buyer); the **date and time for completion**; provisions on the property's physical state and **risk** (commonly passing to the buyer from exchange, making buildings insurance from exchange important); **timed completion, notice to complete** procedures and consequences of delay (interest on the purchase price, and ultimately rescission rights) if a party fails to complete on the contractual date; and standard mechanics for apportioning outgoings. Under **s2 Law of Property (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1989**, the contract must incorporate all agreed terms in one document (or exchanged identical parts) and be signed by or on behalf of each party -- an "**exchange of contracts**" is the conveyancing mechanism by which each party's conveyancer confirms telephonically (per the Law Society's Formulae for exchange) or otherwise that their signed part is released, at which point the contract becomes binding.

#### PPC.12 Purchase: pre-contract searches and enquiries
**Pre-contract searches and enquiries.** The buyer's conveyancer carries out **searches** to uncover matters not apparent from title alone: the **local land charges search (CON29/LLC1)** with the local authority, revealing planning history/permissions, building regulation approvals, road schemes, enforcement notices, conservation area/listed building status and other local land charges; a **water and drainage search**, confirming whether the property is connected to mains water/drainage; an **environmental search**, assessing contaminated land risk, flooding risk and other environmental hazards that could affect value, insurability or lending; a **coal mining search** (in coal mining areas) or other regional mineral searches (e.g. tin, brine); a **Chancel repair liability search/insurance** consideration (historic liability for some parishioners to contribute to church chancel repairs); and a **company search** where the seller is a company (confirming good standing and any registered charges). **Pre-contract enquiries** are additional questions raised directly with the seller's conveyancer (supplementing the seller's own disclosures in the Property Information Form/TA6) about matters such as disputes, notices received, guarantees for works carried out, boundary responsibility, and shared facilities/access. The buyer's conveyancer reviews all search results and enquiry replies before advising the client whether it is safe to proceed to exchange, or whether further investigation, a retention, indemnity insurance, or renegotiation of price/terms is needed.

#### PPC.13 Financing a purchase: cash, mortgage, government-backed schemes
**Financing a purchase.** A purchase may be funded by **cash** (no lender involved, simplifying the transaction but still requiring the usual AML source-of-funds checks) or by **mortgage** -- a loan secured by a legal charge over the property, typically requiring a **valuation** (for the lender's purposes only, not a structural survey) and formal **mortgage offer** setting out the loan amount, term, interest rate/type and any conditions, which the buyer's conveyancer (often also acting for the lender under a joint-representation arrangement, subject to conflict rules and lender instructions such as the **UK Finance Mortgage Lenders' Handbook**) must review and report on before exchange, ensuring funds will be available in time for completion. **Government-backed schemes** exist to widen access to home ownership, varying over time and by nation (England/Wales) -- historically including **Help to Buy equity loan** schemes (largely closed to new applicants in England) and **shared ownership** (a buyer purchases a percentage share of a property, paying rent on the remainder, with an option to "staircase" and buy further shares over time, typically via a housing association landlord); current scheme availability and eligibility criteria change, so should always be checked against a live government source before advising a client. Whatever the funding source, the buyer's conveyancer must confirm funds (deposit and balance) will be in place in time for exchange and completion, and, for mortgage funding, satisfy any conditions in the mortgage offer before drawdown.
