## Test items
1. Which search would specifically reveal whether a property is affected by a planned road-widening scheme? Choose every correct option.
   A. Water and drainage search
   B. Local land charges search (CON29/LLC1)
   C. Coal mining search
   D. Company search
2. Explain the significance of risk passing to the buyer on exchange of contracts under the Standard Conditions of Sale, and what a conveyancer should advise the buyer to do about it. [6 marks]
3. A buyer instructs a conveyancer to purchase a house near a former colliery, funded partly by a mortgage and partly by cash savings. Using IRAC, advise on the searches that should be carried out and the steps needed regarding the mortgage before exchange. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [6] B2 under the Standard Conditions, risk in the property (e.g. damage by fire or other event) commonly passes to the buyer from the point of exchange, even though legal ownership does not transfer until completion; B2 this means the buyer bears the practical/financial risk of loss or damage occurring between exchange and completion, even though they do not yet own the property; B2 the conveyancer should advise the buyer to have buildings insurance in place from exchange (not merely from completion) to protect against this risk.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = which pre-contract searches are appropriate given the location and mixed funding, and what must be done regarding the mortgage before exchange; rule = standard searches include local land charges, water/drainage and environmental searches, with a coal mining search specifically indicated near a former colliery; a lender will require a valuation and a formal mortgage offer, which the conveyancer must review (often also acting for the lender per the Mortgage Lenders' Handbook) and ensure any conditions are satisfied and funds will be available in time for exchange/completion; application = given the former colliery, a coal mining search is clearly indicated (mining subsidence/ground stability risk) alongside the standard local land charges, drainage and environmental searches; the conveyancer must obtain and review the mortgage offer, confirm no unresolved conditions, and ensure the lender's funds (together with the buyer's cash) will be available in time, before advising the client it is safe to exchange; conclusion = advise the buyer that a coal mining search plus the standard package of searches should be undertaken, and that exchange should not take place until the mortgage offer is unconditional/its conditions satisfied and the combined mortgage and cash funds are confirmed as available.
