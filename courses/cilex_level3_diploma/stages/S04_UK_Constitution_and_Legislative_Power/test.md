## Test items
1. Explain the difference between substantive and procedural ultra vires as grounds for challenging delegated legislation. [4 marks]
2. A government minister makes a statutory instrument under powers in an Act that only permits regulations about 'minor technical adjustments' to a licensing scheme, but the instrument actually introduces a significant new criminal offence. A trade body wants to challenge it. Using IRAC, advise. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B2 substantive ultra vires: the content/substance of the delegated legislation exceeds the scope of the power granted by the enabling Act; B2 procedural ultra vires: a required procedure set out in the enabling Act (e.g. a consultation requirement) was not followed in making it.
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether the statutory instrument can be challenged as ultra vires; rule = delegated legislation must stay within the scope of the power the enabling Act grants; substantive ultra vires arises where its content exceeds that scope; conclusion routes through judicial review; application = introducing a significant new criminal offence goes well beyond 'minor technical adjustments', so the instrument arguably exceeds the enabling power; conclusion = the trade body has a good argument that the instrument is substantively ultra vires and could seek judicial review, likely remedy a quashing order.
