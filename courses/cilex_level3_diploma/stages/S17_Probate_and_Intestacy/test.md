## Test items
1. A person dies intestate, survived by a long-term cohabiting partner (never married) and no children or other relatives who can be traced. Who is entitled to the estate under the intestacy rules? Choose every correct option.
   A. The cohabiting partner takes the whole estate automatically, as intestacy treats long-term cohabitants like spouses
   B. The cohabiting partner has no automatic entitlement under intestacy; the estate would pass further down the statutory order (e.g. parents, siblings) or ultimately as bona vacantia to the Crown, though the partner may be able to claim under the Inheritance (Provision for Family and Dependants) Act 1975
   C. The cohabiting partner automatically receives the statutory legacy only
   D. The estate automatically passes to the Crown regardless of any surviving relatives
2. Explain the difference between an executor and an administrator, and the grant each applies for. [6 marks]
3. A person dies without a will, survived by a spouse and two adult children from an earlier marriage, and separately had made a property and financial affairs LPA some years earlier appointing their spouse as attorney. Using IRAC, advise the spouse on (a) their entitlement to the estate and (b) whether the LPA can be used to deal with the deceased's assets now. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [6] B2 an executor is appointed by the deceased's valid will and derives authority from the will itself, applying for a grant of probate to confirm that authority and deal with most estate assets; B2 an administrator is appointed by the court (not the deceased) where there is no valid will, or a will with no effective executor, applying for a grant of letters of administration (or letters of administration with will annexed, where there is a valid will but no acting executor); B2 entitlement to apply as administrator follows a statutory order of priority broadly mirroring intestacy beneficial entitlement.
3. [12] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority, apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion advising the client. Credit: issue = the spouse's entitlement under intestacy given surviving children, and the effect of death on an LPA; rule = on intestacy with a spouse and children, the spouse takes personal chattels, the statutory legacy, and half the remaining estate absolutely, with the other half to the children under the statutory trusts; an LPA is a tool for use during the donor's lifetime incapacity only and automatically ends on the donor's death, at which point a grant of representation is required; application = the spouse does not take the whole estate given the surviving children, but takes chattels, the statutory legacy and half the remainder, with the children sharing the other half; the LPA cannot be used at all now that the donor has died -- the spouse (or another eligible person, following the statutory priority for letters of administration, which favours a surviving spouse) must instead apply for a grant of letters of administration to administer the estate; conclusion = advise the spouse on their statutory entitlement (not the whole estate) and that they must apply for a grant of letters of administration, since the now-defunct LPA gives no authority over estate assets after death.
