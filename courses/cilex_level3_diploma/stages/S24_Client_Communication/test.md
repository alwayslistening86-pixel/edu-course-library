## Test items
1. A trainee sends a client a standard letter before action without checking whether the amount claimed and the deadline dates in the template are correct for this client's specific matter. What is the most significant risk? Choose every correct option.
   A. The letter will automatically be treated as invalid by the court
   B. The letter may contain incorrect or irrelevant content because it was not properly tailored to the client's specific facts, undermining the client's position and falling short of the competence expected
   C. There is no risk, since precedent letters are pre-approved by the firm and never need checking
   D. The client will assume the letter was not really written by a lawyer
2. Explain why active listening and checking understanding matter particularly when communicating verbally with a client who is going through a stressful situation (e.g. bereavement or a dispute). [6 marks]
3. Explain the difference between verbal, non-verbal and written communication, giving one example of each in a legal practice context, and one risk specific to written communication that does not apply in the same way to a verbal conversation. [8 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [6] B2 clients contacting a lawyer during a stressful event may find it harder to absorb or process information, or may not feel able to ask for clarification themselves; B2 active listening (giving full attention, not interrupting, picking up on cues about what the client actually needs or is worried about) helps ensure the trainee correctly understands the client's real concerns and instructions; B2 checking understanding, e.g. by summarising back what has been said/agreed, reduces the risk of miscommunication and reassures the client they have been heard and understood correctly, which is particularly important where emotional stress may affect how information is received; B2 appropriate empathy alongside professionalism supports client trust and a more accurate, complete exchange of information -- any 3 for full marks.
3. [8] B2 verbal communication is spoken exchange, e.g. a telephone call updating a client on progress, allowing immediate clarification; B2 non-verbal communication is conveyed through tone, body language, eye contact and appearance, e.g. a calm, attentive manner in a face-to-face meeting reassuring an anxious client; B2 written communication is text-based, e.g. a client care letter or attendance note, which must be clear, accurate and appropriately formal; B2 a risk specific to written communication: it creates a permanent record that can be relied on later (including in evidence or a complaint/claim), so inaccuracies or poorly chosen wording have lasting consequences in a way a passing remark in conversation typically does not, making careful drafting particularly important.
