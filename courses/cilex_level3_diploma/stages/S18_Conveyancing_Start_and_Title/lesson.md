# S18_Conveyancing_Start_and_Title - Lesson: The conveyancing transaction overview, taking instructions, and deducing/investigating title

## Goal
The learner describes the stages of a conveyancing transaction from instruction to post-completion, the initial steps and checklists for taking instructions from seller and buyer, and how registered title is deduced and investigated.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PPC.8 - Overview of the conveyancing transaction: stages from instruction to post-completion
- PPC.9 - Taking instructions and initial steps: seller and buyer advice, checklists
- PPC.10 - Deducing and investigating title: registered title, classes of title, requisitions

## How to teach this
Ask: a buyer's conveyancer receives the seller's title information from HM Land Registry. What are they actually checking for, and what happens if something looks wrong? Teach each topic as: the rule (the statute section or case authority, stated precisely), why it exists (the policy/purpose CILEX's own module text gives), and how it is applied to facts. CILEX's Foundation-stage exams (F1-F4) are 60 multiple-choice and short 'automated' (fill-in/matching) questions testing precise, exam-ready knowledge -- push the learner to state rules exactly (case names, section numbers, dates) rather than paraphrase loosely. F5 and F6 also include practical scenario tasks (a communication/drafting exercise for F5; producing research documents for F6) -- give the learner realistic client scenarios and paralegal tasks, not just questions about the skill in the abstract. Throughout, reinforce that a CILEX trainee at this level must recognise the limits of their own authority and know when to escalate to a supervisor -- this is an explicit learning outcome in F2, F3 and F5, not incidental. Law changes: this course states the law as understood to be current in September 2026 (England and Wales); flag to the learner that specific figures (SDLT/LTT thresholds, IHT nil-rate bands, fee levels) and any pending reform (e.g. the Bill of Rights Bill, which CILEX's own F1 material lists as syllabus content but which had not been enacted -- the Human Rights Act 1998 remains in force) should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, cilex.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### PPC.8 Overview of the conveyancing transaction: stages from instruction to post-completion
**Overview of the conveyancing transaction.** A typical freehold sale/purchase proceeds through broadly sequential stages: (1) **taking instructions** from the client and opening the file (including AML/client due diligence); (2) the **pre-contract stage** -- the seller's conveyancer prepares and issues the draft contract and supporting title/property information (the "**pre-contract package**"), the buyer's conveyancer raises pre-contract enquiries and carries out searches, and, if the buyer needs one, a mortgage offer is obtained; (3) **reporting to the client** on title, searches, the mortgage offer and the contract terms, followed by **exchange of contracts** (the point the transaction becomes legally binding, with a deposit paid and a fixed completion date agreed); (4) the **pre-completion stage** -- final searches/pre-completion checks, preparing the transfer deed (TR1) and, for the seller, a redemption statement to pay off any existing mortgage; (5) **completion** -- the purchase money is transferred and the transaction completes, keys are released; (6) **post-completion** -- paying Stamp Duty Land Tax/Land Transaction Tax, registering the transfer (and any new mortgage) at HM Land Registry, and (for the seller) redeeming their mortgage and accounting to the client for net proceeds. Until **exchange of contracts**, either party can withdraw without legal liability (subject to costs incurred); this is often summarised as the principle that "the deal is not binding until exchange".

#### PPC.9 Taking instructions and initial steps: seller and buyer advice, checklists
**Taking instructions and initial steps.** At the outset, the conveyancer must: verify the client's identity and carry out client due diligence (money laundering regulations); confirm who the client is and obtain clear instructions on the property, price, and any special requirements (e.g. a related sale and purchase needing simultaneous exchange/completion, a specific moving date, or funding arrangements); send a **client care letter** setting out the scope of retainer, costs, complaints procedure and regulatory information; check for **conflicts of interest** (e.g. acting for both buyer and seller, or for co-buyers with differing interests -- generally to be avoided or approached with caution and full disclosure/consent where permitted); and, for a seller, obtain the **title documents**/official copies and complete standard **protocol forms** (e.g. a Property Information Form/TA6 disclosing matters such as boundaries, disputes, notices, guarantees and utilities, and a Fittings and Contents Form/TA10 specifying what is included in the sale) to allow the pre-contract package to be prepared; for a buyer, discuss **survey options** (a basic valuation survey commissioned by the lender for mortgage purposes only, a HomeBuyer Report, or a full/building survey for older or unusual properties) and confirm how the purchase will be **funded** (see S19) and whether a mortgage offer is needed before exchange. A checklist approach reduces the risk of a missed step causing delay or liability later in the transaction.

#### PPC.10 Deducing and investigating title: registered title, classes of title, requisitions
**Deducing and investigating title.** For **registered land**, the seller "deduces" title by supplying **official copies of the register** (comprising the **property register** -- describing the land and any rights benefiting it, e.g. easements; the **proprietorship register** -- naming the registered proprietor(s) and the **class of title**; and the **charges register** -- listing encumbrances such as mortgages, restrictive covenants and other third-party rights burdening the land) together with the **title plan**. **Classes of title** reflect the strength of the guarantee HMLR gives: **absolute title** (the best class, for freehold or leasehold, guaranteeing the registered proprietor's ownership subject only to entries on the register, overriding interests and, for leasehold, matters affecting the landlord's title); **good leasehold title** (guarantees the leasehold interest but not that the landlord validly had the right to grant the lease, because the landlord's title was not investigated/proved at first registration); **qualified title** (granted where a specific defect or unresolved doubt in title is identified and excluded from the guarantee); and **possessory title** (granted where ownership is based on possession rather than a documented title, e.g. under adverse possession, or where original title deeds are lost). The buyer's conveyancer investigates title by carefully checking the official copies against the contract, raising **requisitions on title** -- formal written queries or requirements addressed to the seller's conveyancer to resolve doubts, obtain undertakings (e.g. to discharge a mortgage on completion) or clarify entries (e.g. the effect of a restriction or covenant) -- and confirming there are no unexpected adverse entries (e.g. undisclosed charges, restrictive covenants inconsistent with the buyer's intended use, or notices protecting a third party's interest) before proceeding to exchange.
