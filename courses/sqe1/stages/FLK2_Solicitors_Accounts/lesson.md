# FLK2_Solicitors_Accounts - Lesson: Solicitors Accounts

## Goal
The learner correctly classifies client versus business money, operates the client account, corrects breaches of the SRA Accounts Rules, and records transactions accurately, at day-one-competence standard, and answers SQE1-style single-best-answer questions on all of it.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.1 - Client money: definition and requirement to pay into client account: learn and apply: client money
- ACC.2 - Client account operation and withdrawals: learn and apply: client account operation and withdrawals
- ACC.3 - Separation of client and business money: learn and apply: separation of client and business money
- ACC.4 - Interest on client money: learn and apply: interest on client money
- ACC.5 - Correcting breaches of the SRA Accounts Rules: learn and apply: correcting breaches of the accounts rules
- ACC.6 - Ledger records, reconciliation and billing (agency/principal disbursements): learn and apply: ledger records, reconciliation and billing
- ACC.7 - Accountants' reports and record retention: learn and apply: accountants' reports and record retention

## How to teach this
Open with a scenario: a firm receives a cheque from a client for a house purchase that includes both the purchase price and the firm's estimated fee, and separately receives a cheque that turns out to be entirely for costs already billed. Ask the learner to decide, before any teaching, which account each sum belongs in and why - that single classification decision (client money or the firm's own money) is the foundation every other rule in this subject builds on.

#### ACC.1 Client money: definition and requirement to pay into client account
Client money is money held or received by a solicitor for a client, or for a specified third party (such as a client's mortgage lender or the other side's costs), relating to regulated services - this includes advance payments for costs not yet billed, and money held as trustee where the solicitor also acts in the trust. It must be paid promptly into a client account, except money that is entirely for costs already billed to the client (which may be paid into the business account, or into the client account and transferred out promptly). Teach the learner the core test: is any part of this sum still "held for" the client rather than earned by the firm.

#### ACC.2 Client account operation and withdrawals
A client account may only be used to hold client money (plus a small, limited amount of the firm's own money where necessary, e.g. to open the account or maintain a minimum balance) and must be clearly designated and held at an authorised bank or building society. Withdrawals from a client account are only permitted for the purpose for which the money is held, or as properly required by the client's instructions, and must not leave the account in debit for a particular client (no using one client's money to cover a shortfall for another). Teach the learner that every withdrawal needs a proper authority and purpose before it happens, not after.

#### ACC.3 Separation of client and business money
The SRA Accounts Rules require strict separation: client money must never be mixed with the firm's own (business) money, and the firm's own money (once costs are billed and the client informed) must be transferred out of the client account within a reasonable time, not left sitting there. Mixed receipts (part client money, part costs) must be split correctly between the two accounts, or the whole sum held as client money until it can be split. Teach the learner this is a bright-line rule: any doubt about classification is resolved by treating the money as client money until it is properly billed and transferred.

#### ACC.4 Interest on client money
A firm must account to the client for a fair sum of interest on client money held, unless the client agrees otherwise (e.g. in a written arrangement, or where the amount/period involved makes interest negligible and this is disclosed) - firms typically operate their own interest policy setting thresholds, published and applied consistently. Teach the learner that "must a firm pay interest" is generally yes by default, subject to a genuine, disclosed agreement or policy to the contrary, not a discretionary favour to the client.

#### ACC.5 Correcting breaches of the SRA Accounts Rules
Any breach (e.g. money wrongly paid from or into the client account, a client account left in debit, money not transferred out promptly once billed) must be corrected promptly on discovery, including replacing any shortfall immediately, typically from the firm's own resources. Breaches must be recorded, and material breaches reported as required to the SRA. Teach the learner the recurring day-one skill: spot that a breach has occurred, identify what the correcting entry must be to restore the client account to where it should be, and recognise the obligation to report where material.

#### ACC.6 Ledger records, reconciliation and billing (agency/principal disbursements)
Firms must keep accurate, up-to-date client ledgers for each client/matter (recording every receipt and payment) and a separate business account ledger, reconciled against the bank statements at least every five weeks, with any discrepancy investigated and corrected promptly. Disbursements are recorded as agency payments (the firm has already paid a third party, e.g. Land Registry fees, and is recovering that exact sum) or, in limited circumstances, as principal disbursements where VAT treatment can differ. On billing, costs and any VAT are transferred from client to business account only once a bill has been delivered or the client has been notified in writing. Teach the learner to check billing has genuinely happened before treating money as earned.

#### ACC.7 Accountants' reports and record retention
A firm holding client money must, in most circumstances, obtain an accountant's report (from a qualified reporting accountant) confirming compliance with the Accounts Rules, submitted within six months of the end of the relevant accounting period, unless an exemption applies (e.g. all client money held is only for costs, or the average balance falls below the small-firm exemption threshold). Accounting records must be retained for at least six years, available for inspection by the SRA on request. Teach the learner that the accountant's report is an external check on exactly the rules taught in this stage, not a separate topic.

## Explicitly not here
Full double-entry bookkeeping mechanics (debits and credits across ledger columns) beyond the level of classifying and correcting a transaction are outside day-one competence for this course's purposes; the standard is issue-spotting the correct classification, entry and correction, not producing a full set of accounts.
