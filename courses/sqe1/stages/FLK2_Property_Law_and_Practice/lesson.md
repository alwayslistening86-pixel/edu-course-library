# FLK2_Property_Law_and_Practice - Lesson: Property Law and Practice

## Goal
The learner runs a residential or commercial freehold or leasehold transaction from title investigation through exchange, completion and post-completion, spots lender, planning and tax issues, and answers SQE1-style single-best-answer questions on all of it, at day-one-competence standard.

## How to teach this
Open with a scenario: a couple is buying a freehold house with a mortgage, selling their existing leasehold flat at the same time, and the seller of the house is also negotiating a new business lease for premises they will use to run a shop. Ask the learner to map, in order, every step from taking instructions to registration - that sequencing (title, searches, finance, contract, exchange, completion, post-completion) is what SQE1 tests, because most distractors work by putting a real rule at the wrong stage of the transaction.

#### PROP.1 Freehold title investigation (registered and unregistered)
Registered title is investigated from the Land Registry entries: the proprietorship register (who owns it, any restrictions), the property register (a description of the land and any rights benefiting it, e.g. easements), and the charges register (encumbrances burdening it, e.g. mortgages, restrictive covenants, notices of leases). Unregistered title is investigated by an epitome of title: a root of title at least 15 years old plus every document in the chain since, checked for an unbroken chain of ownership, and searched against the Land Charges Register for registered land charges (e.g. a puisne mortgage as a Class C(i) charge, or a restrictive covenant as a Class D(ii) charge) which bind a purchaser even without notice. Teach the learner to state which system applies before analysing what it reveals.

#### PROP.2 Pre-contract searches, enquiries and the Conveyancing Protocol
Standard pre-contract searches include a local land charges search and enquiries of the local authority (LLC1/CON29, revealing planning history, road adoption, enforcement notices), a water and drainage search, an environmental search (contamination, flood risk), and, where relevant, coal mining or other regional searches. Pre-contract enquiries (e.g. Commercial Property Standard Enquiries) ask the seller about boundaries, disputes, guarantees and services. The Law Society Conveyancing Protocol sets out a standard sequence and standard forms (e.g. TA6 Property Information Form, TA10 Fittings and Contents Form) for residential transactions to speed up and standardise the process. Teach the learner that a search or enquiry answered "yes" or negatively is a live issue to resolve before exchange, not after.

#### PROP.3 Mortgage finance and lender requirements
A buyer's solicitor acting also for the lender (common on standard residential purchases, subject to the Lenders' Handbook / CML Handbook rules) must report to the lender on title defects, check the property provides adequate security, and ensure the mortgage offer's conditions are satisfied before completion, avoiding a conflict of interest and ceasing to act for the lender if a serious conflict arises. Redemption of any existing mortgage on a sale must be arranged so the seller's lender receives a redemption statement and releases its charge on completion. Teach the learner to distinguish acting for buyer and lender, and buyer and seller (with the seller's own conveyancer never also acting for the buyer).

#### PROP.4 Contract, exchange and completion (freehold)
The contract (commonly on the Standard Conditions or Standard Commercial Property Conditions of Sale) sets out the price, deposit (usually 10%, though this can be negotiated), the property description, and any special conditions. Exchange of contracts (by telephone, using one of the Law Society formulae, once both signed parts are held by each side's solicitor) makes the contract binding; before exchange, either party can withdraw without breach. Completion is the actual transfer of the legal estate against payment of the balance of the price, followed by registration at the Land Registry (for registered land, or first registration for unregistered land triggered by the sale). Teach the learner: no binding obligation before exchange, no legal title passes before completion and registration.

#### PROP.5 Remedies for delayed completion
Time is not automatically "of the essence" for completion unless the contract expressly makes it so, or a notice to complete is served after the contractual completion date has passed (giving the defaulting party typically 10 working days to complete). Once time is of the essence, the innocent party can terminate and claim damages, forfeit or recover the deposit, and claim interest for the delay under the contract's default interest provisions. Teach the learner to check first whether time is of the essence, since that single fact controls which remedy is available for a late completion.

#### PROP.6 Lease grant, assignment and key lease covenants
Granting a lease creates a new legal estate from the landlord; assigning a lease transfers an existing tenant's remaining term to a new tenant, usually requiring landlord's consent (often "not to be unreasonably withheld or delayed" under a qualified covenant) and frequently an Authorised Guarantee Agreement making the outgoing tenant guarantee the incoming tenant's performance. Key lease covenants to check are rent, repair (and its standard - "good and substantial repair" is stricter than mere "tenantable"), user (permitted use of the premises), alienation (assignment, underletting, charging), and insurance. Teach the learner to identify which covenant a fact pattern raises before deciding on breach or remedy.

#### PROP.7 Business lease security of tenure (Landlord and Tenant Act 1954 Part II)
A business tenancy within the Act (occupied by the tenant for business purposes) continues automatically after the contractual term ends unless it is validly "contracted out" (following the prescribed pre-tenancy notice and declaration procedure) or ends under one of the Act's specific grounds. A tenant can apply for a new tenancy; a landlord can oppose only on one of the seven statutory grounds in s.30 (e.g. persistent rent arrears, breach of repairing obligations, the landlord's intention to redevelop or to occupy the premises itself), some of which entitle the tenant to statutory compensation if it succeeds. Teach the learner to check first whether the lease is "inside" or "contracted out of" the Act.

#### PROP.8 Planning law basics
"Development" is statutorily defined (Town and Country Planning Act 1990) as the carrying out of building, engineering, mining or other operations, or a material change of use of land or buildings; some works and changes are excluded or fall within permitted development rights and need no separate planning permission. Building regulations control (distinct from planning permission) governs construction standards - structural safety, fire safety, energy efficiency - and requires separate approval. Breach can lead to an enforcement notice from the local planning authority, with time limits and rights of appeal. Teach the learner to distinguish "does this need planning permission" from "does this satisfy building regulations" as two separate questions.

#### PROP.9 Property taxation: SDLT/LTT, VAT, CGT
Stamp Duty Land Tax (England and Northern Ireland) or Land Transaction Tax (Wales) is charged on the buyer on the chargeable consideration for land transactions, on a banded/progressive basis, with a return and payment deadline after completion. VAT may apply to commercial property (some supplies are exempt unless the seller has "opted to tax", making the sale standard-rated) but is not normally charged on residential property. Capital Gains Tax is charged on an individual seller's gain on disposal of an investment or business property (private residence relief usually exempts a main home). Teach the learner the recurring issue-spot: which tax, on whom, and does an exemption or election change the answer.

## Explicitly not here
Detailed SDLT/LTT banding calculations and full commercial lease drafting are outside day-one competence; the standard here is issue-spotting the correct tax, procedural step or covenant, not producing a full computation or a drafted clause.
