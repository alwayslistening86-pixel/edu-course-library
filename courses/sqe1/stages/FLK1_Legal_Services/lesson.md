# FLK1_Legal_Services - Lesson: Legal Services

## Goal
The learner applies the SRA's regulatory framework, anti-money-laundering obligations, equality and financial services obligations, and identifies the correct funding option for a client's matter, at day-one-competence standard.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LGS.1 - SRA regulatory principles, reserved legal activities, professional indemnity insurance: learn and apply: sra regulatory principles
- LGS.2 - Equality Act 2010 obligations: learn and apply: equality act obligations
- LGS.3 - Anti-money laundering: scope, reporting, POCA 2002 offences, due diligence: learn and apply: anti-money laundering
- LGS.4 - Financial services regulation and FSMA 2000: learn and apply: financial services regulation
- LGS.5 - Funding options: retainer, CFAs, DBAs, legal aid, third-party funding, ATE insurance: learn and apply: funding options for legal services

## How to teach this
Open with: a new client wants to instruct the firm to help sell a business for cash, is vague about the source of the funds, and asks about payment options because they cannot afford a large fee upfront. Have the learner identify every regulatory obligation this triggers before teaching each item - this is deliberately the same "spot every issue in one paragraph" structure used throughout FLK1, and mirrors how Legal Services questions are actually set.

#### LGS.1 SRA regulatory principles, reserved legal activities, professional indemnity insurance
The SRA regulates solicitors and firms through a set of overarching Principles (e.g. acting with independence, integrity, in the best interests of each client, and upholding public trust) applied through risk-based, outcomes-focused regulation rather than prescriptive rulebooks alone. Reserved legal activities (e.g. conducting litigation, exercising rights of audience, administering oaths, probate activities) may only be carried out by authorised persons; carrying one out without authorisation is a criminal offence. Firms must maintain professional indemnity insurance meeting minimum terms and conditions to protect clients if things go wrong, and other regulated providers of legal services (e.g. licensed conveyancers, alternative business structures) sit alongside solicitors in the wider regulatory landscape.

#### LGS.2 Equality Act 2010 obligations
The Equality Act 2010 prohibits direct and indirect discrimination, harassment and victimisation in the provision of services (including legal services) based on protected characteristics (e.g. age, disability, race, sex, religion or belief, sexual orientation). Solicitors and firms must comply with the Act both as employers and as service providers to clients, and the SRA's own regulatory requirements reinforce this obligation as a professional, not merely a general legal, duty. Teach the learner to recognise Equality Act issues both in client-facing conduct and in a firm's own employment practices.

#### LGS.3 Anti-money laundering: scope, reporting, POCA 2002 offences, due diligence
Anti-money laundering legislation (implementing international standards) requires firms to apply customer due diligence (verifying client identity and, where relevant, the source of funds) before or during certain transactions, with enhanced due diligence for higher-risk situations. A solicitor who, in the course of practice, becomes suspicious that a transaction may involve the proceeds of crime must consider their reporting obligations - to the firm's Money Laundering Reporting Officer in the first instance, who reports on to the National Crime Agency - and must not "tip off" the client. The Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 creates direct-involvement offences (e.g. concealing, transferring or acquiring criminal property) and non-direct-involvement offences (e.g. failure to disclose a suspicion in the regulated sector), with defined defences (e.g. authorised disclosure before acting). Teach the learner that a vague or evasive answer about the source of funds is itself a classic exam trigger for this item.

#### LGS.4 Financial services regulation and FSMA 2000
The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and related secondary legislation create a regulatory framework requiring authorisation to carry on specified regulated activities in relation to specified investments, with a general prohibition on carrying on a regulated activity in the UK without authorisation or exemption. Solicitors' firms are not usually directly authorised but may be exempt for certain incidental activities connected to their legal work; a solicitor must be able to recognise when advice or assistance strays into a specified activity in relation to a specified investment, since this can require referral to an authorised firm. Teach the learner this is primarily an issue-spotting skill: recognising the trigger, not applying the full regulatory regime.

#### LGS.5 Funding options: private retainer, CFAs, DBAs, legal aid, third-party funding, legal expenses insurance
A private retainer (client pays the firm directly, by hourly rate or fixed fee) is the default; a conditional fee agreement ("no win, no fee") provides for a success fee if the case succeeds, payable only in specified circumstances; a damages-based agreement takes the solicitor's fee as a percentage of damages recovered, subject to regulatory caps in certain proceedings. Civil legal aid remains available, subject to means and merits testing, for limited categories of case. Third-party litigation funding (an unconnected funder finances the claim in exchange for a share of any recovery) and after-the-event/legal expenses insurance (covering the risk of having to pay the other side's costs) are also relevant funding routes. Teach the learner to match a client's actual financial position and case type to the realistic funding option(s), since more than one may be genuinely available.

## Explicitly not here
The detailed SRA Code of Conduct provisions on individual conduct rules (e.g. specific client care letter content) are assumed background professional conduct knowledge rather than itemised separately here; this stage focuses on the regulatory structure, AML, equality, financial services and funding content statements the SRA specifies.
