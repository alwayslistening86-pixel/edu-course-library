# A-level History (Edexcel 9HI0): Route B - Tudor England, the German Reformation and Rebellion under the Tudors - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
Paper 1: two short interpretation-extract questions (20 marks total) plus two essays chosen from three on the 1B themes (20 marks each). Paper 2: two essays chosen from the 2B.1 key topics (20 marks each). Paper 3: one source-utility question (20 marks) plus two essays chosen from three on Topic 31 (20 marks each). Questions are original, in Edexcel's style; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests. The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest.

## 8 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. [Paper 1, Section A] Two historians' extracts (not reproduced here; the tutor supplies two short original extracts, one arguing Elizabeth's government remained firmly in control throughout the 1590s, one arguing it faced a genuine crisis) are provided. Using both extracts and your own knowledge, evaluate which offers the more convincing view of the state of Elizabethan government in its last years. [20 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section B] 'The break with Rome, 1533-34, was the most significant religious change of the period 1509-88.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]
3. [Paper 1, Section B] 'Popular resistance to Tudor rule declined after 1570 mainly because of more effective government control.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]
4. [Paper 2] 'The printing press was the most important reason for the rapid spread of Luther's ideas, 1517-21.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]
5. [Paper 2] 'The Peace of Augsburg, 1555, was primarily the result of Charles V's political weakness rather than the strength of Lutheran belief.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]
6. [Paper 3, Section A] Source: a royal proclamation issued shortly after the suppression of Kett's Rebellion (1549), condemning the rebels as traitors and warning against further unlawful assembly. With reference to its provenance, content and your own knowledge, evaluate the value of this source for an enquiry into the government's view of the causes of the rebellion. [20 marks]
7. [Paper 3, Section B] 'Religious motivation was more important than political or dynastic motivation in Tudor rebellions.' Assess the validity of this view, with reference to rebellions from across the period 1485-1603. [20 marks]
8. [Paper 3, Section B] 'Government responses to rebellion became consistently more effective across the period 1485-1603.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]
