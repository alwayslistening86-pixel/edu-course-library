## Test items
1. Explain how the position of English Catholics changed between 1559 and the 1580s. [6 marks]
2. 'The Elizabethan religious settlement succeeded because it was deliberately ambiguous.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 1559: settlement left ambiguity clergy and laity could initially work within/around; B1 recusancy fines from the 1560s-70s made non-attendance increasingly costly; B1 the papal bull Regnans in Excelsis (1570) made loyalty to Elizabeth and to Rome appear incompatible; B1 Jesuit/seminary missions from the 1570s tried to sustain Catholic practice but were treated as a security threat; B1 the Northern Earls' Revolt (1569-70) discredited open Catholic resistance and pushed many nobles to conformity; B1 by the 1580s Catholicism increasingly a persecuted, minority, often gentry-based recusant community rather than a majority default.

Credit: deliberate ambiguity over Communion wording, 'Supreme Governor' concession, retention of some traditional ceremony alongside Protestant doctrine, all aimed at broad acceptability; against this - the settlement also succeeded because of firm enforcement (fines, deprivation of nonconforming clergy), Elizabeth's long, stable reign giving it time to embed, and the weakening of the Catholic alternative through recusancy law and the loss of aristocratic leadership after 1569-70; a strong answer weighs ambiguity against enforcement and circumstance.
