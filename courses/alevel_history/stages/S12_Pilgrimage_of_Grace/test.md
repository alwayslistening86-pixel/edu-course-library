## Test items
1. Explain why the Pilgrimage of Grace, despite its scale, ultimately failed to achieve its aims. [6 marks]
2. 'Religious grievance was the most important cause of the Pilgrimage of Grace, 1536-37.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 Aske and the Pilgrims trusted Norfolk's negotiated promises of pardon and a future Parliament rather than pressing their military advantage; B1 the rising was framed as loyal petition against 'evil counsellors', not rebellion against the King, limiting its own willingness to use force decisively; B1 Henry never intended to honour the concessions once the immediate threat had dispersed; B1 Bigod's unauthorised rising (1537) gave Henry a pretext to abandon the pardon entirely; B1 lack of artillery and a swollen river prevented the Pilgrims forcing a decisive early battle while they held the advantage; B1 exemplary executions of Aske and other leaders removed the movement's leadership once its bargaining position had collapsed.
2. [20] Mark out of 20 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing: Level 1 (1-4) generalised/descriptive; Level 2 (5-8) limited explanation/analysis or mostly narrative with some analytical comment; Level 3 (9-12) explains relevant factors with some support but uneven coverage, reaching a partially supported judgement; Level 4 (13-16) well-organised, explains a range of factors with good evidence and a supported judgement; Level 5 (17-20) fully focused, weighs a range of factors with precise, wide-ranging evidence, sustained analysis and a clearly substantiated judgement. Credit: the immediate trigger (dissolution of the smaller monasteries) and wider fear of further religious change as strong evidence for religion; against/alongside this, economic grievances (taxation, enclosure, rents) and Northern political/regional resentment of Cromwell and 'new men' as contributing and overlapping causes; a strong answer weighs how far religious grievance provided the unifying language/cause even where individual participants' motives were mixed, reaching a supported judgement.
