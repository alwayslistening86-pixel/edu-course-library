## Test items
1. Explain why Charles V was unable to suppress Lutheranism in Germany, despite defeating the Schmalkaldic League militarily in 1547. [6 marks]
2. 'Political circumstances, rather than the strength of Lutheran belief, best explain the survival of Lutheranism by 1555.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]
3. Essay technique for Paper 2 (2 x 20-mark essays in 1h30). Plan before writing: identify 3-4 factors/themes the question requires; for each, a paragraph with a clear point, precise supporting knowledge (names, dates, specific detail), and explicit analysis of *how* it answers the question (not just narrative); finish with a judgement that directly answers the question set, weighing the factors rather than just summarising them. State one common error to avoid in these essays. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 his attention/resources were repeatedly diverted by war with France and the Ottoman threat, limiting sustained action against the Lutherans; B1 even Catholic princes were wary of increased imperial power and gave only limited support; B1 military victory at Mühlberg (1547) did not resolve the underlying political/religious settlement; B1 Maurice of Saxony's defection and renewed war forced concessions (Peace of Passau, 1552); B1 the Peace of Augsburg (1555) had to legally recognise Lutheranism (cuius regio, eius religio) to secure lasting peace; B1 the Papacy's slow, divided response and the late Council of Trent meant no coordinated Catholic alternative was ready in time.
2. [20] Mark out of 20 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing: Level 1 (1-4) generalised/descriptive, little or no analysis; Level 2 (5-8) limited explanation/analysis or a mostly narrative answer with some analytical comment; Level 3 (9-12) explains relevant factors with some support but uneven coverage, reaching a partially supported judgement; Level 4 (13-16) a well-organised answer explaining a range of factors with good evidence and a supported judgement, though balance may be slightly uneven; Level 5 (17-20) fully focused, explains and weighs a range of factors with precise, wide-ranging evidence, sustained analysis and a clearly substantiated judgement. Credit: political case - princely protection from Frederick the Wise onward, the Schmalkaldic League, Charles V's distraction by France and the Ottomans, Maurice of Saxony's defection, and the negotiated Peace of Augsburg; case for belief/doctrine - the depth of conviction created by justification by faith, the vernacular Bible, the Catechisms and the German Mass, which gave Lutheranism a durable popular and institutional base that mere political protection could not by itself have created or sustained; a strong answer weighs both, perhaps concluding political circumstance determined the timing and terms of survival while doctrinal substance determined its depth and permanence.
