## Test items
1. Explain why anticlericalism was widespread in Germany by 1515. [6 marks]
2. 'Economic and social conditions in Germany by 1515 were more important than religious grievances in creating an environment in which Luther's ideas could flourish.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 resentment of clerical wealth and tax exemption; B1 bishops holding secular as well as spiritual power, blurring worldly and religious authority; B1 poor quality/education of much of the lower clergy undermining spiritual credibility; B1 corruption of the Renaissance papal court, widely known and criticised; B1 papal financial demands (annates, appointment fees, indulgences) seen as exploiting Germany specifically; B1 Christian humanist criticism of clerical ignorance had already prepared some educated opinion to be receptive to such criticism.
2. [20] Mark out of 20 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing: Level 1 (1-4) generalised/descriptive, little or no analysis; Level 2 (5-8) limited explanation/analysis or a mostly narrative answer with some analytical comment; Level 3 (9-12) explains relevant factors with some support but uneven coverage, reaching a partially supported judgement; Level 4 (13-16) a well-organised answer explaining a range of factors with good evidence and a supported judgement, though balance may be slightly uneven; Level 5 (17-20) fully focused, explains and weighs a range of factors with precise, wide-ranging evidence, sustained analysis and a clearly substantiated judgement. Credit: economic case - growing, literate urban centres able to print and circulate ideas fast, rising peasant grievance feeding radicalisation, trade routes aiding the spread of pamphlets; religious case - deep, longstanding anticlericalism, papal financial exploitation, the specific trigger of the indulgences controversy and Tetzel's campaign; a strong answer weighs the two rather than treating them as separate, noting how economic conditions (literacy, print, urban wealth) were the *means* by which religious grievance became a mass movement.
