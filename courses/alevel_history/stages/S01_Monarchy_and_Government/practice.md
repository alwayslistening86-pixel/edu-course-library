# S01_Monarchy_and_Government - Practice: Tudor monarchs, succession and gender; Parliament; the Crown's principal servants

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Give two ways in which the role of Parliament changed between 1509 and 1588. [4 marks]
2. Explain why gender mattered to Mary I's and Elizabeth I's exercise of power. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 before 1529 it met rarely and mainly voted taxation, developed; B2 the Reformation Parliament (1529-36) used statute to enact religious change, establishing 'King-in-Parliament', developed; B2 Elizabethan MPs grew more confident/vocal on succession and religion, developed -- any 2 developed points for full marks.
2. [3] B1 no precedent for an unmarried female monarch ruling alone; B1 pressure to marry to secure succession/provide a 'king consort'; B1 contrasting responses: Mary married Philip II (unpopular, provoked Wyatt's Rebellion); Elizabeth stayed unmarried and used the 'Gloriana' image to assert authority.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish an attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
