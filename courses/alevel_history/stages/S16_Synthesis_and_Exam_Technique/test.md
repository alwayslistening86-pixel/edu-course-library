# S16_Synthesis_and_Exam_Technique - Test: Synoptic comparison across 1485-1603, and exam technique for all three papers

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in Edexcel's style: source or interpretation evaluation and levels-marked essay questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, under rough time pressure. Then mark against the mark schemes below (Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing, and this course's approximation of the utility/interpretations levels for source questions) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. 'No Tudor rebellion between 1485 and 1603 posed a genuine threat to the survival of the dynasty.' Assess the validity of this view, drawing on rebellions from across the period. [20 marks]
2. Give one reason a strong essay plan under timed exam conditions should still identify a clear overall judgement before writing begins. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [20] Mark out of 20 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing: Level 1 (1-4) generalised/descriptive; Level 2 (5-8) limited explanation/analysis or mostly narrative with some analytical comment; Level 3 (9-12) explains relevant factors with some support but uneven coverage, reaching a partially supported judgement; Level 4 (13-16) well-organised, explains a range of factors with good evidence and a supported judgement; Level 5 (17-20) fully focused, weighs a range of factors with precise, wide-ranging evidence, sustained analysis and a clearly substantiated judgement. Credit: evidence for the view - every rising was ultimately defeated and no monarch was overthrown by force; evidence against - the real scale of the Pilgrimage of Grace (30,000-40,000, gentry/noble participation) and the severity and duration of Tyrone's Rebellion (a major defeat at Yellow Ford, direct Spanish intervention, years of war) suggest some risings came genuinely close to serious crisis even if none ultimately succeeded; a strong synoptic answer draws specific comparative evidence from at least three rebellions studied rather than generalising, and reaches a judgement that distinguishes 'threat contained' from 'no real threat existed'.
2. [2] B1 a plan that fixes the judgement in advance lets every paragraph be built to support (or knowingly qualify) it, rather than drifting into a narrative account; B1 examiners reward a sustained, supported line of argument across the whole answer more highly than a list of points with no overall conclusion.
