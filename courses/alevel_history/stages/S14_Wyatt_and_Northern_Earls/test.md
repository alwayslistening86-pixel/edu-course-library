## Test items
1. Explain why the Northern Earls' Revolt (1569-70) provoked a harsher government response than Wyatt's Rebellion (1554). [6 marks]
2. 'Political and dynastic motives mattered more than religion in both Wyatt's Rebellion, 1554, and the Northern Earls' Revolt, 1569-70.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 the Northern Earls' Revolt combined religion and a direct dynastic alternative (Mary, Queen of Scots) in a way Wyatt's did not achieve so concretely; B1 it threatened Elizabeth's own security and succession more directly, given the unresolved succession question; B1 several hundred executions followed as deliberate, wide-scale deterrence across the North; B1 by contrast Wyatt's rebellion, though reaching London, had failed to secure a proven link to an alternative claimant (Elizabeth was suspected but not convicted); B1 government by 1570 had also developed a more systematic capacity for repression (lords lieutenant, county militias) than in 1554; B1 the timing just before the papal bull of 1570 heightened the government's sense of an organised international Catholic threat requiring firm deterrence.
2. [20] Mark out of 20 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing: Level 1 (1-4) generalised/descriptive; Level 2 (5-8) limited explanation/analysis or mostly narrative with some analytical comment; Level 3 (9-12) explains relevant factors with some support but uneven coverage, reaching a partially supported judgement; Level 4 (13-16) well-organised, explains a range of factors with good evidence and a supported judgement; Level 5 (17-20) fully focused, weighs a range of factors with precise, wide-ranging evidence, sustained analysis and a clearly substantiated judgement. Credit: Wyatt's Rebellion's immediate trigger was the dynastic/foreign-policy fear of the Spanish marriage, with the Elizabeth link an explicitly political hope, while for the Northern Earls the plan to marry Mary, Queen of Scots to Norfolk was an explicitly dynastic act; against/alongside this, both risings were inseparably bound up with religion (anti-Catholic feeling driving Wyatt's supporters, and the explicit aim of restoring Catholicism driving the Northern Earls, underlined by mass being celebrated at Durham Cathedral); a strong answer weighs how far the two motives can be separated at all in either case, rather than asserting one clearly outweighed the other.
