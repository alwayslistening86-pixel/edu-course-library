## Test items
1. Explain why Henry VII treated Lambert Simnel with notable leniency but had Perkin Warbeck executed. [6 marks]
2. 'Foreign backing was the main reason the Perkin Warbeck conspiracy posed a far more serious threat to Henry VII than the Lambert Simnel conspiracy.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 Simnel was a low-status boy manipulated by others, of limited independent danger once defeated; B1 pardoning/employing him defused his value as a martyr figure without costing Henry much; B1 Warbeck's threat was cumulatively far greater and longer-sustained, with sustained state-level foreign backing including a Scottish invasion; B1 Warbeck's identity claim (a possibly still-living prince) was harder to neutralise by leniency alone; B1 by 1499 Henry needed dynastic security certainty ahead of Prince Arthur's Spanish marriage; B1 the alleged escape plot gave direct legal justification for removing both Warbeck and Warwick together.
2. [20] Mark out of 20 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended writing: Level 1 (1-4) generalised/descriptive; Level 2 (5-8) limited explanation/analysis or mostly narrative with some analytical comment; Level 3 (9-12) explains relevant factors with some support but uneven coverage, reaching a partially supported judgement; Level 4 (13-16) well-organised, explains a range of factors with good evidence and a supported judgement; Level 5 (17-20) fully focused, weighs a range of factors with precise, wide-ranging evidence, sustained analysis and a clearly substantiated judgement. Credit: Warbeck's unusually broad and sustained coalition (Margaret of Burgundy, Maximilian I, briefly Charles VIII, and above all James IV's invasion) as evidence foreign backing was decisive, versus Simnel's more limited Burgundian-funded mercenary force; against/alongside this, Warbeck's harder-to-disprove identity claim (the still-uncertain fate of the real Duke of York, versus the imprisoned and displayable Earl of Warwick) and the conspiracy's much greater duration (eight years versus one) as independently significant factors; a strong answer weighs foreign backing against these other factors rather than treating it as the sole cause of the difference in danger.
