## Test items
1. Compare the causes of the Western Rebellion and Kett's Rebellion, both in 1549. [6 marks]
2. 'Kett's Rebellion, 1549, was primarily caused by the failure of local government rather than by enclosure itself.' Assess the validity of this view. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 Western Rebellion: primarily religious, triggered directly by the new English Book of Common Prayer and desire to restore Latin mass; B1 particularly strong in conservative, partly non-English-speaking Cornish communities; B1 Kett's Rebellion: primarily economic/social, centred on enclosure, rents and failures of local landlord/official conduct; B1 both, however, occurred within weeks of each other in 1549 reflecting wider strain (rapid Edwardian religious change and economic hardship together) and weakness in Somerset's government; B1 both rebel groups professed loyalty to Edward VI rather than seeking to overthrow him; B1 both were ultimately crushed by royal armies with significant loss of life and exemplary punishment of leaders.

Credit: the 29 articles' emphasis on gentry/officials failing to enforce existing anti-enclosure law and abusing their position, and Kett's own status as a landowner rather than a poor peasant, as evidence for a governance-failure reading; against this, the central, repeated demand across the articles concerning enclosure and rent itself as the primary substantive grievance, with governance failure as the mechanism that allowed it to fester rather than the root cause; a strong answer weighs cause against mechanism/aggravating factor rather than treating them as simple alternatives.
