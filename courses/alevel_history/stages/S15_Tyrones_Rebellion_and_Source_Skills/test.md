## Test items
1. Explain why the government's initial military response to Tyrone's Rebellion largely failed before 1601. [6 marks]
2. [Source-based, Section A style] Source: a letter from an English official in Ireland to the Privy Council, 1598, written shortly after the Battle of the Yellow Ford, warning that 'unless greater force be sent with speed, we shall lose this whole kingdom to the traitor Tyrone, who groweth daily in strength and in the favour of the wild Irish.' With reference to its provenance, content and your own knowledge, evaluate the value of this source for an enquiry into the seriousness of the threat posed by Tyrone's Rebellion in 1598. [20 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 O'Neill's capable leadership and adoption of modern military organisation/tactics; B1 the major English defeat at the Battle of the Yellow Ford (1598), the worst English defeat in Ireland in the period; B1 Elizabeth's government simultaneously managing faction, succession anxiety and the Essex crisis, diverting attention and resources; B1 Essex's own failed 1599 command in Ireland wasted resources and time and fed into his subsequent disgrace/rebellion; B1 sustained Spanish diplomatic and eventual direct military support (landing at Kinsale, 1601) prolonged Irish resistance; B1 the scale, cost and distance of the campaign strained government capacity in a way earlier English risings had not.
2. [20] Mark out of 20 (this course's approximation of Edexcel's source-utility levels): the answer must evaluate what the source shows and how useful it is for the stated enquiry, using its provenance (who, when, why written), its content/tone, and the learner's own contextual knowledge to judge its value and its limitations. Level 5 (17-20): sophisticated, well-supported evaluation of both value and limitation, precisely grounded in the source and in context. Lower levels: more description of the source's content with less evaluation of its provenance or its limits. Credit: value - written by an official close to events immediately after a real, serious English defeat, so likely to reflect genuine alarm and accurate first-hand assessment of the military situation; limitation - an official writing to request more resources/troops has an incentive to exaggerate the danger to secure a stronger response, and reflects one individual's perspective rather than the full strategic picture; own knowledge should confirm the Yellow Ford defeat was genuinely serious and the rebellion did pose a major threat by 1598, supporting some of the source's claims while still noting its persuasive purpose limits its reliability as a neutral assessment.
