# S15_Media_Technology_Core - Lesson: Media and technology — vocabulary, present/perfect contrast, comparatives and connectives

## Goal
The learner discusses phones, social media and technology with balanced opinions, comparatives and well-linked sentences.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T3.2-vocab - Media and technology: vocabulary: phones, social networks, streaming, gaming, news, online safety
- AQA-FR-T3.2-pres-perfect-F - Present and perfect tenses, technology use: describe habitual technology use in the present and a specific past use in the perfect
- AQA-FR-T3.2-comparatives-F - Comparatives, technology: compare two forms of media or technology using comparatives
- AQA-FR-T3.2-opinion-F - Balanced opinions on technology: give a two-sided opinion on technology (c'est pratique... mais...)
- AQA-FR-T3.2-obj-pron-F - Preverbal object pronouns, media: use le/la/les correctly before the verb when discussing apps, videos or messages
- AQA-FR-T3.2-connectives-F - Connectives: link ideas with mais, cependant, donc, parce que instead of short disconnected sentences

## How to teach this
Ask: 'How do you use your phone normally, and what did you do online yesterday?' Push the learner to link ideas with connectives rather than a string of short disconnected sentences — this is what Higher-tier Writing specifically rewards here.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-vocab Media and technology: vocabulary
Core vocabulary: le portable (mobile phone), les réseaux sociaux (social media), une application, en ligne (online), l'écran (screen), dangereux/dangereuse, l'information (news item; les informations, plural, means "the news"), la publicité (advertising), le harcèlement (bullying, including cyberbullying), télécharger (to download). Cover streaming, gaming and online safety alongside social media, since AQA's task types often ask for both benefits and dangers.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-pres-perfect-F Present and perfect tenses, technology use
Describe habitual use in the present (j'utilise mon portable pour écouter de la musique) and a specific past use in the perfect (hier, j'ai passé deux heures sur les réseaux sociaux). Combining both tenses in one answer, as with other topics, demonstrates the range AQA's mark schemes reward.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-comparatives-F Comparatives, technology
Compare two forms of media or technology: Instagram est plus populaire que Facebook chez les jeunes; lire un livre est plus reposant que regarder un écran. As always, the adjective agrees with the first noun.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-opinion-F Balanced opinions on technology
A strong technology answer gives both sides: c'est pratique pour rester en contact, mais on perd beaucoup de temps (it's practical for staying in touch, but you waste a lot of time). Use d'un côté... de l'autre... or mais/cependant to structure the balance explicitly, rather than listing pros then cons with no signposting.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-obj-pron-F Preverbal object pronouns, media
Object pronouns avoid repeating an app, video or message already named: cette vidéo est drôle, je la regarde souvent; ces messages, je les télécharge tout de suite. As always, the pronoun sits directly before the conjugated verb.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-connectives-F Connectives
Connectives turn short disconnected sentences into a developed answer: mais (but), cependant (however, more formal/Higher-register), donc (so/therefore), parce que (because). "J'aime les réseaux sociaux. Je perds du temps." becomes "J'aime les réseaux sociaux, mais je perds beaucoup de temps, donc je dois faire attention" — one connected, higher-scoring sentence instead of two flat ones.

## Explicitly not here
avant de + infinitive and the three remaining Higher structures for this topic follow next.
