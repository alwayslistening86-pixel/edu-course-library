# S11_Celebrity_Culture_Core - Lesson: Celebrity culture — vocabulary, opinions, comparatives and object pronouns

## Goal
The learner discusses fame and role models with justified opinions, comparatives, the relative pronoun qui and singular object pronouns.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T2.3-vocab - Celebrity culture: vocabulary: sports stars, actors, singers, influencers, fame, privacy, money, social media
- AQA-FR-T2.3-opinion-F - Opinion structures: introduce and justify opinions with à mon avis, je trouve que, je pense que + parce que/car
- AQA-FR-T2.3-comparatives-F - Comparatives about people: compare two celebrities or role models using plus/moins/aussi...que
- AQA-FR-T2.3-rel-qui-F - Relative pronoun qui: use qui as a subject relative pronoun to build longer sentences
- AQA-FR-T2.3-obj-pron-sing-F - Singular preverbal object pronouns le/la/l': place le/la/l' correctly before the verb to avoid repeating a noun

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Name someone famous you admire and explain why in one connected sentence' — this is opinion-heavy content, which trains exactly the justified-opinion language AQA's AO2 levels reward most highly.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-vocab Celebrity culture: vocabulary
Core vocabulary: célèbre (famous — note the adjective form never changes for gender, though the article/noun does: un acteur célèbre, une actrice célèbre), la star, un chanteur/une chanteuse (singer), un acteur/une actrice, un influenceur/une influenceuse, la vie privée (privacy), gagner (to earn/win), admirer, un modèle (role model), le succès. Themes to cover: sports stars, actors, singers, influencers; advantages of fame (l'argent, le succès, voyager) against drawbacks (perdre sa vie privée, les paparazzis, la pression).

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-opinion-F Opinion structures
Opinion frames give shape and length to an answer: à mon avis (in my opinion), je trouve que..., je pense que..., d'un côté... de l'autre... (on one hand... on the other...). Every opinion should be justified with parce que or car — an unjustified opinion caps the development mark even when the grammar is accurate. Worked example: "À mon avis, être célèbre a des avantages, car on gagne beaucoup d'argent, mais les stars n'ont jamais de vie privée" shows a balanced, justified opinion in one sentence.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-comparatives-F Comparatives about people
Comparatives (plus/moins/aussi + adjective + que) let learners compare two celebrities or weigh fame against ordinary life: cette chanteuse est plus généreuse que cet acteur; être célèbre est moins simple qu'on le pense. As always, the adjective agrees with the first noun mentioned.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-rel-qui-F Relative pronoun qui
Qui (who/which, as a subject) links two clauses when the relative pronoun is the subject of the following verb: une chanteuse qui donne de l'argent à des associations (a singer who gives money to charities). Unlike que, qui is followed directly by a verb with no subject in between — test it by checking whether the clause already has its own subject; if not, use qui.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-obj-pron-sing-F Singular preverbal object pronouns le/la/l'
Le, la and l' (before a vowel) replace a singular noun already mentioned and sit directly before the conjugated verb — the reverse word order from English, and a very common error area: je l'admire (I admire him/her — NOT "je admire lui"), je le trouve généreux. With the perfect tense, the object pronoun still goes before the auxiliary: je l'ai vu à la télé hier soir.

## Explicitly not here
Superlatives, plural object pronouns and the conditional for this topic follow in the next stage.
