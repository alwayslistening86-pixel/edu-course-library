# S08_Free_Time_Extended - Lesson: Free-time activities — aimer + infinitive and Higher-tier time structures

## Goal
The learner justifies likes and dislikes with aimer/adorer/détester + infinitive and, at Higher tier, uses être en train de, venir de and depuis + present.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T2.1-aimer-infin-F - aimer / adorer / détester + infinitive: give and justify a like/dislike using aimer, adorer, détester with an infinitive
- AQA-FR-T2.1-etre-train-H - être en train de (Higher): use être en train de + infinitive for an action in progress
- AQA-FR-T2.1-venir-de-H - venir de + infinitive (Higher): use venir de + infinitive for something that has just happened
- AQA-FR-T2.1-depuis-H - depuis + present, hobbies (Higher): use depuis + present for a hobby continuing from the past

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What are you in the middle of at the moment, what have you just finished, and how long have you been doing it?' — three closely related but distinct Higher structures, best taught together so learners see the contrast.

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-aimer-infin-F aimer / adorer / détester + infinitive
These opinion verbs are followed directly by an infinitive with no linking preposition: j'adore lire, je déteste faire du sport, elle aime jouer au tennis. Always add a reason with parce que or car to move from a bare statement into developed, GCSE-credit-worthy language: j'adore lire parce que ça me détend après l'école.

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-etre-train-H être en train de (Higher)
Être en train de + infinitive expresses an action happening right now, more vivid than the simple present: je suis en train de lire un roman (I am in the middle of reading a novel). The infinitive must follow directly — a common error is using en train de with a noun instead ("je suis en train de la lecture" is wrong).

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-venir-de-H venir de + infinitive (Higher)
Venir de + infinitive expresses an action that has just finished, equivalent to English "have just done": je viens de finir un livre (I have just finished a book); elle vient de rentrer. Conjugate venir normally (je viens, tu viens, il vient, nous venons, vous venez, ils viennent) and keep the infinitive after de.

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-depuis-H depuis + present, hobbies (Higher)
As in earlier topics, depuis + a present-tense verb expresses an ongoing action started in the past: je joue de la guitare depuis trois ans (I have been playing guitar for three years). Keep the verb in the present, not the perfect — this specific error is flagged repeatedly across AQA mark schemes wherever depuis appears.
