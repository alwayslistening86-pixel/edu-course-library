# S13_Travel_Tourism_Core - Lesson: Travel and tourism — vocabulary, past trips, weather description and place prepositions

## Goal
The learner narrates a holiday using the perfect (avoir and être), the imperfect for weather, correct place prepositions and preverbal object pronouns.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T3.1-vocab - Travel and tourism: vocabulary: holidays, transport, accommodation, places of interest, problems while travelling
- AQA-FR-T3.1-perfect-avoir-etre-F - Perfect tense (avoir and être) for past trips: narrate a past holiday using both avoir and être perfect forms correctly
- AQA-FR-T3.1-imperfect-weather-F - Imperfect for weather and description: describe past weather and atmosphere using the imperfect (il faisait beau)
- AQA-FR-T3.1-place-prep-F - Prepositions with place names: choose à/en/au/aux correctly before cities, feminine, masculine and plural country names
- AQA-FR-T3.1-obj-pron-F - Preverbal object pronouns, travel context: use preverbal object pronouns to avoid repetition in travel narratives

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Where did you go on holiday, what was the weather like, and what did you visit?' — this is the classic three-time-frame topic and most past-paper Writing tasks fit naturally here.

#### AQA-FR-T3.1-vocab Travel and tourism: vocabulary
Core vocabulary: les vacances (holidays), voyager, l'hôtel, la plage (beach), le billet (ticket), la valise (suitcase), réserver (to book), un endroit (a place), le séjour (stay), visiter (to visit a place), rendre visite à (to visit a person — a key distinction, see below). Add transport words (le train, l'avion, en voiture) and accommodation types (un camping, une auberge de jeunesse) to widen the range of a response.

#### AQA-FR-T3.1-perfect-avoir-etre-F Perfect tense (avoir and être) for past trips
Holiday narratives need both perfect auxiliaries working together: nous avons visité un château (avoir, for the action of visiting) and nous sommes allés en Normandie (être, for the verb of motion, with agreement — sommes allées if the subject is feminine). Sequence several actions in one narrative to show range: nous sommes arrivés, avons déposé nos valises, et avons exploré la ville.

#### AQA-FR-T3.1-imperfect-weather-F Imperfect for weather and description
Weather and atmosphere use the imperfect, since they describe an ongoing state rather than a single event: il faisait beau (the weather was fine), il faisait chaud/froid, il pleuvait (it was raining), c'était superbe (it was wonderful). A common error is using the present tense for past weather ("il fait beau" instead of "il faisait beau" when the whole account is set in the past) — check the whole narrative is anchored consistently in the past.

#### AQA-FR-T3.1-place-prep-F Prepositions with place names
Place prepositions follow strict rules by gender/number of the place name: à + city (à Paris, à Lyon); en + feminine country or one starting with a vowel (en France, en Espagne, en Iran); au + masculine country (au Maroc, au Canada, au Portugal); aux + plural country (aux États-Unis, aux Pays-Bas). These same prepositions are reused for "to/in," which halves the learning load once mastered. Also distinguish visiter (a place: je visite le musée) from rendre visite à (a person: je rends visite à mon oncle) — mixing these up is a frequently flagged error.

#### AQA-FR-T3.1-obj-pron-F Preverbal object pronouns, travel context
Object pronouns avoid repeating a place or thing already named: le musée était fascinant, je l'ai visité deux fois (I visited it twice). As before, the pronoun sits directly before the verb, including before the auxiliary in the perfect.

## Explicitly not here
The simple future, pronoun y and après avoir + infinitive for this topic follow in the next stage.
