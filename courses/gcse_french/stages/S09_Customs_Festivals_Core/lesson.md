# S09_Customs_Festivals_Core - Lesson: Customs, festivals and celebrations — vocabulary, narrating customs and plans

## Goal
The learner describes French and francophone festivals and family celebrations, combining the perfect and imperfect in narrative, on + present for customs, and the near future for plans.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T2.2-vocab - Customs, festivals and celebrations: vocabulary: French and francophone festivals (14 juillet, Fête de la musique, Chandeleur, galette des rois, Noël), family celebrations
- AQA-FR-T2.2-perfect-imperfect-narr-F - Perfect and imperfect together in narrative: combine the perfect for events and the imperfect for setting/weather in a short narrative
- AQA-FR-T2.2-on-present-F - on + present for customs: use on + third-person-singular present to describe general customs
- AQA-FR-T2.2-near-future-F - Near future for festival plans: use aller + infinitive to describe plans for an upcoming celebration

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What happens every year at Christmas or your family's biggest celebration, and what happened last time?' Note that AQA's Listening and Reading passages are often set in French-speaking countries, so real cultural knowledge (not just grammar) earns comprehension marks here.

#### AQA-FR-T2.2-vocab Customs, festivals and celebrations: vocabulary
Well-attested French and francophone examples worth knowing by name: le 14 juillet (Bastille Day, the national holiday, with a parade and fireworks), la Fête de la musique (21 June, free concerts nationwide), la Chandeleur (2 February, crêpes), la galette des rois (Epiphany cake with a hidden charm), le réveillon de Noël (Christmas Eve dinner). Core vocabulary: la fête, célébrer, le réveillon, le feu d'artifice (fireworks), le défilé (parade), le cadeau (present), l'anniversaire, une tradition, se retrouver (to get together), en famille. Compare with celebrations the learner already knows (Eid, Diwali) as also marked in France's diverse communities, since AQA rewards cultural comparison.

#### AQA-FR-T2.2-perfect-imperfect-narr-F Perfect and imperfect together in narrative
A strong festival narrative pairs the perfect for what happened with the imperfect for the setting: nous sommes allés à Paris ; il faisait chaud et il y avait beaucoup de monde (we went to Paris; it was hot and there were a lot of people). Remember the past participle agreement after être in the perfect (nous sommes allés/allées). This exact combination — event in the perfect, atmosphere in the imperfect — is one of the most reliably rewarded narrative patterns across AQA's Writing and Speaking mark schemes.

#### AQA-FR-T2.2-on-present-F on + present for customs
"On" + a third-person-singular present verb is the natural way to describe a general custom, equivalent to English "people/we" in a general sense: en France, on mange des crêpes à la Chandeleur; on regarde le défilé le matin. On always takes the il/elle verb ending, even though it can mean "we" — a common error is conjugating the following verb as if for "ils" (on mangent is wrong; it must be on mange).

#### AQA-FR-T2.2-near-future-F Near future for festival plans
Aller + infinitive states a concrete plan for an upcoming celebration: nous allons fêter son anniversaire en famille; je vais préparer un repas spécial. This remains the safest Foundation-tier way to talk about future celebrations, alongside the Higher-tier simple future taught in the next stage.
