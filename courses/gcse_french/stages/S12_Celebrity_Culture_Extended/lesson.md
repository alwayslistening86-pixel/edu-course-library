# S12_Celebrity_Culture_Extended - Lesson: Celebrity culture — Higher-tier superlatives, plural pronouns and conditional

## Goal
The learner uses superlatives, plural object pronouns and the regular conditional to give a more developed view on fame.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T2.3-superlatives-H - Superlatives about celebrities (Higher): form superlatives such as le/la plus célèbre
- AQA-FR-T2.3-obj-pron-pl-H - Plural object pronouns les (Higher): use les as a preverbal plural object pronoun
- AQA-FR-T2.3-conditional-H - Regular conditional, wishes (Higher): use the regular conditional to express a wish about fame (j'aimerais, je détesterais)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Who is the most generous celebrity you know of, do you follow several celebrities on social media, and would you want to be famous yourself?' — three prompts, three Higher structures.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-superlatives-H Superlatives about celebrities (Higher)
Le/la plus + adjective picks out an extreme: c'est le joueur le plus généreux (he's the most generous player); c'est la chanteuse la plus célèbre du monde. As elsewhere, the article is repeated when the adjective follows the noun, and bon becomes the irregular le meilleur/la meilleure.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-obj-pron-pl-H Plural object pronouns les (Higher)
Les replaces a plural noun already mentioned, following the same preverbal rule as le/la: je les suis sur les réseaux sociaux (I follow them on social media); je les admire tous. Combine confidently with the perfect: je les ai vus en concert.

#### AQA-FR-T2.3-conditional-H Regular conditional, wishes (Higher)
The conditional expresses a hypothetical wish about fame, often contrasted with its downside: j'aimerais voyager partout, mais je détesterais perdre ma vie privée (I would like to travel everywhere, but I would hate to lose my privacy). This contrast structure (j'aimerais... mais je détesterais...) is a strong, exam-ready pattern for a balanced Higher-tier opinion.
