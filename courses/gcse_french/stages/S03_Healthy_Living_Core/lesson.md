# S03_Healthy_Living_Core - Lesson: Healthy living and lifestyle — vocabulary, partitive articles, advice language

## Goal
The learner discusses food, exercise and health habits using partitive articles, il faut and modal verbs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T1.2-vocab - Healthy living and lifestyle: vocabulary: food and drink, exercise and sport frequency, sleep, stress and wellbeing, risks of smoking/alcohol/drugs
- AQA-FR-T1.2-partitive-F - Partitive articles: use du/de la/des for 'some' and switch to de/d' after a negative
- AQA-FR-T1.2-il-faut-F - il faut + infinitive: give impersonal advice with il faut followed by an infinitive
- AQA-FR-T1.2-modal-F - Modal verbs devoir, pouvoir, vouloir, savoir (present): conjugate and use the four key modal verbs in the present followed by an infinitive

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What do you eat in a normal day, and what should you eat more or less of?' Push the learner past "je mange bien" into specific foods and specific advice — this is exactly where the partitive article becomes unavoidable.

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-vocab Healthy living and lifestyle: vocabulary
Core vocabulary: la santé (health), en forme (fit), manger équilibré (to eat a balanced diet), les légumes (vegetables), les fruits, le sommeil (sleep), se coucher (to go to bed), se réveiller (to wake up), fumer (to smoke), boire de l'alcool, le stress, fatigué(e), dangereux/dangereuse, une drogue. Add specific foods (la viande, le poisson, les féculents) and activity phrases (faire du sport, faire de l'exercice) so answers are concrete rather than generic. As with all AQA 8652 topics, these words are drawn from the Appendix 2 prescribed list; treat any word a learner needs but cannot find there as legitimate extra vocabulary rather than off-syllabus.

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-partitive-F Partitive articles
The partitive article expresses "some" and is compulsory in French where English often omits it: du (masculine), de la (feminine), de l' (before a vowel), des (plural) — je mange des légumes, je bois de l'eau, elle mange du pain. After a negative, all four collapse to de/d': je ne mange pas de viande (not "je ne mange pas de la viande" — this is the single most common error). Contrast with the definite article, used for general likes/dislikes: j'aime les légumes (I like vegetables in general) versus je mange des légumes (I eat some vegetables).

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-il-faut-F il faut + infinitive
Il faut is an impersonal structure ("it is necessary to / you must") always followed by the infinitive, never a conjugated verb: il faut manger cinq fruits et légumes par jour; il faut dormir huit heures. It works for any subject without changing form, which makes it a reliable, high-value phrase for advice tasks. Negative: il ne faut pas fumer (you must not smoke) — note this is a strong prohibition, stronger than "on ne devrait pas."

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-modal-F Modal verbs devoir, pouvoir, vouloir, savoir (present)
Four irregular modal verbs, all followed by an infinitive. Devoir (must/should): je dois, tu dois, il doit, nous devons, vous devez, ils doivent. Pouvoir (can/to be able to): je peux, tu peux, il peut, nous pouvons, vous pouvez, ils peuvent. Vouloir (want): je veux, tu veux, il veut, nous voulons, vous voulez, ils veulent. Savoir (to know how to): je sais, tu sais, il sait, nous savons, vous savez, ils savent — used specifically for learned skills (je sais nager), not general ability. Worked example: "On doit faire attention à ce qu'on mange, mais on peut se permettre un dessert de temps en temps" (You have to be careful what you eat, but you can allow yourself a dessert now and then).
