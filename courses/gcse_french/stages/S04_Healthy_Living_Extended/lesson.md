# S04_Healthy_Living_Extended - Lesson: Healthy living and lifestyle — imperative, routine reflexives and Higher structures

## Goal
The learner gives commands with the imperative, describes routine with reflexive verbs, and uses ne...plus/ne...que, en and depuis at Higher tier.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T1.2-imperative-F - The imperative: form positive and negative commands for tu/vous/nous
- AQA-FR-T1.2-reflexive-couche-F - Reflexive verbs of routine (se coucher etc.): use reflexive verbs of daily routine linked to health (se coucher, se réveiller, se détendre)
- AQA-FR-T1.2-ne-plus-que-H - ne...plus and ne...que (Higher): use ne...plus (no longer) and ne...que (only) accurately
- AQA-FR-T1.2-en-H - The pronoun en (Higher): replace du/de la/des + noun with en before the verb
- AQA-FR-T1.2-depuis-H - depuis + present tense (Higher): use depuis with a present-tense verb for an action still continuing

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Tell your little brother three things he must and must not do to stay healthy' — this naturally produces the imperative, which learners often avoid because English "you should" feels safer than a direct command.

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-imperative-F The imperative
The imperative drops the subject pronoun and, for -er verbs, drops the final -s from the tu form: (tu) manges → Mange des légumes! (vous) mangez → Mangez bien! (nous) mangeons → Mangeons ensemble! Negative commands wrap ne...pas around the verb: Ne fume pas! Ne bois pas trop de café! Reflexive imperatives move the pronoun after the verb with a hyphen in the positive (Couche-toi tôt!) but before it in the negative (Ne te couche pas trop tard!). This is a frequent slip: learners often keep "te" before the verb even in a positive command.

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-reflexive-couche-F Reflexive verbs of daily routine
Health and lifestyle relies on routine reflexives: se coucher (to go to bed), se réveiller (to wake up), se lever (to get up), se détendre (to relax), se sentir (to feel) — je me sens fatigué, elle se sent stressée. Full present of se coucher: je me couche, tu te couches, il/elle se couche, nous nous couchons, vous vous couchez, ils/elles se couchent. These combine naturally with time expressions (je me couche à onze heures) and with il faut (il faut se coucher tôt — note the reflexive pronoun changes to match an implied "on/tu" subject in general advice, or stays "se" in the infinitive form after il faut).

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-ne-plus-que-H ne...plus and ne...que (Higher)
Two more negative-style structures beyond ne...pas: ne...plus (no longer/not anymore) — je ne mange plus de bonbons (I no longer eat sweets, showing changed behaviour, a strong Higher-tier marker); ne...que (only) — which is not a true negative in meaning but uses the same ne...X frame — je ne bois que de l'eau (I only drink water). Both wrap around the verb exactly like ne...pas, and both collapse a following partitive to de: je ne mange plus de viande.

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-en-H The pronoun en (Higher)
En replaces "du/de la/des + noun" already mentioned, avoiding repetition — a genuine marker of fluency examiners reward. "Tu manges des légumes?" "Oui, j'en mange tous les jours" (Yes, I eat some every day) rather than repeating "des légumes." En sits directly before the conjugated verb, like other object pronouns: je n'en bois pas (I don't drink any).

#### AQA-FR-T1.2-depuis-H depuis + present tense (Higher)
Depuis + a present-tense verb describes something that started in the past and is still true now — a construction English expresses with "have been doing," which tempts learners into the wrong tense in French. Je fais du sport depuis deux ans (I have been doing sport for two years — NOT j'ai fait du sport depuis deux ans, which is a commonly penalised error). Depuis can also take a specific point in time: je ne mange plus de viande depuis janvier.
