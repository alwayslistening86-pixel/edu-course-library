# S07_Free_Time_Core - Lesson: Free-time activities — vocabulary, perfect vs imperfect, jouer/faire, frequency

## Goal
The learner discusses hobbies and leisure using jouer à/de, faire de, frequency adverbs, and chooses correctly between the perfect and imperfect.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T2.1-vocab - Free-time activities: vocabulary: sport, music, cinema and TV, reading, hobbies, shopping, going out, frequency expressions
- AQA-FR-T2.1-perfect-imperfect-F - Perfect vs imperfect for habits and events: choose the perfect for single past events and the imperfect for repeated past habits
- AQA-FR-T2.1-jouer-faire-F - jouer à / jouer de / faire de: use jouer à for sports/games, jouer de for instruments, and faire de for activities
- AQA-FR-T2.1-freq-adverbs-F - Frequency adverbs: place frequency adverbs (souvent, de temps en temps, jamais) correctly in a sentence

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What did you do last Saturday, and what do you usually do on Saturdays?' — the same day, two different tenses, which is the exact contrast this stage teaches.

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-vocab Free-time activities: vocabulary
Core vocabulary: le temps libre (free time), un passe-temps (a hobby), le sport, la musique, le cinéma, la télé, lire (to read), sortir (to go out), faire du shopping, se détendre (to relax), s'amuser (to have fun), le week-end. These words sit within AQA's Appendix 2 prescribed list for this topic; combine them immediately with an opinion and a reason (j'adore le cinéma parce que ça me détend) rather than listing hobbies alone, since GCSE credit goes to developed, justified statements.

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-perfect-imperfect-F Perfect vs imperfect for habits and events
The perfect reports a single completed event (samedi dernier, j'ai gagné un match — last Saturday I won a match); the imperfect describes a repeated habit or background state (quand j'étais petit, je jouais au foot tous les samedis — when I was little I used to play football every Saturday). The time marker often signals which is needed: "hier," "samedi dernier," "une fois" point to the perfect; "d'habitude," "tous les samedis," "quand j'étais petit" point to the imperfect. A common error is using the perfect for a described habit ("je suis allé chaque samedi" should be "j'allais chaque samedi").

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-jouer-faire-F jouer à / jouer de / faire de
Three different structures for three different activity types, frequently swapped by mistake: jouer à + definite article + sport or game (jouer au foot, jouer aux cartes); jouer de + definite article + musical instrument (jouer de la guitare, jouer du piano); faire de + definite article + most other activities (faire de la natation, faire du vélo, faire de l'athlétisme). The contraction rules apply as usual: à + le → au, de + le → du. "Jouer du foot" or "jouer à la guitare" are common, examiner-flagged errors.

#### AQA-FR-T2.1-freq-adverbs-F Frequency adverbs
Frequency adverbs give texture to a hobby description and are cheap, reliable marks: souvent (often), rarement (rarely), de temps en temps (from time to time), tous les samedis (every Saturday), une fois par semaine (once a week), jamais (never, needs ne). Most short adverbs (souvent, rarement, toujours) go directly after the conjugated verb (je joue souvent au tennis); time-phrase adverbials (tous les samedis, le week-end) more often open or close the sentence.
