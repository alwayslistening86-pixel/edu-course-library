# S06_Education_Work_Extended - Lesson: Education and work — comparatives, ambition verbs and Higher-tier time frames

## Goal
The learner compares school subjects, expresses ambition with vouloir/pouvoir, and at Higher tier uses the simple future, conditional, superlatives and depuis + present.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T1.3-comparatives-F - Comparatives: form plus/moins/aussi...que comparatives about subjects, jobs or people
- AQA-FR-T1.3-vouloir-pouvoir-F - vouloir / pouvoir + infinitive: use vouloir and pouvoir with an infinitive to talk about ambitions and ability
- AQA-FR-T1.3-simple-future-H - Simple future, regular verbs (Higher): form the simple future of regular verbs for firm predictions (je travaillerai)
- AQA-FR-T1.3-conditional-H - Regular conditional (Higher): form the regular conditional (j'aimerais) for polite wishes
- AQA-FR-T1.3-superlatives-H - Superlatives (Higher): form le/la plus/moins + adjective superlatives
- AQA-FR-T1.3-depuis-H - depuis + present, education context (Higher): use depuis + present to say how long you have studied something

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Which subject is more useful than another, and what job would you like to do in ten years?' — this naturally needs a comparative and, at Higher tier, the future or conditional rather than just "je veux."

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-comparatives-F Comparatives
Comparatives use plus/moins/aussi + adjective + que: les maths sont plus difficiles que l'anglais (maths are more difficult than English); l'histoire est aussi intéressante que la géographie. The adjective still agrees with the first noun as normal. One irregular to flag: bon → meilleur (better), not "plus bon."

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-vouloir-pouvoir-F vouloir / pouvoir + infinitive
Vouloir (to want) and pouvoir (to be able to/can) followed directly by an infinitive express ambition and possibility without needing a new tense: je veux devenir médecin (I want to become a doctor); on peut choisir plusieurs matières en seconde. These are reliable Foundation-tier structures for talking about future work, usable well before the simple future is secure.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-simple-future-H Simple future, regular verbs (Higher)
The simple future (will do) is formed from the infinitive (dropping the final -e of -re verbs) plus endings -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont: je travaillerai, tu réussiras, elle deviendra... wait — devenir is irregular (deviendr-), but regular -er/-ir verbs are straightforward: je continuerai mes études, nous choisirons nos matières. This is a firmer, more confident claim about the future than the near future, and AQA's mark schemes reward using both across a Higher response for range.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-conditional-H Regular conditional (Higher)
The regular conditional (would do) uses the same stem as the simple future plus imperfect endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient): j'aimerais travailler à l'étranger (I would like to work abroad); je voudrais devenir professeur. It is the polite, hypothetical register — ideal for expressing wishes or softened opinions, and a clear Higher-tier marker when used alongside the indicative tenses.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-superlatives-H Superlatives (Higher)
Superlatives use le/la/les plus/moins + adjective, agreeing with the noun: la matière la plus difficile (the most difficult subject); le métier le moins bien payé (the least well-paid job). With adjectives that follow the noun, the article is repeated: la classe la plus intéressante. Irregular: le/la meilleur(e) (the best).

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-depuis-H depuis + present, education context (Higher)
As with the health topic, depuis + present states something ongoing: j'étudie le français depuis cinq ans (I have been studying French for five years) — never the perfect tense here, which is a commonly flagged error in AQA mark schemes for this exact structure.
