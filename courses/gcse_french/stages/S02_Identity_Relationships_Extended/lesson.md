# S02_Identity_Relationships_Extended - Lesson: Identity and relationships with others — reflexives, negation and Higher structures

## Goal
The learner uses singular and plural reflexive verbs, the three core negatives, and the relative pronoun que to build longer sentences about relationships.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T1.1-reflexive-F - Singular reflexive verbs: use common singular reflexive verbs (s'appeler, s'entendre avec) correctly
- AQA-FR-T1.1-negation-F - Negation: ne...pas / ne...jamais / ne...rien: form and use the three core negative structures accurately, including ne dropping in speech but not in writing
- AQA-FR-T1.1-plural-reflexive-H - Plural reflexive verbs (Higher): use plural reflexive forms such as nous nous disputons
- AQA-FR-T1.1-rel-que-H - Relative pronoun que (Higher): link clauses with que as a direct-object relative pronoun

## How to teach this
Ask: 'How do you say "I get on well with my sister" and "we never argue"?' Use the gap in the learner's answer to introduce reflexive verbs and negation together, since they combine constantly in this topic.

#### AQA-FR-T1.1-reflexive-F Singular reflexive verbs
Reflexive verbs use a pronoun that matches the subject: je m'appelle, tu t'appelles, il/elle s'appelle. For relationships, the key verb is s'entendre (bien/mal) avec (to get on well/badly with): je m'entends bien avec ma sœur; il s'entend mal avec son frère. Other useful singular reflexives: se disputer (to argue) — but as a reciprocal action it often appears in the plural (see next item). Remember the reflexive pronoun always sits directly before the conjugated verb, even in a negative (je ne m'entends pas bien avec...).

#### AQA-FR-T1.1-negation-F Negation: ne...pas / ne...jamais / ne...rien
The three core negatives wrap around the verb: ne (n' before a vowel) + verb + pas/jamais/rien. Il ne se moque jamais de moi (he never makes fun of me). Je ne fais rien le week-end (I do nothing at the weekend). In speech the ne is often dropped (je sais pas), but GCSE writing tasks require the full form for full marks — a very common lost-mark pattern is writing "je fume jamais" instead of "je ne fume jamais." With a reflexive verb, ne goes before the reflexive pronoun: elle ne s'entend pas bien avec son frère.

#### AQA-FR-T1.1-plural-reflexive-H Plural reflexive verbs (Higher)
Higher-tier learners extend reflexives to the plural, especially for mutual/reciprocal relationships: nous nous entendons bien, nous nous disputons rarement, ils se disputent souvent. Note the doubled pronoun pattern (nous nous..., vous vous..., ils/elles se...) and that se disputer genuinely needs a plural or "avec" construction since arguing is a two-person action. Worked example: "Mes parents et moi, nous nous disputons rarement, mais mon frère et ma sœur se disputent tout le temps" shows both first- and third-person plural reflexives in one sentence.

#### AQA-FR-T1.1-rel-que-H Relative pronoun que (Higher)
Que (that/which/who, as a direct object) links two clauses into one, which examiners reward as a marker of Higher-level complexity. Compare "J'ai un ami. Je l'admire beaucoup" with the linked version "J'ai un ami que j'admire beaucoup" (I have a friend that I admire a lot). Que is followed directly by a subject + verb, unlike qui which is followed by a verb (qui is covered later, in Celebrity culture). Common error: using qui when the noun is the object of the following verb — test it by checking whether a subject already exists after the pronoun; if yes, use que.
