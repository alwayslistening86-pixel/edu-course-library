# S19_Listening_and_Reading_Papers - Lesson: Listening and Reading papers — comprehension, dictation and translation into English

## Goal
The learner applies exam technique for Paper 1 (Listening) and Paper 3 (Reading): comprehension, dictation, and French-to-English translation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-P1-comprehension - Listening Paper: Section A comprehension: answer comprehension questions in English or non-verbally on French audio, using the reading time before playback
- AQA-FR-P1-dictation - Listening Paper: Section B dictation: write down French sentences heard three times, scoring on both meaning and accurate spelling/grammar (Foundation 20+ words, Higher 30+ words)
- AQA-FR-P3-comprehension - Reading Paper: Section A comprehension: answer comprehension questions in English or non-verbally on French written texts
- AQA-FR-P3-translation - Reading Paper: French-to-English translation: translate a French passage into natural, accurate English (Foundation 35+ words, Higher 50+ words)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What's different about answering a question in English about French audio, versus writing down exactly what you hear?' Two receptive skills, two very different techniques — use this contrast to set up the stage.

#### AQA-FR-P1-comprehension Listening Paper: Section A comprehension
Paper 1 (Listening) is worth 25% overall: Foundation 40 marks in 35 minutes, Higher 50 marks in 45 minutes, both including reading and checking time before the recording plays. Section A tests comprehension, answered in English or non-verbally (ticking boxes, matching). Use the reading period before the audio starts to preview every question and predict likely vocabulary. Answers do not need to be full sentences, but must capture the specific detail asked for — a vague or overly general answer loses marks even if the general topic is understood correctly.

#### AQA-FR-P1-dictation Listening Paper: Section B dictation
Section B is a dictation: each sentence is played three times (an initial full play, then in shorter chunks, then a final full play to check), and the learner writes exactly what is heard. Foundation requires at least 20 words, Higher at least 30. Marks are given for both meaning conveyed and accurate spelling/grammar, so listen specifically for silent verb endings (the -s, -ent and -e endings that are often not clearly heard but must still be written) and for accents, which change meaning or are simply required for correct spelling (é/è/ê). Write what is actually heard, not what you guess the sentence "should" say from context.

#### AQA-FR-P3-comprehension Reading Paper: Section A comprehension
Paper 3 (Reading) is worth 25% overall: Foundation 50 marks in 45 minutes, Higher 50 marks in 60 minutes. Section A tests comprehension of written French texts, again answered in English or non-verbally. As with Listening, look for the specific detail the question asks for rather than translating the whole passage in your head first — this wastes time under exam conditions.

#### AQA-FR-P3-translation Reading Paper: French-to-English translation
Section B is a French-to-English translation: Foundation at least 35 words, Higher at least 50. Translate for natural, accurate English meaning while keeping the original tense and every element of meaning — a translation that "sounds nice" but drops a negative, a tense, or a specific detail loses marks. Watch for false friends: actuellement means "currently" (not "actually"), assister à means "to attend" (not "to assist").

## Explicitly not here
Speaking (Paper 2) follows in the next stage, then Writing (Paper 4).
