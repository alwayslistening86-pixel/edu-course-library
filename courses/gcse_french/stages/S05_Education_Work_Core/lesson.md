# S05_Education_Work_Core - Lesson: Education and work — vocabulary, perfect and imperfect tenses, near future

## Goal
The learner talks about school and work experience in the past, present and immediate future using the perfect, imperfect for description and aller + infinitive.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T1.3-vocab - Education and work: vocabulary: school subjects, timetable, school rules/uniform, work experience, jobs and careers, plans after Year 11
- AQA-FR-T1.3-perfect-avoir-F - Perfect tense with avoir: form and use the perfect tense with avoir for completed past actions
- AQA-FR-T1.3-perfect-etre-F - Perfect tense with être and agreement: identify the 'house of être' verbs and reflexives, and agree the past participle with the subject
- AQA-FR-T1.3-imperfect-desc-F - Imperfect tense for description: use the imperfect (c'était...) to describe how something was, contrasted with the perfect for events
- AQA-FR-T1.3-near-future-F - Near future (aller + infinitive): form the near future with aller and an infinitive for plans

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What did you do in school yesterday, what is school normally like, and what will you study next year?' — three different time frames in one answer is exactly what AQA's Writing mark scheme rewards, and exactly what most learners avoid without explicit teaching.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-vocab Education and work: vocabulary
Core vocabulary: le collège, le lycée, la matière (school subject), l'emploi du temps (timetable), le règlement (rules), l'uniforme, réussir (to succeed/pass), échouer (to fail), le stage (work experience placement — a frequent false-friend trap, not "a stage"), le métier (job/trade), un emploi (a job), gagner de l'argent (to earn money), un apprentissage (apprenticeship), la fac (university, informal), devenir (to become). These sit within AQA's Appendix 2 list for this topic and should be drilled in full sentences about the learner's own school and plans.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-perfect-avoir-F Perfect tense with avoir
Most verbs form the perfect with avoir + past participle: j'ai fait un stage, elle a réussi son examen, nous avons étudié l'histoire. Regular past participles: -er verbs → -é (étudié), -ir verbs → -i (fini), -re verbs → -u (vendu). Common irregulars to learn by heart for this topic: faire → fait, avoir → eu, être → été, voir → vu, lire → lu. Worked example: "L'année dernière, j'ai fait un stage dans une école primaire" (Last year I did work experience in a primary school).

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-perfect-etre-F Perfect tense with être and agreement
A fixed set of verbs (often taught as the "house of être": aller, venir, arriver, partir, sortir, entrer, rester, monter, descendre, naître, mourir, retourner, tomber) plus all reflexive verbs take être instead of avoir in the perfect, and the past participle then agrees in gender and number with the subject: elle est allée (feminine), ils sont partis (masculine plural), nous sommes resté(e)s. A very common error is using avoir with these verbs ("j'ai allé" is wrong; it must be "je suis allé(e)"). After être used for identity (not the perfect tense), jobs take no article: je suis infirmière, not je suis une infirmière.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-imperfect-desc-F Imperfect tense for description
The imperfect describes how something was — background, opinion or ongoing state in the past — contrasted with the perfect for the single completed event. Formed from the nous form of the present, minus -ons, plus imperfect endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient): c'était fatigant mais intéressant (it was tiring but interesting). A strong Education and work answer pairs the two tenses: "J'ai fait un stage ; c'était fatigant mais intéressant" — the event in the perfect, the judgement on it in the imperfect.

#### AQA-FR-T1.3-near-future-F Near future (aller + infinitive)
The near future (going to do something) uses the present tense of aller + an infinitive: je vais étudier les sciences, elle va faire un apprentissage, nous allons continuer nos études. This is the safest, most reliable way to talk about plans after Year 11 at Foundation tier, and remains useful at Higher tier alongside the simple future (taught next stage) for variety.

## Explicitly not here
Comparatives, vouloir/pouvoir + infinitive and the Higher-tier future, conditional, superlative and depuis structures for this topic follow in the next stage.
