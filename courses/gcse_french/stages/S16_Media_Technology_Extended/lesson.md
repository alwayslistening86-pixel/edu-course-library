# S16_Media_Technology_Extended - Lesson: Media and technology — Higher-tier avant de, present participle, depuis and passive

## Goal
The learner uses avant de + infinitive, the present participle, depuis + present and the present passive to write more sophisticated technology answers.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T3.2-avant-de-H - avant de + infinitive (Higher): use avant de + infinitive correctly (same-subject construction)
- AQA-FR-T3.2-present-participle-H - Present participle en + -ant (Higher): use en + present participle for two simultaneous actions
- AQA-FR-T3.2-depuis-H - depuis + present, technology (Higher): use depuis + present to say how long you have owned or used something
- AQA-FR-T3.2-passive-H - Present passive, media (Higher): recognise and form simple present passive statements about information/news

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What do you do just before bed, what can you do at the same time as something else, and how long have you had your phone?' — three related Higher-tier structures.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-avant-de-H avant de + infinitive (Higher)
Avant de + infinitive means "before doing something," used when the subject of both actions is the same: avant de me coucher, je ne regarde jamais mon téléphone. A common error is following avant de with a conjugated verb instead of the infinitive ("avant de je me couche" is wrong; it must be "avant de me coucher").

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-present-participle-H Present participle en + -ant (Higher)
En + present participle (formed from the nous form of the present, minus -ons, plus -ant) expresses two simultaneous actions: en regardant des vidéos, je fais mes devoirs (while watching videos, I do my homework). This is a genuine marker of stylistic range at Higher tier.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-depuis-H depuis + present, technology (Higher)
As in other topics, depuis + present states how long something has continued: j'ai un portable depuis trois ans (I have had a phone for three years). Keep the present tense, not the perfect.

#### AQA-FR-T3.2-passive-H Present passive, media (Higher)
The present passive describes information or news being shared, without naming who shares it: les informations sont partagées très vite sur les réseaux sociaux (news is shared very quickly on social media). Compare with the active equivalent (on partage les informations très vite) to show the same idea in two registers.
