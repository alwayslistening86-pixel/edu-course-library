# S18_Environment_Extended - Lesson: The environment and where people live — Higher-tier relative clauses, negatives, conditional, passive and future

## Goal
The learner writes an extended, Higher-tier answer on the environment using qui/où/que, ne...plus/ne...que, the conditional, the passive and the simple future.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AQA-FR-T3.3-rel-qui-F - Relative pronoun qui, environment: use qui to add detail about a place or problem
- AQA-FR-T3.3-rel-ou-que-H - Relative pronouns où/que, environment (Higher): use où and que to build complex sentences about where you live
- AQA-FR-T3.3-ne-plus-que-H - ne...plus / ne...que, environment (Higher): use ne...plus and ne...que in environmental arguments
- AQA-FR-T3.3-conditional-H - Regular conditional, environment (Higher): use the conditional to express environmental wishes (j'aimerais habiter...)
- AQA-FR-T3.3-passive-H - Present passive, environment (Higher): form present passive statements about recycling/protection measures
- AQA-FR-T3.3-simple-future-H - Simple future, consequences (Higher): use the simple future to state a consequence (si nous ne faisons rien, le climat changera)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Describe a problem in your area, where you would prefer to live instead, and what will happen if nothing changes' — this draws together every remaining Higher-tier structure for this topic.

#### AQA-FR-T3.3-rel-qui-F Relative pronoun qui, environment
Qui adds detail about a place or problem as the subject of the following verb: j'habite dans une ville qui a beaucoup de circulation (I live in a city that has a lot of traffic). As in Celebrity culture, qui is followed directly by a verb with no subject between them.

#### AQA-FR-T3.3-rel-ou-que-H Relative pronouns où/que, environment (Higher)
Où and que build longer, more complex sentences about where the learner lives: j'habite dans une ville où il y a peu de transports en commun (I live in a city where there is little public transport); c'est le problème que je trouve le plus grave.

#### AQA-FR-T3.3-ne-plus-que-H ne...plus / ne...que, environment (Higher)
Ne...plus and ne...que strengthen an environmental argument: il ne faut plus gaspiller l'eau (we must no longer waste water); on ne devrait boire que de l'eau du robinet (we should only drink tap water). Both wrap around the verb as usual.

#### AQA-FR-T3.3-conditional-H Regular conditional, environment (Higher)
The conditional expresses an environmental wish, often as an alternative to reality: j'aimerais habiter à la campagne, loin de la pollution (I would like to live in the countryside, away from pollution).

#### AQA-FR-T3.3-passive-H Present passive, environment (Higher)
The present passive describes measures being taken, with être + past participle agreeing with the subject: le plastique est recyclé dans notre quartier (plastic is recycled in our neighbourhood); les espaces verts sont protégés (green spaces are protected); l'eau est gaspillée si on ne fait pas attention. Using the passive here shifts focus onto the environmental measure itself rather than who carries it out, which is often exactly the emphasis a strong answer about local or national action needs.

#### AQA-FR-T3.3-simple-future-H Simple future, consequences (Higher)
The simple future states a consequence, often after "si" + present: si nous ne faisons rien, le climat changera (if we do nothing, the climate will change). This conditional-style "si + present, ... + future" pattern is a reliable, exam-ready way to argue for action.
