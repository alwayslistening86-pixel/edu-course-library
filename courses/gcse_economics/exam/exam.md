# GCSE Economics (AQA 8136) - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
Paper 1 covers Topics 1-6 (S01-S09): Section A (10 multiple-choice questions plus calculation/short/extended-response questions) and Section B (five further mixed questions). Paper 2 covers Topics 7-11 (S10-S18): the same Section A / Section B structure. Questions are original; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests, keeping each paper to its real topic split and AQA's real question styles (including diagram-based questions, described precisely in words). The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest.

## 10 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. [Paper 1] Define opportunity cost, and give an example of the opportunity cost of a government spending an extra £1bn on defence rather than education. [3 marks]
2. [Paper 1] A rise in the price of bus fares causes demand for train travel (a substitute) to rise at every price. Explain, using a diagram description, what happens to the train travel demand curve. [3 marks]
3. [Paper 1] A good has a price elasticity of demand of -0.4. State whether demand is elastic or inelastic, and explain what would happen to a firm's total revenue if it raised the price of this good. [3 marks]
4. Which of these best defines a positive externality? Choose every correct option.
   A. a benefit of an economic activity that falls on a third party not directly involved in the transaction
   B. a cost a firm pays to produce a good
   C. the profit a firm earns
   D. a tax placed on a good with harmful side-effects
5. [Paper 1] A firm sells 800 units at £6 each. Fixed costs are £1,200 and variable costs are £2.50 per unit. Calculate total revenue, total cost and profit, showing your working. [4 marks]
6. [Paper 2] A country's exports total £520bn and imports total £545bn over a year. Calculate the current account balance and state whether this is a surplus or a deficit. [2 marks]
7. [Paper 2] Explain how the Bank of England's Monetary Policy Committee could use interest rates to bring inflation that is above the 2% target back down. [3 marks]
8. Which of these is a type of unemployment caused specifically by a long-term decline in a particular industry or region? Choose every correct option.
   A. structural unemployment
   B. frictional unemployment
   C. seasonal unemployment
   D. cyclical unemployment
9. [Paper 2] A basket of goods that cost £220 in the base year costs £231 this year. Calculate the CPI this year and the rate of inflation, showing your working. [3 marks]
10. [Paper 2] Discuss whether globalisation has, on balance, benefited workers in developed countries such as the UK. [8 marks]

## Answer key for the ready-made items (tutor only)

2. [3] B1 the whole demand curve for train travel shifts to the right (a rise in demand, not a movement along it); B1 axes correctly described: price on the vertical axis, quantity demanded on the horizontal axis, a new curve drawn further to the right of the original; B1 because bus and train travel are substitutes, so a rise in the price of one raises demand for the other.
3. [3] B1 inelastic, since the magnitude (0.4) is less than 1; B1 quantity demanded falls proportionally less than the price rises; B1 so total revenue would rise if the firm raised the price.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [4] M1 TR = 800 x 6 = £4,800; M1 TVC = 800 x 2.5 = £2,000, TC = 1,200+2,000 = £3,200; A1 profit = 4,800-3,200 = £1,600; B1 correct method (TR-TC) clearly shown.
6. [2] M1 520-545; A1 -£25bn, a deficit of £25bn.
7. [3] B1 raise the base interest rate; B1 this makes borrowing more expensive and saving more attractive, reducing consumer spending and business investment; B1 lower demand in the economy helps reduce inflationary pressure, bringing inflation back towards target.
8. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
9. [3] M1 CPI = 231/220 x 100 = 105; A1 inflation rate = (231-220)/220 x 100 = 5%; B1 correct method shown.
10. [8] Level 3 (7-8): well-balanced, developed discussion with a clear, justified overall judgement, covering both benefits (e.g. access to cheaper imported goods raising real incomes; new export opportunities/jobs in globally competitive UK sectors such as financial and business services) and drawbacks (e.g. job losses in industries unable to compete with lower-cost overseas production, and downward pressure on some wages), reaching a reasoned conclusion. Level 2 (4-6): reasonable discussion of both sides but less developed or less well-balanced. Level 1 (1-3): one-sided or limited discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.

## Grading
Mark short answers and multiple-choice against the mark schemes; mark calculations with method shown (a correct final answer with no valid method shown earns only what the mark scheme allows for a bare answer); mark extended-response questions by levels. A pass needs at least 50% of the 160 marks overall and at least 40% on each paper. Report the total, the percentage per paper and the weakest topic areas. AQA's grade boundaries change every series, so the course does not claim a predicted grade.
