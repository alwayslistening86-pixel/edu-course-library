# S11_Growth_and_Government_Objectives - Test: Government economic objectives and economic growth

## How to run this

Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how the objective of low unemployment could conflict with the objective of low inflation. [3 marks]
2. Distinguish between nominal GDP and real GDP, explaining why the distinction matters. [3 marks]
3. Nominal GDP rises from £1,800bn to £1,890bn while prices rise by 4% over the same period. Calculate real GDP in year 2 and the approximate real growth rate, showing your working. [4 marks]
4. Explain one benefit and one cost of economic growth. [4 marks]
5. Discuss whether a government should prioritise economic growth over environmental sustainability. [6 marks]
6. GDP per capita is best described as: Choose every correct option.
   A. GDP divided by the size of the population
   B. the total value of a country's exports
   C. the rate of change of prices
   D. government spending divided by tax revenue

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 policies that reduce unemployment (e.g. low interest rates boosting spending and job creation) tend to raise demand in the economy; B1 if demand rises faster than the economy's capacity to supply, this pushes up inflation; B1 so pursuing one objective too strongly can undermine the other.
2. [3] B1 nominal GDP is the value of output measured at current prices, unadjusted for inflation; B1 real GDP adjusts for inflation, measuring the true change in the volume of output; B1 the distinction matters because a rise in nominal GDP could just reflect rising prices, not more output actually being produced.
3. [4] M1 nominal growth = (1,890-1,800)/1,800 x 100 = 5%; M1 real GDP = 1,890/1.04 = £1,817.3bn; A1 real growth = (1,817.3-1,800)/1,800 x 100 ≈ 0.96% (accept ~1%); A1 correct explanation that most of the nominal 5% growth was inflation, not real growth.
4. [4] B1 benefit, e.g. rising average living standards/incomes, or more jobs, or higher tax revenue without raising tax rates; B1 correctly explained; B1 cost, e.g. inflationary pressure, greater inequality if gains are unevenly shared, or environmental damage; B1 correctly explained.
5. [6] Level 2 (4-6): balanced discussion weighing growth's benefits (living standards, jobs, tax revenue) against environmental costs (resource depletion, pollution, long-term damage to future growth potential), recognising these can conflict and that policy (e.g. green growth, regulation) can attempt to balance both, with a reasoned overall judgement. Level 1 (1-3): one-sided or limited discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.
6. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
