# S07_Market_Structures - Practice: Market structures: competitive and non-competitive markets

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with full working before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Identify whether each of these is closer to a competitive or a non-competitive market structure, giving one reason: (a) an independent market selling fresh fruit and vegetables; (b) the national rail network in an area served by only one train operator. [3 marks]
2. Explain why firms in a monopoly can generally charge higher prices than firms in a competitive market. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [3] B1 (a) competitive, with a valid reason, e.g. many sellers, similar products, low barriers to entry; B1 (b) non-competitive, with a valid reason, e.g. one dominant supplier/high barriers to entry; B1 both parts correctly identified (competitive and non-competitive respectively).
2. [2] B1 a monopoly faces little or no competition/has high barriers to entry protecting it; B1 so it has significant power to set (rather than take) price, and consumers have no easy alternative supplier to switch to.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt (including full working for any calculation) before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
