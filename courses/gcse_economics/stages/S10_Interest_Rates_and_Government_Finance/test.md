# S10_Interest_Rates_and_Government_Finance - Test: Interest rates and government income and expenditure

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-response questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how a rise in interest rates is likely to affect consumer spending. [3 marks]
2. Explain how a fall in interest rates is likely to affect business investment. [2 marks]
3. £8,000 is saved at a simple interest rate of 2.5% per year for 4 years. Calculate the total interest earned and the final balance, showing your working. [3 marks]
4. Explain what is meant by a progressive tax, and give an example of one used in the UK. [3 marks]
5. State two main sources of UK government revenue and two main areas of UK government spending. [4 marks]
6. Which of these is an indirect tax? Choose every correct option.
   A. VAT
   B. income tax
   C. National Insurance
   D. corporation tax

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 saving becomes more rewarding, encouraging consumers to save more; B1 borrowing becomes more expensive (higher repayments), discouraging borrowing; B1 both effects reduce consumer spending overall.
2. [2] B1 borrowing to invest becomes cheaper; B1 so firms are more likely to invest in new machinery/expansion.
3. [3] M1 interest per year = 8,000 x 2.5% = £200; A1 total interest = 200 x 4 = £800; A1 final balance = 8,000+800 = £8,800.
4. [3] B1 a progressive tax takes a higher proportion (percentage) of income as income rises; B1 example, e.g. UK income tax, with a tax-free allowance and rising rate bands; B1 correct link, e.g. this helps reduce income inequality since higher earners pay a larger share of their income.
5. [4] B1 revenue, e.g. income tax; B1 revenue, e.g. VAT/National Insurance/corporation tax; B1 spending, e.g. the NHS/health; B1 spending, e.g. welfare/pensions/education (any two each, correctly categorised).
6. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
