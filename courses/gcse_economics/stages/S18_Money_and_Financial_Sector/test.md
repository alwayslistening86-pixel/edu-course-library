## Test items
1. Explain what is meant by money acting as a 'medium of exchange', and why this function is important. [2 marks]
2. Distinguish between money's role as a store of value and its role as a unit of account. [3 marks]
3. Explain two roles played by the Bank of England. [4 marks]
4. Explain why commercial banks and building societies are important to the wider economy. [3 marks]
5. Which UK institution sets the base interest rate through its Monetary Policy Committee? Choose every correct option.
   A. the Bank of England
   B. the Treasury
   C. a commercial high street bank
   D. the Office for National Statistics

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 money is universally accepted in payment for goods/services; B1 this avoids the need for barter/a direct swap of goods (a 'double coincidence of wants'), making trade far easier.
2. [3] B1 store of value: money can be held and retains (broadly) its value for use later; B1 unit of account: money is a common measure allowing the value of different goods/services to be compared; B1 both roles correctly and clearly distinguished.
3. [4] B1 setting the base interest rate (via the Monetary Policy Committee) to influence inflation/the wider economy; B1 correctly explained; B1 acting as lender of last resort / overseeing financial stability, or issuing banknotes; B1 correctly explained.
4. [3] B1 they channel funds from savers (who have a surplus) to borrowers (who want to spend/invest); B1 by taking deposits and paying interest, then lending that money out at a higher rate of interest; B1 without this, saving and borrowing/investment would be far harder to coordinate, holding back spending, investment and growth.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
