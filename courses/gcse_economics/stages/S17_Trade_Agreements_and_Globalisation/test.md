# S17_Trade_Agreements_and_Globalisation - Test: Free-trade agreements and globalisation

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-response questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain what is meant by free trade, and one argument in favour of it. [3 marks]
2. Explain what a free-trade agreement is, using the EU as an example. [3 marks]
3. Explain two drivers of globalisation. [4 marks]
4. Discuss the benefits and drawbacks of globalisation for consumers and workers in a developed country such as the UK. [6 marks]
5. Which of these is most likely to be a drawback of globalisation for workers in a less developed country? Choose every correct option.
   A. risk of exploitation through low wages or poor working conditions in some cases
   B. guaranteed higher wages for every worker
   C. an end to all foreign investment
   D. no effect on the environment

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 free trade is trade between countries without barriers such as tariffs or quotas; B1 argument in favour, e.g. it allows countries to fully exploit specialisation/comparative advantage; B1 correctly explained, e.g. raising global efficiency/living standards.
2. [3] B1 a formal agreement between countries to reduce/remove trade barriers between themselves; B1 the EU is an example, historically a single market with free movement of goods/services/capital/people between members; B1 correct further detail, e.g. a customs union with a common external tariff on non-members.
3. [4] B1 improving technology, e.g. cheaper/faster transport and communication (including the internet); B1 correctly explained; B1 the growth of multinational companies shifting production to lower-cost countries/coordinating global supply chains; B1 correctly explained.
4. [6] Level 2 (4-6): balanced discussion, e.g. consumers benefit from wider choice and generally lower prices as goods can be produced more cheaply abroad, but some domestic workers/industries lose out to overseas competition (structural unemployment), with a reasoned overall judgement on balance. Level 1 (1-3): one-sided or limited discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
