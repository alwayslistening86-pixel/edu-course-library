## Test items
1. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of privatisation and deregulation as a supply-side policy. [4 marks]
2. Explain how a subsidy could be used to correct the market failure caused by a positive externality, such as staff training. [3 marks]
3. Explain two supply-side policies a government could use to raise the economy's long-run productive potential. [4 marks]
4. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of using regulation, rather than taxation, to reduce a negative externality such as air pollution. [6 marks]
5. Which of these is a supply-side policy aimed at raising the economy's long-run productive potential, rather than managing demand? Choose every correct option.
   A. increased government investment in education and training
   B. a cut in the Bank of England's base interest rate
   C. an increase in government spending on unemployment benefits
   D. a rise in VAT

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 advantage, e.g. increased competition can raise efficiency and lower prices for consumers; B1 correctly explained; B1 disadvantage, e.g. private firms may prioritise profit over public interest, e.g. under-investing in an essential service; B1 correctly explained (accept reduced regulation allowing greater negative externalities).
2. [3] B1 a subsidy lowers the cost of providing training to the firm; B1 encouraging the firm to provide more training than it otherwise would, closer to the socially optimal level; B1 because the market alone under-provides training, as the firm does not capture the wider benefit to other employers/the economy.
3. [4] B1 investment in education/training, raising the skills and productivity of the workforce; B1 correctly explained; B1 lower direct taxes (income tax or tax on business profits), increasing the incentive to work/invest; B1 correctly explained (accept trade union reform or privatisation/deregulation).
4. [6] Level 2 (4-6): balanced discussion, e.g. regulation (a limit or ban) gives certainty over the maximum harm allowed and does not rely on firms responding 'correctly' to a price signal, but can be costly/rigid to enforce and does not raise revenue the way a tax does; taxation lets the market find its own least-cost way to reduce the externality and raises revenue, but requires the tax to be set at roughly the right level to work well; reasoned overall judgement given. Level 1 (1-3): one-sided or limited discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
