## Test items
1. Distinguish between a fixed cost and a variable cost, giving one example of each. [2 marks]
2. A firm sells 1,000 units at £12 each. Fixed costs are £2,000 and variable costs are £5 per unit. Calculate total revenue, total cost, average cost and profit, showing your working. [5 marks]
3. Explain two different objectives a firm might pursue other than maximising profit. [4 marks]
4. A pharmaceutical firm holds the only licence to produce a life-saving drug with very inelastic demand. Discuss the moral and ethical considerations this raises for the firm's pricing decision. [4 marks]
5. A firm's total revenue is £8,000 and its total cost is £9,500. What is its result? Choose every correct option.
   A. a loss of £1,500
   B. a profit of £1,500
   C. a profit of £8,000
   D. break-even

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 a fixed cost does not change with output, e.g. rent; B1 a variable cost changes directly with output, e.g. raw materials.
2. [5] M1 TR = 1,000 x 12 = £12,000; M1 TVC = 1,000 x 5 = £5,000; M1 TC = 2,000+5,000 = £7,000; A1 AC = 7,000/1,000 = £7; A1 profit = 12,000-7,000 = £5,000.
3. [4] B1 growth -- expanding market share/size, sometimes accepting lower short-term profit; B1 correctly explained; B1 survival -- especially in a downturn or against strong competition, simply staying in business; B1 correctly explained.
4. [4] Level 2 (3-4): balanced discussion, e.g. the firm could exploit inelastic demand by charging a very high price to maximise profit, but this risks patients being unable to afford a life-saving treatment and reputational/regulatory backlash; some firms choose lower prices or subsidised access for ethical reasons or to protect their reputation. Level 1 (1-2): one-sided or limited point made.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
