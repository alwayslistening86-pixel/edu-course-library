## Test items
1. Explain why the demand curve slopes downwards from left to right. [2 marks]
2. Distinguish between a movement along the supply curve and a shift of the supply curve, giving a cause of each. [4 marks]
3. A council caps rents on a type of flat below the free-market equilibrium price. Using the concept of excess demand, explain the likely consequence. [3 marks]
4. Explain how equilibrium price is determined in a free market, and what happens if price is initially set above equilibrium. [4 marks]
5. At a market price above equilibrium, which of these occurs? Choose every correct option.
   A. excess supply, so price tends to fall
   B. excess demand, so price tends to rise
   C. the market is already balanced
   D. quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 as price falls, quantity demanded rises (and vice versa) -- the law of demand; B1 lower prices make a good more attractive/affordable to more consumers.
2. [4] B1 a movement along the curve is caused by a change in the good's own price; B1 example, e.g. price rises, quantity supplied extends; B1 a shift of the curve is caused by a non-price factor, e.g. a change in production costs, technology, number of producers, or a tax/subsidy; B1 example correctly stated, e.g. a subsidy shifts supply right.
3. [3] B1 at the capped price, quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied -- excess demand; B1 because the price is now below the level that would balance the market, more tenants want the flats than landlords are willing to let at that price; B1 consequence, e.g. a shortage of available flats/long waiting lists, since price can no longer rise to ration demand.
4. [4] B1 equilibrium is the price where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, at the intersection of the demand and supply curves; B1 above equilibrium, quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded -- excess supply; B1 unsold stock builds up; B1 producers cut price until equilibrium is restored.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
