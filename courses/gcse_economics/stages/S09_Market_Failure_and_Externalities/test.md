## Test items
1. Define market failure. [2 marks]
2. Distinguish between private cost and social cost, and use this to define a negative externality. [3 marks]
3. Explain, with an example, a positive consumption externality. [3 marks]
4. Explain two ways a government could intervene to correct the market failure caused by a good with a negative externality, such as cigarettes. [4 marks]
5. Discuss whether government intervention is always the best way to deal with market failure caused by externalities. [6 marks]
6. Which of these best describes a negative production externality? Choose every correct option.
   A. a firm's pollution imposes a cost on third parties not reflected in the market price
   B. a firm pays higher wages to attract better workers
   C. a consumer buying a good that only affects themselves
   D. a government subsidy lowering a firm's production costs

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 the inability of the free market to allocate resources efficiently; B1 leading to over- or under-production/consumption of a good relative to the socially optimal level.
2. [3] B1 private cost is the cost borne only by the buyer/seller directly involved in a transaction; B1 social cost is the total cost to society, including effects on third parties; B1 a negative externality exists where social cost exceeds private cost (a third party bears an unpaid-for cost).
3. [3] B1 a positive consumption externality is where the social benefit of consuming a good exceeds the private benefit to the consumer; B1 because a third party also gains a benefit not reflected in the market price; B1 correct example, e.g. vaccination reducing others' risk of catching a disease, or education raising others' benefit from a more informed/skilled society.
4. [4] B1 a tax (e.g. tobacco duty) to raise the price and discourage consumption towards the socially optimal level; B1 correctly explained; B1 regulation, e.g. banning advertising or restricting where the good can be consumed/sold; B1 correctly explained (accept information campaigns).
5. [6] Level 2 (4-6): balanced discussion, e.g. intervention can correct the misallocation (tax/subsidy/regulation moving output towards the social optimum) but also has costs/risks -- government may misjudge the correct level of tax/subsidy, intervention has administrative/enforcement costs, and firms/consumers may find ways around regulation; a reasoned overall judgement given. Level 1 (1-3): one-sided or limited discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.
6. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
