## Test items
1. Distinguish between production and productivity, with an example of each changing independently. [3 marks]
2. Explain two factors that can raise a firm's labour productivity. [4 marks]
3. Explain what is meant by economies of scale, and describe two different types. [5 marks]
4. Explain why a very large firm might experience diseconomies of scale. [3 marks]
5. Which of these is a technical economy of scale? Choose every correct option.
   A. using specialised, large-scale production-line machinery that only pays off at high output
   B. borrowing money at a lower rate of interest because the firm is large
   C. buying raw materials in bulk at a lower price per unit
   D. hiring a specialist marketing department

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 production is the total quantity produced; B1 productivity is output per unit of input (e.g. per worker); B1 correct example, e.g. hiring more workers can raise production without raising productivity, or training existing workers can raise productivity without hiring anyone new.
2. [4] B1 more/better capital (machinery, technology) giving each worker more to work with; B1 correctly explained; B1 training/skills of the workforce, or worker motivation; B1 correctly explained.
3. [5] B1 economies of scale are falls in long-run average cost as the scale of output increases; B1 e.g. purchasing economies -- bulk-buying raw materials at a lower price per unit; B1 correctly explained; B1 e.g. technical economies -- large-scale specialised machinery/production lines become worthwhile; B1 correctly explained (accept financial, managerial or risk-bearing economies).
4. [3] B1 communication problems -- harder for information/instructions to flow accurately through a huge organisation; B1 poor motivation/coordination -- workers can feel undervalued, and coordinating many departments/sites becomes harder; B1 these raise average cost, outweighing further economies of scale.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
