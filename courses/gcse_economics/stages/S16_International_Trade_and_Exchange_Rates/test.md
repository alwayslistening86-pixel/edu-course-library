# S16_International_Trade_and_Exchange_Rates - Test: International trade and exchange rates

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-response questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no calculator unless the question is a genuinely complex calculation. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why international trade allows countries to achieve a higher standard of living than if each tried to be self-sufficient. [3 marks]
2. Explain how an exchange rate is determined under a floating exchange rate system. [3 marks]
3. At £1 = $1.50, a UK-made product priced at £600 is sold to US customers. Calculate its price in dollars. If the pound then weakens to £1 = $1.35, calculate the new dollar price, and explain the likely effect on US demand for the product. [4 marks]
4. Explain one effect of a stronger pound (appreciation) on UK consumers and one effect on UK exporters. [4 marks]
5. If the pound depreciates (weakens) against the dollar, which of these is most likely? Choose every correct option.
   A. UK exports become cheaper for US buyers, and imports from the US become more expensive for UK buyers
   B. UK exports become more expensive for US buyers, and imports become cheaper
   C. there is no effect on trade
   D. only imports are affected, not exports

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 countries can specialise in producing what they are relatively best at, using resources more efficiently; B1 and trade for the goods/services other countries produce more efficiently; B1 raising total output/choice/living standards compared with self-sufficiency.
2. [3] B1 by the supply of, and demand for, the currency in the foreign exchange market; B1 higher demand for the currency (e.g. from exports/investment inflows) pushes its value up; B1 higher supply of the currency being sold (e.g. imports/investment outflows) pushes its value down.
3. [4] M1 600 x 1.50 = $900; M1 600 x 1.35 = $810; B1 the product is now cheaper in dollar terms; B1 so US demand for it is likely to rise (the UK exporter becomes more price-competitive).
4. [4] B1 consumers: imports become cheaper in pound terms; B1 correctly explained, e.g. benefiting consumers buying foreign goods; B1 exporters: UK goods become more expensive in foreign-currency terms; B1 correctly explained, e.g. reducing overseas demand/harming competitiveness.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
