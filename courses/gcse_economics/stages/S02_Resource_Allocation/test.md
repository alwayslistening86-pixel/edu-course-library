## Test items
1. Explain how the price mechanism allocates resources in a market economy, with an example. [3 marks]
2. Distinguish between a factor market and a product market, giving an example of each. [2 marks]
3. Distinguish between a good and a service, giving one example of each. [2 marks]
4. Explain two benefits and one cost of specialisation and division of labour. [4 marks]
5. Which sector of the economy is now the largest in the UK by output and employment? Choose every correct option.
   A. the tertiary sector
   B. the primary sector
   C. the secondary sector
   D. the public sector

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 prices act as signals/incentives to producers and consumers; B1 a rise in price for a good signals higher demand and encourages producers to reallocate resources towards supplying more of it (and vice versa for a fall); B1 correct example, e.g. rising demand for electric cars leading firms to shift investment/labour into producing them.
2. [2] B1 a factor market is where factors of production (e.g. labour) are bought and sold, e.g. the labour market; B1 a product market is where finished goods/services are bought and sold, e.g. the market for laptops.
3. [2] B1 a good is a physical/tangible item that can be stored, e.g. a television; B1 a service is intangible and usually consumed as it is produced, e.g. a taxi ride.
4. [4] B1 benefit: workers become faster/more skilled at their narrow task, raising output/productivity; B1 benefit: allows investment in task-specific machinery/countries to trade what they produce best, raising living standards; B1 cost: work can become repetitive/monotonous, reducing motivation; B1 (alt cost) overdependence on one skill/industry risks structural unemployment if demand for it falls.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
