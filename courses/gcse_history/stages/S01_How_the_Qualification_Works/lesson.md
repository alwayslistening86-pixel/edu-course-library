# S01_How_the_Qualification_Works - Lesson: How the qualification works: three papers, four objectives, SPaG and extended writing

## Goal
The learner can describe the three Edexcel papers, what each assessment objective rewards, how the 8 SPaG marks are earned and how to build a sustained extended answer.

## Syllabus items taught here
- H-STRUCT - Qualification structure: three papers, 168 marks, weightings
- H-AO1 - AO1: knowledge and understanding of key features and characteristics: Recall and select precise knowledge of the periods studied
- H-AO2 - AO2: explain and analyse using second-order concepts: Explain and analyse events and periods using causation, consequence, change, continuity, similarity, difference and significance
- H-AO3 - AO3: analyse, evaluate and use contemporary sources: Make substantiated judgements from sources in the context of events studied
- H-AO4 - AO4: analyse and evaluate interpretations: Judge how and why interpretations differ, using own knowledge
- H-SPAG - Spelling, punctuation, grammar and use of specialist terminology (8 marks): Earn the 8 SPaG marks on Paper 1 Q5/6 and Paper 3 Q3(d)
- H-EXT - Extended writing, sustained reasoning and stimulus points: Construct a coherent, relevant, substantiated line of reasoning in 8+ mark answers

## How to teach this
Open with: 'If I said a GCSE History exam only tests whether you can remember facts, what would you say to that?' Let the learner argue, then show that only 35% of the marks are for knowledge (AO1) and that the same 35% again is for explaining and analysing (AO2), with 15% each for sources (AO3) and interpretations (AO4). Keep the specification's table on screen while teaching.

#### H-STRUCT Qualification structure: three papers, 168 marks, weightings
The Edexcel GCSE (9-1) History 1HI0 is assessed entirely by three written papers, all sat in May/June of one year. Paper 1 (thematic study and historic environment) lasts 1 hour 20 minutes and is worth 52 marks (30% of the qualification); for this learner it is Crime and punishment in Britain, c1000-present with Whitechapel, c1870-c1900. Paper 2 (period study and British depth study) lasts 1 hour 50 minutes and is worth 64 marks (40%); here it is The American West, c1835-c1895 (Booklet P) and Early Elizabethan England, 1558-88 (Booklet B). Paper 3 (modern depth study) lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 52 marks (30%); here it is Weimar and Nazi Germany, 1918-39. The total is 168 marks, of which 8 are for SPaG. Marks are added into one total and graded 9 to 1; individual papers are not graded. There is no coursework.

#### H-AO1 AO1: knowledge and understanding of key features and characteristics
AO1 (35% of the qualification) rewards demonstrating knowledge and understanding of the key features and characteristics of the periods studied. It is examined alone in the 'describe two features' questions (for example, two features of the beat constable's work in Whitechapel) and it underpins every explanation question, because an AO2 answer with no precise facts cannot score highly. Precision means names, dates, places, numbers and technical terms, such as 'the Homestead Act of 1862 gave 160 acres to settlers who farmed it for five years'. Teach the learner to make each fact do a job: a feature is a fact plus a short development that shows it belongs to the period.

#### H-AO2 AO2: explain and analyse using second-order concepts
AO2 (35%) asks the learner to explain and analyse events and periods using second-order concepts: causation, consequence, change, continuity, similarity, difference and significance. Every 12-mark 'Explain why' question and every 16-mark 'How far do you agree' question is AO1 plus AO2. Analysis means connecting facts into reasons ('the railroad made cattle drives to Abilene profitable, which is why McCoy built stockyards there') and ranking those reasons. A mark-scheme-style level ladder rewards connected, sustained analysis, and a final judgement that is supported. Concepts are taught in detail in the next stage.

#### H-AO3 AO3: analyse, evaluate and use contemporary sources
AO3 (15%) rewards analysing, evaluating and using sources contemporary to the period to make substantiated judgements in the context of the events studied. It is tested on Paper 1 (two source questions: how useful are Sources A and B, and a follow-up enquiry) and on Paper 3 (an inference question and a usefulness question). Judgements must be substantiated: use the source's content, its provenance (who, when, why, what kind of source) and the learner's own contextual knowledge. A source is not useful or useless in general; it is useful for a specific enquiry. Precise practice on source skills follows in the source-skills stage and again in each paper's question-type stage.

#### H-AO4 AO4: analyse, evaluate and make judgements about interpretations
AO4 (15%) is tested only in Paper 3 (Weimar and Nazi Germany here). Students study two later written interpretations, explain how they differ, suggest why they differ, and evaluate one of them using the other and their own knowledge. Interpretations differ legitimately because historians ask different questions, select different evidence, weigh it differently, write for different audiences, and hold different values or hold a different viewpoint. Interpretations are not the same as sources: they are later accounts, so provenance is about the historian's approach, not eyewitness position. Never say an interpretation is wrong merely because it is not a primary source.

#### H-SPAG Spelling, punctuation, grammar and use of specialist terminology (8 marks)
Of the 168 marks, 8 are for spelling, punctuation, grammar and specialist terminology (SPaG): up to 4 on Paper 1 Question 5/6 and up to 4 on Paper 3 Question 3(d). They are awarded on a performance scale: threshold (1 mark), intermediate (2-3) and high (4), broadly rewarding accurate spelling and punctuation, clear control of grammar and meaning, and a wide range of specialist terms such as 'transportation', 'Bloody Code', 'Reichstag', 'Manifest Destiny'. These marks sit on the two long judgement answers, so the learner should leave a minute to proofread, spell key terms correctly and use specialist vocabulary deliberately. Paper 2 has no SPaG marks.

#### H-EXT Extended writing, sustained reasoning and stimulus points
Questions targeting AO2 and worth 8 or more marks reward the construction of a coherent, relevant, substantiated and logically structured line of reasoning. Many extended questions give stimulus points (bullet prompts). The learner may use them, but must include additional points of their own, otherwise the answer is penalised in the mark scheme. Teach a simple frame: point, precise evidence, explanation of why it matters for the question, link back to the question, then a reasoned judgement or conclusion that weighs the factors. Plan for 90 seconds, write, and check the conclusion answers the exact question asked, not a neighbouring one.

## Explicitly not here
The seven second-order concepts and source/interpretation skills come next; content stages begin after that. Question-by-question technique is taught inside each paper's own stages.
