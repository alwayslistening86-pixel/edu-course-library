# S31_Weimar_and_Nazi_Germany_Paper_3_Question_Types - Lesson: Paper 3 question types: sources, causation and interpretations

## Goal
The learner can answer every remaining Paper 3 question type: explain why (causation), source usefulness, how interpretations differ, why they differ, and the extended how-far-do-you-agree interpretation question with SPaG.

## Syllabus items taught here
- Q-P3-2 - Paper 3 Q2(a)/(b): explain why (causation, choice of two): Answer the 12-mark causation question
- Q-P3-3a - Paper 3 Q3(a): how useful are Sources A and B: Answer the 8-mark usefulness question
- Q-P3-3b - Paper 3 Q3(b): how do Interpretations 1 and 2 differ: Answer the 4-mark 'how differ' question
- Q-P3-3c - Paper 3 Q3(c): suggest why the interpretations differ: Answer the 4-mark 'why differ' question
- Q-P3-3d - Paper 3 Q3(d): how far do you agree with Interpretation 2 (16 plus 4 SPaG): Answer the 20-mark interpretation-evaluation question

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Paper 3 is worth 52 marks in 1 hour 30 minutes. Today we learn exactly what each remaining question wants: explaining why, judging sources, and, uniquely to this paper, comparing historical interpretations.'

#### Q-P3-2 Paper 3 Q2(a)/(b): explain why (causation, choice of two)
Q2 is a 12-mark AO1/AO2 causation question, usually offering a choice of two topics (the learner answers one), with stimulus points, asking the learner to explain why something happened (for example 'Explain why the Nazis were able to establish a dictatorship by 1934'). Technique matches the Paper 1 and Paper 2 equivalents: address each stimulus point with full 'because' explanation, add at least one own point, and show connections between causes where relevant, organising by cause rather than by narrating a simple timeline; two or three fully explained causes score more highly than many undeveloped ones, and the learner should choose whichever of the two offered topics they can support with the most specific, accurate detail.

#### Q-P3-3a Paper 3 Q3(a): how useful are Sources A and B
Q3(a) is an 8-mark AO3 question giving two contemporary sources connected to Weimar or Nazi Germany and asking how useful they are for a stated enquiry, following the same technique as the Paper 1 equivalent: comment on content, provenance (nature, origin, purpose) and reach a judgement on usefulness for the specific enquiry named, using own knowledge to test what each source shows, and cross-referencing the two sources against each other. A strong answer avoids generic claims about sources being useful 'because they are primary sources' and instead ties usefulness closely to the exact enquiry focus given in the question, which will typically concern an aspect of Weimar or Nazi Germany specifically.

#### Q-P3-3b Paper 3 Q3(b): how do Interpretations 1 and 2 differ
Q3(b) is a 4-mark AO4 question, unique to Paper 3, giving two short historical interpretations (extracts summarising differing historians' views on an aspect of Weimar or Nazi Germany) and asking the learner to identify and explain how they differ. Technique: identify the main point of disagreement precisely (not just a vague sense that they differ), and support the identification with brief reference to what each interpretation actually claims, for example one interpretation emphasising Hitler's personal skill and another emphasising structural weaknesses of the Weimar state. Avoid simply summarising each interpretation separately without stating the specific difference between them.

#### Q-P3-3c Paper 3 Q3(c): suggest why the interpretations differ
Q3(c) is a 4-mark AO4 question asking the learner to suggest reasons why the two given interpretations differ, for example because the historians used different evidence or sources, focused on different factors or time periods, or interpreted the same evidence differently due to their own perspective or the purpose of their writing. Technique: suggest at least one plausible, specific reason (different emphasis, different evidence, different historical approach) rather than a vague claim that 'historians always disagree'; where possible, tie the reason to something in the content of the given interpretations themselves rather than only general statements about historical interpretation in the abstract.

#### Q-P3-3d Paper 3 Q3(d): how far do you agree with Interpretation 2 (16 plus 4 SPaG)
Q3(d) is the 20-mark question (16 content marks plus 4 SPaG), asking the learner how far they agree with one of the given interpretations, using both the interpretations themselves and the learner's own contextual knowledge of Weimar and Nazi Germany. Technique: explain what the chosen interpretation claims, use own knowledge to support or challenge specific parts of it, consider the other interpretation's alternative view for balance, and reach and justify a clear final judgement on how far the interpretation is convincing, rather than simply agreeing or disagreeing without evidence. As with Paper 1's equivalent extended question, plan first, use full sentences and paragraphs, and use specialist terminology (interpretation, provenance, causation) accurately and correctly for the SPaG marks.

## Explicitly not here
The full cumulative exam, which draws on all three papers together.
