# S03_Crime_Process_of_Change_and_Medieval_Crime - Practice: Crime and punishment: the process of change and medieval crime, c1000-c1500

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Sort into person, property and authority: murder of a neighbour, stealing a sheep, minting false coins, killing a deer in a royal forest, burning a barn.
2. Explain in three sentences why many medieval peasants did not think of poaching as a real crime.
3. State two ways in which the Norman Conquest changed the law on crime, with a date for one of them.
4. Choose one of the three key factors and give an example of it from any period you already know, then explain what it caused.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
