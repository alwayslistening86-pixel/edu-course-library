# S28_Fieldwork_Evaluation_and_School_Enquiries - Lesson: Evaluating fieldwork, plus the Cleveleys and Salford Quays enquiries in full

## Goal
The learner suggests other useful data and judges the reliability of conclusions, and can plan, justify, analyse and evaluate both the Cleveleys coastal enquiry and the Salford Quays regeneration enquiry as completed pieces of work.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.2.6c - Enquiry strand 6: suggestions for other useful data: propose extensions
- 3.3.2.6d - Enquiry strand 6: how reliable the conclusions were: judge reliability
- 3.3.2.7a - Cleveleys coastal enquiry: question, methods and data: plan and justify the Cleveleys enquiry
- 3.3.2.7b - Cleveleys coastal enquiry: analysis, conclusion and evaluation: analyse and evaluate
- 3.3.2.8a - Salford Quays regeneration enquiry: question, methods and data: plan and justify the Salford Quays enquiry
- 3.3.2.8b - Salford Quays regeneration enquiry: analysis, conclusion and evaluation: analyse and evaluate

## How to teach this
Ask: 'If the Cleveleys fieldwork were repeated on a different day, with different weather or tide conditions, would you expect exactly the same results? What does that tell you about how confident you can be in your conclusion?'

#### 3.3.2.6c Enquiry strand 6: suggestions for other useful data
Evaluating an enquiry includes suggesting what other data could usefully be collected to extend or strengthen it: for example, repeating the Cleveleys beach profile survey at a different time of year to check whether the pattern is seasonal, or collecting resident interview data at Salford Quays to add a social perspective alongside the environmental quality survey.

#### 3.3.2.6d Enquiry strand 6: how reliable the conclusions were
Judging reliability means considering how confident the enquiry's conclusions can genuinely be, given the sample size, the data collection problems and limitations already identified, and whether the data collected was sufficient and appropriate to properly test the original hypothesis; a conclusion drawn from a small, single-day sample is less reliable than one supported by a larger, repeated data set.

#### 3.3.2.7a Cleveleys coastal enquiry: question, methods and data
The Cleveleys coastal enquiry investigated a question such as 'How effective is coastal management at Cleveleys?', linked to coastal process and management theory. Primary data (such as beach profiles and sediment size measured using a systematic sample along transects at defended and less-defended points) and secondary data (such as historical maps showing past erosion) were collected at fieldwork locations chosen to compare areas with different levels of coastal engineering.

#### 3.3.2.7b Cleveleys coastal enquiry: analysis, conclusion and evaluation
Analysis compared beach profiles and sediment characteristics between defended and less-defended sections, using appropriate statistics to summarise the pattern and identifying any anomalies. The conclusion used this evidence to judge how effectively the coastal management scheme was reducing erosion. Evaluation considered the sample size, weather and time constraints on the day, and suggested further data (such as monitoring over a longer period) that would strengthen confidence in the conclusion.

#### 3.3.2.8a Salford Quays regeneration enquiry: question, methods and data
The Salford Quays regeneration enquiry investigated a question such as 'How successful has the regeneration of Salford Quays been?', linked to urban change and regeneration theory. Primary data (such as an environmental quality survey and land-use survey across different zones, using a stratified sample) and secondary data (such as historical maps or reports showing the former dockland use) were collected at locations chosen to represent the different regenerated zones.

#### 3.3.2.8b Salford Quays regeneration enquiry: analysis, conclusion and evaluation
Analysis compared environmental quality and land-use patterns across the different zones sampled, using appropriate statistics and noting any anomalies. The conclusion used this evidence to judge how far regeneration had improved the area compared with its former derelict state. Evaluation considered limitations such as the subjectivity of an environmental quality survey and the sample's size and timing, and suggested further data (such as resident or visitor surveys) that would strengthen the conclusion.
