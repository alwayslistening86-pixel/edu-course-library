# S01_Scheme_of_Assessment_and_AOs - Lesson: How AQA GCSE Geography is assessed: three papers and four assessment objectives

## Goal
The learner can describe the structure, timing and marks of Papers 1-3, name the question types used, and explain what each assessment objective (AO1-AO4) rewards.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 2.2-P1 - Paper 1 structure: Living with the physical environment: know the sections, marks (33/25/30 with two of Q3-5), timing and question types
- 2.2-P2 - Paper 2 structure: Challenges in the human environment: know the sections and marks (33/30/25 with Q3 plus one of Q4-6)
- 2.2-P3 - Paper 3 structure: Geographical applications: know issue evaluation (37 marks), fieldwork (39 marks) and the pre-release booklet
- 2.2-QT - Question types and command words: multiple-choice, short answer, levels of response, extended prose
- 4.2-AO1 - AO1 knowledge of locations, places, processes, environments and scales: recall precise place and process knowledge (15 percent)
- 4.2-AO2 - AO2 geographical understanding and interrelationships: explain concepts and links between places, environments and processes (25 percent)
- 4.2-AO3 - AO3 apply, interpret, analyse and evaluate to make judgements: apply knowledge to information and issues, reach judgements (35 percent incl 10 percent fieldwork)
- 4.2-AO4 - AO4 select and use skills and techniques: use geographical skills and communicate findings (25 percent incl 5 percent fieldwork)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'GCSE Geography has three exams. What do you think each one is about, given the course covers natural hazards, cities, economies and two fieldwork enquiries?' Build the picture before naming papers.

#### 2.2-P1 Paper 1 structure: Living with the physical environment
Paper 1 is called 'Living with the physical environment'. It lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 88 marks, 35% of the GCSE. It has three sections: Section A The challenge of natural hazards, Section B The living world, Section C Physical landscapes in the UK. In Section C the learner answers on the two UK landscape options they studied (here: Coastal and Glacial). Marks are distributed across short-answer, structured and extended-response questions.

#### 2.2-P2 Paper 2 structure: Challenges in the human environment
Paper 2 is called 'Challenges in the human environment'. It lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 88 marks, 35% of the GCSE. It has three sections: Section A Urban issues and challenges, Section B The changing economic world, Section C The challenge of resource management (with the learner's chosen option, here Water). Every section is compulsory; within Section C only the studied option is examined.

#### 2.2-P3 Paper 3 structure: Geographical applications
Paper 3 is called 'Geographical applications'. It lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 76 marks, 30% of the GCSE. Section A is Issue evaluation, based on a pre-released resources booklet the learner studies beforehand, worth 37 marks. Section B is Fieldwork, examining the two compulsory enquiries (here Cleveleys and Salford Quays) with unfamiliar fieldwork contexts too, worth 39 marks.

#### 2.2-QT Question types and command words
Across the three papers, question types include multiple-choice, short answer (define, state, give reasons), levels-of-response extended prose (6, 8, 9 or 12 marks), and map/graph/photograph-based questions. Command words matter: 'describe' asks what a resource shows; 'explain' asks why or how, using connectives like 'because' and 'this leads to'; 'assess' or 'evaluate' asks for a weighed judgement with a conclusion; 'suggest' allows reasoned application to an unfamiliar case.

#### 4.2-AO1 AO1 knowledge of locations, places, processes, environments and scales
AO1 is worth 15% of the GCSE. It rewards accurate recall: naming specific places (Lagos, not just 'a city in an LIC'), stating correct processes (hydraulic action, not just 'erosion'), and giving real figures from case studies (magnitude, death toll, percentage change). Precision is what separates a Level 1 from a Level 3 answer at AO1.

#### 4.2-AO2 AO2 geographical understanding and interrelationships
AO2 is worth 25%. It rewards explaining how and why things happen and link together: for example, explaining why wealth affects the number of deaths in an earthquake, or how a river of migrants into a city creates both economic opportunity and housing pressure. AO2 answers use connecting words ('because', 'as a result', 'this means that') rather than a list of facts.

#### 4.2-AO3 AO3 apply, interpret, analyse and evaluate to make judgements
AO3 is worth 35%, including a minimum of 10% assessed through fieldwork. It rewards using evidence (a graph, a data table, a case study) to answer an unfamiliar question, weighing options against each other, and reaching a supported judgement rather than just describing both sides. This is the assessment objective most tested in the Paper 3 Issue evaluation section.

#### 4.2-AO4 AO4 select and use skills and techniques
AO4 is worth 25%, including a minimum of 5% assessed through fieldwork. It rewards using and communicating geographical skills: reading grid references and contours from an OS map, plotting and interpreting graphs, calculating statistics, and justifying fieldwork methods. AO4 is examined throughout all three papers wherever a map, graph, photograph or data set appears, not only in a dedicated 'skills' section.

## Explicitly not here
The physical and human content itself starts in the next stage; fieldwork enquiry skills in detail come much later.
