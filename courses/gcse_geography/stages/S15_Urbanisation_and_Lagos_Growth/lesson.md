# S15_Urbanisation_and_Lagos_Growth - Lesson: Global urbanisation trends and the growth of Lagos

## Goal
The learner explains global patterns and causes of urbanisation and the rise of megacities, and describes Lagos's location, importance, causes of growth and social and economic opportunities.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.1a - Global pattern of urban change: describe urbanisation trends
- 3.2.1b - Urban trends in HICs and LICs: compare trends
- 3.2.1c - Factors affecting the rate of urbanisation: migration and natural increase: push-pull theory
- 3.2.1d - The emergence of megacities: define and locate
- 3.2.1e - Lagos: location and importance: regional, national, international
- 3.2.1f - Lagos: causes of growth: natural increase and migration
- 3.2.1g - Lagos: social opportunities of urban growth: services, water, energy
- 3.2.1h - Lagos: economic opportunities of urban growth: industry as a stimulus

## How to teach this
Ask: 'More than half the world's population now lives in cities, a milestone only reached in the past couple of decades. Why might people be leaving the countryside for cities in such large numbers?'

#### 3.2.1a Global pattern of urban change
Urbanisation is the increasing proportion of a country's population living in urban (town and city) areas rather than rural areas. Globally, urbanisation has accelerated rapidly since the mid-twentieth century, and for the first time in history more than half the world's population now lives in urban areas, a proportion that continues to rise, especially in lower-income and newly emerging economies (LICs and NEEs).

#### 3.2.1b Urban trends in HICs and LICs
In higher-income countries (HICs), urbanisation happened earlier (mainly during the Industrial Revolution) and rates of urban growth are now slow, as most people already live in cities; some HIC cities even see counter-urbanisation, as people move out to suburbs or rural areas. In lower-income countries (LICs) and newly emerging economies (NEEs), urbanisation is happening later but much faster, driven by industrialisation and rural-urban migration, often outpacing the ability of city infrastructure and services to keep up.

#### 3.2.1c Factors affecting the rate of urbanisation: migration and natural increase
Two processes drive urban growth. Rural-urban migration is people moving from the countryside to cities, often explained by push-pull theory: push factors (poverty, lack of jobs or services, conflict) drive people away from rural areas, while pull factors (more jobs, higher wages, better healthcare and education) attract them to cities. Natural increase is where a city's birth rate exceeds its death rate, so its population grows from within, in addition to migration.

#### 3.2.1d The emergence of megacities
A megacity is a city with a population of over 10 million people. The number of megacities has grown rapidly, and increasingly they are located in LICs and NEEs rather than HICs, reflecting where the fastest urban growth is now happening. Lagos, Nigeria, is a leading example of a rapidly growing NEE megacity.

#### 3.2.1e Lagos: location and importance
Lagos is located on the Atlantic coast of south-western Nigeria, partly built on islands and lagoons. It is Nigeria's largest city and its commercial and financial centre (though not the political capital, which is Abuja), and one of the most important cities in West Africa for trade, industry and finance, with a major port handling much of Nigeria's international trade.

#### 3.2.1f Lagos: causes of growth
Lagos's rapid growth results from both rural-urban migration (pulled by the prospect of jobs, higher wages and services, and pushed from rural areas by poverty and limited opportunity) and natural increase (a relatively young population with a birth rate exceeding the death rate). Its position as Nigeria's main commercial hub has made it a particularly strong magnet for migrants from across Nigeria and neighbouring West African countries.

#### 3.2.1g Lagos: social opportunities of urban growth
Urban growth has brought social opportunities to Lagos: greater (though still uneven) access to services such as schools and hospitals than in many rural areas, improved access to piped water and electricity in developed parts of the city, and a wider range of social and cultural amenities than rural areas can typically offer.

#### 3.2.1h Lagos: economic opportunities of urban growth
Economic opportunities include a large and growing formal job market in finance, manufacturing and trade, plus a huge informal economy (street trading, small-scale services) that provides income for many without formal jobs. Industry acts as a stimulus for further growth: as factories and businesses employ workers, those workers spend wages locally, creating further jobs in shops and services (the multiplier effect).
