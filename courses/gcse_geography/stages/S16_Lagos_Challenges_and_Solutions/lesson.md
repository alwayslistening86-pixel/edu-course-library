# S16_Lagos_Challenges_and_Solutions - Lesson: Challenges of rapid growth in Lagos, and urban planning solutions

## Goal
The learner explains the challenges of squatter settlements, service provision, unemployment, crime and environmental issues in Lagos, and evaluates urban planning improving quality of life for the urban poor.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.1i - Lagos: managing slums and squatter settlements: Makoko and others
- 3.2.1j - Lagos: clean water, sanitation and energy: challenge of provision
- 3.2.1k - Lagos: access to health and education: challenge of provision
- 3.2.1l - Lagos: unemployment and crime: informal economy and crime
- 3.2.1m - Lagos: environmental issues: waste, pollution, congestion
- 3.2.1n - Urban planning improving quality of life for the urban poor: Lagos BRT and Makoko

## How to teach this
Ask: 'If a city's population grows faster than its housing, water pipes and hospitals can be built, what do you think happens?'

#### 3.2.1i Lagos: managing slums and squatter settlements
Lagos's rapid, largely unplanned growth has produced extensive slum and squatter settlements, where housing is self-built, often from scrap materials, without formal land ownership, planning permission or reliable infrastructure. Makoko, a well-known example, is a low-income settlement built partly on stilts over the lagoon, home to a large population living with very limited formal services.

#### 3.2.1j Lagos: clean water, sanitation and energy
Providing clean water, sanitation and reliable electricity has struggled to keep pace with Lagos's growth. Many residents, especially in informal settlements, lack piped water and instead rely on wells, water vendors or unsafe sources; sanitation is often inadequate, raising disease risk; and power cuts are common, so many households and businesses depend on expensive private generators.

#### 3.2.1k Lagos: access to health and education
Rapid population growth has placed severe pressure on health and education services: hospitals and clinics, especially in informal settlements, can be overcrowded, under-resourced or simply too far away, and schools can be oversubscribed or unaffordable for the poorest families, meaning access to good healthcare and education in Lagos is highly uneven between wealthier and poorer areas.

#### 3.2.1l Lagos: unemployment and crime
Formal employment has not grown fast enough to absorb Lagos's rapidly rising population, leading many residents to rely on the informal economy (unregistered street trading and small services) for income, which is often insecure and low-paid. Poverty, unemployment and overcrowding are also linked to higher crime rates in parts of the city, particularly in some of the poorer, less-policed informal settlements.

#### 3.2.1m Lagos: environmental issues
Environmental issues include waste management struggling to keep up with the volume produced (leading to informal dumping and blocked drains, which worsens flooding), air and water pollution from traffic, industry and inadequate sanitation, and traffic congestion, since road infrastructure has not expanded to match the growing population and vehicle numbers.

#### 3.2.1n Urban planning improving quality of life for the urban poor
Lagos has introduced schemes to improve quality of life for the urban poor: the Lagos Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) system, introduced from 2008, provides dedicated bus lanes offering cheaper, faster and more reliable transport for lower-income residents who cannot afford private cars. In Makoko, small-scale upgrading and community projects (such as improved sanitation facilities and a floating school project) have aimed to improve basic living conditions without displacing residents, though large-scale improvement remains limited by the settlement's informal, unplanned nature.
