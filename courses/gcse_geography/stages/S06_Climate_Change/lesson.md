# S06_Climate_Change - Lesson: Evidence, causes and management of climate change

## Goal
The learner explains the evidence for climate change over time, distinguishes natural from human causes, and evaluates mitigation and adaptation strategies.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.1.1.4a - Evidence for climate change from the Quaternary to the present: use ice cores, records and other evidence
- 3.1.1.4b - Natural causes of climate change: orbital changes, volcanic activity, solar output
- 3.1.1.4c - Human causes of climate change: fossil fuels, agriculture, deforestation
- 3.1.1.4d - Effects of climate change on people and the environment: overview of effects
- 3.1.1.4e - Managing climate change: mitigation: alternative energy, carbon capture, trees, agreements
- 3.1.1.4f - Managing climate change: adaptation: agriculture, water supply, sea level rise

## How to teach this
Ask: 'How could scientists possibly know what the temperature was thousands of years ago, before thermometers existed?' Use this to introduce proxy evidence.

#### 3.1.1.4a Evidence for climate change from the Quaternary to the present
The Quaternary period (the last c. 2.6 million years) has seen repeated glacial (cold) and interglacial (warm) periods. Evidence for past climate includes ice cores (trapped air bubbles reveal past atmospheric gas concentrations and temperature), tree rings (width reflects growing conditions), pollen analysis, and historical/written records. Since the Industrial Revolution, direct temperature records and satellite data show a clear warming trend.

#### 3.1.1.4b Natural causes of climate change
Natural causes include orbital changes (the Milankovitch cycles: changes in the Earth's orbit, tilt and wobble alter how much solar energy reaches Earth over tens of thousands of years), variations in solar output (the Sun's energy output varies slightly over time), and volcanic activity (large eruptions throw ash and sulphur dioxide into the atmosphere, which can reflect sunlight and cause short-term cooling).

#### 3.1.1.4c Human causes of climate change
Human causes include burning fossil fuels (coal, oil and gas) for energy and transport, which releases carbon dioxide; agriculture, especially livestock farming, which releases methane; and deforestation, which both releases stored carbon and removes trees that would otherwise absorb carbon dioxide. These human ('anthropogenic') greenhouse gas emissions have increased sharply since industrialisation.

#### 3.1.1.4d Effects of climate change on people and the environment
Effects include rising average temperatures and more frequent heatwaves, melting ice sheets and glaciers, rising sea levels threatening low-lying coasts and islands, changing rainfall patterns causing drought in some regions and flooding in others, and shifting habitats and species ranges, which threatens food security and biodiversity and can increase migration and conflict over resources.

#### 3.1.1.4e Managing climate change: mitigation
Mitigation means tackling the causes of climate change, to reduce future warming. Strategies include switching to alternative/renewable energy (wind, solar, hydro), carbon capture and storage (trapping carbon dioxide from power stations before it reaches the atmosphere), planting trees (afforestation) to absorb carbon dioxide, and international agreements between governments to cut emissions.

#### 3.1.1.4f Managing climate change: adaptation
Adaptation means adjusting to the effects of climate change that are already happening or are now unavoidable. Strategies include changing agricultural methods (drought-resistant crops, altered planting times), managing water supply more carefully during droughts, and improving defences against rising sea levels (such as higher flood barriers) to protect people and property that are already at risk.
