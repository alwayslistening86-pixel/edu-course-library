# S24_Issue_Evaluation_Skills - Lesson: Paper 3 issue evaluation: the pre-release booklet, question sequence and reaching a justified decision

## Goal
The learner explains the nature and synoptic demands of issue evaluation, how to use the pre-release resource booklet, the typical question sequence, and how to weigh stakeholders and interrelationships to justify a decision.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.1a - Issue evaluation: nature, synoptic requirement and scales: know what the section demands
- 3.3.1b - Issue evaluation: the pre-release resource booklet and its sources: prepare with the booklet
- 3.3.1c - Issue evaluation: question sequence leading to the extended judgement: context, analysis, decision
- 3.3.1d - Issue evaluation: stakeholders, advantages, disadvantages and alternatives: critical perspective
- 3.3.1e - Issue evaluation: physical and human interrelationships and justified decisions: justify a decision by impact on people and environment

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Paper 3 gives you the resources booklet in advance. Why do you think AQA lets you prepare with it beforehand, rather than seeing it for the first time in the exam?'

#### 3.3.1a Issue evaluation: nature, synoptic requirement and scales
Issue evaluation is a decision-making exercise based around a real or realistic geographical issue, usually connected to a UK location, drawing together knowledge and skills from across the whole course (a 'synoptic' task) rather than testing one topic in isolation. It usually asks the learner to consider the issue at more than one scale, for example how a local decision connects to wider regional or national factors.

#### 3.3.1b Issue evaluation: the pre-release resource booklet and its sources
Around a month or so before the exam, AQA releases a resources booklet containing maps, data, graphs, photographs and text about the issue, which the learner can study in advance (though a clean copy is also given in the actual exam). Preparing with the booklet means becoming familiar with its content, sources and figures in advance, so that during the exam more time can be spent applying and evaluating rather than reading it for the first time under time pressure.

#### 3.3.1c Issue evaluation: question sequence leading to the extended judgement
Section A questions usually build up in a sequence: early, shorter questions test basic understanding of the resources (describing or explaining a specific piece of evidence), before a final, longer extended question (often worth around 9 or 12 marks) asks the learner to weigh up the options and reach a justified decision or recommendation, drawing on the whole booklet.

#### 3.3.1d Issue evaluation: stakeholders, advantages, disadvantages and alternatives
A strong answer identifies the relevant stakeholders (the different groups affected by the issue, such as local residents, businesses, government or environmental groups, who may have conflicting interests), weighs the advantages and disadvantages of each option presented in the booklet, and considers realistic alternatives, rather than assuming only the options explicitly listed are possible.

#### 3.3.1e Issue evaluation: physical and human interrelationships and justified decisions
The strongest answers explain how physical and human factors interact in the issue (for example, how a physical constraint such as flood risk affects a human decision such as where to build housing), and reach a decision that is clearly justified by evidence from the booklet, rather than simply asserting an opinion. A justified decision explains why the chosen option is better than the alternatives, using specific evidence.
