# S18_Urban_Sprawl_Regeneration_and_Sustainability - Lesson: Urban sprawl, the Salford Quays regeneration project, and sustainable urban living

## Goal
The learner explains urban sprawl and the rural-urban fringe, the Salford Quays regeneration project, features of sustainable urban living, and strategies to reduce urban traffic congestion.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.1v - Urban sprawl, the rural-urban fringe and commuter settlements: explain impacts
- 3.2.1w - Urban regeneration project: Salford Quays and MediaCityUK: reasons and main features
- 3.2.1x - Features of sustainable urban living: water and energy conservation, recycling, green space
- 3.2.1y - Urban transport strategies to reduce congestion: evaluate strategies

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Salford Quays used to be Manchester's derelict docks. Now it hosts BBC and ITV studios, flats and a shopping centre. What had to change for that to happen?'

#### 3.2.1v Urban sprawl, the rural-urban fringe and commuter settlements
Urban sprawl is the outward, often low-density spread of a city's built-up area into surrounding countryside, consuming farmland and green space. This creates a rural-urban fringe, a transitional zone at a city's edge mixing urban land uses (housing estates, retail parks, business parks) with rural ones. Sprawl also fuels the growth of commuter settlements: nearby towns and villages where many residents live but travel into the city to work, increasing commuting traffic.

#### 3.2.1w Urban regeneration project: Salford Quays and MediaCityUK
Salford Quays, the former Manchester Docks, fell derelict after commercial shipping declined through the later twentieth century as ships outgrew the canal and dock system. From the 1980s the area was regenerated: the docks were cleaned up and redeveloped with offices, housing, leisure facilities (including the Lowry arts centre and Imperial War Museum North) and, from around 2011, MediaCityUK, which attracted the BBC and ITV to relocate major operations there, creating thousands of jobs and transforming a derelict industrial site into a modern economic and cultural hub.

#### 3.2.1x Features of sustainable urban living
Features of sustainable urban living include: water conservation (efficient fittings, rainwater harvesting), energy conservation (better-insulated, energy-efficient buildings and renewable energy use), comprehensive recycling and waste reduction schemes, and protecting and creating green space within the city (parks, green roofs) to support wellbeing, biodiversity and to help manage surface water and air quality.

#### 3.2.1y Urban transport strategies to reduce congestion
Strategies to reduce urban traffic congestion include: expanding and improving public transport (trams, buses and trains) to give people an alternative to driving, congestion charging (charging vehicles to enter busy central areas at peak times), promoting cycling and walking through dedicated infrastructure, and park-and-ride schemes that let commuters park on the edge of a city and travel in by public transport rather than driving all the way into the centre.
