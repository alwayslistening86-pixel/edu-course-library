# S02_Natural_Hazards_and_Tectonic_Processes - Lesson: What a natural hazard is, and how plate tectonics causes earthquakes and volcanoes

## Goal
The learner defines natural hazard, classifies hazard types, and explains plate tectonic theory and the processes at constructive, destructive and conservative margins.

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Is an earthquake in the middle of the ocean, with nobody nearby, a natural hazard?' Use the answer (probably not, because a hazard needs a threat to people, property or the environment) to build the definition.

#### 3.1.1.1a Definition of a natural hazard
A natural hazard is a natural event (geological, atmospheric or biological) that has the potential to cause damage, destruction, injury or loss of life. The key word is 'potential': the event itself (say, a volcanic eruption on an uninhabited island) is not automatically a hazard unless people, property, infrastructure or the environment are at risk from it.

#### 3.1.1.1b Types of natural hazard
Natural hazards are usually grouped by cause: geological hazards (from the Earth's structure and movement, such as earthquakes, volcanic eruptions and landslides), meteorological/atmospheric hazards (from weather and climate, such as tropical storms, drought and extreme cold), and biological hazards (from living organisms, such as wildfires or disease outbreaks). This course focuses on tectonic and weather hazards, as required by the specification.

#### 3.1.1.1c Factors affecting hazard risk
Hazard risk (the chance of being affected) depends on several factors: how close a place is to the hazard source, the level of a country's development and preparedness (wealthier places can afford building codes, warning systems and emergency response), population density (more people in a hazardous zone raises risk), and level of urbanisation (rapid, unplanned city growth increases exposure). Risk is therefore not the same everywhere the hazard itself occurs.

#### 3.1.1.2a Plate tectonics theory
The Earth's structure has a thin, rigid outer crust, a mantle beneath it, and a core at the centre. The crust is broken into tectonic plates that float on the semi-molten mantle. Convection currents in the mantle, driven by heat from the core, cause slow movement of the plates (a few centimetres a year), a process also assisted by ridge push and slab pull at plate edges.

#### 3.1.1.2b Global distribution of earthquakes and volcanoes and plate margins
Earthquakes and volcanoes are not spread evenly across the world: they are concentrated in narrow belts that follow tectonic plate boundaries, such as the Pacific 'Ring of Fire'. Away from plate margins, in the middle of plates, tectonic hazards are rare. This distribution is strong evidence that plate movement, not chance, causes most tectonic hazards.

#### 3.1.1.2c Constructive margins: processes
At a constructive (divergent) margin, two plates move apart. Magma rises from the mantle to fill the gap, cooling to form new crust and creating features like mid-ocean ridges (for example the Mid-Atlantic Ridge). This produces mostly gentle, effusive volcanic eruptions and shallow-focus earthquakes, generally less destructive than those at destructive margins.

#### 3.1.1.2d Destructive margins: processes
At a destructive (convergent) margin, two plates move towards each other. Where an oceanic plate meets a continental plate, the denser oceanic plate is forced down (subducted) beneath the continental plate at a deep-ocean trench, melting to form magma that rises and can erupt explosively. Friction as the plates grind together also causes powerful, often deep-focus earthquakes.

#### 3.1.1.2e Conservative margins: processes
At a conservative (transform) margin, two plates slide past each other in roughly the same or opposite directions, without creating or destroying crust. Friction locks the plates together until built-up pressure is suddenly released, causing an earthquake. No volcanic activity is associated with conservative margins because no magma is generated. The San Andreas Fault in California is the standard named example.
