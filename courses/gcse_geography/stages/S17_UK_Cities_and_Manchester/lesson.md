# S17_UK_Cities_and_Manchester - Lesson: UK population distribution and Manchester's migration, opportunities and challenges

## Goal
The learner describes UK population and city distribution, and explains Manchester's importance, the impacts of migration, and its social, economic and environmental opportunities and challenges.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.1o - Distribution of UK population and major cities: overview
- 3.2.1p - Manchester: location and importance: UK and wider world
- 3.2.1q - Manchester: impacts of national and international migration: growth and character
- 3.2.1r - Manchester: social and economic opportunities: culture, recreation, jobs, transport
- 3.2.1s - Manchester: environmental opportunities, urban greening: green space
- 3.2.1t - Manchester: social and economic challenges: deprivation and inequality
- 3.2.1u - Manchester: environmental challenges: dereliction, brownfield, waste

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Manchester was once known as "Cottonopolis", the world's leading centre for cotton manufacturing. What do you think happened to the city as that industry declined?'

#### 3.2.1o Distribution of UK population and major cities
The UK's population is unevenly distributed, concentrated in and around its major cities, especially in England: London and the wider South East hold the largest concentration, with other major population centres including Birmingham, Manchester, Leeds and Glasgow. Population density is generally higher in urban lowland areas and lower in remote rural and upland areas such as the Scottish Highlands.

#### 3.2.1p Manchester: location and importance
Manchester is a major city in North West England, historically the world's first industrialised city and the centre of the UK's cotton textile industry during the Industrial Revolution. Today it is one of the UK's most important economic centres outside London, sometimes described as a leading city of the 'Northern Powerhouse', with major importance for finance, media, higher education and culture, both nationally and internationally.

#### 3.2.1q Manchester: impacts of national and international migration
Manchester's growth and character have been shaped by successive waves of migration: internal (national) migration from other parts of the UK, and international migration, historically including large Irish and, later, South Asian and Eastern European communities, alongside more recent international student and professional migration linked to the city's universities and growing economy. This has made Manchester a culturally diverse city, visible in its neighbourhoods, food, festivals and community life.

#### 3.2.1r Manchester: social and economic opportunities
Social opportunities include a rich cultural and recreational scene (music, sport, including two major football clubs, museums and nightlife) and world-class universities. Economic opportunities include a strong and diversifying jobs market (finance, media and technology alongside traditional industry), and significantly improved public transport (trams, rail) connecting the wider city region and supporting continued economic growth.

#### 3.2.1s Manchester: environmental opportunities, urban greening
Regeneration in Manchester has created environmental opportunities, including new and improved green spaces, tree planting and 'urban greening' projects within redeveloped areas, and improvements to formerly polluted waterways such as canals, which have been cleaned up and incorporated into regenerated, attractive areas including Salford Quays.

#### 3.2.1t Manchester: social and economic challenges
Social and economic challenges include areas of significant deprivation and inequality, where some inner-city and outer estate neighbourhoods have much higher unemployment, lower incomes and poorer health outcomes than the city's more prosperous areas, despite Manchester's overall economic growth; this uneven distribution of the benefits of growth remains a persistent challenge.

#### 3.2.1u Manchester: environmental challenges
Environmental challenges include dereliction and contaminated 'brownfield' land left behind by the decline of traditional manufacturing industries, and ongoing issues of waste management and air pollution linked to traffic congestion, both of which regeneration schemes across the city have had to address as part of redevelopment.
