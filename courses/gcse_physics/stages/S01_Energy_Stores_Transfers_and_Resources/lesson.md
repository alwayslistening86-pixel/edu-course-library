# S01_Energy_Stores_Transfers_and_Resources - Lesson: Energy stores, transfers, power, efficiency and energy resources

## Goal
The learner describes energy stores and transfers, calculates energy changes, power and efficiency, and evaluates energy resources.

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A roller coaster car climbs a hill, then races down. Where is the energy at each point, and where does it end up?' Have the learner track stores and transfers before giving equations.

#### 4.1.1.1 Energy stores and systems
A system is an object or group of objects. Energy is stored as kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal, chemical, magnetic, electrostatic and nuclear energy. In a closed system there is no net change in the total energy. Equations to use: kinetic energy = 0.5 x mass x speed squared (J); gravitational potential energy = mass x g x height, with g = 9.8 N/kg; elastic potential energy = 0.5 x spring constant x extension squared.

#### 4.1.1.2 Changes in energy
When a system changes, energy is transferred between stores by mechanical work (a force moving an object), electrical work (charge moving), heating (thermal transfer from a hotter to a cooler object) and radiation (light and sound waves). A ball falling loses gravitational potential energy and gains kinetic energy; a braking car transfers kinetic energy to thermal energy.

#### 4.1.1.3 Energy changes in systems
The energy needed to raise the temperature of a substance depends on mass, the substance and the temperature rise: change in thermal energy = mass x specific heat capacity x temperature change (J = kg x J/kg degree C x degree C). The specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise 1 kg by 1 degree Celsius. Water has a high value (4200 J/kg degree C), so it warms and cools slowly.

#### 4.1.1.4 Power
Power is the rate at which energy is transferred or work is done: power = energy transferred / time = work done / time, in watts (1 W = 1 J/s). A 2 kW kettle transfers 2000 J each second, so in 120 s it transfers 240 000 J. An energy transfer of 1 kJ in 10 s is 100 W.

#### 4.1.2.1 Energy transfers in a system
Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred usefully, stored or dissipated (wasted, mostly by heating the surroundings). Friction and air resistance dissipate energy; lubrication reduces friction; thermal insulation reduces heat loss. The rate of conduction depends on the thermal conductivity and thickness of walls: thick walls of low conductivity material lose thermal energy more slowly.

#### 4.1.2.2 Efficiency
Efficiency = useful output energy transfer / total input energy transfer (or useful power out / total power in). Multiply by 100 to give a percentage. A lamp with 100 J input and 20 J of useful light output is 20 per cent efficient. Higher tier: describe ways to increase efficiency, such as insulation, lubrication and better design, and evaluate the trade-offs in cost.

#### 4.1.3 National and global energy resources
Non-renewable resources: coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear fuel. Renewable: bio-fuel, wind, hydroelectric, geothermal, tidal, solar and water waves. Compare reliability, environmental impact (carbon dioxide, land use, visual and noise effects), cost and availability: wind depends on weather, tidal is predictable but expensive, fossil fuels are reliable but emit carbon dioxide. Energy resource choices depend on political, social, economic and environmental factors.
