# S15_Induction_Generators_and_Transformers - Lesson: Induced potential, generators, microphones and transformers

## Goal
The learner explains the generator effect, applications in alternators, dynamos and microphones, and calculates transformer potential differences and currents.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.7.3.1 - Induced potential: learn and apply: induced potential
- 4.7.3.2 - Uses of the generator effect: learn and apply: uses of the generator effect
- 4.7.3.3 - Microphones: learn and apply: microphones
- 4.7.3.4 - Transformers: learn and apply: transformers

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A bicycle dynamo lights a lamp with no battery. Where does the electrical energy come from, and why does pedalling get harder when the lamp is on?'

#### 4.7.3.1 Induced potential (Higher tier)
If an electrical conductor moves relative to a magnetic field, or if the magnetic field around it changes, a potential difference is induced across its ends (generator effect). If the conductor is part of a complete circuit, a current is induced. The direction of the induced current opposes the change that produced it. Induced potential increases with a stronger magnet, faster movement, or more turns.

#### 4.7.3.2 Uses of the generator effect (Higher tier)
An alternator generates alternating current by rotating a coil in a magnetic field, using slip rings to keep the connection; a dynamo generates direct current using a commutator. Graphs of potential difference against time show the alternator's a.c. shape and the dynamo's d.c. shape. Power stations use generators driven by turbines.

#### 4.7.3.3 Microphones (Higher tier)
Microphones convert pressure variations in sound waves into variations in current in electrical circuits: sound waves make a diaphragm move, which moves a coil in the field of a magnet (or a magnet in a coil), inducing a varying potential difference. This is the reverse of a loudspeaker.

#### 4.7.3.4 Transformers (Higher tier)
A transformer changes the size of an alternating potential difference: a primary coil and a secondary coil are wound on an iron core; a changing current in the primary creates a changing magnetic field in the core that induces a potential difference in the secondary. Vp / Vs = Np / Ns. A step-up transformer has more turns on the secondary; step-down has fewer. For a 100 per cent efficient transformer, Vs x Is = Vp x Ip. Transformers work only with a.c.
