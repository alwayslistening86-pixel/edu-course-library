## How to teach this
Ask: 'When a stadium crowd does a Mexican wave, what is travelling around the stadium, and what is not?' It leads to energy transfer without matter transfer.

#### 4.6.1.1 Transverse and longitudinal waves
Waves transfer energy and information without transferring matter. In transverse waves the oscillations are perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer (ripples on water, light and all electromagnetic waves, S-waves). In longitudinal waves the oscillations are parallel to the direction of energy transfer, forming compressions and rarefactions (sound, ultrasound, P-waves).

#### 4.6.1.2 Properties of waves
Amplitude is the maximum displacement from the rest position; wavelength is the distance from one point to the same point on the next wave; frequency is the number of waves passing a point per second (Hz); period T = 1/f. Wave speed = frequency x wavelength (v = f lambda). A wave of frequency 50 Hz and wavelength 6.0 m travels at 300 m/s. Wave speed is set by the medium.

#### 4.6.1.3 Reflection of waves
Waves can be reflected, absorbed or transmitted at a boundary between two materials. In reflection, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, measured from the normal. Smooth surfaces give specular reflection; rough surfaces give diffuse reflection. Refraction is a change of direction when a wave changes speed as it crosses into another medium: light bends towards the normal when entering a denser medium. Higher tier: use wavefront diagrams, where wavefronts compress when the wave slows.

#### 4.6.1.4 Sound waves
Sound waves are longitudinal vibrations that travel through solids, liquids and gases, but not a vacuum, and are converted to vibrations in solids such as the eardrum and small bones. Humans hear frequencies from about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz (20 kHz). Higher tier: the range of human hearing is limited by the structure of the ear, because the eardrum and small bones can vibrate effectively only over this range of frequencies.

#### 4.6.1.5 Waves for detection and exploration
Ultrasound (frequency above 20 kHz) reflects from boundaries: it is used for medical imaging (scanning a fetus), and in sonar to find depths. Seismic waves from earthquakes are used to study the Earth: P-waves are longitudinal and travel through solids and liquids; S-waves are transverse and travel only through solids. Since S-waves are not detected on the far side of the Earth, the outer core must be liquid, and the refraction of waves shows the Earth's layered structure.

#### RP8 Required practical 8: wave properties
Make observations to identify suitable apparatus to measure the frequency, wavelength and speed of waves in a ripple tank and in a solid (for example a string or rod). In a ripple tank, use a strobe light or a video to measure frequency, and a ruler on a projected image to find wavelength; speed = f x wavelength. Use a long line of waves to reduce the percentage error in wavelength.
