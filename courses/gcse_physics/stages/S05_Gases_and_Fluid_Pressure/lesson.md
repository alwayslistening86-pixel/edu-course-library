# S05_Gases_and_Fluid_Pressure - Lesson: Gas particles, gas pressure, pressure in fluids and atmospheric pressure

## Goal
The learner explains the behaviour of gases using particle motion, calculates pressure, explains pressure in liquids, upthrust and atmospheric pressure.

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Why does a bicycle pump get hot, and why do your ears pop when you dive to the bottom of a pool?'

#### 4.3.3.1 Particle motion in gases
Gas particles move randomly in all directions at high speed. The temperature of a gas is related to the average kinetic energy of the particles: higher temperature means faster particles. At absolute zero (-273 degrees Celsius, 0 K) the particles have the minimum internal energy. Kelvin temperature = Celsius + 273.

#### 4.3.3.2 Pressure in gases
Gas pressure is caused by collisions of the particles with the walls of a container, each collision exerting a small force. In a fixed volume, raising temperature increases the average speed of the particles, so collisions are harder and more frequent: pressure rises. Decreasing the volume at constant temperature raises the collision rate, so pressure rises.

#### 4.3.3.3 Increasing the pressure of a gas (Higher tier)
Doing work on a gas (for example compressing it with a pump) transfers energy to the gas, increasing its internal energy and so its temperature. For a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, pressure x volume = constant (p1 V1 = p2 V2). Halving the volume doubles the pressure.

#### 4.5.5.1.1 Pressure in a fluid 1
Pressure = force normal to a surface / area of that surface (p = F / A), in pascals (Pa, N/m2). A liquid's pressure at a point increases with depth and with the density of the liquid, and acts in all directions. Objects immersed experience upthrust caused by the pressure difference between the upper and lower surfaces: the deeper surface has greater pressure. Floating occurs when upthrust equals weight.

#### 4.5.5.1.2 Pressure in a fluid 2 (Higher tier)
Pressure due to a column of liquid = height x density x gravitational field strength (p = h rho g). Water at 10 m depth: 10 x 1000 x 9.8 = 98 000 Pa. Upthrust equals the weight of the fluid displaced, so an object floats if its density is less than the fluid's density.

#### 4.5.5.2 Atmospheric pressure
The atmosphere exerts pressure because gravity holds air near the Earth and air particles collide with surfaces. Atmospheric pressure decreases with increasing height, because there is less air above (a shorter column of air), so a lower weight of air. At sea level it is about 100 000 Pa.
