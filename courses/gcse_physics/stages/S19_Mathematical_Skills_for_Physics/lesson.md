## How to teach this
Use physics contexts (speed, acceleration, force, resistance, half-life) so that maths is a tool for physics, not a separate topic.

#### M1a Maths: decimal form
Recognise and use numbers in decimal form: 0.25 A is 250 mA; 0.005 m is 5 mm. Align place values when comparing decimals and keep zeros that show the precision of a reading, such as 2.50 V.

#### M1b Maths: standard form
Write large and small numbers as a x 10 to the power n with a between 1 and 10: the speed of light is 3 x 10 to the power 8 m/s; an atom's radius is 1 x 10 to the power -10 m. Multiply by adding powers and divide by subtracting them, and enter numbers using the EXP button.

#### M1c Maths: ratios, fractions and percentages
Use ratios, fractions and percentages: efficiency as a percentage, the turns ratio of a transformer (4:1), magnification as a ratio, and half-life fractions such as 1/8 remaining after three half-lives. Simplify ratios by dividing both parts by their common factor.

#### M1d Maths: estimating results
Estimate answers by rounding to one significant figure: a car of 1000 kg at 20 m/s has kinetic energy about 0.5 x 1000 x 400 = 200 000 J. Use estimates to check that a calculator answer of the wrong order of magnitude is spotted.

#### M2a Maths: significant figures
Give answers to an appropriate number of significant figures, usually that of the least precise data. Count from the first non-zero digit: 0.004372 to 2 significant figures is 0.0044. Do not round intermediate steps.

#### M2b Maths: arithmetic means
The mean is the sum of the values divided by the number of values. For readings 0.32, 0.34 and 0.33 A the mean is 0.33 A. Identify anomalous results before averaging and exclude them with a reason.

#### M2c Maths: frequency tables, bar charts and histograms
A frequency table records how many times each value occurs. Bar charts show categories with gaps; histograms show continuous grouped data without gaps. Construct and read them with labelled axes and scales, for example the count rate of a radioactive source in bins.

#### M2f Maths: mean, mode and median
The mean is the sum divided by the count; the mode is the most common value; the median is the middle value when data are ordered. For 3, 4, 4, 5, 14 the mean is 6, the mode 4 and the median 4. The median is less affected by an outlier.

#### M2g Maths: scatter diagrams and correlation
A scatter diagram plots two variables. A pattern rising to the right is positive correlation, falling is negative, no pattern is none; draw a line of best fit. A correlation does not prove one variable causes the other, and a third factor may be responsible.

#### M2h Maths: order of magnitude
An order of magnitude compares sizes to the nearest power of ten. A nucleus (10 to the power -15 m) and an atom (10 to the power -10 m) differ by five orders of magnitude. The Earth's diameter (10 to the power 7 m) and the Sun's (10 to the power 9 m) differ by two.

#### M3a Maths: symbols
Understand and use =, <, <<, >>, >, proportional to, and approximately equal to. Examples: the mass of an electron << the mass of a proton; current is proportional to potential difference for an ohmic conductor; force proportional to extension for a spring.

#### M3b Maths: changing the subject of an equation
Rearrange by performing the same operation on both sides. From V = I R: I = V / R and R = V / I. From v = f lambda: lambda = v / f. From F = m a: a = F / m. Check that units still work.

#### M3c Maths: substituting values with units
Substitute numerical values into equations using appropriate units: for E = 0.5 k e squared, convert extension from cm to m first. Write the value and its unit at each step and check the final unit is correct, such as J for energy.

#### M3d Maths: solving simple algebraic equations
Solve simple equations by inverse operations. For a resultant force problem 12 = 3 a gives a = 4 m/s2; for a series circuit V total = I (R1 + R2) gives R2 after substituting the numbers. Check the answer by substituting back.

#### M4a Maths: translating information between graphical and numeric form
Read values off a graph to fill in a table, and plot table values onto a graph. For a velocity-time graph, read the speed at 4 s and record it with its unit. Use the scale carefully, especially where it is not 1 per square.

#### M4b Maths: y = mx + c
A straight line follows y = m x + c, where m is the gradient and c is the y-intercept. If y is directly proportional to x, c = 0 and the line passes through the origin, as with force and extension for a spring within its limit.

#### M4c Maths: plotting two variables
Plot the independent variable on the x-axis and the dependent variable on the y-axis, choose scales that fill the grid, label axes with quantity and unit, plot points accurately as crosses, and draw a line or smooth curve of best fit rather than joining dots.

#### M4d Maths: slope and intercept
Gradient = change in y divided by change in x, using a large triangle on a straight line. For a distance-time graph the gradient is speed; for a velocity-time graph it is acceleration; for a force-extension graph it is the spring constant. The intercept is where the line meets the y-axis.

#### M4e Maths: tangent to a curve as rate of change
On a curve, draw a tangent at the point of interest (a straight line that touches the curve there) and calculate its gradient. This gives the instantaneous rate of change, for example the speed at a moment on a distance-time curve, or the acceleration on a velocity-time curve.

#### M4f Maths: area under a graph by counting squares
The area between a curve and the x-axis has physical meaning: the area under a velocity-time graph is the distance travelled, and under a force-extension graph is the work done (elastic energy). Count whole squares, and treat part squares by combining to estimate, then multiply by the value of one square.

#### M5a Maths: angles in degrees
Use angular measures in degrees. In reflection the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, measured from the normal; in refraction, angles are measured to the normal. Use a protractor to measure to the nearest degree.

#### M5b Maths: visualising 2D and 3D forms
Visualise and represent 2D and 3D forms, including 2D representations of 3D objects: draw a solenoid's field or a bar magnet's field lines in 2D, and describe the 3D shape of the field around a wire. Recognise a rectangle, triangle or cube from a drawing.

#### M5c Maths: areas of triangles and rectangles, surface areas and volumes of cubes
Area of rectangle = length x width; triangle = 0.5 x base x height; volume of a cube = side cubed; surface area of a cube = 6 x side squared. Use these to find pressure (force / area), the area under graphs, and density (mass / volume).
