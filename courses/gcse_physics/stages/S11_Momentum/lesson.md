# S11_Momentum - Lesson: Momentum, conservation of momentum and changes in momentum

## Goal
The learner calculates momentum, applies conservation of momentum in collisions and explosions, and relates force to the rate of change of momentum.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.5.7.1 - Momentum is a property of moving objects: learn and apply: momentum is a property of moving objects
- 4.5.7.2 - Conservation of momentum: learn and apply: conservation of momentum
- 4.5.7.3 - Changes in momentum: learn and apply: changes in momentum

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Why is a slow-moving lorry much harder to stop than a fast-moving bicycle, and why do airbags and crumple zones help?'

#### 4.5.7.1 Momentum is a property of moving objects
Momentum = mass x velocity (p = m v), in kg m/s. It is a vector, with the same direction as the velocity. A 1000 kg car at 20 m/s has momentum 20 000 kg m/s; a 70 kg runner at 8 m/s has 560 kg m/s.

#### 4.5.7.2 Conservation of momentum
In a closed system the total momentum before an event equals the total momentum after it. When a 2 kg trolley moving at 3 m/s collides and sticks to a stationary 1 kg trolley, total momentum is 6 kg m/s before, so both move at 6/3 = 2 m/s after. In an explosion, such as a gun firing, the total momentum stays zero: the bullet moves forward and the gun recoils backwards.

#### 4.5.7.3 Changes in momentum
Force = change in momentum / time taken (F = change in p / t). The same change in momentum over a longer time needs a smaller force, which is why crumple zones, seat belts, airbags and cushioned surfaces reduce injury by extending the impact time. A 0.15 kg ball changing velocity by 20 m/s in 0.010 s experiences a force of 300 N.
