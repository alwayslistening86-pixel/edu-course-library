# S18_Working_Scientifically_Analysis_and_Vocabulary - Lesson: Working scientifically: analysis, evaluation and scientific language

## Goal
The learner presents, converts and analyses data, draws reasoned conclusions, evaluates accuracy and precision, communicates findings, and uses scientific vocabulary, units, prefixes and significant figures correctly.

## How to teach this
Give a small messy data set from an earlier practical and ask: 'What does this show, how sure are you, and what would make you surer?'

#### WS3.1 Presenting observations
Present observations and data using tables, bar charts (categorical data), histograms, line graphs and scatter graphs (continuous data), choosing the type to suit the data. Label axes with quantity and unit, choose scales that use more than half the graph area, and plot points accurately.

#### WS3.2 Translating data between forms
Data can be moved between forms: a table into a graph, a graph into a table, and a written description into a formula. For example, reading a rate from a graph and recording it in a table, or translating 'doubles every 10 degrees' into an equation.

#### WS3.3 Mathematical and statistical analysis
Calculate means, ranges, percentages, percentage change, rates and ratios. Identify anomalous results and exclude them from the mean with justification. The range of repeat readings is a simple measure of spread.

#### WS3.4 Distributions and uncertainty
Repeat readings vary; the range of repeat values shows uncertainty, and a smaller range shows better precision. The uncertainty in a mean can be estimated as half the range. Distributions are represented using frequency tables and histograms.

#### WS3.5 Interpreting observations and identifying patterns
Describe patterns in data: positive correlation, negative correlation, no correlation, directly proportional, levelling off, or rising then falling. Quote figures with units from the data when describing a graph or pattern.

#### WS3.6 Reasoned explanations relating data to hypotheses
A conclusion states whether the data support the hypothesis, quotes evidence and gives a scientific explanation. Do not claim more than the data show: a positive correlation between two variables does not by itself show that one causes the other.

#### WS3.7 Evaluating data: accuracy, precision and error
Accurate results are close to the true value; precise results are close together. Repeatable means the same person gets the same result again; reproducible means different people or methods get the same. Random error causes scatter; systematic error shifts all results the same way. Anomalies should be identified and explained where possible.

#### WS3.8 Communicating findings
Report findings in a clear structure: aim, method, results (tables and graphs), conclusion and evaluation. Use scientific language, quote units, and match the detail to the audience, whether a written report, a poster, an oral talk or an extended exam answer.

#### WS4.1 Scientific vocabulary and definitions
Use scientific terms precisely and give definitions when asked, distinguishing similar terms such as accurate and precise, hazard and risk, mass and weight, and reliable and valid. Precise vocabulary earns marks in extended answers.

#### WS4.2 Scientific quantities and how they are determined
Know the quantities used in the course and how each is measured: for example mass with a balance, volume with a measuring cylinder, time with a stopwatch, temperature with a thermometer, and derived quantities such as density or rate found by calculation from measured values.

#### WS4.3 SI units and nomenclature
Use SI units (kilogram, metre, second, kelvin, ampere, mole) and their derived forms, such as joule, newton, watt and pascal. Use correct symbols and the accepted system of naming: IUPAC names for chemicals in chemistry, and standard names for structures and processes in biology and physics.

#### WS4.4 Prefixes and powers of ten
Use prefixes for multiples and submultiples: kilo (10 to the power 3), centi (10 to the power -2), milli (10 to the power -3), micro (10 to the power -6) and nano (10 to the power -9). Use standard form for very large and very small numbers, and order of magnitude to compare sizes.

#### WS4.5 Converting between units
Convert between related units by multiplying or dividing by the right power of ten: 1 dm3 = 1000 cm3, 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 km = 1000 m, 1 mm = 1000 micrometres, 1 hour = 3600 s. Always write the unit and check the answer is a sensible size.

#### WS4.6 Significant figures in calculations
Give answers to a number of significant figures consistent with the data, usually the fewest used in the calculation. Count from the first non-zero digit: 0.004372 to 2 significant figures is 0.0044. Do not round intermediate steps; round only the final answer.
