# S14_Magnets_and_Electromagnetism - Lesson: Magnets, magnetic fields, electromagnets, the motor effect, motors and loudspeakers

## Goal
The learner describes magnets and fields, the magnetic effect of a current, the motor effect using Fleming's left-hand rule, and the operation of motors and loudspeakers.

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A crane at a scrapyard can lift a car and then drop it at the flick of a switch. How can a magnet be switched on and off?'

#### 4.7.1.1 Poles of a magnet
A magnet has two poles, north and south. Like poles repel and unlike poles attract: the force is non-contact and is strongest at the poles. A permanent magnet has its own magnetic field; an induced magnet is a magnetic material that becomes magnetic when placed in a magnetic field and loses most magnetism when the field is removed. Magnetic materials include iron, steel, cobalt and nickel.

#### 4.7.1.2 Magnetic fields
The region around a magnet where a force acts on another magnet or magnetic material is a magnetic field. Field lines show its shape and direction, from north to south, and are closer together where the field is stronger. A plotting compass shows the direction of the field. The Earth's magnetic field is like a bar magnet, produced by its molten iron core, which explains why a compass points north.

#### 4.7.2.1 Electromagnetism
A current in a wire creates a magnetic field around it in concentric circles; the field is stronger nearer the wire or with a larger current. Coiling the wire into a solenoid gives a strong, uniform field inside similar to that of a bar magnet. An electromagnet has an iron core; its strength increases with more current, more turns and an iron core. It can be switched on and off, which makes it useful in scrapyard cranes, relays and doorbells.

#### 4.7.2.2 Fleming's left-hand rule (Higher tier)
A conductor carrying a current at right angles to a magnetic field experiences a force (the motor effect). Fleming's left-hand rule: first finger = magnetic Field, second finger = Current, thumb = direction of force (Motion). Force = magnetic flux density x current x length (F = B I l), where B is in teslas. For a 0.20 m wire carrying 5.0 A in a field of 0.30 T, F = 0.30 x 5.0 x 0.20 = 0.30 N.

#### 4.7.2.3 Electric motors (Higher tier)
A coil of wire in a magnetic field carrying a current experiences forces on opposite sides in opposite directions, causing it to rotate. A commutator (split-ring) reverses the current every half turn so the coil keeps rotating in the same direction. The speed rises with a larger current, a stronger field or more turns.

#### 4.7.2.4 Loudspeakers (Higher tier)
An alternating current in the coil of a loudspeaker interacts with the field of a permanent magnet, so the coil and attached cone move backwards and forwards; the cone pushes on the air and produces sound waves of the same frequency as the alternating current. It is an application of the motor effect.
