# S16_Research_Project_and_Speaking - Lesson: The individual research project and the speaking exam

## Goal
The learner chooses, researches and presents an individual research project in French (not on the works studied for Paper 2), discusses it spontaneously for 9-10 minutes, and handles a stimulus-card discussion on any sub-theme, using strategies for spontaneous speech.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.5 - Individual research project

## How to teach this
Demandez : « Quel sujet lié à un pays francophone vous passionne vraiment ? » Teach in French as much as the learner can manage, switching to English to explain grammar. Each theme lesson gives key vocabulary, facts and arguments with at least one French-speaking country's context, then a grammar point used in context. Get the learner producing French every session: short spoken or written answers, then longer arguments. Statistics about French-speaking societies change; check recent sources (INSEE, Le Monde, France 24) before quoting them.

#### 3.5 Individual research project
**The individual research project (IRP).** Choose a subject or key question that interests you, linked to a French-speaking country or countries (it may relate to a theme or sub-theme, or to a work, but not to No et moi or Au revoir les enfants, which you write about in Paper 2). Research in French from a range of sources, including the internet; take notes on facts, statistics, opinions and your own analysis. Formulate a title as a question (e.g. « Le hip-hop sénégalais est-il un outil de contestation politique ? », « Pourquoi la langue bretonne a-t-elle failli disparaître ? », « Quel avenir pour les langues créoles aux Antilles ? »). AQA requires you to submit a form listing the title, key findings and at least two sources (check the current form with your teacher).

**The speaking exam (Paper 3, 60 marks).** Part 1 (25 marks): you choose one of two stimulus cards on a sub-theme, prepare for 5 minutes, then discuss for 5-6 minutes: answer the printed questions, then develop your opinions and respond to the examiner's challenges. Part 2 (35 marks): a 2-minute presentation of your IRP's key findings, then a 9-10 minute discussion. Marks reward knowledge and understanding (of the card's topic and your research), analysis and justified opinions, interaction and spontaneity, range and accuracy of language, and pronunciation and intonation.

**Strategies.** Develop answers (point → example → opinion → justification); use complex structures naturally (subjunctive, si clauses, relative pronouns); ask the examiner questions ('Qu'en pensez-vous ?'); if you don't understand, ask for repetition (« Pourriez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ? »); use fillers sparingly; don't learn a script word for word: the discussion must be spontaneous.
