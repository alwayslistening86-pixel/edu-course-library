# ACC_S05_Further_Ratio_Analysis_and_Interpretation - Lesson: Further ratio analysis and interpretation

## Goal
The learner calculates and interprets a fuller set of profitability, liquidity and efficiency ratios to assess a set of financial statements, and understands the limitations of ratio analysis.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.5a - Efficiency and liquidity ratios
- ACC.5b - Limitations of ratio analysis

## How to teach this
Ask: two companies have identical profit margins, but one takes twice as long to collect cash from customers. Are they equally healthy? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ACC.5a Efficiency and liquidity ratios
**Efficiency ratios**: **receivables collection period** = (trade receivables/revenue) x 365 (days taken, on average, to collect cash from credit customers); **payables payment period** = (trade payables/cost of sales or purchases) x 365; **inventory holding period** = (inventory/cost of sales) x 365. Together with the current ratio and quick (acid test) ratio (= (current assets - inventory)/current liabilities, a stricter liquidity test that excludes the least liquid current asset), these show how efficiently working capital is managed. *Worked example*: trade receivables £18,000, revenue £146,000: collection period = (18,000/146,000) x 365 = 45 days.

#### ACC.5b Limitations of ratio analysis
**Limitations of ratio analysis**: ratios are only as reliable as the financial statements they are calculated from (different accounting policies between entities can distort comparability); they need a benchmark (prior year, budget, or a competitor) to be meaningful in isolation; they are based on historical, often year-end, figures which may not reflect current conditions (e.g. a seasonal business's year-end inventory); and they do not explain the causes behind a movement, only flag that one has occurred, so ratios are a starting point for investigation, not a conclusion in themselves.
