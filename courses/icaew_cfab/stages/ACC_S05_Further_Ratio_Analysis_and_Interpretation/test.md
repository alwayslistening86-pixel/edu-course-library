## Test items
1. Current assets are £50,000, of which inventory is £22,000; current liabilities are £35,000. Calculate the quick (acid test) ratio. [3 marks]
2. Trade payables are £16,000 and cost of sales is £128,000. Calculate the payables payment period in days. [2 marks]
3. Which is a genuine limitation of ratio analysis? Choose every correct option.
   A. Ratios are always calculated identically by every accounting system, so they are never distorted
   B. Ratios need a benchmark such as a prior period or competitor to be meaningful
   C. Ratios explain the underlying cause of every change automatically
   D. Ratios cannot be calculated from a set of financial statements
4. Explain why comparing the current ratio of two companies in different industries can be misleading, even if both ratios are calculated correctly. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 quick assets = 50,000 - 22,000 = £28,000; A1 quick ratio = 28,000/35,000 = 0.80:1; B1 correct method: inventory is excluded from quick assets because it is the least liquid current asset and may not convert to cash quickly.
2. [2] M1 (16,000/128,000) x 365; A1 = 46 days.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] B3 different industries have structurally different working capital needs (e.g. a supermarket holds fast-moving inventory and collects cash quickly, so can operate safely with a lower current ratio than a manufacturer with long production cycles); a 'good' ratio in one industry may be poor in another, so cross-industry comparison without adjustment is misleading.
