# TAX_S01_Objectives_Types_of_Tax_and_Ethics - Lesson: Objectives, types of tax and ethics

## Goal
The learner explains the objectives of taxation, the main UK taxes and who is liable for each, the UK tax year and sources of tax law, and the ethical framework (including tax avoidance versus evasion) that applies to tax practice.

## Syllabus items taught here
- TAX.1a - Objectives of taxation
- TAX.1b - Main UK taxes and who bears them
- TAX.1c - UK tax year and sources of tax law
- TAX.1d - Ethics in tax practice, avoidance versus evasion
- TAX.1e - Conflicts of interest and money laundering in tax practice

## How to teach this
Ask: is it illegal to arrange your affairs to pay less tax - and if not, what's the line between that and a crime? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### TAX.1a Objectives of taxation
**Objectives of taxation**: raising revenue to fund government spending is the primary objective, but tax policy also pursues **economic** objectives (e.g. incentivising investment through capital allowances, or discouraging certain behaviour through duties), **social justice** objectives (e.g. a progressive income tax structure, where the average tax rate rises with income), and **environmental** objectives (e.g. taxes on carbon emissions or landfill). The government also increasingly pursues digitalisation of tax administration (HMRC's 'Making Tax Digital' programme), aiming for more accurate, timely reporting with fewer errors.

#### TAX.1b Main UK taxes and who bears them
**Main UK taxes and who bears them**: **income tax** (individuals, on income); **National Insurance contributions (NIC)** (employees, employers and the self-employed, funding state benefits/pensions); **capital gains tax (CGT)** (individuals, on gains from disposing of chargeable assets); **corporation tax** (companies, on their taxable total profits); **inheritance tax (IHT)** (on a person's estate on death, and certain lifetime transfers); **VAT** (a tax on the supply of most goods and services, ultimately borne by the end consumer but collected and accounted for by VAT-registered businesses); and **stamp taxes** (e.g. Stamp Duty Land Tax on land/property transactions).

#### TAX.1c UK tax year and sources of tax law
**The UK tax year and sources of law**: the tax year for individuals runs from **6 April to the following 5 April** (e.g. the 2026/27 tax year runs from 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027); a company's accounting period (which may follow any dates the company chooses) instead governs when its corporation tax is assessed. UK tax law derives from **legislation** (Acts of Parliament, e.g. the annual Finance Act following the Budget, plus secondary legislation), **case law** (court decisions interpreting the legislation), and **HMRC guidance/manuals and press releases** (HMRC's interpretation, not itself law, but influential in practice).

#### TAX.1d Ethics in tax practice, avoidance versus evasion
**Ethics in tax practice**: the fundamental ethical principles from the ICAEW Code of Ethics (see the Assurance module) apply fully to tax work - a tax adviser must not knowingly help a client evade tax, must act with integrity, and must apply professional scepticism to information a client provides. **Tax avoidance** is legally arranging one's affairs to minimise tax within the law (e.g. using an ISA allowance) - lawful, though HMRC and professional bodies distinguish acceptable planning from **aggressive/abusive avoidance** (contrived arrangements with no commercial purpose beyond avoiding tax, which HMRC can challenge, e.g. under the General Anti-Abuse Rule). **Tax evasion** is illegally failing to pay tax that is legally due (e.g. deliberately hiding income from HMRC) - a criminal offence.

#### TAX.1e Conflicts of interest and money laundering in tax practice
**Conflicts of interest and money laundering in tax practice**: an adviser acting for two clients with opposing tax interests must manage the conflict (e.g. informed consent, separate teams, or declining to act - see the Assurance module's safeguards). Tax evasion is a predicate offence for money laundering purposes, so the reporting obligations covered in the Law module (POCA/Money Laundering Regulations) apply directly to suspected tax evasion identified during tax work.
