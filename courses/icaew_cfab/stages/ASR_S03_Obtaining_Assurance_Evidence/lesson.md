# ASR_S03_Obtaining_Assurance_Evidence - Lesson: Obtaining assurance evidence

## Goal
The learner explains why assurance work is documented, the methods of obtaining evidence and their relative reliability, and when management representations or referral to a more senior colleague are needed.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ASR.3a - Reasons for assurance documentation
- ASR.3b - Methods of obtaining evidence
- ASR.3c - Reliability of different evidence types
- ASR.3d - Sufficiency and quality of evidence, sampling risk
- ASR.3e - Written management representations
- ASR.3f - Issues requiring referral

## How to teach this
Ask: is evidence obtained directly by the auditor (e.g. physically counting inventory) more or less reliable than evidence provided by the client's own staff? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ASR.3a Reasons for assurance documentation
**Why document evidence**: working papers provide the evidential basis for the opinion given, evidence that the engagement was planned and performed in accordance with professional standards, and support for quality control review, future engagements, and (if needed) legal or regulatory scrutiny.

#### ASR.3b Methods of obtaining evidence
**Methods of obtaining evidence**: **tests of controls** (checking whether a control actually operated as designed, e.g. inspecting evidence of authorisation signatures) support a lower assessed control risk; **substantive procedures** test for material misstatement directly, split into **tests of detail** (e.g. agreeing an invoice to a ledger entry) and **substantive analytical procedures** (e.g. comparing this year's gross margin to last year's and investigating any unexpected variance). Modern engagements increasingly use **data analytics** (testing entire populations of transactions rather than a sample, to spot unusual patterns) and **remote/virtual auditing techniques** (e.g. a live video inventory count) where circumstances allow.

#### ASR.3c Reliability of different evidence types
**Strengths, weaknesses and reliability of evidence**: evidence obtained **directly by the practitioner** (e.g. physical observation, recalculation) is more reliable than evidence obtained **indirectly or by inference**; evidence from an **independent external source** (e.g. a bank confirmation letter) is more reliable than evidence generated internally by the client; **written** evidence is more reliable than **oral** evidence; and evidence from an entity with **strong internal controls** is more reliable than from one with weak controls. No single method is best in every situation - the choice depends on what is being tested and the assessed risk.

#### ASR.3d Sufficiency and quality of evidence, sampling risk
**Sufficiency and quality of evidence**: evidence must be both **sufficient** (enough of it) and **appropriate** (relevant and reliable). When evidence is gathered by **sampling** rather than testing 100% of a population, **sampling risk** arises - the risk that the sample's characteristics differ from the population as a whole, potentially leading to an incorrect conclusion; a larger, more representative sample reduces (but never eliminates) sampling risk.

#### ASR.3e Written management representations
**Written management representations** are obtained where no other sufficient appropriate evidence is reasonably expected to exist (e.g. management's intentions regarding a specific course of action, or confirmation that all related-party relationships have been disclosed) - they support, but never substitute for, other evidence that is available.

#### ASR.3f Issues requiring referral
**Issues requiring referral**: matters involving significant judgement, a suspicion of fraud, a potential legal or regulatory breach, or a disagreement with management over a material issue should be referred to a more senior/experienced colleague (e.g. the engagement partner) rather than resolved unilaterally by a junior team member, consistent with the firm's quality control procedures.

## Explicitly not here
Ethical and regulatory matters (including how to handle a suspected fraud once identified) are ASR_S04.
