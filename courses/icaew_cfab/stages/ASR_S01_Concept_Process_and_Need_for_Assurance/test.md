## Test items
1. An unmodified (unqualified) audit opinion means: Choose every correct option.
   A. the directors have guaranteed the company will not fail
   B. no fraud of any kind exists anywhere in the company
   C. the auditor found no material misstatement, and the financial statements give a true and fair view
   D. the audit found the financial statements to be perfectly accurate
2. Total assets are £6,500,000. Using a benchmark of 1% of total assets, calculate materiality, and briefly explain what performance materiality is used for. [4 marks]
3. Which best describes the 'expectation gap'? Choose every correct option.
   A. The gap between an audited and unaudited set of accounts
   B. The difference between what users believe assurance provides and what it actually provides
   C. The difference between profit under IFRS and UK GAAP
   D. The gap between materiality and performance materiality
4. Which distinguishes fraud from error? Choose every correct option.
   A. Fraud is always larger in value than error
   B. Fraud is intentional; error is unintentional
   C. Only error is a misstatement; fraud is not
   D. Error is always committed by management
5. Explain why professional scepticism matters even when management appears entirely trustworthy and cooperative. [3 marks]
6. Reasonable assurance (as given in a statutory audit) is: Choose every correct option.
   A. absolute assurance, i.e. a guarantee
   B. a high, but not absolute, level of assurance, expressed positively
   C. a low level of assurance, expressed negatively
   D. not a recognised level of assurance

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
2. [4] M1 6,500,000 x 0.01; A1 = £65,000; B2 performance materiality is set lower than overall materiality, to reduce to an appropriately low level the risk that the total of uncorrected and undetected misstatements exceeds overall materiality.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B3 professional scepticism requires the practitioner to maintain a questioning mind and critically assess evidence regardless of past experience of management's honesty, because even normally trustworthy management can make errors, face pressure to misstate results, or be unaware of a problem; accepting explanations uncritically risks missing a genuine material misstatement.
6. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
