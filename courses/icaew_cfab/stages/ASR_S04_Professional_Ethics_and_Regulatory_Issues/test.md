## Test items
1. Which fundamental ethical principle is most directly threatened when an accountant reviews figures they themselves originally prepared? Choose every correct option.
   A. Confidentiality (self-interest threat)
   B. Objectivity (self-review threat)
   C. Professional competence (advocacy threat)
   D. Professional behaviour (familiarity threat)
2. Which describes a principles-based ethical code, as ICAEW uses? Choose every correct option.
   A. A detailed rule is provided for every conceivable situation
   B. A conceptual framework of fundamental principles requires professional judgement to identify and address threats
   C. No ethical guidance is provided at all
   D. Only criminal law applies to accountants
3. An audit engagement partner has served the same client for 12 years and has become personally close to the finance director. Identify the threat this creates and a safeguard. [3 marks]
4. A client's managing director threatens to have the audit firm replaced unless the auditor agrees to a favourable treatment of a disputed balance. This is an example of: Choose every correct option.
   A. a self-interest threat
   B. an advocacy threat
   C. an intimidation threat
   D. a familiarity threat
5. Under the ICAEW Code, confidentiality may be overridden: Choose every correct option.
   A. whenever the accountant personally judges it convenient
   B. never, under any circumstances
   C. where there is a legal or professional right or duty to disclose, such as a suspected money laundering report
   D. only if the client has gone out of business
6. Explain the difference between independence of mind and independence in appearance, and why both matter. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
3. [3] B2 this creates a familiarity threat, because a long, close relationship may make the partner too sympathetic to the client's interests or less inclined to challenge them; B1 a safeguard is periodic rotation of the engagement partner (and/or key audit staff), as required for certain engagements by ethical and regulatory rules.
4. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
5. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
6. [3] B3 independence of mind is the actual state of mind that allows an opinion to be expressed without being compromised by threats to objectivity; independence in appearance is whether a reasonable and informed third party would conclude that independence had been compromised, even if it had not been in fact; both matter because assurance relies on user confidence, which requires not only that the practitioner is actually independent but that they are seen to be independent.
