# ASR_S04_Professional_Ethics_and_Regulatory_Issues - Lesson: Professional ethics and regulatory issues

## Goal
The learner explains the role of regulatory and professional bodies, the fundamental ethical principles and the threats to them, applies the conceptual framework to identify safeguards, and explains confidentiality (including GDPR) and the ISSB's role.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ASR.4a - Role of regulatory bodies and ethical codes
- ASR.4b - Fundamental principles of the ICAEW Code of Ethics
- ASR.4c - Threats to integrity, objectivity and independence
- ASR.4d - Safeguards to eliminate or reduce threats
- ASR.4e - Confidentiality and GDPR
- ASR.4f - Work outside expertise or experience

## How to teach this
Ask: why might an auditor who also prepares a client's financial statements struggle to audit them objectively? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ASR.4a Role of regulatory bodies and ethical codes
**Regulatory and professional bodies**: in the UK, the **Financial Reporting Council (FRC)** sets auditing and ethical standards and oversees audit quality for major public interest entities; the **ICAEW**, as a Recognised Supervisory Body, licenses and regulates its members conducting audit work and issues the **ICAEW Code of Ethics** (based on the IESBA International Code of Ethics for Professional Accountants). A **rules-based** code sets specific, detailed rules for every situation (clear but potentially rigid, and unable to anticipate every scenario); a **principles-based** code (the approach ICAEW and IESBA take) sets a conceptual framework of fundamental principles, requiring professional judgement to identify threats and apply safeguards in the specific circumstances - more flexible, but relies more on individual judgement.

#### ASR.4b Fundamental principles of the ICAEW Code of Ethics
**Fundamental ethical principles** (ICAEW Code of Ethics, mirroring IESBA): **integrity** (be straightforward and honest); **objectivity** (do not let bias, conflict of interest or undue influence override professional judgement); **professional competence and due care** (maintain the knowledge/skill to provide a competent service, and act diligently); **confidentiality** (respect the confidentiality of information acquired, not disclosing it without proper authority unless there is a legal/professional right or duty to do so); **professional behaviour** (comply with laws and regulations, avoid bringing the profession into disrepute).

#### ASR.4c Threats to integrity, objectivity and independence
**Threats to the fundamental principles** (the conceptual framework: identify, evaluate, address): **self-interest threat** (a financial or other interest inappropriately influences judgement, e.g. holding shares in an audit client); **self-review threat** (evaluating one's own prior work or judgement, e.g. auditing financial statements the firm itself helped prepare); **advocacy threat** (promoting a client's position to the point objectivity is compromised, e.g. acting as an advocate in litigation for an audit client); **familiarity threat** (a close/long relationship leads to being too sympathetic to a client's interests, e.g. a long-serving engagement partner); **intimidation threat** (being deterred from acting objectively by actual or perceived pressure, e.g. a threat to replace the auditor over a disagreement).

#### ASR.4d Safeguards to eliminate or reduce threats
**Safeguards** reduce a threat to an acceptable level: examples include using a different partner/team for a non-audit service, an independent quality/technical review of the work, rotating the engagement partner periodically, or - where no safeguard can reduce a threat to an acceptable level - declining or ending the engagement. Independence (freedom from threats that would compromise objectivity, both **independence of mind** and **independence in appearance**) is especially critical for audit and other assurance engagements.

#### ASR.4e Confidentiality and GDPR
**Confidentiality and GDPR**: a professional accountant must not disclose client information acquired through a professional relationship to third parties without proper authority, nor use it for personal advantage, except where disclosure is required or permitted: with client consent, where required by law (e.g. a court order, or a legal duty to report suspected **money laundering** under the Proceeds of Crime Act/Money Laundering Regulations - see the Law module), or where there is a professional duty/right to disclose (e.g. to respond to an inquiry by ICAEW as regulator, or in the public interest). Personal data handled in the course of an engagement must also be processed in line with **UK GDPR** and the Data Protection Act 2018 (see the Law module for the legal detail).

#### ASR.4f Work outside expertise or experience
**Work outside expertise**: accepting an engagement (or an aspect of one) beyond one's competence threatens professional competence and due care; the appropriate response is to decline, seek additional training/support, or involve someone suitably qualified, rather than proceed regardless.
