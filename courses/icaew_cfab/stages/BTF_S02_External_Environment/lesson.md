# BTF_S02_External_Environment - Lesson: The external environment of business (PESTLE)

## Goal
The learner analyses the political, legal, economic, social, technological and environmental (PESTLE) factors affecting a business, and explains the basics of competitive analysis.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BTF.2a - PESTLE analysis
- BTF.2b - Macroeconomic factors
- BTF.2c - Microeconomic factors
- BTF.2d - Social and demographic factors
- BTF.2e - Competitive factors (Porter's Five Forces)

## How to teach this
Ask a learner to name one recent change in interest rates, technology, or regulation that could change how a business they know operates - then map it to a PESTLE category. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### BTF.2a PESTLE analysis
**PESTLE analysis** structures the external factors affecting a business: **Political** (government stability, trade policy, taxation policy); **Economic** (see BTF.2b); **Social** (demographics, consumer attitudes, lifestyle trends); **Technological** (automation, digital disruption - see BTF_S05); **Legal** (employment law, consumer protection, competition law - detailed in the Law module); **Environmental** (climate change, resource scarcity, regulation on emissions).

#### BTF.2b Macroeconomic factors
**Macroeconomic factors**: **economic growth/GDP** (rising GDP generally means more consumer spending and business investment); **inflation** (the general rise in prices over time, eroding purchasing power and raising costs); **interest rates** (the cost of borrowing - higher rates raise a business's finance costs and can dampen consumer spending); **exchange rates** (affect the cost of imports and the competitiveness of exports); **unemployment** (affects labour supply/cost and consumer demand); and government fiscal/monetary policy, which influences all of the above. *Worked example*: a business borrows £500,000 at a variable rate that rises from 5% to 7%. Extra annual interest cost = 500,000 x (0.07 - 0.05) = £10,000.

#### BTF.2c Microeconomic factors
**Microeconomic factors**: how supply and demand interact for a specific good/market, including **price elasticity of demand** (how sensitive quantity demanded is to a price change - a necessity tends to be inelastic, a luxury or a good with many substitutes tends to be elastic), the structure of the market (perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly), and how competitors' pricing and product decisions affect an individual firm.

#### BTF.2d Social and demographic factors
**Social and demographic factors**: an ageing population, changing household composition, urbanisation, and shifting consumer attitudes (e.g. towards sustainability, health, or remote working) all change what and how businesses should sell, and how they should structure their workforce.

#### BTF.2e Competitive factors (Porter's Five Forces)
**Competitive factors**: **Porter's Five Forces** (a common CFAB-level framework) analyses an industry's competitiveness through the threat of new entrants, the bargaining power of suppliers, the bargaining power of buyers, the threat of substitute products/services, and the intensity of rivalry among existing competitors - together shaping how much profit potential the industry offers a typical firm within it.
