# BTF_S05_Business_Technology_Data_and_Cybersecurity - Lesson: Business technology, data and cybersecurity

## Goal
The learner explains how technology and data are used in business, the risks of digital disruption and cybersecurity threats, and basic data protection principles.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BTF.5a - Automation, RPA and AI in business
- BTF.5b - Financial systems and technology
- BTF.5c - Cybersecurity risk and controls
- BTF.5d - Data protection principles
- BTF.5e - Career skills and lifelong learning

## How to teach this
Ask: what could go wrong for a business that automates a process without first checking that the data feeding it is accurate? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### BTF.5a Automation, RPA and AI in business
**Digital disruption and technology**: **automation** and **robotic process automation (RPA)** perform repetitive, rules-based tasks (e.g. reconciling transactions) faster and more consistently than manual processing, freeing staff for judgement-based work, but requiring investment, staff retraining, and controls over what the automated process does; **artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning** increasingly support forecasting, fraud detection and data analysis, but raise questions of transparency, bias in training data, and the need for human review of outputs (see the Assurance and Management Information modules for how this affects evidence-gathering and decision-making respectively).

#### BTF.5b Financial systems and technology
**Financial systems and technology**: modern accounting systems integrate with banking (automated bank feeds/reconciliation), e-invoicing, and enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems that link finance with operations, sales and procurement in one database, reducing duplicate data entry but concentrating risk if the system itself is compromised or poorly controlled.

#### BTF.5c Cybersecurity risk and controls
**Cybersecurity and data risk**: threats include phishing (deceptive emails/messages to obtain credentials or install malware), ransomware (malicious software that encrypts data until a ransom is paid), and data breaches (unauthorised access to or loss of data); mitigating controls include firewalls, access controls (least-privilege access, strong authentication), regular software patching, staff training and awareness, and tested backup/disaster-recovery procedures - many overlap with the internal controls covered in the Assurance module.

#### BTF.5d Data protection principles
**Data protection**: personal data (information relating to an identifiable living individual) processed by a business must be handled in accordance with **UK GDPR** and the **Data Protection Act 2018** (detailed in the Law module) - processed lawfully, fairly and transparently, for specified purposes, kept accurate and no longer than necessary, and kept secure; a breach can result in regulatory fines and reputational damage alongside any legal liability.

#### BTF.5e Career skills and lifelong learning
**Career skills and lifelong learning**: as technology changes how accounting and finance work is done, professionals are expected to maintain and develop their technical and digital skills continuously (linking to the professional competence and due care principle in the Assurance module), rather than treating initial qualification as the end of learning.

## Explicitly not here
The legal detail of data protection and computer misuse offences is in the Law module.
