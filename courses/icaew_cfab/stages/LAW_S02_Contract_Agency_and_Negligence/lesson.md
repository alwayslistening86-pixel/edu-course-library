# LAW_S02_Contract_Agency_and_Negligence - Lesson: Contract, agency and negligence

## Goal
The learner explains when a legally binding contract exists and how it can end, the role and authority of an agent (including a partner), and when a negligence or vicarious liability claim arises.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LAW.2a - When a legally binding contract exists
- LAW.2b - Termination of a contract and breach remedies
- LAW.2c - Agency: roles, duties and authority
- LAW.2d - Partnerships and partner authority
- LAW.2e - Negligence and negligent misstatement
- LAW.2f - Vicarious liability

## How to teach this
Ask: if two friends agree over coffee that one will fix the other's car for free 'sometime', is that a legally binding contract? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### LAW.2a When a legally binding contract exists
**When a contract is legally binding**: a valid contract requires **offer** (a clear proposal capable of acceptance) and **acceptance** (unqualified agreement to the offer's exact terms - a counter-offer destroys the original offer), **consideration** (something of value given by each party - a promise made for nothing, a 'gratuitous promise', is generally not enforceable), **intention to create legal relations** (presumed present in a commercial/business context, presumed absent in a purely social/domestic arrangement, though both presumptions can be rebutted), and **capacity** (the parties must have legal capacity to contract, e.g. minors and some categories of person have limited capacity).

#### LAW.2b Termination of a contract and breach remedies
**Termination of a contract**: by **performance** (both sides fully perform their obligations), by **agreement** (the parties mutually agree to end it), by **frustration** (an unforeseen event makes performance impossible or radically different through no fault of either party, e.g. the subject matter is destroyed), or by **breach**. A **breach of contract** occurs when a party fails to perform an obligation as agreed; remedies include **damages** (compensation, generally aiming to put the innocent party in the position they would have been in had the contract been performed), and, for a sufficiently serious breach (of a **condition**, a major term, rather than a **warranty**, a minor term), the innocent party may also **terminate/rescind** the contract, being released from further performance.

#### LAW.2c Agency: roles, duties and authority
**Agency**: an **agent** acts on behalf of a **principal**, creating a legal relationship directly between the principal and a third party. An agent's **authority** may be **actual** (expressly given, or implied from the role) or **apparent/ostensible** (where the principal's conduct leads a third party reasonably to believe the agent has authority, even if it was not actually given). Agents owe duties of good faith, obedience to lawful instructions, care and skill, and must avoid undisclosed conflicts of interest.

#### LAW.2d Partnerships and partner authority
**Partnerships as agency**: under the Partnership Act 1890, each partner is generally an agent of the firm and the other partners for the purposes of the partnership's business, so a partner's acts within their **apparent authority** to carry on business of the kind carried on by the firm bind the firm and all partners jointly (and, for wrongful acts, jointly and severally) - even if that partner had no actual authority for that specific act, unless the third party knew of the limitation.

#### LAW.2e Negligence and negligent misstatement
**Negligence**: a claimant must prove the defendant owed them a **duty of care**, **breached** that duty (fell below the standard of a reasonable person in that position), and that the breach **caused** loss which was not too remote. **Negligent misstatement** (relevant to professional advisers, including accountants) can create liability for a careless statement relied on by someone to whom a duty of care was owed - historically limited to situations of a 'special relationship' of proximity and reasonable reliance, to avoid unlimited liability to an unlimited class of people.

#### LAW.2f Vicarious liability
**Vicarious liability**: an employer can be held liable for a tort (civil wrong, e.g. negligence) committed by an employee, where the wrong is committed in the course of their employment - even though the employer did nothing wrong itself - because the employer is best placed to bear/insure the risk and benefits from the employee's work; an employer is not normally vicariously liable for an independent contractor's acts, or for an employee's acts wholly outside the scope of their employment (a 'frolic of their own').
