## Test items
1. Which is NOT one of the essential elements of a legally binding contract? Choose every correct option.
   A. Offer and acceptance
   B. Consideration
   C. Intention to create legal relations
   D. Written form, in every case
2. An event occurs after a contract is signed that makes performance impossible through no fault of either party (e.g. the specific goods to be sold are destroyed by fire before delivery). Name the doctrine that would end the contract, and explain its effect. [3 marks]
3. An employee, acting entirely for their own purposes and wholly outside their job duties during work hours, assaults a customer. Is the employer likely to be vicariously liable? Choose every correct option.
   A. Yes, always, regardless of the circumstances
   B. No, this would likely be considered outside the course of employment (a 'frolic of their own')
   C. Only if the employer expressly authorised the assault
   D. Vicarious liability never applies to any employee conduct
4. An accountant carelessly gives financial advice to a family friend at a social gathering, with no engagement, fee or indication the advice would be relied on for a specific transaction. The friend later suffers a loss relying on it. Explain whether a negligent misstatement claim is likely to succeed. [3 marks]
5. A partner in an ordinary partnership signs a contract of a type usual for the firm's business, without the other partners' actual knowledge. Are the other partners bound? Choose every correct option.
   A. No, only the signing partner is bound
   B. Yes, generally, because the partner acted within their apparent authority to carry on the firm's usual business
   C. Only if all partners signed personally
   D. Only if the partnership has fewer than two partners

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: D (exactly these options, no others)
2. [3] B2 this is frustration; B1 the contract is automatically discharged/terminated, and neither party is in breach, though there may be statutory rules on recovering money already paid or expenses incurred.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] B3 unlikely to succeed - negligent misstatement liability generally requires a special relationship of proximity, with the adviser knowing (or it being reasonably foreseeable) that the specific advice would be relied on for a particular purpose/transaction; casual social advice with no such relationship or reasonable reliance is unlikely to meet that threshold.
5. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
