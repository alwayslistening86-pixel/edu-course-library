## Test items
1. Which is normally an exempt asset for CGT purposes? Choose every correct option.
   A. A buy-to-let investment property
   B. An individual's only or main private residence, in most circumstances
   C. Shares in a private trading company
   D. A business's freehold factory
2. An asset bought for £8,500 (plus £200 acquisition costs) is sold for £15,000 (less £300 disposal costs). Calculate the chargeable gain before any exemptions or reliefs. [2 marks]
3. A gift made by an individual directly to their adult child, more than 7 years before the donor's death, is generally: Choose every correct option.
   A. always chargeable to inheritance tax regardless of timing
   B. a Potentially Exempt Transfer that becomes fully exempt once the 7-year survival period has passed
   C. immediately chargeable at the lifetime rate
   D. exempt only if made to a spouse
4. Explain what taper relief does, and clarify a common misconception about what it reduces. [3 marks]
5. Which is a chargeable person for capital gains tax purposes (rather than paying tax on gains through corporation tax instead)? Choose every correct option.
   A. A UK-resident individual
   B. A UK-resident company, on its own chargeable gains
   C. Both; the same tax applies to individuals and companies
   D. Neither individuals nor companies pay tax on gains

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [2] M1 gain = (15,000-300)-(8,500+200); A1 = £6,000.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] B3 taper relief reduces the inheritance tax charged on a lifetime gift that becomes chargeable because the donor died within 7 years, on a sliding scale depending on how many complete years (3 to 7) elapsed between the gift and death; a common misconception is that it reduces the value of the gift itself - it does not, it reduces only the tax charged on that value.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
