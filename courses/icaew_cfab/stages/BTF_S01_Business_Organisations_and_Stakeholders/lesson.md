# BTF_S01_Business_Organisations_and_Stakeholders - Lesson: Business organisations, stakeholders and objectives

## Goal
The learner explains the main types of business organisation, their advantages and disadvantages, and identifies and analyses the stakeholders in a business.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BTF.1a - Types of business organisation
- BTF.1b - Stakeholders and Mendelow's matrix
- BTF.1c - Business objectives

## How to teach this
Ask: why might a growing sole trader decide to become a limited company, and what would they give up by doing so? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### BTF.1a Types of business organisation
**Types of business organisation**: a **sole trader** (one owner, unlimited liability, full control, simple to set up, but liable personally for all business debts); an ordinary **partnership** (two or more owners sharing profit/loss and, in England and Wales, unlimited joint and several liability under the Partnership Act 1890 unless structured as an LLP); a **limited liability partnership (LLP)**, combining partnership flexibility with limited liability for members; and a **limited company** (a separate legal person from its owners - see the Law module - offering limited liability to shareholders, but with more regulatory/reporting burden and, for a public limited company (plc), the ability to offer shares to the public). Each choice trades off control, liability, cost/complexity of formation and ongoing compliance, and access to finance.

#### BTF.1b Stakeholders and Mendelow's matrix
**Stakeholders** are any individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, an organisation's activities: internal (employees, management), connected (shareholders, lenders, customers, suppliers), and external (government, the local community, pressure groups). **Mendelow's power/interest matrix** classifies stakeholders by their power to influence the organisation and their interest in it: high power/high interest stakeholders should be **managed closely** (key players); high power/low interest should be **kept satisfied**; low power/high interest should be **kept informed**; low power/low interest need only **minimal effort**. Stakeholder interests can conflict (e.g. shareholders wanting higher dividends versus employees wanting higher pay), and management must balance these competing claims.

#### BTF.1c Business objectives
**Business objectives** commonly include profit maximisation, growth, market share, and increasingly a broader set of **environmental, social and governance (ESG)** objectives reflecting stakeholder and regulatory expectations, not only shareholders. A **mission statement** sets out an organisation's fundamental purpose; objectives should ideally be SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound).
