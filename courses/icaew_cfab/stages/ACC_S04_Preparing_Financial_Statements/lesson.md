# ACC_S04_Preparing_Financial_Statements - Lesson: Preparing financial statements

## Goal
The learner identifies the components and purpose of a full set of financial statements and prepares a statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position for a sole trader, partnership or limited company from an adjusted trial balance.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.4a - Components and purpose of financial statements
- ACC.4b - Preparing statements for sole traders, partnerships and companies
- ACC.4c - Interpreting financial statements with ratios

## How to teach this
Ask what the two core financial statements tell a lender that the other cannot: one about a period, one about a moment in time. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ACC.4a Components and purpose of financial statements
**Components and purpose of financial statements**: the **statement of profit or loss** reports income and expenses for a period, arriving at profit (or loss) for the period; the **statement of financial position** reports assets, liabilities and equity at a single point in time (the accounting equation, Assets = Liabilities + Equity); a **statement of cash flows** reports cash inflows and outflows for the period, classified into operating, investing and financing activities; and **notes** provide supporting detail and required disclosures. Together they let a user assess performance, position and liquidity.

#### ACC.4b Preparing statements for sole traders, partnerships and companies
**Preparing statements for different entity types**: a **sole trader's** equity is simply capital introduced plus retained profits less drawings; a **partnership's** financial statements additionally include an appropriation account (dividing profit between partners per the partnership agreement - salaries, interest on capital, then the profit-sharing ratio) and separate current accounts per partner; a **limited company's** equity comprises share capital, share premium and retained earnings (reserves), and its statement of profit or loss ends in profit after tax, from which any dividend is later deducted directly from retained earnings (not as an expense).
*Worked example, sole trader statement of profit or loss extract*: revenue £120,000, cost of sales £72,000, so gross profit = £48,000 (gross profit margin = 40%). Operating expenses £28,000, so profit for the year = £20,000.

#### ACC.4c Interpreting financial statements with ratios
**Interpreting the financial statements with ratios** (an introductory extension used to check the statements make sense): **gross profit margin** = gross profit/revenue x 100; **operating profit margin** = operating profit/revenue x 100; **current ratio** = current assets/current liabilities (a rough test of short-term liquidity); **return on capital employed (ROCE)** = operating profit/(total equity + non-current liabilities) x 100. *Worked example*: current assets £45,000, current liabilities £30,000: current ratio = 1.50:1. Operating profit £40,000, capital employed (equity + non-current liabilities) £250,000: ROCE = 16.0%.

## Explicitly not here
Consolidated financial statements for a group are not examined at this introductory level.
