# ACC_S03_Adjustments_and_Correction_of_Errors - Lesson: Adjustments to accounting records and correction of errors

## Goal
The learner identifies and corrects omissions and errors in the accounting records, including through journal entries and a suspense account, and adjusts for accruals, prepayments, depreciation and irrecoverable debts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.3a - Identifying omissions and errors
- ACC.3b - Correcting errors via journal entries
- ACC.3c - Suspense accounts and period-end adjustments

## How to teach this
Ask: if the trial balance still balances, does that guarantee the accounts are free of errors? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ACC.3a Identifying omissions and errors
**Identifying omissions and errors**: a trial balance can still balance even with errors present. Errors **not** revealed by an imbalance include: error of omission (a transaction left out entirely), error of commission (posted to the wrong account of the correct type, e.g. one supplier's account instead of another), error of principle (posted to the wrong type of account, e.g. an expense treated as an asset), error of original entry (the wrong amount used, but posted consistently as both debit and credit), reversal of entries (debit and credit swapped), and compensating errors (two errors of equal and opposite value). An imbalance in the trial balance (one-sided errors, e.g. a debit posted with no matching credit) indicates a genuine error and is temporarily plugged with a **suspense account**.

#### ACC.3b Correcting errors via journal entries
**Correcting errors**: each correction is made via a **journal entry** (debit one account, credit another, with a narrative explaining why). *Worked example*: a £250 cash sale was correctly debited to cash but credited in error to Rent Expense instead of Sales. Correcting journal: Debit Rent Expense £250 (remove the wrong credit), Credit Sales £250 (record it correctly) - net effect, Rent Expense is corrected down by £250 and Sales is corrected up by £250.

#### ACC.3c Suspense accounts and period-end adjustments
**Suspense accounts**: opened automatically when the trial balance does not balance, for the amount of the difference, so a trial balance can still be extracted while the cause is investigated; once found, the error is corrected and the suspense account is cleared to nil. **Period-end adjustments** (also examinable here): **accruals** (an expense incurred but not yet invoiced/paid is added as an expense and a current liability) and **prepayments** (an expense paid in advance is deducted from the expense and shown as a current asset); **depreciation** (systematically allocating a tangible non-current asset's depreciable cost - cost less residual value - over its useful life, e.g. straight-line = (cost - residual value)/useful life); and **irrecoverable (bad) debts** (written off as an expense when recovery is judged unlikely) and an **allowance for doubtful debts** (a general provision against receivables expected, but not yet known, to be irrecoverable). *Worked example, straight-line depreciation*: a machine costs £18,000, residual value £2,000, useful life 4 years: annual depreciation = (18,000 - 2,000)/4 = £4,000/year.
