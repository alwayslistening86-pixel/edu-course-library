# MI_S05_Technology_Data_and_Ethics_in_Decision_Making - Lesson: Technology, data and ethics in decision making

## Goal
The learner explains the purpose, sources and comparability of data used in decision-making, common data and spreadsheet errors, and ethical issues in preparing and using management information.

## Syllabus items taught here
- MI.5a - Data purpose, types, sources and comparability
- MI.5b - Data collection and analysis principles
- MI.5c - Data errors and spreadsheet risk
- MI.5d - Visualisations, graphs and charts
- MI.5e - Ethics in preparing and using management information

## How to teach this
Ask: could a manager present two entirely accurate charts of the same data that lead readers to opposite conclusions? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### MI.5a Data purpose, types, sources and comparability
**Data purpose, types and sources**: **primary data** is collected first-hand for a specific purpose (e.g. a customer survey); **secondary data** already exists and is reused (e.g. published market statistics). Data may be **quantitative** (numeric) or **qualitative** (descriptive); internal sources (sales records, payroll) and external sources (government statistics, market research) are both used. **Comparability** requires data to be collected/measured consistently (e.g. the same definitions, the same period length) before it is meaningfully compared across time or between entities.

#### MI.5b Data collection and analysis principles
**Data collection and analysis principles**: data should be **accurate**, **complete**, **relevant** to the decision, **timely**, and **cost-effective** to collect (worth more than it costs); poor data quality at collection undermines every later analysis, however sophisticated - 'garbage in, garbage out'.

#### MI.5c Data errors and spreadsheet risk
**Data errors and data protection**: common errors include transposition (e.g. entering 549 instead of 594), duplication, omission, and rounding errors compounding across many calculations; a spreadsheet's greatest risk is that a single formula error can propagate silently through an entire model, so checking, version control and restricting who can edit key cells all matter (linking to the internal controls covered in the Assurance module). Data protection principles for personal data used in management information are covered in the Business, Technology and Finance and Law modules.

#### MI.5d Visualisations, graphs and charts
**Visualisations, graphs and charts**: a bar chart suits comparing discrete categories, a pie chart suits proportions of a whole, a line graph suits a trend over time, and a scatter diagram suits the relationship between two variables; a poorly chosen chart type, a truncated axis, or an inconsistent scale can mislead a reader even from entirely accurate underlying data, so choosing and reading charts critically matters as much as the analysis itself.

#### MI.5e Ethics in preparing and using management information
**Ethics in preparing and using management information**: presenting information selectively, choosing a misleading chart format, or suppressing an inconvenient variance to protect a bonus target all breach the fundamental ethical principle of integrity/objectivity even where no figure is individually falsified; **data bias** (e.g. a forecast built only on data from a favourable period, or an AI model trained on unrepresentative data) can distort decisions even when no one intends deception, so professional scepticism about the data itself - not only about people - is part of ethical practice.
