# ACC_S02_Maintaining_Financial_Records_and_Double_Entry - Lesson: Maintaining financial records and double-entry bookkeeping

## Goal
The learner explains the purpose of financial records, identifies sources of accounting information, and records transactions using double-entry bookkeeping.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.2a - Purpose of financial records and financial statements
- ACC.2b - Sources of accounting information and double-entry bookkeeping
- ACC.2c - Recording transactions per applicable standards

## How to teach this
Ask: why does every transaction need to be recorded twice (a debit and a credit) rather than just once? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ACC.2a Purpose of financial records and financial statements
**Purpose of financial records and financial statements**: financial records (the books of prime entry, ledgers) exist to capture every transaction accurately and completely, so that reliable financial statements can be prepared for owners, management, HMRC and other users, and so that the business can be controlled (e.g. knowing what customers owe, what is owed to suppliers, how much cash is available).

#### ACC.2b Sources of accounting information and double-entry bookkeeping
**Sources of accounting information**: source documents (sales and purchase invoices, credit notes, receipts, cheques/bank transfers, payroll records) are the primary evidence for each transaction. They are first entered into a **book of prime entry** (sales day book, purchases day book, cash book, journal), then posted to the **general ledger** as **debits and credits** under **double-entry bookkeeping**: every transaction affects at least two ledger accounts, and total debits always equal total credits (keeping the accounting equation, Assets = Liabilities + Equity, in balance). The rule: debit increases assets and expenses, credit increases liabilities, equity and income (and vice versa to decrease them).

#### ACC.2c Recording transactions per applicable standards
*Worked example, double entry*: a business sells goods for £1,500 cash. Debit Cash £1,500 (asset increases), Credit Sales/Revenue £1,500 (income increases). It then pays a supplier invoice of £600 by bank transfer: Debit Payables £600 (liability decreases), Credit Bank £600 (asset decreases). After both transactions, cash/bank has increased by £900 net.
