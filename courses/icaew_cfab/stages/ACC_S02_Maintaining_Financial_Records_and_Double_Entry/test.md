## Test items
1. A business receives £3,000 cash from a credit customer who owed that amount. State the debit and credit entries. [2 marks]
2. Under double-entry bookkeeping, a credit entry to a liability account: Choose every correct option.
   A. decreases the liability
   B. increases the liability
   C. has no effect on the liability
   D. is only used for errors
3. A business pays £450 wages by bank transfer. State the debit and credit entries and name the book of prime entry the payment would typically first be recorded in. [3 marks]
4. Which is a book of prime entry? Choose every correct option.
   A. The trial balance
   B. The sales day book
   C. The statement of financial position
   D. The statement of cash flows
5. Why must total debits equal total credits in a double-entry system? Choose every correct option.
   A. It is a legal requirement with no accounting reason
   B. Every transaction affects at least two accounts, keeping the accounting equation in balance
   C. It only applies to cash transactions
   D. It applies only to limited companies

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 Debit Cash/Bank £3,000 (asset increases); A1 Credit Receivables £3,000 (asset decreases - the receivable is settled).
2. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
3. [3] M1 Debit Wages expense £450; A1 Credit Bank £450; B1 the payment is first recorded in the cash book.
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
