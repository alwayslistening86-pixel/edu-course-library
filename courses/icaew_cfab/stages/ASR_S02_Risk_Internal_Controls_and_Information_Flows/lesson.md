# ASR_S02_Risk_Internal_Controls_and_Information_Flows - Lesson: Risk, internal controls and information flows

## Goal
The learner explains audit risk and its components, why effective internal control matters, the main types of control, and the role of the internal audit function.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ASR.2a - Audit risk and its components
- ASR.2b - Business risk and its impact on audit risk
- ASR.2c - Why effective internal control matters

## How to teach this
Ask: could a company with a brilliant accounting system still produce misstated financial statements if only one person handles cash from start to finish? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ASR.2a Audit risk and its components
**Audit risk** = the risk that the auditor expresses an inappropriate opinion when the financial statements are materially misstated. It is analysed as **Audit risk = Inherent risk x Control risk x Detection risk**. **Inherent risk** is the susceptibility of a balance/transaction class to misstatement before considering controls (e.g. a complex estimate like a provision is inherently riskier than a simple cash balance). **Control risk** is the risk that the entity's internal controls fail to prevent or detect a material misstatement. **Detection risk** is the risk that the auditor's own procedures fail to detect a material misstatement that exists - the only component the auditor can directly control, by adjusting the extent/nature/timing of procedures.

#### ASR.2b Business risk and its impact on audit risk
**Business risk** (a risk to the entity achieving its objectives, e.g. losing a major customer, a cyberattack, regulatory change) can translate into audit risk where it increases the likelihood of a material misstatement (e.g. going concern doubt following the loss of a major customer increases the risk that the going concern basis, and asset valuations built on it, are misstated).

#### ASR.2c Why effective internal control matters
**Why effective internal control matters**: a good system of internal control helps an entity run its operations efficiently, safeguard assets, ensure the reliability of financial and management information, and comply with laws and regulations - reducing (though never eliminating) the risk of error and fraud.

#### ASR.2d Fundamental principles of effective controls
**Fundamental principles of effective controls**: **segregation of duties** (splitting authorisation, custody/execution and recording of a transaction across different people, so no one person can commit and conceal an error or fraud alone); **authorisation and approval** (transactions above a threshold need sign-off from an appropriate level of management); **physical controls** (locks, restricted access, safes); **arithmetical and accounting controls** (reconciliations, control accounts, sequential numbering of documents); **management controls** (review of performance reports, budgetary control); **supervision**; and **organisation** (a clear, documented structure of reporting lines and responsibilities).

#### ASR.2e Main business processes and information flows
**Main business processes and information flows**: e.g. the sales/revenue cycle (order -> despatch -> invoice -> cash receipt -> receivables ledger), the purchases cycle (requisition -> order -> goods received -> invoice -> payment -> payables ledger), and payroll (time recording -> calculation -> authorisation -> payment -> posting); understanding how information flows through a process, and where a control could fail, underpins both control evaluation and evidence-gathering.

#### ASR.2f Types of internal control
**Types of control**: **preventative controls** stop an error/fraud before it happens (e.g. requiring dual authorisation for a bank payment above a threshold); **detective controls** identify an error/fraud after it has occurred (e.g. a monthly bank reconciliation). Both matter: prevention is generally preferable, but detective controls provide a safety net where prevention fails, and their combination in layers ('defence in depth') is stronger than either alone.

#### ASR.2g Controls mitigating cyber and digital-transformation risk
**Controls mitigating modern risks**: cyber-risk controls (firewalls, access controls, staff training against phishing), controls over IT change and access, and controls addressing supply-chain and digital-transformation risk (e.g. due diligence over new digital suppliers, reconciling automated/robotic process automation (RPA) outputs) increasingly sit alongside traditional manual controls as businesses digitise.

#### ASR.2h Internal audit function
**The internal audit function** (where one exists) is a monitoring control in itself: an independent function within the entity (though separate from external audit) that reviews and evaluates the effectiveness of risk management, control and governance processes, reporting to those charged with governance (often an audit committee) rather than to operational management, to preserve its objectivity.
