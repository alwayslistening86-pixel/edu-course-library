# LAW_S04_Criminal_Law_in_Business - Practice: Criminal law in business and professional services

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner predicts or writes first, then runs it to check. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. An accountant forms a suspicion, but not certain knowledge, that a client's transactions involve money laundering. Explain their legal obligation, and why it overrides normal client confidentiality. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [3] B3 the accountant has a legal duty under POCA/the Money Laundering Regulations to report the suspicion to the firm's MLRO (and potentially, via the MLRO, to the National Crime Agency); this reporting duty overrides normal confidentiality because the law creates a specific statutory exception - and failing to report a genuine suspicion, or tipping off the client, is itself a criminal offence.
