# LAW_S04_Criminal_Law_in_Business - Lesson: Criminal law in business and professional services

## Goal
The learner explains the main criminal offences relevant to business and professional services: money laundering, bribery, fraud and cybercrime, and protection for employees who report wrongdoing.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LAW.4a - Anti-money laundering offences and obligations
- LAW.4b - Bribery Act 2010 offences
- LAW.4c - Fraud Act 2006 and cybercrime offences
- LAW.4d - Protection from dismissal for reporting workplace malpractice

## How to teach this
Ask: why does UK law impose a specific legal duty on accountants (not just a moral one) to report a suspicion of money laundering? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### LAW.4a Anti-money laundering offences and obligations
**Anti-money laundering**: the **Proceeds of Crime Act 2002 (POCA)** and the **Money Laundering Regulations** create offences (e.g. concealing, disguising, converting, transferring or removing criminal property; and 'tipping off' a suspect that a report has been made) and impose obligations on accountants and other regulated professionals: customer due diligence, ongoing monitoring, and, critically, a legal duty to report knowledge or **suspicion** of money laundering to the firm's Money Laundering Reporting Officer (MLRO) and, in turn, potentially to the National Crime Agency - failure to report (where a regulated person had reasonable grounds for suspicion) is itself a criminal offence. This duty to report overrides the normal duty of client confidentiality (see the Assurance module).

#### LAW.4b Bribery Act 2010 offences
**Bribery**: the **Bribery Act 2010** creates offences of offering, promising or giving a bribe; requesting, agreeing to receive or accepting a bribe; bribing a foreign public official; and a strict-liability corporate offence of a commercial organisation **failing to prevent bribery** by a person associated with it (e.g. an employee or agent), for which the only defence is that the organisation had 'adequate procedures' in place to prevent bribery.

#### LAW.4c Fraud Act 2006 and cybercrime offences
**Fraud**: the **Fraud Act 2006** creates a single offence of fraud, committable by **false representation** (dishonestly making a false representation, knowing it is or might be untrue/misleading, intending to make a gain or cause a loss), by **failing to disclose information** where there is a legal duty to do so, or by **abuse of position** (dishonestly abusing a position in which one is expected to safeguard another's financial interests). **Cybercrime** offences under the **Computer Misuse Act 1990** cover unauthorised access to computer material, unauthorised access with intent to commit a further offence, and unauthorised modification of computer material. **Fraudulent trading** (knowingly carrying on a company's business with intent to defraud creditors) and **insider dealing** (dealing in securities using unpublished price-sensitive information) are further specific business-related offences.

#### LAW.4d Protection from dismissal for reporting workplace malpractice
**Protection for reporting workplace malpractice**: the **Public Interest Disclosure Act 1998** (amending the Employment Rights Act 1996) protects a worker from dismissal or other detriment for making a **protected disclosure** ('whistleblowing') about certain types of wrongdoing (e.g. a criminal offence, a failure to comply with a legal obligation, danger to health and safety, or environmental damage), provided the disclosure is made reasonably and, generally, to an appropriate person/body - encouraging staff to report serious wrongdoing without fear of retaliation.
