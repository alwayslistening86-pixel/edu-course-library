# ACC_S01_Concepts_Ethics_and_Regulatory_Framework - Lesson: Accounting concepts, ethics and the regulatory framework

## Goal
The learner explains the standard-setting process, the qualitative characteristics of useful financial information, fundamental accounting concepts, the accrual basis, and the ethical/sustainability context of accounting.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.1a - Standard-setting process and authority

- ACC.1c - Effect of transactions under the IFRS Conceptual Framework
- ACC.1d - Fair presentation and true and fair view

## How to teach this
Ask: if a company could choose any accounting policy it liked each year to make profit look best, would its financial statements still be useful to a lender deciding whether to renew a loan? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### ACC.1a Standard-setting process and authority
**Standard-setting and authority**: the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) issues IFRS Accounting Standards, which UK-listed companies must follow for their consolidated accounts; UK unlisted companies may follow IFRS or UK GAAP (FRS 102). The **IFRS Conceptual Framework for Financial Reporting** underpins the standards: it defines the elements of financial statements (asset, liability, equity, income, expense), sets recognition criteria (an item is recognised when it meets the definition of an element and providing information about it is useful), and guides standard-setters and preparers where no standard directly applies.

#### ACC.1b Qualitative characteristics of financial information
**Qualitative characteristics of useful financial information** (Conceptual Framework): the two **fundamental** characteristics are **relevance** (capable of making a difference to a user's decisions - has predictive and/or confirmatory value, and is material) and **faithful representation** (complete, neutral and free from error - not necessarily perfectly accurate, but a faithful depiction). Four **enhancing** characteristics support these: **comparability** (across periods and between entities, aided by consistent policies), **verifiability** (different knowledgeable observers could reach consensus), **timeliness** (available in time to influence decisions), and **understandability** (clearly and concisely presented to users with reasonable business knowledge).

#### ACC.1c Effect of transactions under the IFRS Conceptual Framework
**Effect of transactions under the Conceptual Framework**: every transaction is analysed into its effect on the accounting equation, **Assets = Liabilities + Equity**. *Worked example*: a business buys a £2,500 van for cash. Assets: van +£2,500, cash -£2,500 (net effect on total assets: £0); liabilities and equity are unaffected, so the equation still balances.

#### ACC.1d Fair presentation and true and fair view
**Fair presentation and true and fair view**: financial statements prepared in compliance with applicable accounting standards are presumed to give a fair presentation (IFRS) / true and fair view (UK Companies Act term) of the entity's financial position and performance; in the rare case that compliance would be misleading, additional disclosure (or, exceptionally, departure) is required, with the reasons explained.

#### ACC.1e Accounting concepts and principles
**Accounting concepts and principles**: **going concern** (statements are prepared on the assumption the business will continue operating for the foreseeable future, unless there is intention/necessity to liquidate); **business entity concept** (the business is accounted for separately from its owner(s)); **money measurement** (only items expressible in monetary terms are recorded); **historical cost** (assets are normally recorded at original cost, though some standards require or permit revaluation/fair value); **consistency** (the same accounting policies are applied period to period, changed only for good reason); **prudence** (a degree of caution in exercising judgement under uncertainty, without deliberately understating assets/income or overstating liabilities/expenses - prudence is not a licence to create hidden reserves).

#### ACC.1f Accrual basis versus cash basis accounting
**Accruals (accrual) basis versus cash basis**: under the **accrual basis**, income and expenses are recognised when earned/incurred, not necessarily when cash changes hands (e.g. a sale on credit is recognised as revenue immediately, with a receivable recorded); this is the basis required by IFRS/UK GAAP for statutory accounts. Under the **cash basis** (available to some small unincorporated businesses for tax purposes), income and expenses are recognised only when cash is received or paid. *Worked example*: a business invoices a customer £4,000 in December but is paid in January. Accrual-basis December revenue = £4,000 (revenue recognised on delivery/invoice); cash-basis December revenue = £0 (no cash received yet).

#### ACC.1g Ethical and sustainability considerations
**Ethical and sustainability considerations**: accountants preparing or auditing financial statements must apply the fundamental ethical principles (integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality, professional behaviour - see the Assurance module for the full framework); management bias towards a particular reported outcome (e.g. smoothing profit to hit a bonus target) threatens neutrality and faithful representation. **Sustainability reporting** is an increasingly significant complement to the financial statements, covering an entity's environmental, social and governance (ESG) impacts and dependencies alongside its financial performance.

#### ACC.1h ISSB aims and standards
The **International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB)**, created by the IFRS Foundation, issues **IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards** (IFRS S1: general sustainability-related disclosures; IFRS S2: climate-related disclosures) so that sustainability information can be reported with the same rigour, comparability and connection to the financial statements as financial information itself.
