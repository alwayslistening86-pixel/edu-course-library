# MI_S01_Costing_and_Pricing - Lesson: Costing and pricing

## Goal
The learner classifies costs, calculates unit costs using marginal and absorption costing, sets cost-based prices, explains price elasticity, and calculates a basic transfer price.

## Syllabus items taught here
- MI.1a - Uses of cost information and cost classification
- MI.1b - Marginal and absorption costing
- MI.1c - Selecting an appropriate costing method
- MI.1d - Cost-based pricing
- MI.1e - Price mechanism and elasticity
- MI.1f - Transfer pricing

## How to teach this
Ask: two identical products, made in the same factory, could be reported with different unit costs depending only on which costing method is used - how can that be? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### MI.1a Uses of cost information and cost classification
**Uses of cost information**: management uses cost information for pricing, decision-making, cost control, inventory valuation and performance measurement (see MI_S03). Costs are classified by **behaviour** - **fixed** (constant in total across a relevant range of activity, e.g. factory rent), **variable** (constant per unit, total rises with activity, e.g. direct material) - and by **traceability** - **direct** (traceable to a specific cost unit, e.g. the wood in a chair) or **indirect/overhead** (shared across cost units, e.g. factory supervisor's salary).

#### MI.1b Marginal and absorption costing
**Absorption costing** charges each unit with a share of all production costs, including fixed production overhead, via an **overhead absorption rate (OAR)** = budgeted overhead / budgeted activity level (e.g. per labour hour or machine hour). **Marginal costing** charges each unit only with its variable production cost, treating fixed production overhead as a period cost written off in full. *Worked example*: budgeted fixed production overhead £80,000, budgeted machine hours 10,000, so OAR = £8.00/machine hour. A unit taking 2 machine hours and £15 of direct material/labour has an absorption cost of £31.00 (= 15 + 2 x 8.00), but a marginal cost of just £15 (fixed overhead excluded).

#### MI.1c Selecting an appropriate costing method
**Selecting a costing method**: absorption costing is required for external financial reporting (inventory must include a fair share of production overhead under IAS 2) and shows a 'full' unit cost useful for long-run pricing; marginal costing better supports short-run decisions (see MI_S04) because it separates the cost that genuinely varies with the next unit produced from the fixed cost that does not.

#### MI.1d Cost-based pricing
**Cost-based pricing**: **cost-plus pricing** sets the selling price as unit cost plus a percentage mark-up (mark-up on cost) or, expressed differently, so that cost is a target percentage of the selling price (margin on selling price). *Worked example*: unit cost £18, mark-up 40% on cost: price = 18 x 1.40 = £25.20. Alternatively, unit cost £18, target margin 25% of selling price: price = cost / (1 - margin) = 18 / (1 - 0.25) = £24.00.

#### MI.1e Price mechanism and elasticity
**Price mechanism and elasticity**: **price elasticity of demand** = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price. Demand is **elastic** (elasticity > 1 in absolute value) if quantity changes proportionately more than price - typical of goods with close substitutes; **inelastic** (< 1) if quantity changes proportionately less than price - typical of necessities. A firm facing elastic demand loses more in volume than it gains in price from a price rise (so revenue falls), while a firm facing inelastic demand gains overall revenue from a price rise. *Worked example*: a 10% price rise causes a 4% fall in quantity demanded: elasticity = -4%/10% = -0.4 (inelastic, since |-0.4| < 1), so total revenue would be expected to rise.

#### MI.1f Transfer pricing
**Transfer pricing**: when one division of a business supplies goods/services to another division, a **transfer price** must be set for internal reporting purposes; common bases include **market price** (if an external market exists, generally preferred as it doesn't distort either division's reported performance), **cost-based** (variable cost, or cost plus a margin), or **negotiated price** (agreed between the divisions). *Worked example, cost-plus transfer price*: the supplying division's full cost per unit is £40; using cost plus 20%, transfer price = 40 x 1.20 = £48.00.
