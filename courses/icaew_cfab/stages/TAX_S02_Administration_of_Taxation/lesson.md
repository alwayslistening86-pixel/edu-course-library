# TAX_S02_Administration_of_Taxation - Lesson: Administration of taxation

## Goal
The learner explains record-keeping requirements, the PAYE and self-assessment systems, due dates for returns and payments, and the consequences of late or incorrect submissions.

## Syllabus items taught here
- TAX.2a - Record retention requirements
- TAX.2b - PAYE and Class 1 NIC administration
- TAX.2c - Self-assessment for individuals and companies
- TAX.2d - VAT and other reporting due dates
- TAX.2e - Interest, penalties and HMRC enquiries

## How to teach this
Ask: why does HMRC require records to be kept for years after a tax return is filed, not just until the return is submitted? Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### TAX.2a Record retention requirements
**Record retention**: taxpayers must keep records supporting their tax returns for a minimum period - broadly, individuals must keep records for at least 22 months after the end of the tax year for a return filed on time (longer if filed late, or if the taxpayer is in business, where the period extends to 5 years after the 31 January filing deadline); companies must generally keep records for at least 6 years from the end of the accounting period. Failure to keep adequate records can itself attract a penalty, independent of whether the tax return figures turn out to be correct.

#### TAX.2b PAYE and Class 1 NIC administration
**PAYE and Class 1 NIC**: **Pay As You Earn (PAYE)** is the system by which an employer deducts income tax and employee (Class 1) NIC from an employee's wages at source, and pays employer (Class 1 secondary) NIC on top, remitting all of it to HMRC - so most employees never file a tax return, as their tax is collected in-year, automatically. An employee's **PAYE tax code** (e.g. the standard code derived from the personal allowance) tells the employer how much tax-free pay to allow before deducting tax; an incorrect code under- or over-deducts tax during the year, corrected later through a code adjustment or a self-assessment reconciliation.

#### TAX.2c Self-assessment for individuals and companies
**Self-assessment**: individuals with more complex tax affairs (e.g. the self-employed, higher earners with untaxed income, or anyone HMRC requires to) must file a **self-assessment tax return**; companies file a **corporation tax return (CT600)** and self-assess their own corporation tax liability. Filing/payment due dates: for individuals, a paper return is due by 31 October following the tax year end, an online return by the following **31 January**, with the balancing tax payment also due by that **31 January** (plus, for some taxpayers, **payments on account** towards the following year, due 31 January and 31 July); for companies, the return is generally due 12 months after the end of the accounting period, but tax itself is normally due 9 months and 1 day after the accounting period end (earlier, and quarterly, for the largest companies).

#### TAX.2d VAT and other reporting due dates
**VAT and other reporting**: VAT-registered businesses file a VAT return (usually quarterly) with payment due 1 month and 7 days after the VAT period end; inheritance tax on death is reported and paid by the personal representatives, normally within 6 months of the end of the month of death before interest starts to accrue on unpaid tax; stamp taxes (e.g. Stamp Duty Land Tax) generally require filing and payment within a short fixed window (e.g. 14 days) of the transaction.

#### TAX.2e Interest, penalties and HMRC enquiries
**Interest and penalties**: HMRC charges **interest** on tax paid late (running from the due date until payment) and, separately, **penalties** for late filing (e.g. an automatic fixed penalty, rising with further delay) and for **inaccuracies** in a return, which are risk-based: a higher penalty applies where the inaccuracy was **deliberate** (and higher still if also **concealed**) than where it resulted from a lack of **reasonable care**, and no penalty applies for an innocent error despite taking reasonable care. **HMRC enquiries**: HMRC can open an enquiry into a return within a fixed window (generally 12 months from the date an online return was filed, for a return filed on time); a taxpayer who disagrees with an HMRC decision generally has a statutory right to request a review and/or appeal to the independent tax tribunal.
