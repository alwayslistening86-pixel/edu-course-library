# S17_Magnetic_Fields_and_Induction - Test: Magnetic fields and electromagnetic induction

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A 500-turn coil of area 3.0 x 10^-3 m^2 lies with its plane perpendicular to a 0.40 T field. The field is reduced to zero in 0.050 s. Calculate the average induced emf. [3 marks]
2. A coil of 80 turns and area 0.012 m^2 rotates at 50 revolutions per second in a field of 0.15 T. Calculate the peak emf and the rms emf. [3 marks]
3. Use Lenz's law to explain why a magnet dropped through a copper tube falls more slowly than one dropped outside it. [3 marks]
4. In a mass spectrometer, singly charged ions of mass 3.8 x 10^-26 kg move at 4.0 x 10^5 m/s in a field of 0.25 T. Calculate the radius of their path. [2 marks]
5. In required practical 10, a balance reading changes by 2.4 g when a current of 3.0 A flows in a 5.0 cm length of wire in the field. Calculate B. [3 marks]
6. An oscilloscope shows a sine wave 3.2 divisions from peak to trough, with Y-gain 5.0 V/div; one cycle spans 4.0 divisions at 2.0 ms/div. Find the rms voltage and the frequency. [4 marks]
7. Explain why electrical power is transmitted at high voltage, and why transformer cores are laminated. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 Δ(NΦ) = 500 x 0.40 x 3.0 x 10^-3; M1 ÷ 0.050; A1 12 V.
2. [3] M1 ε0 = BANω with ω = 100π; A1 45.2 V; A1 rms 32.0 V.
3. [3] B1 changing flux in the tube induces eddy currents; B1 which create a magnetic field opposing the change (the magnet's motion); B1 so an upward force acts on the magnet (energy is dissipated as heat).
4. [2] M1 r = mv/(BQ); A1 0.380 m.
5. [3] M1 F = 0.0024 x 9.81 = 0.0235 N; M1 B = F/(Il); A1 0.157 T.
6. [4] M1 peak = 1.6 x 5.0 = 8.0 V; A1 rms 5.66 V; M1 T = 8.0 ms; A1 f = 125 Hz.
7. [4] B1 for a given power, higher V means lower I; B1 power loss I^2 R is reduced; B1 laminations insulate layers of the core; B1 reducing eddy currents and the heating they cause.
