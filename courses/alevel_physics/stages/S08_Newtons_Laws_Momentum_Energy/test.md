# S08_Newtons_Laws_Momentum_Energy - Test: Newton's laws, momentum, work, energy and power

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A 60 kg skydiver falls at terminal velocity. State the drag force and explain, using Newton's laws, why the velocity is constant. Identify the Newton's third law pair for the skydiver's weight. [4 marks]
2. A force-time graph for a tennis ball of mass 0.058 kg hit by a racket is a triangle of peak 1200 N and duration 6.0 ms. The ball arrives at 20 m s^-1 and leaves in the opposite direction. Calculate the impulse and the ball's final speed. [4 marks]
3. A crane lifts a 1200 kg load through 15 m in 25 s at constant speed. The crane's motor has an input power of 12 kW. Calculate the useful power output and the efficiency. [3 marks]
4. A 0.50 kg ball is dropped from 2.0 m and rebounds to 1.4 m. Calculate the energy lost in the bounce and state whether the collision is elastic. [2 marks]
5. Explain, in terms of momentum, why a seat belt reduces injuries in a collision. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 589 N; B1 resultant force zero, so no acceleration (first law); B1 constant velocity; B1 the skydiver's gravitational pull on the Earth.
2. [4] M1 area = (1/2)(1200)(6.0 x 10^-3); A1 3.6 N s; M1 3.6 = 0.058(v + 20); A1 v = 42 m s^-1 (in the opposite direction).
3. [3] M1 useful power = mgh/t = 7060 W; A1 7060 W; A1 efficiency 59%.
4. [2] B1 0.50 x 9.81 x 0.6 = 2.94 J; B1 inelastic (kinetic energy not conserved).
5. [3] B1 the change in momentum is fixed; B1 the belt stretches, increasing the time of the stop; B1 so the force (rate of change of momentum) is smaller.
