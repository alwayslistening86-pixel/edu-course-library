# S08_Newtons_Laws_Momentum_Energy - Lesson: Newton's laws, momentum, work, energy and power

## Goal
The learner applies Newton's three laws, momentum and its conservation, impulse and force-time graphs, elastic and inelastic collisions, work, power and efficiency, and conservation of energy.

## How to teach this
Ask why an airbag reduces injury in a crash when the car stops either way. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). #### 3.4.1.5 Newton's laws of motion
First law: a body stays at rest or at constant velocity unless a resultant force acts. Second: F = ma (resultant force, constant mass). Third: if A exerts a force on B, B exerts an equal and opposite force of the same type on A. Draw free-body diagrams showing only the forces on one object. *Example:* a 1500 kg car with a 4000 N driving force and 1000 N resistance: a = 2.0 m s^-2.

#### 3.4.1.6 Momentum
Momentum p = mv (kg m s^-1 or N s). Force = rate of change of momentum: F = Δ(mv)/Δt. Impulse = FΔt = Δ(mv) = the area under a force-time graph. Momentum is conserved in all collisions and explosions (with no external forces). Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy; inelastic don't. *Example:* a 0.40 kg ball hits a wall at 12 m s^-1 and rebounds at 8.0 m s^-1 in 0.050 s: Δp = 0.40 x 20 = 8.0 N s, average force 160 N. Crumple zones and airbags increase the collision time, reducing the force for the same change in momentum.

#### 3.4.1.7 Work, energy and power
Work W = Fs cos θ (J). Power P = ΔW/Δt = Fv (W). Efficiency = useful output power / input power. *Example:* a motor lifts a 250 kg load at 0.80 m s^-1 while drawing 3.0 kW: useful power = 250 x 9.81 x 0.80 = 1960 W, efficiency 65%. Work done = area under a force-displacement graph.

#### 3.4.1.8 Conservation of energy
Energy is conserved. For a falling object without resistance, ΔE_p = mgΔh equals ΔE_k = (1/2)mv^2. *Example:* a pendulum bob released 0.20 m above its lowest point reaches root(2 x 9.81 x 0.20) = 1.98 m s^-1. With resistance, work done against friction accounts for the lost mechanical energy.
