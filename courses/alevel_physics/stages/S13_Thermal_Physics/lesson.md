#### 3.6.2.1 Thermal energy transfer
Internal energy is the sum of the randomly distributed kinetic and potential energies of the particles. It changes by heating or by work done (first law of thermodynamics). During a change of state the potential energy changes but the kinetic energy (temperature) stays constant. Q = mcΔT (specific heat capacity c) and Q = ml (specific latent heat of fusion or vaporisation). *Example:* heating 0.50 kg of water (c = 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1) from 20 °C to 100 °C and boiling 0.10 kg of it (l = 2.26 x 10^6 J/kg): 1.68 x 10^5 + 2.26 x 10^5 = 3.94 x 10^5 J. Continuous-flow heating: P = (Δm/Δt) c ΔT. Mixtures: heat lost by the hot body = heat gained by the cold one (no losses).

#### 3.6.2.2 Ideal gases
Gas laws for a fixed mass: Boyle's law pV = constant (constant T); Charles's law V/T = constant (constant p); pressure law p/T = constant (constant V). T in kelvin: T/K = θ/°C + 273; absolute zero (0 K) is where the extrapolated volume or pressure of an ideal gas falls to zero. Ideal gas equation: pV = nRT = NkT, with R = 8.31 J mol^-1 K^-1, k = R/N_A = 1.38 x 10^-23 J K^-1, N_A = 6.02 x 10^23 mol^-1; molar mass links mass to moles. Work done on or by a gas at constant pressure = pΔV (the area under a p-V graph). *Example:* 0.50 mol at 300 K in 0.012 m^3: p = 0.5 x 8.31 x 300/0.012 = 1.04 x 10^5 Pa.

#### 3.6.2.3 Molecular kinetic theory model
Brownian motion (smoke particles jostled randomly by unseen molecules) is evidence for molecules. Assumptions of kinetic theory: many identical molecules in random motion; volume of molecules negligible; elastic collisions; collision time negligible; no forces except during collisions (so no potential energy). Derivation: a molecule's momentum change 2mu at a wall, collision rate u/2l, force per molecule mu^2/l, sum over N molecules and three directions: pV = (1/3)Nm(c_rms)^2. Combining with pV = NkT: (1/2)m(c_rms)^2 = (3/2)kT = 3RT/(2N_A): average molecular kinetic energy is proportional to absolute temperature. *Example:* at 300 K the mean kinetic energy is 6.21 x 10^-21 J; for nitrogen molecules (m = 4.65 x 10^-26 kg), c_rms = 517 m/s. The gas laws were found empirically (Boyle, Charles); kinetic theory explains them from a theoretical model, a sign of how knowledge develops.

#### RP8 Required practical 8: Boyle's law and Charles's law for a gas
**Boyle's law:** trap air in a sealed syringe or tube with a pressure gauge; change the pressure slowly (so the temperature stays constant) and read the volume; plot V against 1/p: a straight line through the origin. **Charles's law:** a capillary tube with a bead of concentrated sulfuric acid trapping air, in a water bath with a thermometer; heat, stir, wait for equilibrium and read the length of the air column; plot length against temperature and extrapolate to find absolute zero (about -273 °C). Safety: concentrated acid (goggles, gloves), hot water, over-pressure in the syringe.
