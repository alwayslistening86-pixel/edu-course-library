# S13_Thermal_Physics - Test: Thermal physics

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A 2.0 kW kettle heats 1.2 kg of water from 18 °C to boiling in 3.5 minutes. Calculate the energy supplied, the energy gained by the water, and the efficiency. (c = 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1) [4 marks]
2. A gas cylinder of volume 0.050 m^3 holds oxygen (molar mass 0.032 kg/mol) at 1.2 x 10^7 Pa and 17 °C. Calculate the mass of oxygen. [3 marks]
3. State four assumptions of the kinetic theory of an ideal gas. [4 marks]
4. Calculate the root-mean-square speed of helium atoms (mass 6.64 x 10^-27 kg) at 400 K. [2 marks]
5. A gas at 27 °C is heated at constant volume until its pressure doubles. Find its new temperature in °C. [2 marks]
6. Explain in terms of molecules why the pressure of a gas increases when it is heated at constant volume. [3 marks]
7. A gas expands from 0.020 m^3 to 0.035 m^3 at a constant pressure of 1.5 x 10^5 Pa. Calculate the work done by the gas. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 2000 x 210 = 4.20 x 10^5 J; M1 1.2 x 4200 x 82 = 4.13 x 10^5 J; M1 ratio; A1 98%.
2. [3] M1 n = pV/RT = 249 mol; M1 x 0.032; A1 8.0 kg.
3. [4] B1 large number of molecules in random motion; B1 negligible molecular volume; B1 elastic collisions; B1 negligible collision time compared with time between collisions; B1 no intermolecular forces except during collisions -- any 4 for full marks.
4. [2] M1 (1/2)mc^2 = (3/2)kT; A1 1580 m/s.
5. [2] M1 p/T constant: T = 600 K; A1 327 °C.
6. [3] B1 molecules move faster (mean kinetic energy ∝ T); B1 greater momentum change per collision; B1 more frequent collisions with the walls, so greater force per unit area.
7. [2] M1 pΔV; A1 2250 J.
