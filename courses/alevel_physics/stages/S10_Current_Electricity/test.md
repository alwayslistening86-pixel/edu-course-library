# S10_Current_Electricity - Test: Current, potential difference, resistance and resistivity

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A nichrome wire of resistivity 1.1 x 10^-6 Ω m is 0.80 m long with a diameter of 0.25 mm. Calculate its resistance and the current it draws from a 6.0 V supply (ignoring internal resistance). [3 marks]
2. Sketch the I-V characteristic of a filament lamp and explain its shape. [3 marks]
3. Describe how the resistance of an NTC thermistor changes with temperature, and explain why. [2 marks]
4. State what is meant by a superconductor and give one application. [2 marks]
5. In required practical 5, explain why the graph of R against L may not pass through the origin, and why this doesn't affect the value of resistivity. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 A = π(0.125 x 10^-3)^2; A1 R = 17.9 Ω; A1 I = 0.33 A.
2. [3] B1 curve through the origin, symmetrical in both directions, gradient decreasing; B1 temperature rises with current; B1 lattice ions vibrate more, increasing resistance.
3. [2] B1 resistance decreases as temperature increases; B1 more charge carriers are released.
4. [2] B1 a material with zero resistivity below its critical temperature; B1 e.g. MRI magnets, particle accelerators, loss-free power cables.
5. [2] B1 contact resistance at the clips (a systematic offset); B1 ρ is found from the gradient, which is unaffected.
