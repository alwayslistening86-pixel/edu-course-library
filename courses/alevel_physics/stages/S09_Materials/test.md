# S09_Materials - Test: Materials

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A copper wire 1.8 m long and 0.60 mm in diameter extends by 2.5 mm under a load of 50 N. Calculate the Young modulus of copper. [4 marks]
2. A spring obeys Hooke's law with k = 250 N m^-1. It is compressed by 4.0 cm and used to launch a 0.020 kg ball vertically. Calculate the maximum height reached, ignoring air resistance. [3 marks]
3. Sketch and label the stress-strain graph for a ductile metal, and explain what happens if it is unloaded after passing the elastic limit. [4 marks]
4. In required practical 4, state why a long, thin wire is used, and name the two measurements that usually contribute the largest percentage uncertainty. [3 marks]
5. Which statements are correct? Choose every correct option.
   A. The Young modulus is the gradient of the linear part of a stress-strain graph
   B. Brittle materials show large plastic deformation before breaking
   C. Elastic strain energy is the area under a force-extension graph
   D. Stress has the same units as pressure

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 A = π(0.30 x 10^-3)^2 = 2.83 x 10^-7 m^2; M1 stress and strain; M1 E = FL/(AΔL); A1 1.27 x 10^11 Pa.
2. [3] M1 E = (1/2)(250)(0.040)^2 = 0.20 J; M1 mgh = 0.20; A1 h = 1.02 m.
3. [4] B1 linear region to the limit of proportionality; B1 elastic limit and yield, plastic region, breaking stress; B1 unloading line parallel to the loading line; B1 a permanent extension remains.
4. [3] B1 a long thin wire gives a larger, more measurable extension for the same stress; B1 extension; B1 diameter.
5. Correct: A, C, D (exactly these options, no others)
