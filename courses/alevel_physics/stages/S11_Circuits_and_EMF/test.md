# S11_Circuits_and_EMF - Test: Circuits, potential dividers, emf and internal resistance

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A potential divider has a 10 kΩ fixed resistor in series with an LDR across a 9.0 V supply; the output is taken across the LDR. The LDR's resistance is 2.0 kΩ in bright light and 50 kΩ in darkness. Calculate the output in each case and state a use. [4 marks]
2. A cell gives a terminal pd of 1.35 V when the current is 0.30 A and 1.44 V when the current is 0.12 A. Calculate its emf and internal resistance. [4 marks]
3. Three identical cells, each of emf 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.40 Ω, are in series with a 6.0 Ω resistor. Calculate the current and the power wasted inside the cells. [3 marks]
4. In required practical 6, explain why the switch should be opened between readings. [1 mark]
5. State the two conservation laws that underlie Kirchhoff's rules. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 9.0 x 2/(12); A1 1.5 V; A1 9.0 x 50/60 = 7.5 V; B1 e.g. switching on a street light in darkness.
2. [4] M1 ε = 1.35 + 0.30r and ε = 1.44 + 0.12r; M1 subtracts: 0.18r = 0.09; A1 r = 0.50 Ω; A1 ε = 1.50 V.
3. [3] M1 ε = 4.5 V, r = 1.2 Ω; A1 I = 4.5/7.2 = 0.625 A; A1 power I^2 r = 0.469 W.
4. [1] B1 to stop the cell heating up, which would change its internal resistance (and to avoid draining it).
5. [2] B1 charge (currents at a junction); B1 energy (emfs and pds round a loop).
