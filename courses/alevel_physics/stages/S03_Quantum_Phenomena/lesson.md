# S03_Quantum_Phenomena - Lesson: Electromagnetic radiation and quantum phenomena

## Goal
The learner explains the photoelectric effect with photons, threshold frequency, work function and stopping potential; describes excitation and ionisation, energy levels and line spectra; and applies wave-particle duality and the de Broglie wavelength.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.2.1 - The photoelectric effect
- 3.2.2.2 - Collisions of electrons with atoms
- 3.2.2.3 - Energy levels and photon emission
- 3.2.2.4 - Wave-particle duality

## How to teach this
Ask why a dim ultraviolet lamp can eject electrons from zinc when a bright red lamp cannot. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.2.2.1 The photoelectric effect
Light arrives as photons of energy hf. One photon releases at most one electron; the electron escapes only if hf ≥ φ (the work function). hf = φ + E_k(max). Threshold frequency f0 = φ/h: below it, no electrons, however intense the light; above it, intensity increases the number of electrons per second, not their maximum energy. **Stopping potential** V_s: eV_s = E_k(max). *Example:* φ = 2.3 eV, light of 400 nm: E = hc/λ = 3.11 eV, so E_k(max) = 0.808 eV and V_s = 0.808 V; f0 = 5.55 x 10^14 Hz. The wave model can't explain the threshold frequency or the instant emission.

#### 3.2.2.2 Collisions of electrons with atoms
An electron that collides with an atom can **excite** it (raise an orbital electron to a higher level, if it has at least that energy difference) or **ionise** it (remove an electron). The electronvolt: 1 eV = 1.60 x 10^-19 J, the energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 V. In a fluorescent tube, electrons excite mercury atoms, which emit UV; the phosphor coating absorbs UV and re-emits visible light.

#### 3.2.2.3 Energy levels and photon emission
Electrons in atoms occupy discrete energy levels (negative values, 0 at ionisation). A transition between levels emits or absorbs a photon with hf = E1 - E2. This gives **line spectra**: emission lines (bright lines on dark) and absorption lines (dark lines in a continuous spectrum), evidence for discrete levels. *Example:* hydrogen levels -13.6 eV and -3.4 eV: photon energy 10.2 eV, λ = hc/E = 1.22 x 10^-7 m (ultraviolet).

#### 3.2.2.4 Wave-particle duality
Light shows wave behaviour (diffraction, interference) and particle behaviour (photoelectric effect). Particles show wave behaviour: electron diffraction by a crystal. de Broglie wavelength λ = h/(mv) = h/p. *Example:* an electron accelerated through 150 V: E_k = 150 eV, p = root(2mE_k) = 6.61 x 10^-24 kg m s^-1, λ = 1.00 x 10^-10 m, similar to atomic spacing, so diffraction is observed. Faster electrons give smaller λ and narrower diffraction rings.
