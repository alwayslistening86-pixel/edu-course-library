# S03_Quantum_Phenomena - Test: Electromagnetic radiation and quantum phenomena

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Light of frequency 8.5 x 10^14 Hz falls on a metal of work function 2.9 eV. Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons in eV and the stopping potential. [4 marks]
2. Explain why increasing the intensity of light below the threshold frequency does not cause photoelectric emission. [3 marks]
3. An atom has levels -10.4, -5.5 and -1.6 eV. (a) How many different photon energies can be emitted by transitions between these levels? (b) Calculate the longest wavelength emitted. [4 marks]
4. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of a proton travelling at 2.0 x 10^5 m s^-1. [2 marks]
5. Describe how electron diffraction provides evidence for the wave nature of particles, and state the effect of increasing the accelerating voltage. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 hf = 3.52 eV; M1 subtracts 2.9; A1 0.62 eV; A1 V_s = 0.62 V.
2. [3] B1 each photon's energy hf depends only on frequency; B1 one photon interacts with one electron; B1 below f0, hf < φ, so no electron can escape however many photons arrive.
3. [4] B1 three; M1 smallest energy gap 3.9 eV; M1 λ = hc/E; A1 3.19 x 10^-7 m.
4. [2] M1 h/(mv); A1 1.98 x 10^-12 m.
5. [3] B1 electrons passing through a thin crystal (graphite) produce rings like a diffraction pattern; B1 diffraction is a wave property, with λ = h/p comparable to the atomic spacing; B1 higher voltage, larger momentum, smaller λ, smaller rings.
