## How to teach this
Ask how light plus light can give darkness. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). #### 3.3.2.1 Interference
Coherent sources have the same frequency and a constant phase difference. Constructive interference where the path difference is nλ; destructive where it is (n + 1/2)λ. Young's double slits: fringe spacing w = λD/s (s slit separation, D slit-to-screen distance). *Example:* λ = 633 nm, s = 0.50 mm, D = 1.8 m: w = 2.28 mm. White light gives a white central fringe with coloured fringes either side. Lasers: coherent, monochromatic: don't look into the beam. Interference was historic evidence that light is a wave.

#### 3.3.2.2 Diffraction
Diffraction through a single slit: a wide central maximum (twice the width of the others) with dimmer side maxima; the pattern widens if the slit narrows or the wavelength increases. **Diffraction grating**: d sin θ = nλ, d = 1/(lines per metre). Maximum order: n ≤ d/λ. *Example:* 600 lines/mm, λ = 589 nm: d = 1.667 x 10^-6 m; first order θ = 20.7°; the highest order is 2. Gratings are used in spectroscopy to measure wavelengths.

#### 3.3.2.3 Refraction at a plane surface
Refractive index n = c/c_s. Snell's law n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2. The critical angle: sin θc = n2/n1 (n1 > n2); total internal reflection happens when the angle of incidence exceeds θc. *Example:* glass n = 1.50 to air: θc = 41.8°. **Optical fibres:** a high-index core surrounded by lower-index cladding (protects the core and keeps θc suitable); **material dispersion** (different wavelengths travel at different speeds) and **modal dispersion** (different paths) cause pulse broadening, reduced by monochromatic light and a narrow (single-mode) core. Absorption reduces the signal's amplitude.

#### RP2 Required practical 2: interference, Young's slits and the diffraction grating
**Young's slits:** shine a laser through a double slit onto a screen several metres away; measure across several fringes and divide (e.g. across 10 spacings), and measure D with a tape measure; s from the manufacturer or a travelling microscope. λ = ws/D. **Grating:** measure the angle of each order either side and average (θ = arctan(x/D) for the fringe displacement x), then λ = d sin θ/n; using higher orders reduces the percentage uncertainty. **Safety:** don't look along the beam; avoid reflective surfaces.
