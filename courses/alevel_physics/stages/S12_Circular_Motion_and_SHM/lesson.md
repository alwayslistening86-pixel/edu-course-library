# S12_Circular_Motion_and_SHM - Lesson: Circular motion and simple harmonic motion

## Goal
The learner analyses uniform circular motion with angular speed and centripetal acceleration, describes and calculates simple harmonic motion and its energy, applies it to mass-spring systems and pendulums, explains resonance and damping, and carries out required practical 7.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.6.1.1 - Circular motion

- 3.6.1.4 - Forced vibrations and resonance

## How to teach this
Ask what pushes you outwards on a roundabout, and let the learner discover that nothing does. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). #### 3.6.1.1 Circular motion
Angular displacement in radians; angular speed ω = v/r = 2πf = 2π/T. An object moving at constant speed in a circle accelerates towards the centre: a = v^2/r = ω^2 r, so a resultant (centripetal) force F = mv^2/r = mω^2 r must act towards the centre (tension, friction, gravity or a normal reaction supplies it). The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work and the speed stays constant. *Example:* a 1200 kg car rounds a bend of radius 50 m at 15 m/s: F = 1200 x 15^2/50 = 5400 N, supplied by friction. *Example:* at the top of a vertical loop, mg + N = mv^2/r; the slowest speed that keeps contact is where N = 0: v = root(gr).

#### 3.6.1.2 Simple harmonic motion (SHM)
SHM: acceleration proportional to displacement from equilibrium and directed towards it: a = -ω^2 x. Solutions: x = A cos(ωt) (starting at maximum displacement), with v = ±ω root(A^2 - x^2); maximum speed ωA at equilibrium, maximum acceleration ω^2 A at the extremes. Graphs: x, v and a are sinusoidal, v leading x by a quarter cycle and a in antiphase with x. The period does not depend on the amplitude (isochronous). Energy: kinetic energy is greatest at equilibrium, potential energy at the extremes; the total stays constant (no damping). *Example:* A = 0.050 m, f = 2.0 Hz: ω = 12.6 rad/s, v_max = 0.63 m/s, a_max = 7.9 m/s^2.

#### 3.6.1.3 Simple harmonic systems
Mass-spring system: T = 2π root(m/k). Simple pendulum (small angle, below about 10°): T = 2π root(l/g), independent of mass. Both need a restoring force proportional to displacement. Energy moves between kinetic and potential (elastic or gravitational) each quarter cycle. Damping removes energy: light damping (amplitude decays gradually, period almost unchanged), critical damping (returns to equilibrium in the shortest time without oscillating, as in car suspension) and heavy (over-) damping (returns slowly without oscillating). *Example:* a 0.40 kg mass on a spring of k = 25 N/m: T = 0.79 s. *Example:* a 1.0 m pendulum: T = 2.01 s.

#### 3.6.1.4 Forced vibrations and resonance
Free vibrations occur at the natural frequency with no external force. Forced vibrations: a periodic driving force makes the system oscillate at the driving frequency. Resonance: when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, the amplitude is greatest; at resonance the displacement is 90° (π/2) out of phase with the driver (in phase at low frequencies, antiphase at high). More damping lowers and broadens the resonance peak and shifts it slightly lower. Examples: a swing pushed at the right moment, the Millennium Bridge, microwave heating of water molecules, organ pipes; engineers add dampers to avoid destructive resonance.

#### RP7 Required practical 7: simple harmonic motion with a mass-spring system and a simple pendulum
**Method (mass-spring):** hang masses on a spring, displace slightly, time 10 oscillations with a fiducial marker at equilibrium; repeat and average; vary m. Plot T^2 against m: a straight line through the origin with gradient 4π^2/k. **Method (pendulum):** vary l (measured to the bob's centre), keep the angle small, time 10-20 oscillations; plot T^2 against l: gradient 4π^2/g. Use a fiducial marker at the lowest point, where the bob moves fastest, for precise timing.
