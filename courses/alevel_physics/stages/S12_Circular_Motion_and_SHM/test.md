# S12_Circular_Motion_and_SHM - Test: Circular motion and simple harmonic motion

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A fairground ride carries riders in a horizontal circle of radius 8.0 m, completing a revolution every 4.0 s. Calculate the centripetal acceleration and the resultant force on a 60 kg rider. [3 marks]
2. A car goes over a hump-backed bridge whose top is an arc of radius 20 m. Find the greatest speed at which the car stays in contact with the road at the top. [2 marks]
3. A body moves with SHM of amplitude 0.12 m and frequency 1.5 Hz. Starting from maximum displacement at t = 0, find its displacement at t = 0.20 s and its maximum acceleration. [4 marks]
4. A spring extends 5.0 cm when a 0.30 kg mass hangs from it. The mass is pulled down and released. Calculate the period of oscillation. [3 marks]
5. Sketch and describe how the amplitude of a forced oscillator varies with driving frequency for light and heavy damping, and state the phase difference between driver and oscillator at resonance. [4 marks]
6. In required practical 7, a student plots T^2 against l for a pendulum and obtains a gradient of 4.02 s^2 m^-1. Calculate g. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 ω = 2π/4 = 1.571 rad/s; A1 a = ω^2 r = 19.7 m s^-2; A1 F = 1180 N.
2. [2] M1 mg = mv^2/r at N = 0; A1 v = root(gr) = 14.0 m/s.
3. [4] M1 x = 0.12 cos(2π x 1.5 x 0.20) (radians); A1 -0.0371 m; M1 a_max = ω^2 A; A1 10.7 m s^-2.
4. [3] M1 k = 0.30 x 9.81/0.050 = 58.9 N/m; M1 T = 2π root(m/k); A1 0.45 s.
5. [4] B1 peak at the natural frequency; B1 heavier damping gives a lower, broader peak; B1 heavier damping shifts the peak slightly lower in frequency; B1 displacement lags the driver by 90° (π/2) at resonance.
6. [2] M1 g = 4π^2/gradient; A1 9.82 m s^-2.
