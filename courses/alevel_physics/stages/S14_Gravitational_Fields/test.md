# S14_Gravitational_Fields - Test: Fields and gravitational fields

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Calculate the gravitational potential at the Earth's surface and the minimum energy needed for a 500 kg probe to escape from there to infinity. [3 marks]
2. Show that T^2 ∝ r^3 for a circular orbit and use it to find the orbital radius of a moon with period 12 days around a planet of mass 5.7 x 10^26 kg. [5 marks]
3. State three features of a geostationary orbit. [3 marks]
4. A satellite of mass 800 kg moves from a circular orbit of radius 7.0 x 10^6 m to one of radius 8.0 x 10^6 m. Calculate the change in its total energy. [3 marks]
5. Explain why equipotential surfaces around the Earth are closer together near the surface than far away. [2 marks]
6. Which statements about a satellite in a circular orbit are true? Choose every correct option.
   A. A higher orbit has a longer period
   B. A higher orbit has a greater orbital speed
   C. Its total energy is negative
   D. Gravity does no work on it during the orbit

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 V = -GM/R; A1 -6.251 x 10^7 J/kg; A1 3.13 x 10^10 J.
2. [5] M1 GMm/r^2 = mω^2 r; M1 ω = 2π/T; A1 T^2 = 4π^2 r^3/GM; M1 r^3 = GMT^2/4π^2; A1 r = 1.01 x 10^9 m.
3. [3] B1 period 24 h (equal to the Earth's rotation period); B1 above the equator; B1 same direction of rotation as the Earth (west to east) (also: fixed radius about 4.2 x 10^7 m).
4. [3] M1 E = -GMm/2r; M1 difference; A1 increase of 2.84 x 10^9 J.
5. [2] B1 g = -ΔV/Δr; B1 g is larger near the surface (∝ 1/r^2), so equal steps of V occur over smaller distances.
6. Correct: A, C, D (exactly these options, no others)
