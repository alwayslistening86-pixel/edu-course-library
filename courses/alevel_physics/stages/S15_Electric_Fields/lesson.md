# S15_Electric_Fields - Lesson: Electric fields

## Goal
The learner applies Coulomb's law, electric field strength in radial and uniform fields, electric potential and potential gradients, analyses charged particles in uniform fields, and compares gravitational and electric fields.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.7.3.1 - Coulomb's law
- 3.7.3.2 - Electric field strength
- 3.7.3.3 - Electric potential

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to compare the electric and gravitational forces between two protons, and guess the ratio before calculating it. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.7.3.1 Coulomb's law
Coulomb's law: F = Q1Q2/(4πε0 r^2) between point charges in a vacuum (air is a close approximation), with ε0 = 8.85 x 10^-12 F/m; like charges repel. *Example:* two protons at 1.0 x 10^-15 m: F = 230 N. Compared with gravity between them the ratio is about 1.2 x 10^36, so gravity is negligible at atomic scale.

#### 3.7.3.2 Electric field strength
Electric field strength E = F/Q (N/C): the force per unit positive charge. Uniform field between parallel plates: E = V/d (so N/C = V/m). Radial field: E = Q/(4πε0 r^2). A charged particle entering a uniform field at right angles moves in a parabola (constant force, like a projectile): the energy gained crossing pd V is QV. *Example:* an electron entering between plates 2.0 cm apart with 400 V across them feels a force of 3.20 x 10^-15 N and accelerates at 3.51 x 10^15 m/s^2.

#### 3.7.3.3 Electric potential
Electric potential V at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity: V = Q/(4πε0 r), positive near a positive charge and negative near a negative one. Work done moving charge Q: ΔW = QΔV; equipotentials are perpendicular to field lines. E = ΔV/Δr in magnitude (field points down the potential gradient); the area under an E-r graph gives ΔV. *Example:* the potential 0.10 m from a +5.0 nC charge is 450 V. Comparing fields: both inverse square with similar potential and field definitions; gravity acts on mass and only attracts, electric forces act on charge and can repel.
