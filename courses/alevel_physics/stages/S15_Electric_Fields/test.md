# S15_Electric_Fields - Test: Electric fields

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Two point charges of +4.0 μC and -6.0 μC are 0.25 m apart. Calculate the magnitude of the force between them and state its direction. [3 marks]
2. An electron enters midway between parallel plates 1.5 cm apart, moving parallel to them at 3.0 x 10^7 m/s. The plates are 5.0 cm long with 200 V across them. Calculate the vertical deflection when it leaves the plates. [4 marks]
3. Point charges of +3.0 nC and +3.0 nC are 0.20 m apart. Find the electric potential and the field strength at the midpoint. [4 marks]
4. Give two similarities and two differences between gravitational and electric fields. [4 marks]
5. Calculate the ratio of the electric to the gravitational force between an electron and a proton. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 Coulomb's law; A1 3.45 N; B1 attractive.
2. [4] M1 a = eV/(m d) = 2.342 x 10^15 m s^-2; M1 t = 0.050/3.0 x 10^7 = 1.667 x 10^-9 s; M1 y = (1/2)at^2; A1 3.25 mm.
3. [4] M1 V from each: 270 V; A1 total 540 V; M1 fields are equal and opposite; A1 E = 0.
4. [4] B1 B1 similarities: inverse-square force law for point sources; potential ∝ 1/r; field = force per unit mass/charge; field = minus potential gradient. B1 B1 differences: gravity acts on mass, electric on charge; gravity only attracts, electric can attract or repel; gravitational potential always negative, electric can be positive or negative.
5. [2] M1 e^2/(4πε0) ÷ Gm_e m_p (r cancels); A1 2.3 x 10^39.
