#### 3.4.1.1 Scalars and vectors
Scalars have magnitude only (mass, speed, energy); vectors have magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, force). Add vectors with a scale drawing or by calculation; resolve a force F at angle θ into F cos θ and F sin θ. **Equilibrium** of coplanar forces: the resultant is zero, so the vector triangle closes; resolve in two perpendicular directions. *Example:* a 20 N weight hangs from two strings at 30° and 60° to the horizontal: T1 cos 30° = T2 cos 60°, T1 sin 30° + T2 sin 60° = 20, giving T1 = 10.0 N and T2 = 17.3 N.

#### 3.4.1.2 Moments
Moment = force x perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action (N m). Principle of moments: in equilibrium, sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments about any point. A **couple** is a pair of equal, opposite, parallel forces: torque = one force x perpendicular distance between them. Centre of mass: the point where the weight appears to act; for a uniform regular solid it is at the geometric centre.

#### MS 4.1 Use angles in regular 2D and 3D structures
Use angle facts in structures: angles on a straight line sum to 180°, vertically opposite angles, alternate angles on parallel lines, and angles in regular polygons (interior angle 180(n - 2)/n). Needed when resolving forces on slopes: the component of weight along a slope at angle θ is mg sin θ.

#### MS 4.2 Visualise and represent 2D and 3D forms, including 2D representations of 3D objects
Represent 3-D situations in 2-D: free-body diagrams, side views of slopes, ray diagrams and field-line diagrams. Draw each force as an arrow from the point it acts.

#### MS 4.3 Calculate areas of triangles, circumferences and areas of circles, surface areas and volumes of rectangular blocks, cylinders and spheres
Areas and volumes: triangle (1/2)bh; circle πr^2, circumference 2πr; cylinder volume πr^2 h, surface 2πr^2 + 2πrh; sphere volume (4/3)πr^3, surface 4πr^2. *Example:* the cross-sectional area of a wire of diameter 0.28 mm: π(0.14 x 10^-3)^2 = 6.2 x 10^-8 m^2.

#### MS 4.4 Use Pythagoras' theorem and the angle sum of a triangle
Pythagoras for resultants of perpendicular vectors: 3 N and 4 N at right angles give 5 N. Angles in a triangle sum to 180° (used in vector triangles with the sine and cosine rules).

#### MS 4.5 Use sin, cos and tan in physical problems
sin, cos and tan to resolve and to find directions: a resultant of 6 N east and 8 N north has direction arctan(8/6) = 53° north of east.

#### MS 4.6 Use small angle approximations sin θ ≈ θ, tan θ ≈ θ, cos θ ≈ 1
For small angles in radians, sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ θ and cos θ ≈ 1: used in the double-slit derivation, the simple pendulum and the angular size of telescope images.

#### MS 4.7 Understand the relationship between degrees and radians and convert between them
π rad = 180°: 1 rad ≈ 57.3°. Angular speed in rad s^-1: one revolution per second is 2π rad s^-1.
