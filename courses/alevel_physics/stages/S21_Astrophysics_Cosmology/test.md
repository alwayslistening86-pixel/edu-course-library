## Test items
1. A hydrogen line with laboratory wavelength 486.1 nm is observed at 501.4 nm in a galaxy's spectrum. Calculate the recession speed and the distance (H0 = 65 km s^-1 Mpc^-1). [3 marks]
2. Estimate the age of the universe in years from H0 = 70 km s^-1 Mpc^-1 (1 Mpc = 3.08 x 10^22 m), stating the assumption. [3 marks]
3. State three pieces of evidence for the Big Bang theory. [3 marks]
4. Describe the evidence that quasars are extremely distant and powerful, and the accepted explanation. [3 marks]
5. Explain how the transit and radial velocity methods detect exoplanets, and why direct imaging is difficult. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 z = 15.3/486.1 = 0.0315; A1 v = 9440 km/s; A1 d = 145 Mpc.
2. [3] M1 H0 = 70 x 10^3/3.08 x 10^22 = 2.27 x 10^-18 s^-1; A1 1/H0 = 1.40 x 10^10 years; B1 assumes a constant expansion rate.
3. [3] B1 galactic redshifts (Hubble's law: expansion); B1 cosmic microwave background radiation; B1 relative abundance of hydrogen and helium.
4. [3] B1 very large redshift, so (by Hubble's law) very distant; B1 so to appear as bright as they do, they must emit far more power than a galaxy; B1 an active supermassive black hole with an accretion disc.
5. [4] B1 transit: periodic dip in the star's intensity as the planet crosses it; B1 radial velocity: periodic Doppler shift in the star's spectrum as it orbits the common centre of mass; B1 B1 direct imaging is hard because the planet is very faint compared with its star and very close to it in angle.
