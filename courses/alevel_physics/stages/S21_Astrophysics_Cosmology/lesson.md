# S21_Astrophysics_Cosmology - Lesson: Astrophysics: cosmology

## Goal
The learner uses the Doppler effect and redshift, applies Hubble's law to estimate the age of the universe, evaluates the evidence for the Big Bang and dark energy, describes quasars, and explains how exoplanets are detected.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.9.3.1 - Doppler effect
- 3.9.3.2 - Hubble's law
- 3.9.3.3 - Quasars
- 3.9.3.4 - Detection of exoplanets

## How to teach this
Ask what the learner would expect to see if every galaxy were rushing away from us, and whether that makes us the centre. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). #### 3.9.3.1 Doppler effect
For v << c: Δf/f = v/c and the redshift z = Δλ/λ = v/c for a receding source (blue-shift for an approaching one). Spectroscopic binary stars: two stars orbiting each other alternately approach and recede, so their spectral lines split and shift periodically, giving the orbital period and speeds. Eclipsing binaries show dips in the light curve as one star passes in front of the other. *Example:* a line at 656.3 nm observed at 662.9 nm: z = 0.0101, v = 3020 km/s receding.

#### 3.9.3.2 Hubble's law
Hubble's law: v = H0 d. Distant galaxies are receding, with speed proportional to distance, so the universe is expanding. The age of the universe ≈ 1/H0 (assuming a constant rate). With H0 = 65 km s^-1 Mpc^-1, 1/H0 = 4.74 x 10^17 s ≈ 15 billion years. Evidence for the Big Bang: the expansion (redshift of galaxies), the cosmic microwave background radiation (the cooled remnant of the hot early universe, now about 2.7 K, uniform in all directions), and the relative abundance of hydrogen and helium (about 3:1 by mass, as predicted from early nucleosynthesis). Type Ia supernova data point to an accelerating expansion and dark energy; its nature remains controversial.

#### 3.9.3.3 Quasars
Quasars were discovered as very bright radio sources; their optical spectra show very large redshifts, so they are extremely distant (billions of light years) and must be extremely powerful: brighter than whole galaxies, from a volume no larger than a solar system. The current explanation is an active supermassive black hole at the centre of a galaxy, with matter heating as it falls in. Estimate distance from z and Hubble's law, and power from the observed intensity and inverse square law.

#### 3.9.3.4 Detection of exoplanets
Exoplanets are hard to see directly because they are faint and very close (in angle) to a much brighter star. The radial velocity method: the star and planet orbit their common centre of mass, so the star's spectrum shows a periodic Doppler shift. The transit method: the planet passes in front of the star, causing a small, regular dip in the light curve (its depth indicates the planet's size relative to the star). Most known exoplanets were found by transit.
