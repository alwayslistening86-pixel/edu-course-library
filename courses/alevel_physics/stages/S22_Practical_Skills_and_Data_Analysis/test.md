# S22_Practical_Skills_and_Data_Analysis - Test: Practical skills, graphs and data analysis

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A student measures g as 9.62 m s^-2 with a percentage uncertainty of 3%. Does this agree with the accepted value of 9.81 m s^-2? Justify your answer. [2 marks]
2. The intensity I of light from a small lamp is thought to follow I = k/d^n. Describe how to use a graph to find n, and state what gradient would confirm an inverse-square law. [3 marks]
3. A velocity-time graph shows speed rising uniformly from 0 to 12 m/s in 4.0 s, then constant for 6.0 s. Find the displacement and state which feature of the graph gives it. [2 marks]
4. In an experiment to find the Young modulus of a wire, identify the independent, dependent and two control variables. [4 marks]
5. Give three rules for plotting a high-quality graph from experimental data. [3 marks]
6. Solve 0.3 = 3.0t - 4.9t^2 for t (both roots) and interpret them for a ball thrown upwards at 3.0 m/s. [3 marks]
7. Which sketches are correctly described? Choose every correct option.
   A. y = k/x^2 lies below y = k/x for x > 1
   B. y = e^(-x) crosses the x-axis
   C. y = kx^2 is a parabola through the origin
   D. y = sin^2 x lies between 0 and 1

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 percentage difference = 1.9% (or range 9.33 to 9.91); A1 yes: within the 3% uncertainty (9.81 lies in the range).
2. [3] B1 plot log I against log d; B1 straight line: gradient = -n; B1 gradient -2 confirms inverse square.
3. [2] M1 area under graph; A1 24 + 72 = 96 m.
4. [4] B1 independent: load (tension); B1 dependent: extension; B1 B1 control: the same wire (material, diameter), original length, temperature.
5. [3] B1 axes labelled with quantity and unit; B1 scales use at least half the grid with sensible intervals; B1 points plotted accurately (within half a square); B1 best-fit line or smooth curve, not joined dot-to-dot; B1 error bars where appropriate -- any 3 for full marks.
6. [3] M1 quadratic formula; A1 t = 0.126 s and 0.486 s; B1 passing 0.3 m on the way up and on the way down.
7. Correct: A, C, D (exactly these options, no others)
