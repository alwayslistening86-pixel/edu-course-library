# S07_Motion_and_Projectiles - Lesson: Motion along a straight line and projectiles

## Goal
The learner uses displacement, velocity and acceleration with graphs (gradients, areas, tangents), the suvat equations, free fall and projectile motion including the qualitative effect of air resistance, and carries out required practical 3.

## How to teach this
Ask why a dropped ball and one thrown horizontally from the same height land together. Teach from the physics outwards: state the principle, derive or justify the equation, then do a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use AQA's data and formulae booklet values (e.g. g = 9.81 N kg^-1, e = 1.60 x 10^-19 C, h = 6.63 x 10^-34 J s, c = 3.00 x 10^8 m s^-1). #### 3.4.1.3 Motion along a straight line
Speed = distance/time; velocity = rate of change of displacement; acceleration = rate of change of velocity. Gradient of a displacement-time graph = velocity; gradient of v-t = acceleration; area under v-t = displacement. suvat for uniform acceleration: v = u + at; s = (u + v)t/2; s = ut + (1/2)at^2; v^2 = u^2 + 2as. *Example:* a car braking from 25 m s^-1 at 4.0 m s^-2 stops in 78.1 m and 6.25 s. Acceleration due to gravity g ≈ 9.81 m s^-2.

#### 3.4.1.4 Projectile motion
Horizontal and vertical motions are independent; with no air resistance the horizontal velocity is constant and the vertical acceleration is g. *Example:* a ball kicked at 18 m s^-1 at 35° above the horizontal: time of flight 2 x 18 sin 35°/9.81 = 2.10 s; range 31.0 m. **Air resistance** (drag) increases with speed and acts opposite to the velocity: it reduces the range and maximum height, and makes the path asymmetric (steeper descent). A falling object reaches terminal speed when drag equals weight. Friction and lift are other forces from the air or surfaces.

#### RP3 Required practical 3: g by a free-fall method
**Methods:** (a) an electromagnet releases a steel ball; it falls through a light gate or breaks a timing circuit at a trapdoor; (b) a light gate measures the speed of a falling card or picket fence. Vary the drop height h and record t. **Analysis:** s = (1/2)gt^2 (from rest), so plot h against t^2: a straight line through the origin with gradient g/2 (a non-zero intercept suggests a systematic error such as a delay in releasing the ball). **Improvements:** time electronically, repeat, use a small dense ball to reduce air resistance. *Example:* gradient 4.88 m s^-2 gives g = 9.76 m s^-2, 0.5% below the accepted value.

#### MS 3.5 Calculate rate of change from a graph showing a linear relationship
For a straight-line graph, the rate of change is the gradient: Δy/Δx using a large triangle (at least half the line's length), with units (e.g. m s^-1 for a distance-time graph).

#### MS 3.6 Draw and use the slope of a tangent to a curve as a measure of rate of change
For a curve, draw a tangent at the point (a straight line touching the curve there, with equal angles either side) and find its gradient: this is the instantaneous rate of change.

#### MS 3.7 Distinguish between instantaneous and average rate of change
Average rate of change = total change / total time (the gradient of the chord); instantaneous rate = the gradient of the tangent. A car's average speed over a journey is not its speed at any particular moment.
