# S19_Astrophysics_Telescopes - Test: Astrophysics: telescopes

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Draw (describe) the ray diagram for a refracting telescope in normal adjustment, showing where the principal foci are, and define angular magnification. [4 marks]
2. Give three advantages of large reflecting telescopes over large refracting telescopes. [3 marks]
3. The Moon is 3.84 x 10^8 m away. What is the smallest crater diameter a 0.15 m telescope could resolve at 500 nm? [2 marks]
4. Compare the eye and a CCD as detectors for astronomy. [3 marks]
5. Explain why X-ray and ultraviolet telescopes are placed in orbit, but large radio telescopes can be on the ground. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 parallel rays from a distant object enter the objective; B1 they meet at the shared principal focus (between lenses, f_o from objective and f_e from eyepiece); B1 emerge parallel at a greater angle to the axis; B1 M = angle subtended by the final image at the eye ÷ angle subtended by the object at the unaided eye.
2. [3] B1 no chromatic aberration; B1 mirrors can be supported from behind/segmented so they don't sag; B1 only one surface to shape, and no absorption or internal flaws in glass (also lighter and cheaper).
3. [2] M1 θ = 500 x 10^-9/0.15 = 3.33 x 10^-6 rad; A1 1280 m.
4. [3] B1 quantum efficiency: CCD about 80%, eye about 1%; B1 CCD detects a wider range of wavelengths (e.g. IR); B1 CCD can integrate over long exposures and store images, giving fainter detection (also finer resolution possible).
5. [2] B1 the atmosphere absorbs X-rays and UV; B1 it is transparent to most radio wavelengths.
