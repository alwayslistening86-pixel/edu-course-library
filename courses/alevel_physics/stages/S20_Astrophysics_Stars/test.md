# S20_Astrophysics_Stars - Test: Astrophysics: classification of stars

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator and the data booklet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Star X has apparent magnitude 1.0 and star Y 6.0. How many times more intense is the light received from X? [2 marks]
2. A star has surface temperature 12 000 K and radius 2.5 times the Sun's (R_sun = 6.96 x 10^8 m). Calculate its power output. [3 marks]
3. Explain why the Balmer hydrogen absorption lines are weak in both O-class and M-class stars. [3 marks]
4. Describe the evolution of a star like the Sun from the main sequence to its final state, referring to the HR diagram. [4 marks]
5. Explain why type Ia supernovae are useful to cosmologists, and what they revealed. [3 marks]
6. Calculate the Schwarzschild radius of a black hole of mass 4.0 x 10^6 solar masses (M_sun = 1.99 x 10^30 kg). [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 5 magnitudes; A1 100 times.
2. [3] M1 P = σ 4πr^2 T^4; M1 r = 1.74 x 10^9 m; A1 4.47 x 10^28 W.
3. [3] B1 Balmer absorption needs hydrogen electrons in n = 2; B1 in cool M stars few atoms are excited to n = 2; B1 in hot O stars most hydrogen is ionised.
4. [4] B1 main-sequence hydrogen fusion in the core; B1 core hydrogen runs out, the star expands and cools to a red giant (moves up and to the right); B1 outer layers ejected as a planetary nebula; B1 the core becomes a white dwarf (lower left), cooling over time.
5. [3] B1 all reach about the same peak absolute magnitude (standard candles); B1 so m - M gives their distance; B1 distant ones were fainter than expected: the expansion is accelerating (dark energy).
6. [2] M1 2GM/c^2; A1 1.18 x 10^10 m.
