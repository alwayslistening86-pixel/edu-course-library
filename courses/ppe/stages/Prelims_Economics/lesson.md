# Prelims_Economics - Lesson: Introductory Economics - Microeconomics, Macroeconomics, Quantitative Methods

## Goal

## How to teach this
Open with the puzzle: "A government imposes a minimum wage above the market-clearing wage. Basic supply-and-demand analysis predicts this should cause unemployment. Yet real-world studies of minimum wage increases often find little to no measurable unemployment effect. Is the theory wrong, or is something more subtle going on?" Keep returning to this puzzle across the stage as each new tool is introduced.

#### ECOP.MI1 Consumer theory and producer theory
Consumer theory models a household choosing the bundle of goods that maximises utility subject to a budget constraint, generating demand curves and predictions about how demand responds to price and income changes. Producer theory models a firm choosing output and input combinations to maximise profit subject to its production technology and costs, generating supply curves. Teach the learner to derive a simple demand curve from diminishing marginal utility and a simple supply curve from rising marginal cost, then to see market supply and demand as the aggregation of many individual consumer and producer decisions.

#### ECOP.MI2 Market equilibrium: perfect competition and monopoly
Under perfect competition, many small price-taking firms produce where price equals marginal cost, and free entry drives long-run economic profit to zero. A monopoly, facing the whole market demand curve, restricts output and raises price above marginal cost to maximise profit, creating a deadweight loss relative to the competitive outcome. Teach the learner to solve for equilibrium price and quantity under each structure algebraically and graphically, and to explain why the monopoly outcome is inefficient even though it is profit-maximising for the firm.

#### ECOP.MI3 Welfare, market failure and externalities
Consumer and producer surplus give a welfare measure of market outcomes; a competitive equilibrium maximises total surplus absent market failure. Externalities (costs or benefits falling on parties outside the transaction, such as pollution) mean private and social cost or benefit diverge, so the market outcome is inefficient - too much of a good with a negative externality is produced, too little of one with a positive externality. Teach the learner to connect this back to the minimum-wage puzzle: a labour market with a monopsony employer (see ECOP.MI2's competitive benchmark) is itself a form of market failure, which is one genuine resolution economists have proposed.

#### ECOP.MA1 National income accounting and determination of national income and employment
GDP measures the value of final goods and services produced in an economy and can be calculated via output, expenditure or income approaches, which should in principle agree. The determination of national income and employment (in simple Keynesian-style models) depends on aggregate demand - consumption, investment, government spending and net exports - with output adjusting to match planned spending. Teach the learner the basic national accounting identity and how a fall in one component of aggregate demand can, without offsetting policy, reduce output and employment below their potential level.

#### ECOP.MA2 Monetary institutions, money supply and inflation
Central banks control the money supply and short-term interest rates, influencing borrowing, spending and ultimately prices. Inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level; its causes are debated between demand-pull (excess aggregate demand), cost-push (rising input costs) and monetary (persistent excess money growth relative to output) explanations. Teach the learner how a central bank's interest-rate decisions are meant to transmit through borrowing costs, spending and eventually inflation, and why the transmission is neither instant nor perfectly predictable.

#### ECOP.MA3 Balance of payments, exchange rates and long-run growth
The balance of payments records a country's transactions with the rest of the world (the current account for trade and income flows, the capital/financial account for asset flows), and must balance overall. Exchange rates adjust (under floating regimes) to help equilibrate these flows, and are themselves driven by trade competitiveness, interest-rate differentials and capital flows. Long-run economic growth is driven by capital accumulation, labour supply and, most importantly over long horizons, growth in total factor productivity (technology and efficiency). Teach the learner to distinguish short-run demand management (ECOP.MA1-MA2) from the separate, longer-horizon question of what drives sustained growth.

#### ECOP.Q1 Mathematical economics: functions, differentiation, optimisation
Economic models are typically expressed as functions (a demand function, a production function), and differentiation is the standard tool for finding marginal effects (marginal utility, marginal cost, marginal product) and for solving optimisation problems - a consumer maximising utility or a firm minimising cost subject to a constraint, using Lagrangian methods for constrained optimisation. Teach the learner to set up and solve a simple constrained optimisation problem (e.g. utility maximisation subject to a budget line) and to interpret the resulting first-order conditions economically, not just mechanically.

#### ECOP.Q2 Elementary probability and statistical/causal inference
Probability and statistics let economists move from pure theory to testing predictions against real data - exactly what resolved the minimum-wage puzzle in the opening scenario, by measuring the actual employment effect rather than assuming the textbook prediction must hold. Statistical inference (estimation, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals) quantifies uncertainty in an estimate from a finite sample; causal inference asks the harder question of whether an estimated association (e.g. between minimum wage and employment) reflects a genuine causal effect or a confound, which is why credible minimum-wage studies use natural experiments or comparison regions rather than simple correlation.
