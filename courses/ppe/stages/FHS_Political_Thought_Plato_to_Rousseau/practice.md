# FHS_Political_Thought_Plato_to_Rousseau - Practice: Political Thought - Plato to Rousseau (Paper 215)

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Using Hobbes and Locke, explain precisely which premise about the state of nature drives their diverging conclusions about the sovereign's authority.
2. Compare Plato's and Aristotle's methods (ideal theory vs comparative empirical study) using their respective accounts of the best regime.
3. Assess whether Machiavelli's The Prince and Discourses are consistent with each other or offer genuinely separate political advice.
4. Explain how Hume's scepticism about consent challenges both Locke's and Rousseau's contractarian arguments.
5. Using Montesquieu, explain how the separation of powers is meant to protect liberty without relying on any ruler's personal virtue.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
