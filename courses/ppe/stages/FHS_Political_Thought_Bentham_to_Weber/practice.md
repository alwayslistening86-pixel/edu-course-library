# FHS_Political_Thought_Bentham_to_Weber - Practice: Political Thought - Bentham to Weber (Paper 216)

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Explain the genuine tension between Mill's utilitarianism and his liberty principle, and assess whether it is resolved or merely papered over.
2. Compare Hegel's and Marx's accounts of history as two different "engines" of social change.
3. Using Tocqueville and Durkheim, explain two different diagnoses of what threatens social cohesion under modernity.
4. Explain Weber's three types of legitimate authority and apply them to a real modern political leader or institution.
5. Assess whether Marx's critique of liberal rights as merely formal equality is a fair reading of Locke's or Mill's actual position.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
