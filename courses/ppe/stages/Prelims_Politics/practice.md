# Prelims_Politics - Practice: Introduction to the Theory and Practice of Politics

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Using Locke and Rousseau, write two short paragraphs giving contrasting answers to: "Is a government that wins by a landslide under first-past-the-post automatically legitimate?"
2. Explain, using Tocqueville, why associational civil society might protect a democracy from the "tyranny of the majority" - and give one modern example.
3. Apply Mill's harm principle to a real paternalistic law (e.g. compulsory seatbelts or a ban on a recreational drug) and argue both sides.
4. Compare a presidential and a parliamentary system on how each handles a government that loses its legislative majority mid-term.
5. Explain how first-past-the-post can produce Duverger's law effects, and connect this to the populism/identity-politics topic.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
