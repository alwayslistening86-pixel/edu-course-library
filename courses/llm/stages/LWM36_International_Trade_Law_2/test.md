## Test scenario
An English seller and a German buyer contract for the sale of specialist machinery, with the contract silent on governing law but containing a documentary credit payment mechanism. On delivery, 15% of the machinery components are found defective, rendering the whole shipment unusable for the buyer's specific manufacturing process, though this was not stated in the contract. The buyer's bank refuses to pay under the credit, citing the defects, despite the seller having presented fully conforming shipping documents. Advise on (a) whether the bank is entitled to refuse payment, applying the autonomy principle and the fraud exception, (b) whether the CISG is likely to apply to the underlying sale contract and what that would mean for the governing legal regime, and (c) whether the 15% defect would constitute a fundamental breach under the CISG, contrasting this with how English law would classify the same breach.
