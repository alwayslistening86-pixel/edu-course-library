# LWM36_International_Trade_Law_2 - Practice: International Trade Law C-D — Documentary credits and the CISG

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Apply Hamzeh Malas to a buyer who instructs its bank not to pay under a documentary credit because it alleges the goods are defective, with no evidence of documentary fraud.
2. Apply the United City Merchants fraud exception to a case where the beneficiary knowingly presented a bill of lading falsely stating goods were loaded, when they were not.
3. Explain why a sale contract between an English seller and a French buyer, with no express choice of law clause, might or might not be governed by the CISG.
4. Compare how English law and the CISG would each classify a breach where 5% of a large shipment of goods is defective, in terms of the buyer's right to terminate.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
