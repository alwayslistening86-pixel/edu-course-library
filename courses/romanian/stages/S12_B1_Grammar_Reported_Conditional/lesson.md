# S12_B1_Grammar_Reported_Conditional - Lesson: Reported speech, wishing and hypothesising, and joining ideas

## Goal
The learner reports what someone said, uses the conditional-optative to express wishes and polite requests, builds relative clauses, and connects ideas with a range of conjunctions.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-B1-G5 - Reported speech basics: learn and apply: reported speech basics
- RO-B1-G6 - Conditional-optative present: learn and apply: conditional-optative present
- RO-B1-G7 - Relative pronouns and clauses: learn and apply: relative pronouns and clauses
- RO-B1-G8 - Sentence connectors: learn and apply: sentence connectors

## How to teach this
Play a short recorded (or read) statement and ask the learner to report it to someone else: 'What did she say?' Build reported speech from that, then layer in polite wishes, relative clauses and connectors as the conversation grows more complex.

#### RO-B1-G5 Reported speech basics
Direct statements are reported with "că" (that): Ana spune: "Sunt obosită." -> "Ana spune că este obosită." (Ana says that she is tired.) Direct questions are reported with "dacă" for yes/no questions or the original question word: "Vii mâine?" -> "M-a întrebat dacă vin mâine." (He asked me whether I'm coming tomorrow.) "Unde locuiești?" -> "M-a întrebat unde locuiesc." At B1, tense shifting (present -> imperfect when the reporting verb is past) is introduced only for the most common cases; the full system is taught at B2 (RO-B2-G7).

#### RO-B1-G6 Conditional-optative present
The conditional-optative (used for wishes, polite requests, and the "would" meaning) is formed with aș, ai, ar, am, ați, ar + infinitive stem: aș vrea (I would like), ai spune (you would say), ar fi (it/he/she would be). Politeness: "Aș vrea o cafea, vă rog." (I would like a coffee, please -- more polite than "Vreau o cafea.") "Ai putea să mă ajuți?" (Could you help me?) This form is essential for everyday politeness and is heavily used in requests.

#### RO-B1-G7 Relative pronouns and clauses
Care (who/which/that) is the main relative pronoun and agrees only in number, not gender, with what it replaces, but changes with case: "Bărbatul care lucrează aici..." (The man who works here...), "Cartea pe care am citit-o..." (The book that I read -- note "pe care" for the accusative and the resumptive clitic "o"). Cine (who) is used only for people without an antecedent: "Cine vine mai devreme câștigă." (Whoever comes earlier wins.) Ce (what/that) is used for whole-clause reference: "Mi-a spus ce s-a întâmplat." (He told me what happened.)

#### RO-B1-G8 Sentence connectors
Key connectors for B1-level cohesion: deși (although), pentru că / deoarece (because), dacă (if), deci (so/therefore), totuși (however/nevertheless), în plus (in addition), pe de altă parte (on the other hand), de aceea (that's why). Example: "Deși ploua, am mers la plimbare pentru că voiam aer curat. Totuși, ne-am întors devreme." (Although it was raining, we went for a walk because we wanted fresh air. However, we came back early.) These connectors move the learner from simple sentences toward genuinely connected paragraphs.

## Explicitly not here
The full subjunctive-in-all-tenses system, the passive voice and formal register connectors are B2 material.
