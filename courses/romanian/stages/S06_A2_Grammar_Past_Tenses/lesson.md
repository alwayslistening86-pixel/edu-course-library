# S06_A2_Grammar_Past_Tenses - Lesson: Talking about the past and the near future

## Goal
The learner forms and uses the perfect compus correctly with its single auxiliary avea, conjugates common reflexive verbs, and expresses near-future plans.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-A2-G1 - Perfect compus with a avea: learn and apply: perfect compus with a avea
- RO-A2-G2 - Perfect compus: one auxiliary only, avea: learn and apply: perfect compus uses avea only, never a fi, unlike French/Italian
- RO-A2-G3 - Reflexive verbs, present and past: learn and apply: reflexive verbs, present and past
- RO-A2-G4 - Near future with o sa: learn and apply: near future with o sa

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What did you do yesterday, and what will you do tomorrow?' Build the answer, discovering that Romanian needs a new past tense (perfect compus) and a simple future construction.

#### RO-A2-G1 Perfect compus with a avea
Most verbs form the past with a avea (present tense) + past participle. Past participles: -a verbs -> -at (a lucra -> lucrat), -i verbs -> -it (a dormi -> dormit), -e verbs -> -ut mostly (a merge -> mers, irregular; a vedea -> văzut). Conjugation: am lucrat, ai lucrat, a lucrat, am lucrat (noi), ați lucrat, au lucrat -- note noi and eu share "am" but context/pronoun disambiguates. Example: "Ieri am lucrat până târziu și am văzut un film." (Yesterday I worked late and watched a film.)

#### RO-A2-G2 Perfect compus: one auxiliary only, avea
Unlike French or Italian, Romanian never switches auxiliary verb for perfect compus: every verb, including verbs of motion (a merge, a veni, a pleca) and reflexive verbs, uses "a avea" (am, ai, a, am, ați, au) + past participle, never "a fi". So "I went" is "am mers", "I came" is "am venit", "I left" is "am plecat" -- all with avea, not fi, even though these describe motion. This is a well-known error point for learners who already know French or Italian and instinctively reach for "a fi" with such verbs; drilling "am mers/venit/plecat" until automatic prevents the mistake.

#### RO-A2-G3 Reflexive verbs, present and past
Reflexive verbs use a reflexive pronoun (mă, te, se, ne, vă, se) before the verb: a se trezi (to wake oneself up) -> mă trezesc, te trezești, se trezește, ne trezim, vă treziți, se trezesc. In perfect compus, the reflexive pronoun still precedes the avea auxiliary and contracts with it: "M-am trezit la ora șapte." (I woke up at seven -- mă + am fuses to "m-am", using avea, per RO-A2-G2.) Other common reflexives: a se spăla (to wash), a se îmbrăca (to get dressed), a se culca (to go to bed) -- all previewed in RO-A1-V6, now formally conjugated.

#### RO-A2-G4 Near future with o să
The everyday spoken future uses "o să" + subjunctive-form verb (identical to present for most verbs, but "a fi" changes: să fiu, să fii, să fie...): "O să lucrez mâine." (I will work tomorrow.) "O să mergem la mare vara asta." (We're going to go to the seaside this summer.) A second, more formal future exists with "voi/vei/va/vom/veți/vor" + infinitive ("voi lucra"), which learners should recognise in writing and news but "o să" is the natural spoken form to produce actively.

## Explicitly not here
The imperfect tense (for ongoing/habitual past) and the true subjunctive mood come in B1.
