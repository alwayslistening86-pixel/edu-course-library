# S02_A1_Grammar_Numbers_Pronouns - Lesson: Verbs, pronouns, numbers and simple negative sentences

## Goal
The learner conjugates regular present-tense verbs, asks and answers basic questions, counts and tells the time, and builds negative sentences with demonstratives and possessives.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-A1-G6 - Present tense of the four verb conjugations: learn and apply: present tense of the four verb conjugations
- RO-A1-G7 - Subject pronouns and question words: learn and apply: subject pronouns and question words
- RO-A1-G8 - Cardinal numbers, time, days and months: learn and apply: cardinal numbers, time, days and months
- RO-A1-G9 - Negation and basic word order: learn and apply: negation and basic word order
- RO-A1-G10 - Demonstrative and possessive adjectives: learn and apply: demonstrative and possessive adjectives

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What time do you wake up, and what do you do first?' Build the answer together in Romanian, pulling in a verb from each conjugation class as the story unfolds (mă trezesc, mănânc, lucrez, dorm), then branch into questions, numbers and negation.

#### RO-A1-G6 Present tense of the four verb conjugations
Romanian verbs fall into four conjugations by infinitive ending: -a (a lucra, to work), -ea (a vedea, to see), -e (a merge, to go), -i/-î (a dormi, to sleep / a coborî, to descend). Regular -a pattern: eu lucrez, tu lucrezi, el lucrează, noi lucrăm, voi lucrați, ei lucrează. Regular -i pattern: eu dorm, tu dormi, el doarme, noi dormim, voi dormiți, ei dorm. Learners should drill one full verb per class: a lucra, a vedea, a merge, a dormi, noting that stress and vowel changes (o->oa in "doarme") are common.

#### RO-A1-G7 Subject pronouns and question words
Subject pronouns: eu, tu, el/ea, noi, voi, ei/ele. They are usually dropped because the verb ending shows the person, e.g. "Lucrez" already means "I work". Core question words: cine? (who?), ce? (what?), unde? (where?), când? (when?), cum? (how?), de ce? (why?), câți/câte? (how many?). Example: "Unde lucrezi?" (Where do you work?) "Lucrez la Cluj." Practise forming and answering each question type with verbs from the previous item.

#### RO-A1-G8 Cardinal numbers, time, days and months
Numbers 0-10: zero, unu, doi, trei, patru, cinci, șase, șapte, opt, nouă, zece; 11-19 add -sprezece (unsprezece, doisprezece...); tens: douăzeci, treizeci... nouăzeci; 100 = o sută. Telling time: "Cât e ceasul?" -- "Este ora trei" (It's three o'clock), "Este ora trei și jumătate" (half past three), "Este ora patru fără un sfert" (quarter to four). Days: luni, marți, miercuri, joi, vineri, sâmbătă, duminică. Months: ianuarie, februarie, martie... decembrie. These combine constantly with the daily-routine vocabulary taught later.

#### RO-A1-G9 Negation and basic word order
Negate a verb by placing "nu" directly before it: "Nu lucrez azi" (I don't work today), "Nu sunt român" (I am not Romanian). Basic word order is Subject-Verb-Object but is flexible because verb endings mark the person; even so, beginners should stick to SVO: "Eu citesc o carte" (I read a book). Combine with question words: "Nu știi unde este?" (Don't you know where it is?).

#### RO-A1-G10 Demonstrative and possessive adjectives
Demonstratives agree in gender/number: acest băiat (this boy, m.), această fată (this girl, f.), acești băieți / aceste fete (these, pl.); acel/acea (that/those) follow the same pattern. Possessive adjectives also agree with the noun they describe (not the owner, as in French): meu/mea/mei/mele (my), tău/ta/tăi/tale (your, informal), său/sa/săi/sale (his/her), nostru/noastră (our), vostru/voastră (your, pl.), lor (their, invariable). Example: "cartea mea" (my book, feminine noun so "mea"), "scaunul meu" (my chair, masculine so "meu").

## Explicitly not here
Past and future tenses, object pronouns and comparatives come in A2.
