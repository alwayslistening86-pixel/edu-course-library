# S21_C1_Vocab_Register_Nuance - Lesson: Specialised vocabulary, literary/journalistic style and precise word choice

## Goal
The learner uses specialised professional vocabulary in at least one field, reads and imitates literary/journalistic style, and chooses precisely among synonyms by connotation and collocation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-C1-V1 - Specialised and professional vocabulary: learn and apply: specialised and professional vocabulary
- RO-C1-V2 - Literary and journalistic style: learn and apply: literary and journalistic style
- RO-C1-V3 - Synonymy, connotation and collocation: learn and apply: synonymy, connotation and collocation

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to pick a field they know well (medicine, law, IT, education) and explain a concept from it in Romanian. Use that to teach specialised vocabulary, then contrast a short journalistic paragraph with a short literary paragraph (paraphrased, not quoted) to teach style, then finish with precise synonym choice.

#### RO-C1-V1 Specialised and professional vocabulary
Choose vocabulary matched to the learner's field, e.g. medicine: diagnostic (diagnosis), tratament (treatment), simptom (symptom); law: contract (contract), clauză (clause), litigiu (dispute); IT: aplicație (application), server, bază de date (database), cod sursă (source code). Teach the register marker that professional Romanian favours: nominalisation (turning verbs into abstract nouns) -- "a implementa" -> "implementare" (implementation) -- which is far more common in professional writing than in everyday speech.

#### RO-C1-V2 Literary and journalistic style
Journalistic style favours short, information-dense sentences, direct quotations attributed with "a declarat" (declared) or "a afirmat" (stated), and a inverted-pyramid structure (most important fact first). Literary style favours longer, more elaborate sentences, figurative language, and careful description. Practise by writing the same short event (e.g. a local festival) twice: once as a 3-sentence news brief, once as a short descriptive literary paragraph -- without quoting any real published text, only imitating the style patterns.

#### RO-C1-V3 Synonymy, connotation and collocation
Romanian, like English, has many near-synonyms with different connotation: a vorbi (neutral, to speak) vs a flecari (to chatter, mildly negative) vs a discuta (to discuss, more formal/purposeful); frumos (beautiful, general) vs superb (superb, stronger) vs plăcut (pleasant, milder). Collocation matters too: "a lua o decizie" (to make a decision, the natural collocation) rather than "*a face o decizie" (a literal but unnatural calque from English). Practise choosing the correct word from a near-synonym set for six given contexts, justifying the connotation.

## Explicitly not here
Rare/archaic register and full near-native idiomatic range are C2 material.
