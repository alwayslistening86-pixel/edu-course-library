# S23_C2_Grammar_Mastery_Vocab - Lesson: Rare constructions, literary register, full case precision and register range

## Goal
The learner recognises and uses rare/archaic constructions, controls literary register, handles the full clitic pronoun and case system precisely, achieves near-native idiomatic fluency, and commands formal, dialectal and academic register vocabulary.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-C2-G1 - Rare and archaic constructions: learn and apply: rare and archaic constructions
- RO-C2-G2 - Literary register and classic style: learn and apply: literary register and classic style
- RO-C2-G3 - Full clitic pronoun system and case precision: learn and apply: full clitic pronoun system and case precision
- RO-C2-G4 - Near-native idiomatic and cultural fluency: learn and apply: near-native idiomatic and cultural fluency
- RO-C2-V1 - Formal and literary register vocabulary: learn and apply: formal and literary register vocabulary
- RO-C2-V2 - Register range: dialect, slang and academic Romanian: learn and apply: register range, dialect, slang and academic Romanian

## How to teach this
Give the learner a short passage written in a slightly older or more literary style (paraphrased, not a direct quotation from any copyrighted text) and ask what feels different from everyday Romanian. Use their observations to teach rare constructions, literary register, full clitic combination, idiom and register range together, since at C2 these interlock rather than being taught as separate simple rules.

#### RO-C2-G1 Rare and archaic constructions
Literary/older Romanian sometimes uses the simple perfect (perfectul simplu) instead of perfect compus for narrative past, especially in southern regional speech and older literature: "el veni" (he came) instead of "a venit". The long infinitive as a noun is common in formal/legal register: "a face" -> "facerea" (the making/doing), seen in phrases like "la facerea contractului" (at the making of the contract). The suppletive/irregular comparative "mai bine" vs literary "mai bine încă" and inversion for emphasis ("Mult a mai așteptat!" -- How much he waited!) are recognised passively rather than actively drilled, since the goal at C2 is recognition and occasional deliberate use for effect, not everyday production.

#### RO-C2-G2 Literary register and classic style
Classic Romanian literary style (associated with 19th-20th century prose) favours longer periodic sentences, inverted word order for emphasis (verb before subject: "Venea toamna." -- Autumn was coming, rather than "Toamna venea"), and richer descriptive adjective chains. Practise recognising this register in a short paraphrased passage and then writing an original short descriptive paragraph deliberately imitating period, inversion and adjective density -- always original composition, never quotation, in line with copyright limits.

#### RO-C2-G3 Full clitic pronoun system and case precision
At C2, learners combine accusative and dative clitics correctly in sequence: "Mi l-a dat." (He/she gave it to me -- dative "mi" + accusative "l-" + auxiliary "a" + participle "dat"). Word order of combined clitics is fixed: dative before accusative, both before the auxiliary in compound tenses. Also master the reflexive-plus-dative combination: "Mi-am amintit." (I remembered, literally "to-myself I-have reminded" -- reflexive dative "mi" fused with "am"). These combinations are a known mastery marker distinguishing C2 from B2/C1 production, where learners often avoid combining clitics.

#### RO-C2-G4 Near-native idiomatic and cultural fluency
Beyond the C1 idiom set, C2 requires fluent, contextually apt use of proverbs and set cultural references: "Cine se scoală de dimineață, departe ajunge." (Whoever rises early, goes far -- used to praise diligence), "A pune punctul pe i" (to dot the i, i.e. to clarify the crucial detail precisely), "A da din lac în puț" (to jump from the frying pan into the fire, literally "from lake into well"). At C2, the learner should deploy such expressions appropriately and sparingly in context, recognising when a proverb strengthens a point versus when it would sound forced.

#### RO-C2-V1 Formal and literary register vocabulary
Learned/Latinate vocabulary doublets that exist alongside everyday words: a deceda (formal, to pass away) vs a muri (neutral, to die); a se strămuta (formal/legal, to relocate) vs a se muta (neutral, to move house); pretutindeni (literary, everywhere) vs peste tot (neutral). Legal/administrative register: hotărâre (ruling/decision), stipulație (stipulation), în conformitate cu (in accordance with). C2 learners should choose correctly among these doublets by context and audience.

#### RO-C2-V2 Register range: dialect, slang and academic Romanian
Recognise (receptively) regional markers, e.g. Moldavian/Transylvanian lexical variants for common words, without needing to produce them; recognise urban slang (mișto - cool/great, colloquial; a se tunde - slang for "to get a haircut" used loosely for other meanings in youth speech) as informal-only and inappropriate in writing; and command academic Romanian's nominalisation-heavy, passive/impersonal-heavy style (B2-G3, B2-G8, C1-V1 patterns intensified) used in theses and papers. The C2 skill is judging correctly which register belongs in which context, not simply knowing more words.
