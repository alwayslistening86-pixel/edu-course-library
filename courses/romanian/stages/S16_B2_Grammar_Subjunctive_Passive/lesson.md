# S16_B2_Grammar_Subjunctive_Passive - Lesson: The full subjunctive system, conditional perfect and the passive voice

## Goal
The learner uses the subjunctive across present and past forms, forms the conditional perfect for hypotheticals, builds the passive voice, and uses the gerund.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-B2-G1 - Full subjunctive system across tenses: learn and apply: full subjunctive system across tenses
- RO-B2-G2 - Conditional perfect and hypothetical clauses: learn and apply: conditional perfect and hypothetical clauses
- RO-B2-G3 - Passive voice: learn and apply: passive voice
- RO-B2-G4 - The gerund (present participle): learn and apply: the gerund, present participle

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What would you have done differently last year, if you could go back?' This naturally needs conditional perfect and past subjunctive; build outward to the passive and gerund from there.

#### RO-B2-G1 Full subjunctive system across tenses
Beyond the simple present subjunctive (B1), B2 adds the past subjunctive, used to express regret or an unfulfilled wish about the past: "să fi lucrat" (to have worked), formed with să + fi + past participle: "Aș fi vrut să fi știut mai devreme." (I would have liked to have known earlier.) Also drill subjunctive in negative and interrogative contexts: "Nu cred să fi plecat deja." (I don't think he's left already -- expressing doubt about a past event.)

#### RO-B2-G2 Conditional perfect and hypothetical clauses
Conditional perfect: aș fi + past participle (aș fi lucrat, ai fi lucrat, ar fi lucrat...) expresses "would have done". Hypothetical past conditionals pair "dacă" + pluperfect (or imperfect for softer hypotheticals) with the conditional perfect: "Dacă aș fi știut, aș fi venit mai devreme." (If I had known, I would have come earlier.) Present/future hypotheticals use "dacă" + imperfect + conditional present: "Dacă aș avea timp, aș călători mai mult." (If I had time, I would travel more.)

#### RO-B2-G3 Passive voice
The passive is formed with a fi + past participle, agreeing in gender/number with the subject: "Casa a fost construită în 1920." (The house was built in 1920.) "Scrisorile sunt trimise în fiecare zi." (The letters are sent every day.) The agent, when stated, follows "de către": "Legea a fost adoptată de către parlament." (The law was adopted by parliament.) Note that, as taught in B1 (RO-B1-G10), the impersonal "se" construction is often preferred in everyday spoken Romanian over the true passive, which sounds more formal/written.

#### RO-B2-G4 The gerund (present participle)
The Romanian gerund ends in -ând (for -a and most -e/-i verbs) or -ind (for some -i verbs): lucrând (working), mergând (going), dormind (sleeping), fiind (being). It expresses simultaneous action or manner, similar to English "-ing": "A ieșit din cameră plângând." (He/she left the room crying.) "Mergând spre casă, am întâlnit un prieten vechi." (Walking home, I met an old friend.) Unlike English, the gerund is not used to form continuous tenses in standard Romanian (there is no "is working" = "*este lucrând"); the simple present covers that meaning.
