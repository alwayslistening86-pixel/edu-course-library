# S11_B1_Grammar_Imperfect_Subjunctive - Lesson: The imperfect tense and the subjunctive with să

## Goal
The learner uses the imperfect for habitual/ongoing past, contrasts it correctly with perfect compus, and forms and uses the subjunctive after common verbs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-B1-G1 - Imperfect tense: learn and apply: imperfect tense
- RO-B1-G2 - Imperfect versus perfect compus: learn and apply: imperfect versus perfect compus
- RO-B1-G3 - Subjunctive formation with sa: learn and apply: subjunctive formation with sa
- RO-B1-G4 - Subjunctive after common verbs: learn and apply: subjunctive after common verbs

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Describe what your childhood was like -- not one single event, but how things generally were.' Use the contrast with "what happened yesterday" (perfect compus, already known) to introduce the imperfect, then move to the subjunctive, the single most important new structure at B1.

#### RO-B1-G1 Imperfect tense
The imperfect describes ongoing, habitual or background past actions ("was doing", "used to do"). Formation: -a verbs add -am, -ai, -a, -am, -ați, -au to the stem (a lucra -> lucram, lucrai, lucra, lucram, lucrați, lucrau); -i/-e verbs add -eam, -eai, -ea... (a dormi -> dormeam; a merge -> mergeam). A fi is irregular: eram, erai, era, eram, erați, erau. Example: "Când eram copil, locuiam la țară și mergeam la școală pe jos." (When I was a child, I lived in the countryside and used to walk to school.)

#### RO-B1-G2 Imperfect versus perfect compus
Perfect compus reports a single, completed event; imperfect describes background, repeated or interrupted action. Classic pair: "Citeam o carte când a sunat telefonul." (I was reading a book when the phone rang -- imperfect for the ongoing action, perfect compus for the single interrupting event.) "În fiecare vară mergeam la mare" (imperfect, habitual) vs "Anul trecut am mers la mare" (perfect compus, one specific trip). This contrast is a well-known difficulty point and deserves explicit paired practice.

#### RO-B1-G3 Subjunctive formation with să
The Romanian subjunctive (conjunctiv) is formed with "să" + a verb form identical to the present indicative for most persons, EXCEPT the 3rd person singular/plural, which changes: a lucra -> el/ea să lucreze (not "lucrează"); a merge -> el/ea să meargă (not "merge"); a fi is fully irregular: să fiu, să fii, să fie, să fim, să fiți, să fie. Example: "Vreau să lucrez" (I want to work, 1st person = same as indicative) vs "Vreau să lucreze el" (I want him to work, 3rd person changes).

#### RO-B1-G4 Subjunctive after common verbs
The subjunctive is required, not optional, after a wide set of everyday verbs: a vrea să (want to), a putea să (can/be able to), a trebui să (must/should), a-i plăcea să (to like to), a începe să (to begin to). Example: "Trebuie să plecăm acum." (We must leave now.) "Îmi place să citesc." (I like to read -- already previewed informally in A2-V4, now explained grammatically.) Unlike English "to want to work", Romanian never uses a bare infinitive here: "vreau să lucrez", never "vreau a lucra" in normal speech.

## Explicitly not here
Reported speech, the conditional-optative and relative clauses build on this in the next stage.
