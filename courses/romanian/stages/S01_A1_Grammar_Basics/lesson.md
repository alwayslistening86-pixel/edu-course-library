# S01_A1_Grammar_Basics - Lesson: Sounds, gender and articles -- the building blocks of Romanian

## Goal
The learner reads Romanian aloud correctly, names the gender of a noun, and forms basic noun phrases with the definite and indefinite article.

## Syllabus items taught here
- RO-A1-G1 - Alphabet, pronunciation and diacritics: learn and apply: alphabet, pronunciation and diacritics
- RO-A1-G2 - Present tense of a fi and a avea: learn and apply: present tense of a fi and a avea
- RO-A1-G3 - Three genders and plural formation: learn and apply: three genders and plural formation
- RO-A1-G4 - The definite article suffixed to the noun: learn and apply: the definite article suffixed to the noun
- RO-A1-G5 - Indefinite article and basic noun phrases: learn and apply: indefinite article and basic noun phrases

## How to teach this
Write "Bună! Mă numesc Ana." on the board and read it aloud slowly, exaggerating each sound. Ask the learner to repeat it, then ask: 'Which letters looked unfamiliar?' Use that to launch the alphabet section before moving into gender and articles, which are the two features that make Romanian nouns behave differently from English ones from the very first lesson.

#### RO-A1-G1 Alphabet, pronunciation and diacritics
Romanian uses the Latin alphabet plus five extra letters: ă (schwa, like the 'a' in 'about'), â and î (the same central vowel sound, with no close English equivalent -- said by holding the tongue back while making an 'i' sound; written â mid-word and î at the start/end of a word, e.g. român, a începe), ș (sh, as in 'ship') and ț (ts, as in 'cats'). Stress usually falls on the last or second-to-last syllable and is not marked in writing, so it must be learned per word. Key digraphs: ce/ci = 'che/chi' sound (as in 'chess'), che/chi = 'ke/ki' (hard), ge/gi = 'je/ji', ghe/ghi = 'ge/gi' (hard). Practise with: casă (house), țară (country), înțeleg (I understand), și (and).

#### RO-A1-G2 Present tense of a fi and a avea
Romanian's two most essential irregular verbs. A fi (to be): eu sunt, tu ești, el/ea este, noi suntem, voi sunteți, ei/ele sunt. A avea (to have): eu am, tu ai, el/ea are, noi avem, voi aveți, ei/ele au. Example: "Eu sunt profesor. Am doi copii." (I am a teacher. I have two children.) Both verbs are used constantly, including to build other tenses later (a avea forms the perfect compus), so they must be automatic from the first lesson.

#### RO-A1-G3 Three genders and plural formation
Romanian nouns are masculine, feminine, or neuter. Neuter nouns behave like masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural -- a feature with no English equivalent. Masculine plurals usually add -i (un student -> doi studenți); feminine nouns ending in -ă usually change to -e (o casă -> case, houses); neuter nouns add -e or -uri in the plural (un scaun -> două scaune; un tren -> două trenuri). Learners should memorise gender with every new noun, e.g. "o masă (f)" not just "masă".

#### RO-A1-G4 The definite article suffixed to the noun
Unlike English "the", Romanian attaches the definite article to the end of the noun. Masculine/neuter singular: -ul (băiat -> băiatul, the boy); feminine singular: -a (fată -> fata, the girl); masculine plural: -i become -ii (studenți -> studenții); feminine/neuter plural: -ele or -le (case -> casele; scaune -> scaunele). Compare "o carte" (a book) with "cartea" (the book) -- note the -a fuses onto the final vowel. This suffixed article is one of Romanian's most distinctive features among Romance languages.

#### RO-A1-G5 Indefinite article and basic noun phrases
The indefinite article is a separate word before the noun: un (masculine/neuter) and o (feminine) -- un băiat (a boy), o fată (a girl), un scaun (a chair). Plural indefinite normally drops the article and uses "niște" (some) for an unspecified quantity: niște cărți (some books). Build simple noun phrases: "un băiat bun" (a good boy), "o casă mare" (a big house), practising article choice before moving to full sentences.
