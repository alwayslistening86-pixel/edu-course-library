## Test items
1. Explain what must be proved for the actus reus of a 'wound' under the OAPA 1861, and give the mens rea test for s20. [5 marks]
2. Kwame, during a fight, punches Leo once, deliberately aiming for his face and hoping to 'really hurt him badly', breaking his eye socket (a recognised form of GBH). Using IRAC, advise on whether Kwame should be charged under s18 or s20 OAPA 1861. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] B2 a wound requires a break in both layers of the skin (the dermis and epidermis) -- internal bleeding without a break in the skin, or a bruise, is not a wound; B1 GBH (the alternative to a wound) means really serious harm, a question of fact and degree for the jury; B2 s20's mens rea (maliciously) requires only intention or subjective recklessness as to causing some harm, not the wound/GBH itself (R v Mowatt [1968] 1 QB 421).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether Kwame's mens rea supports a s18 or only a s20 charge; rule = s20 requires only intention/subjective recklessness as to some harm (R v Mowatt [1968] 1 QB 421); s18 requires actual intention to cause GBH, and recklessness (even as to serious harm) is not enough (R v Belfon [1976] 1 WLR 741); application = a deliberate, aimed punch with the stated hope of 'really hurting him badly' is strong evidence of an actual intention to cause serious harm, going beyond mere foresight of some harm; conclusion = on these facts Kwame's expressed intention supports a charge under s18 (wounding/GBH with intent), carrying the higher maximum sentence, rather than the lower s20 charge which would suffice for mere recklessness.
