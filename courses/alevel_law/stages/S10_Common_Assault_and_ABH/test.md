## Test items
1. Explain what 'actual bodily harm' means for s47 OAPA 1861, and what mens rea the prosecution must prove. [6 marks]
2. Jonas repeatedly sends a colleague threatening, abusive messages over several weeks, causing her to develop a diagnosed anxiety disorder, but never approaches or touches her. Using IRAC, advise on Jonas's liability under s47 OAPA 1861. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 ABH is any injury calculated to interfere with the health or comfort of the victim, more than merely transient/trifling (R v Chan-Fook [1994] 1 WLR 689); B2 a recognised psychiatric injury can amount to ABH, though mere fear/distress/panic cannot (Chan-Fook; R v Ireland; R v Burstow [1998] AC 147); B2 the mens rea required is only that for the underlying assault or battery -- intention or subjective recklessness as to unlawful force -- not any additional intention/foresight of the ABH itself (R v Savage; DPP v Parmenter [1992] 1 AC 699).
2. Credit: issue = whether Jonas is liable for assault occasioning ABH despite no physical contact; rule = s47 requires an assault or battery occasioning ABH, which can include recognised psychiatric injury (R v Chan-Fook [1994] 1 WLR 689; R v Ireland; R v Burstow [1998] AC 147), and words/messages capable of creating an apprehension of immediate unlawful force can constitute assault (R v Constanza [1997] 2 Cr App R 492); mens rea is only that for the underlying assault (intention/subjective recklessness as to unlawful force); application = the sustained, threatening messages could found assault by creating an apprehension of immediate force (following Constanza/Ireland), and the diagnosed anxiety disorder is a recognised psychiatric injury capable of amounting to ABH; conclusion = Jonas is likely liable for assault occasioning ABH under s47, provided the messages are shown to have caused a genuine apprehension of immediate force, not merely distress.
