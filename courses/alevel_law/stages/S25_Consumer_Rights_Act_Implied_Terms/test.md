## Test items
1. Explain the tiered remedies available to a consumer under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 for goods that are not of satisfactory quality, in the order a consumer would typically progress through them. [8 marks]
2. A consumer buys a washing machine that develops a serious, unrepairable manufacturing fault six weeks after delivery, having used it normally throughout. She tells the retailer she simply wants her money back in full immediately. Using IRAC, advise the retailer on its correct obligations under the Consumer Rights Act 2015. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [8] M2 s20 short-term right to reject -- broadly within the first 30 days, a full refund for rejecting faulty goods; M2 s23 right to repair or replacement -- the trader repairs or replaces (their choice, if both possible and neither disproportionate) within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience; M2 s24 price reduction or final right to reject -- available if repair/replacement is impossible, disproportionate, or has already failed, allowing a price reduction or a final rejection (potentially subject to a deduction for use); M2 credit for correctly noting the general progression (short-term reject first if still available, then repair/replace, then price reduction/final reject).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = which CRA 2015 remedy the consumer is entitled to given the fault arose after the 30-day short-term rejection window; rule = s20 short-term right to reject applies broadly only within the first 30 days; after that, s23 gives the consumer the right to require repair or replacement (trader's choice between the two if both possible/not disproportionate), and only if that is impossible, disproportionate, or has failed does s24 price reduction/final right to reject become available; application = six weeks after delivery is outside the 30-day short-term window, so the consumer is not automatically entitled to an immediate full refund; instead she must first be offered repair or replacement under s23, and only if that route is unavailable, disproportionate, or unsuccessful does she move to s24; conclusion = the retailer should advise the consumer that, while the fault (an unrepairable manufacturing defect) may mean repair is not a realistic option, the correct next step under the CRA 2015 is normally replacement or, if that is also not possible/proportionate, a price reduction or final right to reject under s24, rather than an automatic full refund as if the 30-day window still applied -- though if replacement is also genuinely impossible (e.g. discontinued model), a final rejection with any appropriate deduction for use may in practice look close to what she is asking for.
