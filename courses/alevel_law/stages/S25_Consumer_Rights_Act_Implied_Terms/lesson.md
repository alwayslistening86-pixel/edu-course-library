# S25_Consumer_Rights_Act_Implied_Terms - Lesson: CRA 2015 implied terms and remedies for goods; CRA 2015 implied terms and remedies for services

## Goal
The learner states the Consumer Rights Act 2015's implied terms for the supply of goods and services to consumers, and the tiered remedies each attracts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- STAT.1 - Statutory implied terms in consumer contracts for the supply of goods, Consumer Rights Act 2015: s9 satisfactory quality, s10 fitness for a particular purpose, s11 as described; the tiered consumer remedies -- s20 short-term right to reject, s23 repair or replacement, s24 price reduction or final right to reject
- STAT.2 - Statutory implied terms in consumer contracts for the supply of services, Consumer Rights Act 2015: s49 reasonable care and skill, s52 performance within a reasonable time; the consumer remedies of s55 repeat performance and s56 price reduction

## How to teach this
Ask: a consumer buys a faulty toaster -- what exactly does the law say the retailer must have guaranteed, and what can the consumer actually demand? Teach each rule as: the precise legal test (statute section or case authority, stated exactly, since AQA rewards named, accurate authority), why it exists (the policy AQA's own specification names, e.g. public policy limits on the duty of care, freedom of contract vs consumer protection), and how it applies to facts -- AQA's application and scenario questions (worth over half the marks on every paper) need the rule applied to the specific facts given, not restated in the abstract. Use AQA's own command words precisely: 'explain'/'suggest' target AO1 (accurate, precise legal knowledge); 'advise'/'consider' target AO1+AO2+AO3 together (apply the law to a scenario and reach a reasoned conclusion); 'examine'/'discuss'/'assess' target AO3 (evaluate non-substantive law, or substantive law within a non-substantive framework, e.g. discussing judicial precedent's advantages through a case scenario). Push the learner to build a bank of exact case names, years and the precise principle each establishes -- a vague 'there was a case about...' earns nothing under AQA's marking. Law changes: this course states the law of England and Wales as understood to be current in September 2026; statutory thresholds, ongoing reform and any pending legislation should be checked against a live source (legislation.gov.uk, aqa.org.uk) before a real exam sitting.

#### STAT.1 Statutory implied terms in consumer contracts for the supply of goods, Consumer Rights Act 2015: s9 satisfactory quality, s10 fitness for a particular purpose, s11 as described; the tiered consumer remedies -- s20 short-term right to reject, s23 repair or replacement, s24 price reduction or final right to reject
**Consumer Rights Act 2015: implied terms for goods.** Applies to contracts between a **trader** and a **consumer** (an individual acting wholly or mainly outside their trade, business, craft or profession) for the supply of goods. Key implied terms: **s9, satisfactory quality** -- goods must meet the standard a reasonable person would consider satisfactory, taking account of price, description and all other relevant circumstances, considering factors including fitness for common purposes, appearance/finish, freedom from minor defects, safety and durability (echoing, though not identical to, the older "merchantable quality"/satisfactory-quality concept developed under the Sale of Goods Act 1979, illustrated pre-CRA in *Rogers v Parish (Scarborough) Ltd* [1987] QB 933, where a new car with significant recurring defects, though driveable, was not of the required quality given its price and the reasonable expectations of a buyer paying that much for a new vehicle); **s10, fitness for a particular purpose** -- if the consumer makes known (expressly or by implication) a particular purpose for the goods, and reasonably relies on the trader's skill/judgment, the goods must be fit for that purpose; **s11, as described** -- goods must match any description given. **Remedies** are tiered by s19-s24: **s20, short-term right to reject** -- broadly within the first 30 days, the consumer can reject faulty goods for a full refund; **s23, right to repair or replacement** -- the consumer can require the trader to repair or replace the goods (the trader may choose which, if both are possible, unless one is disproportionate), to be done within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience; **s24, price reduction or final right to reject** -- if repair/replacement is impossible, disproportionate, or has already been tried without success, the consumer can instead claim a price reduction or exercise a final right to reject (with any refund potentially subject to a deduction for use, if goods are rejected after the initial 30-day period, other than for motor vehicles which have their own specific rules).

#### STAT.2 Statutory implied terms in consumer contracts for the supply of services, Consumer Rights Act 2015: s49 reasonable care and skill, s52 performance within a reasonable time; the consumer remedies of s55 repeat performance and s56 price reduction
**Consumer Rights Act 2015: implied terms for services.** Applies to contracts between a **trader** and a **consumer** for the supply of a service. Key implied terms: **s49, reasonable care and skill** -- the trader must perform the service with reasonable care and skill (an objective, professional-standard test, similar in spirit to the tort standard, NEG.2, but implied as a contractual term here); **s52, reasonable time for performance** -- where no time for performance is fixed by the contract, the trader must perform within a reasonable time. (The Act also implies further terms not separately coded in this course, e.g. that information given about the trader/service that the consumer relies on becomes a term of the contract, and, where relevant, that a reasonable price must be paid if none was agreed.) **Remedies**: **s55, right to repeat performance** -- where the service was not performed with reasonable care and skill, or (where relevant) did not match information relied on, the consumer can require the trader to repeat performance, at no extra cost, within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience, so far as repeat performance is possible; **s56, right to a price reduction** -- available where repeat performance is impossible or the trader fails to repeat performance as required, allowing the consumer to recover some or all of the price paid, reflecting the shortfall in what was actually delivered against what was promised. Unlike the goods provisions, there is no equivalent 30-day short-term right to reject a defective service -- the remedies focus on repeat performance and price reduction, reflecting that a service, once performed, often cannot simply be "returned" the way goods can.

## Explicitly not here
Exclusion clauses (including the CRA's own controls on excluding these implied terms) are S24; vitiating factors are S26.
