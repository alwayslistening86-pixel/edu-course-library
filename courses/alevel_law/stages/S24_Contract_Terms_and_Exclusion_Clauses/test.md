## Test items
1. Explain, with authority, why an exclusion clause printed on a document handed over automatically after a contract is already formed may not be validly incorporated. [6 marks]
2. A commercial printing firm's standard terms, printed on the back of every invoice it has sent a regular business customer over the past two years (always in the same wording), exclude liability for any consequential loss caused by late delivery. On this occasion, late delivery causes the customer significant lost profit. Using IRAC, advise on whether the exclusion clause is validly incorporated and effective. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 reasonable notice of an exclusion clause must be given before or at the time the contract is formed, not afterwards; B2 authority: Olley v Marlborough Court Ltd [1949] 1 KB 532 (a hotel room notice seen only after the contract was made at reception came too late); B2 authority: Thornton v Shoe Lane Parking Ltd [1971] 2 QB 163 (a clause on a car park ticket dispensed automatically, only visible after the contract was arguably already formed, and unusually onerous, was not validly incorporated).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether the clause is incorporated by a course of dealing, and whether it validly excludes liability for the loss; rule = a clause can be incorporated by a regular, consistent course of dealing between the same parties on the same terms (Hollier v Rambler Motors (AMC) Ltd [1972] 2 QB 71, where inconsistent past dealing was not enough); as a business-to-business contract, the Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977 s2(2) permits exclusion of negligence-based property/economic loss only if reasonable, and s3 applies a similar reasonableness test to standard-term breach-of-contract exclusions; application = two years of invoices consistently carrying the same clause is a stronger course of dealing than the inconsistent dealing in Hollier, supporting incorporation; whether the clause is reasonable under UCTA then turns on factors such as the parties' relative bargaining strength (both are businesses, reducing but not eliminating scrutiny), whether the customer could reasonably have insured against the loss, and whether the term was freely negotiated or simply imposed; conclusion = the clause is likely validly incorporated by course of dealing, but its effectiveness against the consequential-loss claim depends on the UCTA reasonableness test, which the tutor should have the learner apply to the specific facts (bargaining power, insurability, clarity of wording) rather than assume automatic validity.
