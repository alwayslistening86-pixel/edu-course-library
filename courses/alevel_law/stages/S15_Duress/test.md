## Test items
1. Explain the requirements for duress by threats set out in R v Hasan [2005] UKHL 22. [8 marks]
2. Priya, having voluntarily joined a small local gang some months earlier knowing its members sometimes pressured associates into crime, is later told to deliver a package of stolen goods or be seriously beaten, with no realistic chance to contact police before the deadline. Using IRAC, advise on whether Priya can raise duress by threats to a charge of handling stolen goods. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [8] M2 the threat must be of death or serious injury, operative on the defendant's mind at the time of the offence; M2 the defendant must reasonably believe there was no safe avenue of escape (e.g. going to the police); M2 the threat must be directly connected to the offence committed, and a person of reasonable firmness sharing the defendant's characteristics would have responded the same way; M2 the defence is unavailable where the defendant voluntarily associated with people they knew or ought to have known might subject them to compulsion, even without foreseeing the specific threat.
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether Priya can raise duress by threats given her prior voluntary association with the gang; rule = R v Hasan [2005] UKHL 22 requires a threat of death/serious injury, no safe avenue of escape, and a reasonable-firmness objective test, but excludes the defence where the defendant voluntarily associated with people they knew or ought to have known might subject them to compulsion, even without foreseeing the specific threat; application = joining the gang knowing it sometimes pressured associates into crime is likely to satisfy Hasan's voluntary-association exclusion, even though Priya may not have foreseen this specific threat, and the immediate threat of serious violence with no time to seek help otherwise satisfies the imminence/no-safe-avenue elements; conclusion = despite an otherwise strong threat, Priya's prior voluntary association with a gang she knew might pressure her is likely to exclude the duress defence under Hasan, so the defence is likely to fail.
