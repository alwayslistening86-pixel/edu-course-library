## Test items
1. Explain the three classifications of criminal offence and give an example of each. [6 marks]
2. Consider the advantages and disadvantages of trial by jury compared with trial by magistrates for a criminal case. [12 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 summary offences, tried only in the Magistrates' Court, e.g. most motoring/minor offences; B2 either-way offences, triable in either court depending on seriousness/election, e.g. theft or ABH; B2 indictable-only offences, tried only in the Crown Court, e.g. murder or robbery.

[12] Mark using AQA's generic levels for extended writing (AO1/AO3 blended over 12 marks): Level 1 (1-3) undeveloped; Level 2 (4-6) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis; Level 3 (7-9) accurate knowledge with developing analysis of both systems; Level 4 (10-12) accurate, precise knowledge with sustained analysis and a supported view. Content credit: jury strengths (public participation/legitimacy, jury equity, independence) against weaknesses (no reasoned verdict, risk of struggling with complex evidence, potential susceptibility to prejudice); magistrates' strengths (speed/cost, local community involvement, legal adviser support) against weaknesses (limited legal training relative to a judge, risk of inconsistency between benches, concerns about representativeness); a reasoned comparison rather than two unconnected lists.
