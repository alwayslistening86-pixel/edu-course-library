## Test items
1. Explain the Hart-Devlin debate on the relationship between law and morality. [6 marks]
2. Discuss the extent to which strict liability criminal offences achieve justice. [15 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 Devlin argued law should enforce a shared public morality to preserve the fabric of society (the 'disintegration thesis'); B2 Hart argued, following Mill's harm principle, that the state should only intervene where conduct harms others, not merely because a majority disapproves; B1 the debate reflects the difficulty of identifying a single shared morality in a pluralist society; B1 credit reference to a concrete example (e.g. decriminalisation of homosexuality following the Wolfenden Report/Sexual Offences Act 1967) illustrating the shift Hart's approach favoured.
2. [15] Mark using AQA's generic levels for extended writing (AO1 5 + AO3 10): Level 1 (1-3) limited, undeveloped; Level 2 (4-6) satisfactory knowledge, limited analysis, a limited conclusion; Level 3 (7-9) satisfactory-to-good knowledge, developing analysis; Level 4 (10-12) good knowledge, sustained analysis, partially justified conclusion; Level 5 (13-15) excellent, precise knowledge and sustained, logical reasoning to a substantiated conclusion. Content credit: accurate description of strict liability (no need to prove mens rea for at least one element, Sweet v Parsley [1970] AC 132 presumption of mens rea, Gammon (Hong Kong) Ltd v AG of Hong Kong [1985] AC 1 factors for displacing it); corrective-justice argument that convicting a person not at fault is unjust; counter-argument that strict liability serves public protection/deterrence in regulatory contexts (e.g. food safety, road traffic) where fault is hard to prove and the harm risked is significant, so utilitarian/distributive-justice reasoning can support it; a reasoned, substantiated conclusion either way.
