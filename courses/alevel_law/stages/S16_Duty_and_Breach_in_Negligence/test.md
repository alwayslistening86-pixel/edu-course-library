## Test items
1. Explain two factors a court weighs when assessing whether a defendant has breached the objective standard of care, with a case for each. [6 marks]
2. A small local swimming pool knows one of its regular young swimmers has a serious, undisclosed heart condition that increases the risk of a supervised swim going wrong, but takes no extra precautions beyond its normal lifeguard cover. The swimmer suffers a cardiac event during a session and is seriously injured. Using IRAC, advise on whether the pool breached its duty of care. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] M2 the degree/likelihood of risk -- a small, unlikely risk may not require special precautions (Bolton v Stone [1951] AC 850); M2 the cost and practicability of precautions -- disproportionately costly precautions against a modest risk are not required (Latimer v AEC Ltd [1953] AC 643); M2 credit any other correctly explained factor with authority, e.g. the defendant's own special skill/knowledge raising the standard (Paris v Stepney Borough Council [1951] AC 367).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether the pool fell below the objective standard of care given its knowledge of the swimmer's condition; rule = breach is judged objectively by the standard of a reasonable person in the defendant's position, but a defendant with actual knowledge of a particular claimant's increased vulnerability may be held to a higher standard for that claimant, by analogy with Paris v Stepney Borough Council [1951] AC 367 (higher duty owed to a one-eyed employee known to be more vulnerable to a particular risk); the cost/practicability of precautions is also weighed (Latimer v AEC Ltd [1953] AC 643); application = knowing about the heart condition and doing nothing extra (e.g. briefing lifeguards specifically, or a simple risk assessment) suggests a failure to take reasonably available, low-cost precautions proportionate to a known, serious risk; conclusion = the pool is likely to be found in breach, since a reasonable operator aware of this specific vulnerability would have taken some additional, proportionate step, which does not appear to have happened here.
