## Test items
1. Explain how a conditional fee agreement works and one risk it leaves the client exposed to. [6 marks]
2. Examine the reasons judicial independence is protected in the English legal system, and assess whether the current safeguards are adequate. [15 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 the lawyer's fee is payable only if the case succeeds ('no win, no fee'), often with an agreed success-fee uplift on a win, subject to statutory caps; B2 this gives access to representation without needing to pay privately upfront; B2 risk: if the case is lost, the client can still be liable for the other side's costs (an adverse costs order), commonly covered by taking out after-the-event insurance -- credit any other correctly explained risk, e.g. the success fee reducing the client's damages.
2. [15] Mark using AQA's generic levels for extended writing (AO1 5 + AO3 10): Level 1 (1-3) limited; Level 2 (4-6) satisfactory, limited analysis; Level 3 (7-9) satisfactory-to-good, developing analysis; Level 4 (10-12) good, sustained analysis, partially justified conclusion; Level 5 (13-15) excellent, sustained reasoning to a substantiated conclusion. Content credit: accurate description of security of tenure, immunity from suit and separation from the executive (Constitutional Reform Act 2005, the Judicial Appointments Commission); the rule-of-law rationale (impartial adjudication, including in cases against the state, requires freedom from political pressure); an evaluative assessment (e.g. security of tenure is strong for senior judges but the appointments process/diversity of the judiciary remains debated, or comparison with a jurisdiction where the safeguards are weaker) reaching a substantiated view.
