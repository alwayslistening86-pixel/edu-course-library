## Test items
1. Explain the two questions the court asks under s76 Criminal Justice and Immigration Act 2008 when self-defence is raised, with authority for each. [6 marks]
2. Omar, heavily and voluntarily drunk, mistakenly believes a stranger tapping him on the shoulder to return a dropped wallet is attacking him, and punches the stranger hard, breaking his nose. Using IRAC, advise on whether Omar can raise self-defence. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] M2 was the use of force necessary in the circumstances as the defendant genuinely believed them to be, even if that belief was objectively mistaken, provided honestly held (s76(3); R v Williams (Gladstone) (1984) 78 Cr App R 276); M2 was the degree of force used reasonable in those circumstances, judged objectively but allowing for the difficulty of judging the exact necessary force in the heat of the moment (s76(7)); M2 credit reference to disproportionate/excessive force defeating the defence (R v Clegg [1995] 1 AC 482) or that a mistaken belief induced by voluntary intoxication cannot be relied on (s76(5)).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether Omar's mistaken belief, formed while voluntarily intoxicated, can support self-defence; rule = s76(3) CJIA 2008 allows a genuinely held mistaken belief to found self-defence, but s76(5) specifically excludes a mistaken belief attributable to the defendant's own voluntary intoxication; application = Omar's belief that he was being attacked arose because he was heavily voluntarily drunk, which is precisely the situation s76(5) excludes; conclusion = Omar cannot rely on self-defence based on his intoxicated mistaken belief, and is likely liable for the assault (probably s47 ABH given the broken nose), since voluntary intoxication cannot found the necessary genuine belief for this defence even though it might otherwise be relevant to whether he had the mens rea for a specific intent offence, which is not in issue here.
