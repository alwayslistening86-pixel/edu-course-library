## Test items
1. Explain the two qualifying triggers for loss of control under s55 Coroners and Justice Act 2009, and what s55(6)(c) says about sexual infidelity. [6 marks]
2. Gita, after months of her partner's escalating, credible threats to kill her, finally snaps one evening when he raises his fist toward her again, and stabs him fatally with a kitchen knife she grabs from the counter. Using IRAC, advise on whether Gita can raise loss of control. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 fear of serious violence from the victim against the defendant or an identified other; B2 things done or said amounting to circumstances of an extremely grave character causing the defendant a justifiable sense of being seriously wronged; B2 sexual infidelity alone cannot be a qualifying trigger under s55(6)(c), though it may be considered as part of the wider context alongside another qualifying trigger (R v Clinton [2012] EWCA Crim 2).
2. [10] Mark using issue-rule-application-conclusion (IRAC): identify the issue(s), state the rule precisely with its authority (case or statute, named exactly), apply it to the facts given, and reach a supported conclusion. Credit: issue = whether Gita can rely on the partial defence of loss of control to reduce murder to voluntary manslaughter; rule = s54-55 Coroners and Justice Act 2009 requires a genuine loss of self-control (not revenge), a qualifying trigger (here, fear of serious violence given the immediate raised fist and history of credible threats), and that a person of Gita's sex/age with normal tolerance and self-restraint, in her circumstances, might have reacted similarly; application = the pattern of escalating threats plus the immediate raised fist supports a genuine fear-of-serious-violence trigger under s55(3), and the objective standard takes into account her circumstances (the history of threats) though not simply a lower general capacity for self-control; conclusion = Gita has a strong basis to run loss of control, subject to the jury's assessment of whether a person with normal tolerance in her situation might have reacted as she did.
