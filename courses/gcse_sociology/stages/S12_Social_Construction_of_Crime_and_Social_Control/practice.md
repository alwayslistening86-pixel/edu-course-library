# S12_Social_Construction_of_Crime_and_Social_Control - Practice: The social construction of crime and deviance, and social control: Merton's strain theory, Becker's labelling theory, formal and informal control, and Heidensohn

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Explain the difference between crime and deviance, with an example of behaviour that is deviant but not criminal.
2. Explain Merton's strain theory, including what 'innovation' means as a response to strain.
3. Explain Becker's concept of a 'master status', using a specific example.
4. Explain one form of formal and one form of informal social control, and give an example of each.
5. Explain Heidensohn's argument for why women commit less recorded crime than men.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
