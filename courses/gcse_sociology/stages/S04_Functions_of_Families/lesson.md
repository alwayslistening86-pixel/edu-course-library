# S04_Functions_of_Families - Lesson: Functions of families: differing views, Parsons' functionalist view, core functions, and feminist and Marxist critiques

## Goal
The learner explains Parsons' functionalist view of the family, identifies its sexual, reproductive, economic and educational functions, and explains feminist and Marxist critiques of those functions.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.1a - Differing views of the functions of the family: an overview: introduce the debate
- 3.3.1b - Parsons' functionalist view: primary socialisation and the stabilisation of adult personalities: explain Parsons
- 3.3.1c - Sexual, reproductive, economic and educational functions of the family: identify, describe and explain
- 3.3.1d - Feminist perspective on family functions: explain the critique
- 3.3.1e - Marxist perspective on family functions: explain the critique

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A functionalist says the family exists because it performs useful jobs for society. A feminist might ask: useful for whom, exactly?' Use this to introduce the idea that the same institution can be explained very differently depending on the perspective applied.

#### 3.3.1a Differing views of the functions of the family: an overview
Sociologists disagree about what the family is 'for'. Functionalists see the family as performing positive functions that benefit both individuals and society as a whole. Feminists argue the family more often benefits men at women's expense. Marxists argue the family serves capitalism's interests rather than society's interests as a whole. This stage examines each in turn, starting with the functionalist view.

#### 3.3.1b Parsons' functionalist view: primary socialisation and the stabilisation of adult personalities
Talcott Parsons argued the family performs two 'irreducible' functions that no other institution fully replaces. Primary socialisation teaches young children the basic norms and values of their society. The stabilisation of adult personalities describes how the family provides emotional security and a space to relax from the pressures of the wider world, helping adults function effectively in society.

#### 3.3.1c Sexual, reproductive, economic and educational functions of the family
Beyond Parsons' two core functions, functionalists identify further functions: the sexual function regulates sexual relationships through socially approved structures such as marriage; the reproductive function ensures the continuation of society through new members; the economic function historically involved the family as a unit of production, and today as a unit of consumption and financial support; the educational function overlaps with primary socialisation, preparing children for wider social life before or alongside formal schooling.

#### 3.3.1d Feminist perspective on family functions
Feminists argue that the family's apparently neutral 'functions' actually work to benefit men more than women: for example, the emotional stabilisation function is often provided disproportionately by women's unpaid domestic and emotional labour, while women's reproductive and childcare roles can limit their access to paid work and independence. Feminists argue functionalism ignores this inequality by presenting family functions as equally beneficial to everyone.

#### 3.3.1e Marxist perspective on family functions
Marxists argue the family serves capitalism rather than society as a whole: it reproduces and socialises the next generation of workers at private, family expense rather than the state's; it functions as a unit of consumption that helps sustain demand for capitalist goods; and it can act as a 'safety valve', absorbing the frustrations produced by an unequal, unfair economic system so that wider anger at capitalism is defused rather than directed at changing it.
