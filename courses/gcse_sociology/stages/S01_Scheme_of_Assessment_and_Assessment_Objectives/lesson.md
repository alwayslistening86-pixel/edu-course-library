# S01_Scheme_of_Assessment_and_Assessment_Objectives - Lesson: How AQA GCSE Sociology is examined: paper structure, question types and the three assessment objectives

## Goal
The learner states the structure, timing and weighting of Papers 1 and 2, recognises the question types used, and explains what AO1, AO2 and AO3 each require.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 2.1-P1 - Paper 1 structure: The Sociology of Families and Education: know duration, marks, weighting and section structure
- 2.1-P2 - Paper 2 structure: The Sociology of Crime and Deviance and Social Stratification: know duration, marks, weighting and section structure
- 2.2-QT - Question types: multiple choice, short answer and extended (typically 12-mark) response: recognise and plan for each question type
- 4.1-AO1 - AO1: demonstrate knowledge and understanding of sociological theories, concepts, evidence and methods: recall accurately (40% overall, 20% per paper)
- 4.1-AO2 - AO2: apply knowledge and understanding of sociological theories, concepts, evidence and methods: apply to the question asked (40% overall, 20% per paper)
- 4.1-AO3 - AO3: analyse and evaluate sociological theories, concepts, evidence and methods to construct arguments, make judgements and draw conclusions: analyse, evaluate, judge, conclude (20% overall, 10% per paper)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'If an exam question says "explain two reasons" versus "assess the view that...", are these asking you to do the same thing? What is different about what each one wants from you?' Use this to introduce the idea that different assessment objectives are being tested by different question types.

#### 2.1-P1 Paper 1 structure: The Sociology of Families and Education
Paper 1 lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 100 marks, and counts for 50 per cent of the GCSE. It covers Families and Education, with relevant research methods content examined throughout. It has two sections (Section A: Families; Section B: Education), and each section opens with two multiple-choice questions before moving into short-answer and extended-response questions.

#### 2.1-P2 Paper 2 structure: The Sociology of Crime and Deviance and Social Stratification
Paper 2 also lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 100 marks, and counts for 50 per cent of the GCSE. It covers Crime and Deviance and Social Stratification, again with research methods examined throughout. It has the same two-section pattern (Section A: Crime and Deviance; Section B: Social Stratification), each opening with two multiple-choice questions before short-answer and extended-response questions. Together, Papers 1 and 2 give a combined raw mark out of 200, compared against AQA's annually set grade boundaries.

#### 2.2-QT Question types: multiple choice, short answer and extended (typically 12-mark) response
Multiple-choice questions (usually worth 1 mark each) test straightforward recall or recognition and appear at the start of each section. Short-answer questions (worth a small number of marks) usually ask learners to identify, describe, give reasons for, or briefly explain something specific. Extended-response questions (commonly 12 marks) require a sustained, developed answer that draws on knowledge, applies it to the question, and analyses and evaluates to reach a supported judgement or conclusion — these are marked using the four-level generic bands.

#### 4.1-AO1 AO1: demonstrate knowledge and understanding of sociological theories, concepts, evidence and methods
AO1 rewards accurate recall: correctly naming and explaining sociological theories (for example, functionalism), concepts (for example, labelling), studies, evidence and research methods. AO1 makes up 40 per cent of the overall GCSE (20 per cent of each paper). A strong AO1 answer uses precise sociological vocabulary rather than everyday language, and names specific theorists or studies rather than vague generalisations.

#### 4.1-AO2 AO2: apply knowledge and understanding of sociological theories, concepts, evidence and methods
AO2 rewards applying knowledge specifically to the question asked, rather than simply describing a theory in the abstract. For example, rather than just describing functionalism in general, an AO2 response uses functionalist ideas to directly address a stated question about, say, family functions or crime. AO2 also makes up 40 per cent of the overall GCSE (20 per cent of each paper).

#### 4.1-AO3 AO3: analyse and evaluate sociological theories, concepts, evidence and methods to construct arguments, make judgements and draw conclusions
AO3 rewards going beyond description and application into genuine analysis (breaking an idea down, examining its parts and implications) and evaluation (weighing strengths and weaknesses, comparing competing explanations, and reaching a supported judgement). AO3 makes up 20 per cent of the overall GCSE (10 per cent of each paper) but is usually the mark scheme band that separates a mid-level answer from a top-level one on extended questions.

## Explicitly not here
The specific content of Families, Education, Crime and Deviance, and Social Stratification follows in later stages; this stage covers only how the qualification is structured and marked.
