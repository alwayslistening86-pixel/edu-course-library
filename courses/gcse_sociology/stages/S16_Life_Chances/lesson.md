# S16_Life_Chances - Lesson: Life chances: class, gender, race and ethnicity, sexuality/age/disability/religion, and Devine's revisit of the affluent worker

## Goal
The learner explains how class, gender, race/ethnicity, and sexuality/age/disability/religion each shape life chances, and explains Devine's study.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.6.3a - Class and life chances: explain the factor with evidence
- 3.6.3b - Gender and life chances: explain the factor with evidence
- 3.6.3c - Race and ethnicity and life chances: explain the factor with evidence
- 3.6.3d - Sexuality, age, disability and religion and life chances: explain each factor
- 3.6.3e - Devine: revisiting the affluent worker: explain the study

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Two babies are born on the same day in the same city, into different social positions. Beyond luck, what social factors might make their likely future health, education and income genuinely different?'

#### 3.6.3a Class and life chances
Life chances are a person's opportunities and probable outcomes across areas such as health, education, employment and life expectancy. Social class strongly shapes life chances in Britain: evidence consistently links lower social class with poorer average health outcomes and lower life expectancy, lower average educational attainment, and reduced access to higher-paid, higher-status employment, reflecting unequal access to material resources, opportunity and social/cultural capital.

#### 3.6.3b Gender and life chances
Gender continues to shape life chances, including patterns in occupational segregation (certain jobs remaining disproportionately male- or female-dominated), the gender pay gap (average earnings differences between men and women), and differing exposure to particular risks (for example, women experiencing disproportionately more domestic violence, while men experience higher rates of some other risks such as workplace injury or suicide), all examined by sociologists as evidence of continuing gendered inequality rather than simple individual difference.

#### 3.6.3c Race and ethnicity and life chances
Evidence shows differences in life chances between ethnic groups in Britain across areas including employment (rates and pay), housing, health outcomes and experience of the criminal justice system. Sociologists examine the extent to which such differences reflect ongoing discrimination, structural disadvantage, or other factors, while cautioning against treating any ethnic group as uniform, since class and gender also interact with ethnicity in shaping outcomes.

#### 3.6.3d Sexuality, age, disability and religion and life chances
Sexuality can affect life chances through experience of discrimination or unequal treatment, despite significant legal changes in recent decades (such as equal marriage). Age affects life chances at both ends of the age range (for example, youth unemployment, or reduced opportunity and ageism affecting older workers). Disability can affect life chances through unequal access to employment, education and public spaces, and continuing discrimination. Religion can affect life chances through discrimination or disadvantage linked to religious identity, which can also intersect with ethnicity.

#### 3.6.3e Devine: revisiting the affluent worker
Fiona Devine's research revisited earlier 'affluent worker' studies of relatively well-paid manual workers, examining whether increased affluence had caused such workers to adopt more middle-class identities, attitudes and lifestyles ('embourgeoisement'). Devine found that, despite higher incomes, many affluent workers retained distinctly working-class identities, attitudes and social networks, challenging the simple assumption that rising income alone dissolves class identity and inequality.
