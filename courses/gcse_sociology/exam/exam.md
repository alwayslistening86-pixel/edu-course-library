# GCSE Sociology (AQA 8192) - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` shows a passed test in the micro-profile.

## Format
Two timed written papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes and 100 marks (50% weighting each), together worth 100 per cent of the GCSE (there is no non-exam assessment/coursework component in AQA Sociology 8192). Paper 1, 'The Sociology of Families and Education', has two sections: Section A on Families (drawing on S04-S08) and Section B on Education (drawing on S09-S11), each section opening with two multiple-choice questions before short-answer and extended-response questions. Paper 2, 'The Sociology of Crime and Deviance and Social Stratification', has two sections: Section A on Crime and Deviance (drawing on S12-S14) and Section B on Social Stratification (drawing on S15-S18), in the same style. Research methods (S19-S20) and the sociological approach/key concepts (S01-S03) are examined throughout both papers rather than as standalone sections, so questions on any topic may require applying a named research method or a named perspective (functionalism, Marxism, feminism, interactionism) to that topic's content.

## Example structure
Paper 1, Section A: two multiple-choice questions on families, then short-answer questions on family forms or conjugal roles, then one extended (typically 12-mark) question requiring a developed, evaluative answer on functions of the family, changing relationships, criticisms of the family, or divorce. Section B follows the same pattern for education, with an extended question on, for example, the correspondence principle, educational achievement, or processes within schools. Paper 2 follows the same two-section, multiple-choice-then-short-answer-then-extended pattern for crime and deviance (drawing on social construction, control, patterns of offending, or crime data) and social stratification (drawing on functionalist theory, class, life chances, poverty, or power). At least one question per paper requires interpreting a data source (a table, graph or short sociological extract, drawing on S20) and at least one requires evaluating or justifying a research method (drawing on S19-S20) applied to a stated sociological topic.

## Grading
Apply `rubric.json`'s `exam_rubric` exactly. A pass requires applying sociological perspectives precisely to the specific question asked rather than describing them in the abstract (AO1/AO2), weighing perspectives or explanations against each other rather than presenting them side by side (AO3), and reaching a conclusion that is genuinely supported by that weighing rather than simply asserted (AO3), consistently across both papers.
