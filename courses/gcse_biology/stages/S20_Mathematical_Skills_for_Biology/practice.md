# S20_Mathematical_Skills_for_Biology - Practice: Mathematical requirements for GCSE Biology

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. A bacterium measures 0.0000025 m. Write this in standard form and in micrometres.
2. The counts in six quadrats are 4, 7, 5, 6, 30, 5. Find mean, median and mode and say which average is best and why.
3. Calculate the surface-area-to-volume ratio of cubes with sides 1 cm and 3 cm and explain the biological meaning.
4. Plot rate of reaction against temperature using given data, draw a line of best fit for the linear part and calculate the gradient.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
