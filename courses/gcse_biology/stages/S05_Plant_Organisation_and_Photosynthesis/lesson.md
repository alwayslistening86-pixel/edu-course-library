# S05_Plant_Organisation_and_Photosynthesis - Lesson: Plant tissues, transport and photosynthesis

## Goal
The learner relates plant tissues and transport to function, explains transpiration and translocation, and describes photosynthesis, its rate limiting factors and the uses of glucose.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.2.3.1 - Plant tissues: relate plant tissues to their function
- 4.2.3.2 - Plant organ system: explain transpiration and translocation
- 4.4.1.1 - Photosynthetic reaction: write the photosynthesis equations and describe the reaction as endothermic
- 4.4.1.2 - Rate of photosynthesis: interpret limiting factors and the inverse square law (Higher)
- 4.4.1.3 - Uses of glucose from photosynthesis: list the fates of glucose in plants
- RP6 - Required practical 6: light intensity and photosynthesis: investigate rate using pondweed

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A tree grows metres tall, but has no heart. How does water reach the top, and how does sugar reach the roots?'

#### 4.2.3.1 Plant tissues
The epidermis covers the leaf, palisade mesophyll (packed with chloroplasts) does most photosynthesis, spongy mesophyll has air spaces for gas exchange, xylem carries water and minerals upward, phloem carries sugars, meristem tissue at growing tips divides to produce new cells, and guard cells open and close stomata. Each tissue's structure suits its function.

#### 4.2.3.2 Plant organ system
Water is absorbed by root hair cells and moves up the xylem in the transpiration stream, evaporating from leaf stomata; this pull also brings mineral ions. Translocation moves dissolved sugars (sucrose) in phloem from leaves to growing and storage regions, in both directions. Transpiration rises with higher temperature, lower humidity, more air movement and stronger light, because each speeds evaporation or opens stomata wider. Guard cells control stomata to limit water loss.

#### 4.4.1.1 Photosynthetic reaction
Photosynthesis takes place in chloroplasts using chlorophyll to absorb light. Word equation: carbon dioxide + water gives glucose + oxygen, using light. Symbol equation: 6CO2 + 6H2O gives C6H12O6 + 6O2. It is an endothermic reaction because energy is transferred from the environment to the chloroplasts.

#### 4.4.1.2 Rate of photosynthesis
Rate can be limited by light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature or amount of chlorophyll. On a graph the rate rises then levels off when another factor becomes limiting. At high temperatures enzymes denature and the rate falls. Higher: light intensity is inversely proportional to the square of the distance from the source: doubling the distance cuts intensity to one quarter. Greenhouse growers control light, CO2 and temperature to raise yields, balancing costs and profit.

#### 4.4.1.3 Uses of glucose from photosynthesis
Plants use glucose for respiration, convert it to starch for storage, to fats or oils for storage in seeds, to cellulose for cell walls, and with nitrate ions from soil to make amino acids and then proteins. So plants need minerals as well as light and CO2.

#### RP6 Required practical 6: light intensity and photosynthesis
Investigate the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis using an aquatic plant such as pondweed. Place pondweed in a beaker of water with sodium hydrogencarbonate, position a lamp at measured distances, and count bubbles of oxygen per minute (or measure the volume of gas). Control temperature with a water bath or heat shield and repeat to obtain a mean. Plot rate against distance or 1 divided by distance squared.
