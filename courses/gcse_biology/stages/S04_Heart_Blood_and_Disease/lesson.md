# S04_Heart_Blood_and_Disease - Lesson: The heart, blood vessels, blood and non-communicable disease

## Goal
The learner describes the double circulatory system, blood, coronary heart disease and its treatments, and analyses the effect of lifestyle on non-communicable disease and cancer.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.2.2.2 - The heart and blood vessels: describe the double circulatory system and the vessels
- 4.2.2.3 - Blood: describe blood components and their functions
- 4.2.2.4 - Coronary heart disease: a non-communicable disease: explain CHD and treatments such as statins and stents
- 4.2.2.5 - Health issues: define health and explain how diseases interact
- 4.2.2.6 - The effect of lifestyle on some non-communicable diseases: analyse risk factors for non-communicable disease
- 4.2.2.7 - Cancer: distinguish benign and malignant tumours and describe risk factors

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to trace a drop of blood from the toes back to the toes, naming each chamber and vessel; then ask what could go wrong.

#### 4.2.2.2 The heart and blood vessels
The heart is a double pump: the right side sends deoxygenated blood to the lungs, the left side sends oxygenated blood around the body. Blood enters at the atria, is pumped out from the ventricles (left wall thicker), and valves prevent backflow. Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle, and pacemaker cells in the right atrium set the rhythm. Arteries have thick, elastic, muscular walls for high pressure, veins have valves and wide lumens, capillaries are one cell thick for exchange. Blood flow rate = volume divided by time.

#### 4.2.2.3 Blood
Blood is plasma plus cells. Plasma carries carbon dioxide, digested food, urea and hormones. Red blood cells are biconcave with no nucleus and contain haemoglobin that binds oxygen. White blood cells (phagocytes and lymphocytes) defend against pathogens. Platelets are fragments that help blood clot at wounds.

#### 4.2.2.4 Coronary heart disease: a non-communicable disease
In coronary heart disease, fatty material builds up in the coronary arteries, narrowing them and reducing blood flow to the heart muscle. Stents hold the artery open; statins lower blood cholesterol and slow the build-up; faulty heart valves can be replaced with mechanical or biological valves; heart failure may be treated with a donor heart or artificial heart. Each treatment has risks, such as blood clots, rejection or infection, so a judgement weighs benefit against risk.

#### 4.2.2.5 Health issues
Health is a state of physical and mental well-being. Communicable and non-communicable diseases can interact: a weakened immune system makes infection likelier, viruses inside cells can trigger cancers, immune reactions can cause allergies such as asthma, and severe physical illness can lead to depression. Many diseases have several causes, including diet, stress, and life situation.

#### 4.2.2.6 The effect of lifestyle on some non-communicable diseases
Risk factors raise the chance of disease without guaranteeing it. Smoking is linked to lung disease and cardiovascular disease; obesity to type 2 diabetes; alcohol to liver and brain damage; carcinogens such as ionising radiation to cancer. Correlation is not causation: a link needs a proposed mechanism and a control of other variables. Non-communicable diseases have human costs and financial costs to health services.

#### 4.2.2.7 Cancer
Cancer results from uncontrolled cell division. A benign tumour grows in one place and does not invade other tissue; a malignant tumour invades neighbouring tissue and spreads through the blood to form secondary tumours. Risk factors include smoking, UV exposure, obesity and viral infection, and some genetic inheritance.
