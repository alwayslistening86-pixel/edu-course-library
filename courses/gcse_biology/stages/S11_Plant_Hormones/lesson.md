# S11_Plant_Hormones - Lesson: Plant hormones, tropisms and their uses

## Goal
The learner explains how auxin controls tropisms, describes the uses of plant hormones in agriculture, and investigates plant responses to light or gravity.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.5.4.1 - Control and coordination: explain auxin action and tropisms (Biology-only)
- 4.5.4.2 - Use of plant hormones: describe agricultural uses of auxins, ethene and gibberellins (Biology-only)
- RP8 - Required practical 8: plant responses: investigate light or gravity and seedling growth (Biology-only)

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A plant on a windowsill bends toward the light. It has no eyes or muscles. What could make one side grow more than the other?'

#### 4.5.4.1 Control and coordination
Plants use hormones to coordinate growth. Auxin is made in the shoot tip and moves down. In phototropism, auxin moves to the shaded side of the shoot and makes cells there elongate faster, so the shoot bends towards light. In gravitropism (geotropism), auxin collects on the lower side of a horizontal root and inhibits cell elongation there, so the root grows downwards; in a shoot the same distribution makes the shoot grow upward. Gibberellins end seed dormancy and promote germination; ethene controls cell division and ripening of fruit.

#### 4.5.4.2 Use of plant hormones
Auxins are used as selective weed killers (they make broad-leaved weeds overgrow and die) and in rooting powders to help cuttings root. Ethene is used to control ripening in storage and transport, so fruit arrives ripe at the right time. Gibberellins end seed dormancy, promote flowering, and increase fruit size.

#### RP8 Required practical 8: plant responses
Investigate the effect of light or gravity on the growth of newly germinated seedlings. For light: grow seedlings in boxes with a slit on one side and one with no light control, measure the angle of curvature after several days. For gravity: place seedlings horizontally in the dark and measure the angle of curvature of root or shoot. Control light, temperature, water and seedling age; repeat to reduce variation.
