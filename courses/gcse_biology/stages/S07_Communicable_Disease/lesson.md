# S07_Communicable_Disease - Lesson: Communicable diseases and their causes

## Goal
The learner describes how pathogens cause and spread disease and the specific viral, bacterial, fungal and protist diseases named in the specification.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.3.1.1 - Communicable (infectious) diseases: define pathogens and the ways they spread
- 4.3.1.2 - Viral diseases: describe measles, HIV and tobacco mosaic virus
- 4.3.1.3 - Bacterial diseases: describe salmonella and gonorrhoea
- 4.3.1.4 - Fungal diseases: describe rose black spot
- 4.3.1.5 - Protist diseases: describe malaria and its control

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Name a disease you have had. What caused it, and how did it reach you?' Sort the answers into bacterial, viral, fungal and protist causes.

#### 4.3.1.1 Communicable (infectious) diseases
Pathogens are microorganisms that cause infectious disease: bacteria, viruses, fungi and protists. Bacteria may produce toxins and damage tissue; viruses live and reproduce inside cells and damage them. Spread is by direct contact, by water, or by air (droplets). Spread is reduced by hygiene, destroying vectors, isolating infected individuals and vaccination.

#### 4.3.1.2 Viral diseases
Measles causes fever and a red skin rash, spreads by droplets from coughs and sneezes, and can lead to serious complications such as pneumonia; MMR vaccination prevents it. HIV first causes flu-like symptoms, then attacks the immune cells; it spreads by sexual contact and by exchange of body fluids such as through shared needles; antiretroviral drugs stop virus multiplying, and untreated HIV can progress to AIDS. Tobacco mosaic virus causes a mosaic of discoloured patches on leaves, reducing photosynthesis and so growth.

#### 4.3.1.3 Bacterial diseases
Salmonella causes fever, abdominal cramps, vomiting and diarrhoea, caused by the bacteria's toxins; it spreads by contaminated food, especially raw chicken and eggs; vaccinating poultry helps control it. Gonorrhoea is a sexually transmitted disease with a thick yellow or green discharge and pain on urination; it spreads by sexual contact, is treated with antibiotics (though resistant strains are appearing) and is prevented by using a barrier method such as a condom.

#### 4.3.1.4 Fungal diseases
Rose black spot is a fungal disease: purple or black spots on leaves which then turn yellow and drop early, reducing photosynthesis and growth. It spreads by wind and water, and is treated with fungicides and by stripping and destroying affected leaves.

#### 4.3.1.5 Protist diseases
Malaria is caused by a protist that passes into the blood through mosquito bites; the mosquito is the vector. It causes repeated episodes of fever and can be fatal. Control focuses on preventing breeding of mosquitoes, protecting people with nets and repellents, and treating infected people with drugs.
