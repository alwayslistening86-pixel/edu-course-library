# S19_Working_Scientifically_Analysis_and_Vocabulary - Lesson: Working scientifically: analysis, evaluation and scientific language

## Goal
The learner presents, converts and analyses data, draws reasoned conclusions, evaluates accuracy and precision, communicates findings, and uses scientific vocabulary, units and prefixes correctly.

## Syllabus items taught here
- WS3.1 - Presenting observations: present data using suitable tables and graphs
- WS3.2 - Translating data between forms: convert between tables, graphs and equations
- WS3.3 - Mathematical and statistical analysis: carry out calculations and statistical analysis
- WS3.4 - Distributions and uncertainty: represent distributions and estimate uncertainty
- WS3.5 - Interpreting observations and patterns: identify patterns and correlations
- WS3.6 - Reasoned explanations relating data to hypotheses: draw conclusions linked to the hypothesis
- WS3.7 - Evaluating data: accuracy and precision: judge accuracy, precision and reliability
- WS3.8 - Communicating findings: communicate findings in reports
- WS4 - Scientific vocabulary, quantities, units, symbols and nomenclature (WS 4.1-4.6): use scientific vocabulary, SI units and prefixes

## How to teach this
Give a small messy data set from an earlier practical and ask: 'What does this show, how sure are you, and what would make you surer?'

#### WS3.1 Presenting observations
Present observations and data using tables, bar charts (categorical data), histograms, line graphs and scatter graphs (continuous data), choosing the type to suit the data. Label axes with quantity and unit, choose scales that use more than half the graph area, and plot points accurately.

#### WS3.2 Translating data between forms
Data can be moved between forms: a table into a graph, a graph into a table, and a written description into a formula. For example, reading enzyme rate from a graph and recording it in a table; or translating 'doubling every 20 minutes' into a formula for bacterial number.

#### WS3.3 Mathematical and statistical analysis
Calculate means, ranges, percentages, percentage change, rates and ratios. Identify anomalous results and exclude them from the mean with justification. Use statistics such as the range of repeat readings as a measure of spread.

#### WS3.4 Distributions and uncertainty
Repeat readings will vary; the range of repeat values shows uncertainty, and a smaller range shows better precision. Uncertainty in a mean can be estimated as half the range. Represent distributions using frequency tables and histograms.

#### WS3.5 Interpreting observations and identifying patterns
Describe patterns in data: positive correlation, negative correlation or no correlation; directly proportional; levelling off; rising then falling. Use figures from the data, such as quoting values with units, when describing a graph.

#### WS3.6 Reasoned explanations relating data to hypotheses
A conclusion states whether the data support the hypothesis, quotes evidence, and gives a scientific explanation, for example 'The rate is fastest at 37 degrees Celsius, which supports the hypothesis, because enzymes have an optimum temperature; above this the active site denatures.' Do not claim more than the data show.

#### WS3.7 Evaluating data: accuracy and precision
Accurate results are close to the true value; precise results are close together. Repeatable means the same person gets the same result; reproducible means different people or methods get the same. Reliability grows with repeats, controls and calibrated apparatus, and anomalies should be explained where possible.

#### WS3.8 Communicating findings
Report findings in a clear structure: aim, method, results (tables and graphs), conclusion, evaluation. Use scientific language, quote units, and match the level of detail to the audience, whether a written report, a poster, an oral talk or an extended exam answer.

#### WS4 Scientific vocabulary, quantities, units, symbols and nomenclature
Use correct terms (for example osmosis, diffusion, pathogen). Use SI units and prefixes: mass in kg or g, length in m, cm, mm, micrometre (one thousandth of a millimetre) and nanometre, time in seconds, volume in dm3 or cm3, concentration in mol/dm3 or g/dm3. Convert between units and use standard form appropriately. Use symbols and chemical formulae correctly, such as CO2, H2O and O2.
