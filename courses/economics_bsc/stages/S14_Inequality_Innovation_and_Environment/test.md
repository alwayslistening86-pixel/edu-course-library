## Test items
1. Explain what a Gini coefficient of 0.65 would indicate about income distribution compared with a Gini coefficient of 0.25, using the Lorenz curve. [3 marks]
2. Explain the Arrow replacement effect and what it implies about whether monopolists or competitive challengers have a stronger incentive to innovate. [4 marks]
3. A cap-and-trade scheme sets an emissions cap of 10,000 tonnes for an industry that would otherwise emit 14,000 tonnes. If permits trade at £60/tonne, calculate the cost to the industry of complying exactly at the cap (ignoring abatement costs), and explain one advantage of cap-and-trade over a carbon tax when there is high uncertainty about abatement costs but a legally binding emissions target. [4 marks]
4. Which statements reflect a pluralist (rather than purely mainstream/neoclassical) framing of inequality, as taught in this module? Choose every correct option.
   A. Power and bargaining institutions can directly shape the pre-tax distribution of income
   B. Inequality is entirely explained by differences in individual productivity rewarded by competitive markets
   C. Different schools of economic thought can offer genuinely different, non-equivalent explanations of the same phenomenon
   D. There is only one correct analytical framework for understanding inequality

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 a Gini of 0.65 indicates a Lorenz curve far below (further bowed away from) the 45-degree line of perfect equality, compared with 0.25's curve much closer to that line; B1 0.65 indicates considerably higher income inequality than 0.25; B1 (context) 0.65 would be an unusually high figure among developed economies, comparable to some of the world's most unequal countries.
2. [4] B1 the replacement effect: an incumbent monopolist earns monopoly profit already, so a successful innovation partly replaces (cannibalises) its own existing profit rather than being pure gain; B1 a challenger firm currently earns little or no profit, so the same innovation is pure gain (goes from roughly zero to positive profit) for it; B1 this implies challengers can have a stronger incentive to innovate than a comfortable incumbent; B1 which complicates the simple view that monopoly profits are needed to fund and motivate innovation -- competitive pressure can itself be a spur to innovate.
3. [4] M1 permits needed = cap = 10,000 tonnes; A1 cost = 10,000 x £60 = £600,000; B1 cap-and-trade fixes the quantity of emissions directly, guaranteeing the environmental target (the cap) is met regardless of how costly abatement turns out to be; B1 whereas a carbon tax fixes the price and lets the quantity emitted vary with (uncertain) abatement costs, so it cannot guarantee hitting a specific emissions target.
4. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
