# S04_Market_Structures_with_Calculus - Lesson: Market structures formalised with calculus

## Goal
The learner derives the perfectly competitive long-run zero-profit condition, the monopoly markup formula (the Lerner index) from MR = MC, and the first-order conditions for first-, second- and third-degree price discrimination.

## Syllabus items taught here
- D217-10 - Long-run perfect competition: P = min LRAC
- D217-11 - Monopoly markup and the Lerner index
- D217-12 - First-, second- and third-degree price discrimination

## How to teach this
Ask why a monopolist's price markup over marginal cost depends on the elasticity of demand it faces. Teach each topic by building on what GCSE and A-level Economics already gave the learner: name the A-level idea it extends, then show the calculus/formal derivation or statistical technique that intermediate/degree-level economics adds. Work every numerical example with the learner predicting a step before it is shown; every figure in this course was computed with Python when the course was built. For current economic data (interest rates, inflation, growth, exchange rates), check the latest official sources (ONS, Bank of England, IMF) rather than relying on any figure used here as illustration.

#### D217-10 Long-run perfect competition: P = min LRAC
In long-run perfect competition, free entry and exit drive economic profit to zero: firms enter while price exceeds long-run average cost (attracted by supernormal profit) and exit while price is below it, until P = min(LRAC) = MC at that point. Each firm is a price taker (faces a horizontal demand curve at the market price), so P = MR = MC = min(LRAC) simultaneously in long-run equilibrium.

#### D217-11 Monopoly markup and the Lerner index
A monopolist facing downward-sloping demand sets MR = MC. Writing MR = P(1 + 1/PED) (derived from TR = PQ and the definition of PED), the profit-maximising condition MR = MC rearranges to the Lerner index: (P - MC)/P = -1/PED. This formalises the A-level intuition that monopoly power (markup) is larger the more inelastic demand is. *Example:* PED = -2.5: markup (P-MC)/P = 0.40, i.e. price is set 40% above marginal cost as a share of price.

#### D217-12 First-, second- and third-degree price discrimination
Price discrimination extracts more consumer surplus than a single uniform price. First-degree (perfect) discrimination charges each unit its exact reservation price, capturing all consumer surplus as producer surplus, with no deadweight loss (output rises to the competitive level). Second-degree discrimination uses quantity-based pricing (bulk discounts, tariffs) so consumers self-select. Third-degree discrimination charges different prices to identifiable groups with different elasticities; the profit-maximising rule sets a higher price in the market with the more inelastic demand, since MR must be equal across markets at the common MC: MR1 = MR2 = MC.
