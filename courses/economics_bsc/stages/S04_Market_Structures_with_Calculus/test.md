## Test items
1. Explain why price equals minimum long-run average cost for a perfectly competitive firm in long-run equilibrium. [4 marks]
2. A firm faces PED = -1.5. Using the Lerner index, calculate its profit-maximising price if MC = £12. [3 marks]
3. Explain the difference between second- and third-degree price discrimination, with an example of each. [4 marks]
4. Which conditions hold for a profit-maximising third-degree price discriminator selling in two separable markets? Choose every correct option.
   A. MR1 = MR2 at the optimum
   B. The price is higher in the market with more inelastic demand
   C. MR1 = MR2 = MC at the optimum
   D. Price is equal in both markets

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 supernormal profit attracts entry, shifting industry supply right and lowering price; B1 sub-normal profit causes exit, shifting supply left and raising price; B1 this continues until P = LRAC, eliminating economic profit; B1 the firm's own profit-maximising rule P = MC then pins this at the minimum of LRAC, since that is the only point where a price-taking firm earning zero profit can be in cost-minimising equilibrium.
2. [3] M1 (P-MC)/P = -1/-1.5 = 0.667; M1 P - 12 = 0.667P; A1 P(1-0.667) = 12, P = £36.00.
3. [4] B1 second-degree: prices vary by the quantity bought (self-selecting tariffs), e.g. bulk discounts, multi-buy offers; B1 third-degree: prices vary by an identifiable group, e.g. student/senior cinema tickets; B1 second-degree relies on consumers revealing type by their purchase pattern; B1 third-degree relies on the firm being able to identify and separate groups (prevent resale).
4. Correct: A, B, C (exactly these options, no others)
