## Test items
1. State the first fundamental welfare theorem and list two of its required conditions that real-world markets commonly violate. [3 marks]
2. A factory's MC = 3Q; market demand is P = 90-Q; production causes a constant marginal external cost of £15. Find the unregulated market quantity, the socially efficient quantity, and the Pigouvian tax. [5 marks]
3. Explain the Coase theorem and state why it is rarely a practical solution for an externality like carbon emissions from millions of dispersed sources. [4 marks]
4. Explain the free-rider problem for a public good such as national defence or a lighthouse, and why it leads to under-provision by private markets. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 under certain conditions, every competitive equilibrium is Pareto efficient; B2 any two of: no externalities, no public goods, complete/perfect information, perfect competition (no market power) -- each is commonly violated in real markets, which is why market failure is pervasive.
2. [5] M1 unregulated: P=MC, 90-Q=3Q, Q=22.5; M1 social optimum: P=MSC=3Q+15, 90-Q=3Q+15; A1 Q*=18.8; A1 tax = £15 per unit; B1 the tax raises the firm's effective MC to MC+15, aligning private and social cost so the market outcome matches the social optimum.
3. [4] B1 with clearly defined property rights and zero transaction costs, private bargaining reaches the efficient outcome regardless of who holds the right; B1 it changes only who pays whom, not the final efficient quantity; B1 transaction costs are far from zero when there are millions of affected/emitting parties; B1 the cost of identifying, organising and negotiating with everyone involved makes bargaining impractical, so Pigouvian taxation or regulation is used instead.
4. [3] B1 the good is non-excludable, so no one can be stopped from consuming it whether or not they pay; B1 individuals therefore have an incentive to understate their willingness to pay and let others fund it (free-ride); B1 private suppliers cannot capture enough revenue to cover the cost, so the good is under-provided relative to the socially efficient quantity (where the sum of MRS equals MC).
