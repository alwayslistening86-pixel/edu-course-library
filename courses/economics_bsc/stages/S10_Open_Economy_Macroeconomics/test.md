## Test items
1. Explain why the current account and the financial account of the balance of payments must (approximately) offset each other. [3 marks]
2. Explain, using the Mundell-Fleming model, why expansionary fiscal policy is less effective at raising output under floating exchange rates with high capital mobility than under fixed exchange rates. [5 marks]
3. State the impossible trinity and explain which one of the three a country using a currency board (a hard fixed exchange rate) with open capital markets must give up. [4 marks]
4. The UK interest rate is 4.5% and the eurozone rate is 3%. According to uncovered interest rate parity, what does the market expect to happen to the pound against the euro, and why doesn't this represent a risk-free arbitrage opportunity? [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 the balance of payments records every cross-border transaction exactly once, as either a current-account or a capital/financial-account item, by double-entry bookkeeping; B1 a current account deficit means the country is buying more from abroad than it sells; B1 this must be paid for by a matching net inflow of foreign capital (borrowing, asset sales, reserve drawdown) recorded in the financial account, so the two broadly sum to zero.
2. [5] B1 under floating rates, expansionary fiscal policy raises the interest rate (crowding-out pressure from IS-LM); B1 the higher interest rate attracts capital inflows, increasing demand for the domestic currency; B1 the currency appreciates, making exports less competitive and imports cheaper, reducing net exports; B1 this partially or fully offsets the initial fiscal expansion; B1 under a fixed exchange rate, the central bank must instead expand the money supply to prevent this appreciation and defend the peg, which reinforces rather than offsets the fiscal expansion.
3. [4] B1 a country cannot simultaneously have a fixed exchange rate, free capital mobility and independent monetary policy; B1 it can only hold two of the three at once; B1 a currency board with open capital markets has chosen a fixed rate and free capital flows; B1 so it must give up independent monetary policy -- its money supply and interest rate are dictated by the need to defend the peg.
4. [3] B1 the market expects the pound to depreciate against the euro by roughly the interest differential, about 1.5%; B1 this offsets the extra interest earned by holding pounds, so expected returns are equalised across currencies; B1 it is not risk-free arbitrage because the expected depreciation is not certain -- exchange rates can move by more or less than expected, so holding the higher-interest currency carries exchange-rate risk.
