## Test items
1. Nominal GDP rises from £1,800bn to £1,980bn while real GDP rises from £1,800bn to £1,900bn. Calculate the GDP deflator in the second year (base year = first year, deflator 100) and the inflation rate implied. [4 marks]
2. MPC = 0.6. Government spending rises by £50bn. Calculate the multiplier and the total rise in equilibrium income, and explain one leakage that makes the real-world multiplier smaller than this simple model implies. [4 marks]
3. IS: Y = 400 - 10r. LM: Y = 250 + 15r. Find the equilibrium interest rate and income. [4 marks]
4. Explain why the IS curve slopes downward and why the LM curve slopes upward. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 deflator = 1980/1900 x 100; A1 = 104.21; M1 inflation = deflator rise from 100; A1 = 4.21%.
2. [4] M1 multiplier = 1/(1-0.6) = 2.50; A1 ΔY = 50 x 2.50 = £125bn; B1 leakages such as taxation, imports (spending that leaves the domestic economy) or additional saving reduce the fraction of each spending round that is re-spent domestically; B1 so the real-world multiplier is smaller than this simple closed-economy figure.
3. [4] M1 400-10r = 250+15r; M1 150 = 25r; A1 r = 6; A1 Y = 400 - 10x6 = 340.
4. [4] B1 IS: a higher interest rate raises the cost of borrowing/return required on investment, lowering planned investment; B1 lower investment reduces equilibrium income via the multiplier, so higher r pairs with lower Y (downward slope); B1 LM: higher income raises transactions demand for money; B1 with a fixed money supply, the interest rate must rise to reduce money demand back into balance (higher opportunity cost of holding money), so higher Y pairs with higher r (upward slope).
