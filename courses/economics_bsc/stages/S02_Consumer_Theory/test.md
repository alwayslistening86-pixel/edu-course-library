## Test items
1. Define the marginal rate of substitution and explain why indifference curves are typically drawn convex to the origin. [3 marks]
2. A price fall for an inferior good has offsetting substitution and income effects. Explain both, and state the condition under which the good would be a Giffen good. [4 marks]
3. U = x^0.5 y^0.5, I = 240, Px = 4, Py = 6. Find the utility-maximising bundle and the resulting utility level. [4 marks]
4. Which is/are true of a Giffen good? Choose every correct option.
   A. It is necessarily an inferior good
   B. Its demand curve slopes upward over some range
   C. The substitution effect is positive for a price fall
   D. It must be a luxury good
5. Explain what the Weak Axiom of Revealed Preference tests, and why it matters for testing whether observed consumer data is 'rational'. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 MRS: the rate at which a consumer will give up one good for another while keeping utility unchanged, MUx/MUy; B1 convexity reflects diminishing MRS; B1 consumers place a higher value on variety, so are willing to sacrifice less y for each extra x as x rises.
2. [4] B1 substitution effect: relatively cheaper, so quantity demanded rises, holding utility constant; B1 income effect: for an inferior good, higher real income reduces quantity demanded (negative); B1 for a normal demand curve the (positive) substitution effect dominates; B1 it is a Giffen good only if the negative income effect outweighs the substitution effect, so total quantity demanded falls.
3. [4] M1 x* = 0.5x240/4 = 30; A1 y* = 0.5x240/6 = 20; M1 U = x^0.5 y^0.5; A1 U = 24.49.
4. Correct: A, B, C (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B1 WARP: if bundle A is chosen while B was also affordable, B must never later be chosen while A is affordable; B1 it lets economists test consistency of choices directly from purchase data; B1 without needing to observe or assume a specific utility function.
