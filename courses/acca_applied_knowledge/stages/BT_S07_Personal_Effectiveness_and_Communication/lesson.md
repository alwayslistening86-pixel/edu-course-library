# BT_S07_Personal_Effectiveness_and_Communication - Lesson: Personal effectiveness, conflict and communication

## Goal
The learner explains personal effectiveness (time management, targets, stress), the consequences of ineffectiveness, competence frameworks and personal development, sources of workplace conflict and how to resolve them, and effective communication in business.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BT.E1 - Personal effectiveness
- BT.E2 - Consequences of ineffectiveness at work
- BT.E3 - Competence frameworks and personal development
- BT.E4 - Sources of conflict and techniques for conflict resolution
- BT.E5 - Communicating in business

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Two employees have identical skills and workload, but one always meets deadlines calmly and the other is always firefighting. What's different?' Use the answer to introduce personal effectiveness. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### BT.E1 Personal effectiveness
**Personal effectiveness** is achieving objectives efficiently, making the best use of time and other resources. Time management tools: prioritising tasks by urgency and importance (e.g. an urgent/important matrix - urgent-and-important tasks first, important-but-not-urgent tasks scheduled deliberately rather than left to become crises), setting SMART personal targets (see BT.D6), avoiding unnecessary interruptions and procrastination, and delegating appropriately. Managing **stress**: some pressure can improve performance, but excessive, prolonged stress harms wellbeing, decision-making and productivity; managing it involves realistic workload planning, clear priorities, support from managers/colleagues, and recognising early warning signs before they become a crisis.

#### BT.E2 Consequences of ineffectiveness at work
**Consequences of ineffectiveness at work**: for the individual, missed deadlines, poor quality work, increased stress, damaged reputation and career progression; for the organisation, lost productivity, higher costs, missed business opportunities, poor customer service and damaged morale of colleagues who must cover the gap. Ineffectiveness often compounds - one missed deadline creates pressure that causes further mistakes - so early intervention (support, training, workload review) is usually more effective than waiting for a crisis.

#### BT.E3 Competence frameworks and personal development
**Competence frameworks** set out the knowledge, skills and behaviours expected at a given role or level, used for recruitment, appraisal, and identifying development needs; professional bodies (including ACCA) publish their own competence/professional development frameworks for qualified members. **Personal development** is an ongoing process of identifying gaps against such a framework (or against career goals) and taking action to close them - formal study, on-the-job experience, mentoring, feedback-seeking - often recorded in a personal development plan (PDP) which sets specific goals, actions and timescales, reviewed and updated periodically as skills develop and priorities change.

#### BT.E4 Sources of conflict and techniques for conflict resolution
**Sources of workplace conflict**: competition for scarce resources (budget, staff time), unclear or overlapping roles and responsibilities, differing goals between individuals or departments, poor or inconsistent communication, and personality clashes. **Conflict resolution techniques**: negotiation and compromise (each side gives some ground); collaboration/problem-solving (working together to find a solution that meets both sides' underlying interests, generally the most constructive but most time-consuming approach); accommodation (one side yields, useful when the issue matters much more to the other party); avoidance (postponing or sidestepping the conflict, appropriate only for trivial or poorly-timed disputes, since unresolved conflict tends to resurface); and, where needed, formal mediation by a neutral third party. The right approach depends on how important the issue is and how important the relationship is.

#### BT.E5 Communicating in business
**Communicating in business**: effective communication has a sender, a message, a chosen **channel** (face-to-face, phone, email, written report, video call), a receiver, and (for two-way communication) **feedback** confirming the message was understood as intended; **noise** (distraction, jargon, technical failure, poor timing) can distort the message at any point. Choosing the right channel matters: rich, sensitive or complex messages (a difficult appraisal conversation, a redundancy) usually need a rich, face-to-face channel allowing tone and immediate feedback; simple, routine, easily-referenced information (a meeting time, a policy update) suits a leaner channel such as email. Barriers to effective communication include jargon, cultural and language differences, information overload, and a hierarchical culture where junior staff are reluctant to speak up (undermining feedback and early warning of problems).
