# FA_S06_Accruals_Receivables_Payables_Provisions_and_Capital_Structure - Lesson: Accruals, receivables, payables, provisions and capital structure

## Goal
The learner adjusts for accruals, prepayments, accrued and deferred income, accounts for irrecoverable debts and the allowance for receivables, distinguishes provisions from contingent liabilities/assets, and explains capital structure and finance costs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA.D7 - Accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income
- FA.D8 - Receivables and payables
- FA.D9 - Provisions and contingencies
- FA.D10 - Capital structure and finance costs

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A business pays its annual insurance bill in October, covering the next 12 months, but its year end is 31 December. How much of that payment is really this year's expense?' Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### FA.D7 Accruals, prepayments, accrued income and deferred income
**Accruals and prepayments** apply the matching concept: an **accrual** is an expense incurred in the period but not yet paid/invoiced (a liability at the year end, added to the expense); a **prepayment** is a payment made in the period covering (wholly or partly) a future period (an asset at the year end, deducted from the expense). Similarly, **accrued income** is income earned but not yet received (an asset, added to income) and **deferred income** is cash received in advance for goods/services not yet provided (a liability, deducted from income, released to income as it is earned). *Worked example, prepayment*: insurance of £7,200 is paid on 1 October, covering the year to 30 September next year; at a 31 December year end, 9 months (£{7200 * 9 / 12:,.0f}) relates to next year (a prepayment/asset), and 3 months (£{7200 * 3 / 12:,.0f}) is this year's expense. *Worked example, accrual*: electricity is normally billed quarterly; at the year end, 2 months' unbilled usage is estimated at £900: Dr Electricity expense £900, Cr Accruals (liability) £900.

#### FA.D8 Receivables and payables
**Receivables and payables**. A debt is written off as an **irrecoverable (bad) debt** when recovery is judged remote (Dr Irrecoverable debts expense, Cr Trade receivables, removing it from the receivables balance). An **allowance for receivables** is a prudent estimate of *other* debts that may not be collected (e.g. a percentage of remaining receivables, or specific doubtful balances), shown as a deduction from trade receivables in the statement of financial position without removing the individual debts from the ledger; a *increase* in the allowance is an expense, a *decrease* is (net) income/a reduction in expense. *Worked example*: trade receivables are £120,000 after writing off a £4,000 irrecoverable debt; an allowance of 2% of the remaining balance is then required. Remaining receivables = 120,000 - 4,000 = £{120000 - 4000:,}; allowance required = {120000 - 4000:,} x 0.02 = £{(120000 - 4000) * 0.02:,.0f}; receivables shown in the statement of financial position (net of the allowance) = {120000 - 4000:,} - {(120000 - 4000) * 0.02:,.0f} = £{(120000 - 4000) - (120000 - 4000) * 0.02:,.0f}.

#### FA.D9 Provisions and contingencies
**Provisions and contingencies** (IAS 37). A **provision** is a liability of uncertain timing or amount, recognised when there is a present obligation (legal or constructive) from a past event, an outflow of economic benefit is **probable**, and the amount can be reliably estimated (e.g. a provision for a warranty on goods already sold). A **contingent liability** is only a *possible* obligation, or a present obligation where an outflow is not probable or cannot be reliably measured - not recognised as a liability, but disclosed by note (unless the possibility is remote, in which case not even disclosed). A **contingent asset** is a possible asset whose existence will be confirmed by a future event not wholly within the entity's control - never recognised, only disclosed by note if an inflow is probable (and not disclosed at all if it is merely possible).

#### FA.D10 Capital structure and finance costs
**Capital structure and finance costs**. A company's capital typically comprises **equity** (share capital - the nominal/par value of shares issued, plus **share premium**, the excess received over nominal value - and retained earnings/reserves) and **debt** (loans, debentures/bonds, finance leases). Debt finance carries a contractual obligation to pay **interest** (a finance cost in the statement of profit or loss, regardless of profit levels) and to repay the principal, whereas equity dividends are discretionary (paid only if the company chooses to, out of distributable profits). *Worked example*: a company has a £200,000 loan at a fixed annual interest rate of 6%: the annual finance cost = 200,000 x 0.06 = £{200000 * 0.06:,.0f}, recorded as an expense in the statement of profit or loss regardless of whether the company makes a profit that year, and (to the extent unpaid at the year end) also as an accrued interest liability.
