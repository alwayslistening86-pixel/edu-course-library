# FA_S06_Accruals_Receivables_Payables_Provisions_and_Capital_Structure - Test: Accruals, receivables, payables, provisions and capital structure

## Test items
1. A business pays £9,600 for a 12-month insurance policy starting 1 September. Calculate the prepayment and the expense for the year, at a 31 December year end. [4 marks]
2. An accrual at the year end is: Choose every correct option.
   A. a payment made in advance, treated as an asset
   B. an expense incurred but not yet paid, treated as a liability
   C. income received but not yet earned
   D. always an error requiring correction
3. Trade receivables stand at £95,000. A specific debt of £3,000 is written off as irrecoverable, and an allowance for receivables of 4% of the remaining balance is then required (no previous allowance existed). Calculate the irrecoverable debts expense, the allowance for receivables, and the net receivables shown in the statement of financial position. [5 marks]
4. Under IAS 37, a provision should be recognised when: Choose every correct option.
   A. an outflow is merely possible, not probable
   B. there is a present obligation from a past event, an outflow is probable, and the amount can be reliably estimated
   C. the amount cannot be estimated at all, however probable the outflow
   D. the obligation depends wholly on a future event outside the entity's control
5. A contingent liability that is judged only 'possible' (not probable) should be: Choose every correct option.
   A. recognised as a liability in the statement of financial position
   B. ignored completely, with no disclosure
   C. disclosed by note, but not recognised as a liability
   D. recognised as a provision at its maximum possible amount
6. A company issues a £500,000 loan at a fixed annual interest rate of 5%. Explain how the annual interest is treated in the financial statements, and how this differs from an equity dividend. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 4 months (Sep-Dec) have passed, 8 months remain; M1 expense for the year = 9,600 x 4/12 = £3,200; A1 prepayment (asset) = 9,600 x 8/12 = £6,400; A1 check: 3,200 + 6,400 = 9,600.
2. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
3. [5] M1 remaining receivables = 95,000 - 3,000 = £92,000; A1 irrecoverable debts expense = £3,000 (the amount written off); M1 allowance = 92,000 x 0.04 = £3,680; A1 net receivables = 92,000 - 3,680 = £88,320; B1 correct method: the specific irrecoverable debt is written off first, and the allowance is then calculated on the remaining balance, not the original £95,000.
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
6. [4] M1 annual interest = 500,000 x 0.05 = £25,000, recognised as a finance cost expense in the statement of profit or loss and (if unpaid) an accrued liability, regardless of whether the company makes a profit; B1 the interest is a contractual obligation that must be paid whether or not the company is profitable; B2 by contrast, a dividend on equity shares is discretionary, decided by the directors/shareholders and paid only out of distributable profits, with no contractual obligation to pay it even if the company is profitable.
