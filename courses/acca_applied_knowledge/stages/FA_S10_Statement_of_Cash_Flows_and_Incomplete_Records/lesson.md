# FA_S10_Statement_of_Cash_Flows_and_Incomplete_Records - Lesson: Statement of cash flows and incomplete records

## Goal
The learner explains the structure and purpose of a statement of cash flows (excluding partnerships), and reconstructs missing figures from incomplete accounting records using mark-up, margin and control account techniques.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA.G5 - Statement of cash flows (excluding partnerships)
- FA.G6 - Incomplete records

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A company reports a healthy profit but has run out of cash. How can both be true?' Use the answer to introduce the statement of cash flows as a check profit alone can't give. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### FA.G5 Statement of cash flows (excluding partnerships)
The **statement of cash flows** shows actual cash movements in the period, classified into: **operating activities** (cash generated from the core trading operations - often starting from profit before tax and adjusting for non-cash items such as depreciation, and for changes in working capital such as inventory, receivables and payables); **investing activities** (cash spent on/received from non-current assets and investments, e.g. buying or selling equipment); and **financing activities** (cash from/to owners and lenders, e.g. issuing shares, raising or repaying loans, paying dividends). Unlike profit, cash flow is not affected by accruals-based timing differences, so it shows whether a profitable business is actually generating (or consuming) cash - a business can be profitable yet cash-poor if profit is tied up in growing inventory and receivables, or in servicing debt.

#### FA.G6 Incomplete records
**Incomplete records** techniques reconstruct missing figures when a business has not kept full double-entry records (common for a small trader). **Mark-up** is profit as a percentage of *cost* (cost + mark-up% x cost = selling price); **margin** is profit as a percentage of *selling price* (selling price - margin% x selling price = cost). Missing figures (e.g. sales, purchases, drawings, or a closing cash/bank balance) are found using control accounts (e.g. a total receivables account, with opening balance + credit sales - cash received - irrecoverable debts written off = closing balance, solved for whichever figure is missing) or the accounting equation (opening net assets + capital introduced - drawings + profit = closing net assets, solved for profit if everything else is known). *Worked example, margin/mark-up*: cost of sales is £60,000 and the business prices at a 25% margin (profit is 25% of selling price, so cost is 75% of selling price): selling price = 60,000 / 0.75 = £{60000 / 0.75:,.2f}; gross profit = {60000 / 0.75:,.2f} - 60,000 = £{60000 / 0.75 - 60000:,.2f}. *Worked example, control account*: opening trade receivables £18,000, credit sales for the year £142,000, cash received from receivables £136,000; closing receivables = 18,000 + 142,000 - 136,000 = £{18000 + 142000 - 136000:,}.
