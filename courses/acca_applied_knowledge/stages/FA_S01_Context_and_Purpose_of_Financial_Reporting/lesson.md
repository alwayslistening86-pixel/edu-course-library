# FA_S01_Context_and_Purpose_of_Financial_Reporting - Lesson: The context and purpose of financial reporting

## Goal
The learner explains the scope and purpose of financial statements, who uses them and why, the main elements of financial reports, the regulatory framework, and the duties of those charged with governance.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA.A1 - Scope and purpose of financial statements for external reporting
- FA.A2 - Stakeholders' needs
- FA.A3 - Main elements of financial reports
- FA.A4 - Regulatory framework
- FA.A5 - Duties and responsibilities of those charged with governance

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A bank manager, a supplier and the tax authority all want to see the same company's accounts. Do they read them the same way?' Use the answer to introduce financial statement users. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### FA.A1 Scope and purpose of financial statements for external reporting
**Financial statements** communicate the financial performance and position of a business to those outside it who cannot demand information directly. The main statements are the **statement of profit or loss** (revenue, expenses and profit for a period), the **statement of financial position** (assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time), the **statement of cash flows** (cash movements during a period), and the **statement of changes in equity**. Their overall purpose is to help users make economic decisions - whether to invest, lend, supply on credit, or (for management) how the business is performing.

#### FA.A2 Stakeholders' needs
**Stakeholders' needs** differ: **shareholders/investors** want to assess returns and risk, to decide whether to buy, hold or sell shares; **lenders/finance providers** want to assess whether the business can repay debt and interest (creditworthiness); **suppliers** want to know whether a customer can pay for goods supplied on credit; **customers** may want assurance of the business's ability to continue supplying them (going concern); **employees** want reassurance about job security and the ability to pay wages; **government/tax authorities** need information to assess tax and for economic statistics; the **public** may be interested in a business's contribution to the local economy and environment; and **management** need detailed information for running the business day to day (though management's own detailed reports go well beyond the statutory financial statements - see the MA syllabus).

#### FA.A3 Main elements of financial reports
**Main elements of financial reports**: **assets** (resources controlled by the entity, from past events, from which future economic benefits are expected to flow - e.g. cash, inventory, property); **liabilities** (present obligations arising from past events, expected to result in an outflow of economic benefit - e.g. trade payables, loans); **equity** (the residual interest in the assets after deducting liabilities - the owners' stake); **income** (increases in economic benefits, e.g. revenue and gains); **expenses** (decreases in economic benefits, e.g. cost of sales, operating costs). The fundamental **accounting equation** links three of these: Assets = Liabilities + Equity (Capital).

#### FA.A4 Regulatory framework
**Regulatory framework**: company law sets minimum legal requirements for keeping accounting records and preparing and filing financial statements. **International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS Accounting Standards)**, issued by the **International Accounting Standards Board (IASB)**, set out the recognition, measurement, presentation and disclosure requirements for specific topics (e.g. IAS 2 Inventories, IAS 16 Property, Plant and Equipment) so that financial statements are prepared consistently and are comparable between companies and over time. National regulators and stock exchange listing rules add further requirements for certain entities (e.g. listed companies).

#### FA.A5 Duties and responsibilities of those charged with governance
**Duties and responsibilities of those charged with governance**: directors are legally responsible for keeping proper accounting records, preparing financial statements that give a true and fair view, safeguarding the company's assets, and preventing and detecting fraud and error. An **external audit** (by an independent auditor, for entities required to have one) provides an independent opinion on whether the financial statements give a true and fair view and comply with the applicable financial reporting framework - the auditor does not prepare the financial statements, and an unqualified (clean) audit opinion is not a guarantee against all error or fraud, only reasonable assurance that the statements are free from material misstatement.
