# FA_S12_Interpretation_of_Financial_Statements - Test: Interpretation of financial statements

## Test items
1. Current assets are £180,000 (including inventory of £70,000) and current liabilities are £120,000. Calculate the current ratio and the quick (acid test) ratio. [4 marks]
2. Operating profit is £84,000, equity is £360,000 and non-current liabilities are £140,000. Calculate ROCE. [2 marks]
3. Trade receivables are £42,000 and revenue is £310,000. Calculate the receivables collection period in days (to the nearest whole day). [2 marks]
4. A company's gearing ratio has risen sharply because it took out a large new loan. Which is a direct consequence? Choose every correct option.
   A. Lower financial risk
   B. Higher fixed interest commitments and higher financial risk, especially if profits fall
   C. Guaranteed higher profit
   D. No effect on the statement of financial position
5. Company X has a current ratio of 2.5:1 but a quick ratio of only 0.6:1. Explain what this combination most likely indicates. [3 marks]
6. Which ratio would best help assess whether a company relies heavily on debt finance and therefore carries higher financial risk? Choose every correct option.
   A. Gross profit margin
   B. Inventory holding period
   C. Gearing ratio
   D. Receivables collection period

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 current ratio = 180,000/120,000; A1 = 1.50:1; M1 quick ratio = (180,000-70,000)/120,000; A1 = 0.92:1.
2. [2] M1 ROCE = 84,000/(360,000+140,000) x 100; A1 = 16.8%.
3. [2] M1 42,000/310,000 x 365; A1 = 49 days.
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B3 the large gap between the two ratios indicates that a high proportion of current assets is tied up in inventory rather than cash or receivables; the current ratio alone therefore overstates the company's real short-term liquidity, since inventory is the least liquid current asset and may not be quickly convertible to cash to meet immediate liabilities.
6. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
