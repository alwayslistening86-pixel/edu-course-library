# FA_S12_Interpretation_of_Financial_Statements - Lesson: Interpretation of financial statements

## Goal
The learner explains why financial statements are analysed, calculates and interprets profitability, liquidity, efficiency and gearing ratios, and draws a balanced, evidenced conclusion from a set of ratios.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA.I1 - Importance and purpose of financial statement analysis
- FA.I2 - Ratios
- FA.I3 - Analysis of financial statements

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Two companies report the exact same profit. Does that mean they are equally good investments?' Use the answer to introduce ratio analysis as a way of looking beneath a single number. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### FA.I1 Importance and purpose of financial statement analysis
**Interpretation of financial statements** goes beyond the raw figures to assess performance, position and prospects - used by investors (return and risk), lenders (creditworthiness), management (benchmarking against budget, prior periods or competitors) and others. Ratios only become meaningful in **comparison**: against the same company's prior period(s) (trend analysis), against a budget or target, or against similar companies/industry averages; a ratio in isolation tells a limited story, and further explanation (context, one-off items, changes in accounting policy) is usually needed before drawing conclusions.

#### FA.I2 Ratios
**Key ratios**. **Profitability**: gross profit margin = gross profit/revenue x 100; operating profit margin = operating profit/revenue x 100; **return on capital employed (ROCE)** = operating profit / (total equity + non-current liabilities) x 100, an overall measure of how efficiently capital is used. **Liquidity**: **current ratio** = current assets / current liabilities (a rough guide to short-term solvency, often benchmarked around 1.5-2:1 though this varies by industry); **quick (acid test) ratio** = (current assets - inventory) / current liabilities (excludes inventory, the least liquid current asset, for a stricter test). **Efficiency**: inventory holding period (days) = inventory/cost of sales x 365; receivables collection period (days) = trade receivables/revenue x 365; payables payment period (days) = trade payables/purchases (or cost of sales) x 365. **Gearing**: gearing ratio = debt / (debt + equity) x 100 (or debt/equity x 100), measuring reliance on debt finance - higher gearing means higher fixed interest commitments and higher financial risk, especially if profits fall. *Worked example*: revenue £400,000; gross profit £150,000; operating profit £60,000; current assets £120,000 (including inventory £45,000); current liabilities £80,000; equity £250,000; non-current liabilities £100,000. Gross margin = 150,000/400,000 x 100 = {150000 / 400000 * 100:.0f}%; operating margin = 60,000/400,000 x 100 = {60000 / 400000 * 100:.0f}%; ROCE = 60,000/(250,000+100,000) x 100 = {60000 / (250000 + 100000) * 100:.1f}%; current ratio = 120,000/80,000 = {120000 / 80000:.2f}:1; quick ratio = (120,000-45,000)/80,000 = {(120000 - 45000) / 80000:.2f}:1.

#### FA.I3 Analysis of financial statements
**Analysis of financial statements** combines several ratios into a coherent story, since each on its own can mislead: e.g. rising revenue with a falling gross margin might mean the company is cutting prices to win volume (a strategic choice, not necessarily bad, but worth flagging); a strong current ratio driven mainly by slow-moving inventory (rather than cash or receivables) may overstate real short-term liquidity - which is exactly why the quick ratio strips inventory out. A rising receivables collection period alongside static revenue may signal worsening credit control or a struggling customer base, increasing the risk of irrecoverable debts. Good interpretation states the figures, explains a plausible cause, and notes what further information (industry norms, management commentary, subsequent events) would confirm or challenge that explanation - never a bare number with no interpretation.
