## Test items
1. Budgeted sales are 12,000 units. Opening inventory is 1,500 units and the company wants closing inventory of 1,100 units. Calculate the production budget. [2 marks]
2. An original budget for 2,000 units shows variable cost £30,000 and fixed cost £12,000. Actual output was 2,300 units. Prepare the flexed budget cost. [4 marks]
3. Which of these is the principal budget factor in most organisations? Choose every correct option.
   A. Direct labour hours available
   B. Sales demand
   C. Cash available
   D. Machine capacity
4. The main purpose of a cash budget is to: Choose every correct option.
   A. calculate the year's total profit
   B. identify the timing of cash shortfalls or surpluses so finance can be arranged in advance
   C. value closing inventory
   D. set the selling price
5. When flexing a budget to a higher activity level, which cost behaves as unchanged in total? Choose every correct option.
   A. Variable cost
   B. Fixed cost
   C. Semi-variable cost
   D. Direct material cost
6. Original budget (1,000 units): direct material £6,000, direct labour £4,000, fixed overhead £9,000. Prepare a flexed budget for actual output of 1,150 units. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 production = 12,000 + 1,100 - 1,500; A1 = 11600 units.
2. [4] M1 variable cost/unit = 30,000/2,000 = £15.00; M1 flexed variable cost = 15.00 x 2,300 = £34,500; A1 flexed fixed cost = £12,000 (unchanged); A1 total flexed budget cost = £46,500.
3. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
6. [4] M1 material/unit £6.00, flexed = £6,900; M1 labour/unit £4.00, flexed = £4,600; A1 fixed overhead unchanged £9,000; A1 total flexed budget = £20,500.
