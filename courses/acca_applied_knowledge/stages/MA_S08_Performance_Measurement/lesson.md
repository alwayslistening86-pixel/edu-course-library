# MA_S08_Performance_Measurement - Lesson: Performance measurement

## Goal
The learner calculates and interprets financial performance measures (profitability, ROCE, RI), uses non-financial and balanced-scorecard measures, and explains how to reduce costs and enhance value without harming quality.

## Syllabus items taught here
- MA.F1 - Performance measurement overview
- MA.F2 - Performance measurement - application
- MA.F3 - Cost reductions and value enhancement
- MA.F4 - Monitoring performance and reporting

## How to teach this
Ask whether a manager who cuts a department's budget in half, and whose customer complaints then double, should be praised for 'good cost control'. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### MA.F1 Performance measurement overview
Performance measurement compares actual results against a target (budget, prior period, or a competitor/benchmark), for control and for evaluating managers, divisions and the whole organisation. Good performance measures should be linked to the organisation's objectives, understood by those being measured, and resistant to being 'gamed'. Purely financial measures can encourage short-termism (e.g. cutting essential maintenance to hit this year's cost target), which is why non-financial measures are also used.

#### MA.F2 Performance measurement - application
**Performance measurement - application**. Financial measures: profitability ratios (gross profit margin = gross profit/revenue x 100; operating profit margin = operating profit/revenue x 100); **Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)** = operating profit / capital employed x 100, the classic overall measure of how efficiently capital is used; **Residual Income (RI)** = controllable profit - (capital employed x an imputed cost of capital), which, unlike ROCE, encourages a division to accept any project earning more than the cost of capital, since ROCE can make an already-high-ROCE division reject a good project that would pull its average ROCE down. Non-financial measures: customer satisfaction, on-time delivery %, staff turnover, defect rate, market share - often leading indicators of future financial performance. The **Balanced Scorecard** (Kaplan and Norton) combines measures across four perspectives: financial, customer, internal business process, and learning and growth/innovation, so managers are not driven by financial results alone.
*Worked example*: operating profit £80,000, capital employed £400,000, cost of capital 12%. ROCE = 80,000/400,000 x 100 = {80000 / 400000 * 100:.0f}%. RI = 80,000 - (400,000 x 0.12) = 80,000 - £{400000 * 0.12:,.0f} = £{80000 - 400000 * 0.12:,.0f}.

#### MA.F3 Cost reductions and value enhancement
**Cost reductions and value enhancement**. Cost reduction is a planned, conscious effort to reduce cost without harming the product/service's essential quality or function (distinct from a haphazard cost-cutting exercise that risks damaging quality, morale or long-term capability). Value analysis/value engineering systematically examines each component/function of a product to see whether the same function can be delivered at lower cost (or a better function at the same cost), asking of each feature: does it add value the customer will pay for?

#### MA.F4 Monitoring performance and reporting
**Monitoring performance and reporting**: regular management reports compare actual against target/budget/prior period, using both financial and non-financial data, tailored to the level of management receiving them (a shop-floor supervisor needs operational detail; the board needs summarised, strategic information). Trends over several periods, and benchmarking against other divisions or competitors, give context that a single period's figure alone cannot.
