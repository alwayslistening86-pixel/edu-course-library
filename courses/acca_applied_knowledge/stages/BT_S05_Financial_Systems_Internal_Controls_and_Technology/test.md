## Test items
1. Which technology is best described as software robots carrying out repetitive, rules-based tasks such as data entry? Choose every correct option.
   A. Blockchain
   B. Robotic process automation (RPA)
   C. Cloud computing
   D. Big data
2. Explain why segregation of duties reduces the risk of fraud, and describe one limitation of internal controls generally. [4 marks]
3. Which are recognised benefits of cloud computing for a business's financial systems? Choose every correct option.
   A. Remote access to data and systems
   B. Reduced need for in-house IT infrastructure
   C. Elimination of all cybersecurity risk
   D. Easier scaling of capacity up or down
4. A criminal gains access to a company's payment system by tricking an employee into revealing their login details through a fake email. This is an example of: Choose every correct option.
   A. ransomware
   B. phishing
   C. blockchain
   D. segregation of duties
5. Explain one risk of an organisation becoming over-reliant on AI-generated outputs without adequate human review. [3 marks]
6. Which is a physical (rather than arithmetic or authorisation) internal control? Choose every correct option.
   A. A bank reconciliation
   B. A requirement for the finance director's sign-off on payments above £50,000
   C. Restricting access to the warehouse to authorised staff with swipe cards
   D. A monthly budget-versus-actual review

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. [4] B2 no single person controls a whole transaction from authorisation through custody to recording, so committing and concealing a fraud alone becomes harder, and errors by one person are more likely to be caught by another; B2 limitation: controls can be overridden by collusion between two or more staff, or by senior management override, and no control system gives absolute (only reasonable) assurance.
3. Correct: A, B, D (exactly these options, no others)
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B3 AI models can make systematic errors or embed bias present in their training data or historical patterns, and can be confidently wrong (e.g. plausible but inaccurate figures or conclusions); without human review, such errors can be adopted at scale before being detected, leading to poor decisions, financial loss, or reporting errors.
6. Correct: C (exactly these options, no others)
