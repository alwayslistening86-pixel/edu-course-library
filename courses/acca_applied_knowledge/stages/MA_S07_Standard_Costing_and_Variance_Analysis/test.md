# MA_S07_Standard_Costing_and_Variance_Analysis - Test: Standard costing and variance analysis

## How to run this

## Test items
1. Standard material cost is 4 kg @ £6.00/kg per unit. 2,000 units are made using 8,300 kg costing £48,470. Calculate the material price and usage variances, stating whether each is favourable or adverse. [6 marks]
2. Budgeted fixed overhead is £60,000 for a budgeted output of 3,000 units (OAR £20/unit). Actual fixed overhead incurred is £63,000 and actual output is 3,200 units. Calculate the fixed overhead expenditure variance and the fixed overhead volume variance. [5 marks]
3. Standard profit per unit is £15. Budgeted sales are 4,000 units; actual sales are 3,850 units. Calculate the sales volume variance. [3 marks]
4. A favourable material price variance most likely arose because: Choose every correct option.
   A. more material was used per unit than standard
   B. the actual price paid per kg was lower than the standard price
   C. output was higher than budgeted
   D. labour worked more efficiently than standard
5. In the profit reconciliation from budgeted to actual profit, an adverse variance is: Choose every correct option.
   A. added to budgeted profit
   B. subtracted from budgeted profit
   C. ignored
   D. always caused by a price change
6. Budgeted profit is £50,000. Variances are: sales volume £3,000 favourable, sales price £1,200 adverse, material price £800 adverse, material usage £600 favourable, labour rate £400 favourable, labour efficiency £900 adverse. Reconcile to actual profit. [4 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] M1 actual price = 48,470/8,300 = £5.84/kg; M1 price variance = (6.00 - 5.84) x 8,300 = £1,330; A1 favourable; M1 standard quantity for 2,000 units = 8,000 kg; M1 usage variance = (8,000 - 8,300) x 6.00 = £-1,800; A1 adverse (more material used than the standard allows).
2. [5] M1 expenditure variance = budgeted 60,000 - actual 63,000 = £-3,000; A1 adverse (overspent by £3,000); M1 volume variance = (actual output - budgeted output) x OAR = (3,200 - 3,000) x 20; A1 = £4,000 favourable (more units produced than budgeted, so more overhead absorbed); B1 the two variances measure different things: expenditure compares budgeted and actual spend, while volume compares actual and budgeted output valued at the OAR.
3. [3] M1 volume difference = 3,850 - 4,000 = -150 units; M1 variance = -150 x £15; A1 = £-2,250 adverse (fewer units sold than budgeted, at standard profit per unit).
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
6. [4] B1 favourable variances (sales volume £3,000, material usage £600, labour rate £400) are added; B1 adverse variances (sales price £1,200, material price £800, labour efficiency £900) are subtracted; M1 net variance = 3000 - 1200 - 800 + 600 + 400 - 900 = £1,100; A1 actual profit = 50,000 + 1,100 = £51,100.
