## Test items
1. An ageing population is best classified as which type of external factor? Choose every correct option.
   A. Political
   B. Social/demographic
   C. Technological
   D. Microeconomic
2. Which are Porter's Five Forces? Choose every correct option.
   A. Threat of new entrants
   B. Bargaining power of suppliers
   C. Rate of inflation
   D. Intensity of rivalry among competitors
3. Explain, with an example, how technological change can be both a threat and an opportunity for the same industry. [4 marks]
4. Which best describes 'sustainability' in a business context? Choose every correct option.
   A. Maximising this year's profit regardless of long-term impact
   B. Meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet their own
   C. Avoiding all use of natural resources
   D. A synonym for corporate social responsibility reporting only
5. A market has just two large firms, each aware that a price cut by one will likely be matched by the other. Name this market structure and explain one pricing implication of it. [3 marks]
6. Rising customer bargaining power in Porter's Five Forces is most likely when: Choose every correct option.
   A. there are many small customers and few suppliers
   B. customers are few, large, and can easily switch to a rival supplier
   C. switching suppliers is very costly for the customer
   D. the product is highly differentiated

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
2. Correct: A, B, D (exactly these options, no others)
3. [4] B2 threat: an established business model can be disrupted or made obsolete by new technology (e.g. physical music/video retailers displaced by streaming); B2 opportunity: the same technology lets an adaptable firm reach new customers or cut costs (e.g. a retailer building its own e-commerce and streaming/subscription offering); credit any valid, developed example.
4. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B1 oligopoly; B2 firms are interdependent, so price cuts are often matched (risking a price war that reduces industry profit), which can instead encourage non-price competition (branding, quality, innovation) or tacit collusion on price.
6. Correct: B (exactly these options, no others)
