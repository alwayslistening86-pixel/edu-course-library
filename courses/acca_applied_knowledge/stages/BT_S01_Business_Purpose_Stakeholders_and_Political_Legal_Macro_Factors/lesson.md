# BT_S01_Business_Purpose_Stakeholders_and_Political_Legal_Macro_Factors - Lesson: Business purpose, stakeholders, and political/legal/macroeconomic factors

## Goal
The learner classifies business organisations and their purposes, identifies internal, connected and external stakeholders and how to manage them, and explains political/legal and macroeconomic influences on business.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BT.A1 - Purpose and types of business organisation
- BT.A2 - Stakeholders in business organisations
- BT.A3 - Political and legal factors affecting business
- BT.A4 - Macroeconomic factors

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A supermarket, a hospital trust and a corner shop are all businesses of a sort. Do they exist for the same reason, and do they answer to the same people?' Use the answers to introduce organisational purpose and stakeholders. Have the learner predict the result of every example before running it, and run code for real whenever they can.

#### BT.A1 Purpose and types of business organisation
**Types of business organisation.** A **sole trader** is one individual trading alone: simple to set up, full control, but unlimited personal liability for the business's debts. A **partnership** is two or more people trading together, sharing profits and (in an ordinary partnership) unlimited joint and several liability; a **limited liability partnership (LLP)** gives partners limited liability while keeping partnership-style flexibility. A **company** is a separate legal person from its owners (shareholders), who have **limited liability** (limited to what they invested); a **private limited company** cannot offer shares to the public, a **public limited company** can. Organisations also differ by **sector**: the **private sector** (profit-seeking, owned by individuals/shareholders), the **public sector** (government-owned, funded by taxation, serving public policy goals rather than profit, e.g. the NHS), and the **third/not-for-profit sector** (charities, NGOs, mutuals, pursuing a social or charitable purpose, any surplus reinvested rather than distributed). Purpose commonly includes generating a satisfactory return for owners, but also survival, growth, and (for public/third sector bodies) delivering a service or mission within a budget.

#### BT.A2 Stakeholders in business organisations
**Stakeholders** are any individual or group with an interest in, or affected by, an organisation's activities. **Internal stakeholders** (employees, management) are part of the organisation. **Connected stakeholders** have a direct, usually contractual, relationship with it but are not inside it: shareholders, customers, suppliers, finance providers/lenders. **External stakeholders** have no direct/contractual relationship but are still affected: government, the local community, pressure groups, competitors, the general public. Mendelow's **power/interest matrix** maps stakeholders on two axes (power to affect the organisation, and interest in what it does) into four groups: **minimal effort** (low power, low interest), **keep informed** (low power, high interest), **keep satisfied** (high power, low interest), and **key players** (high power, high interest, to be actively managed and closely engaged). Stakeholders' objectives can conflict (e.g. shareholders wanting higher dividends versus employees wanting higher pay), and management must balance competing claims rather than satisfy every stakeholder fully.

#### BT.A3 Political and legal factors affecting business
**Political factors**: government stability, policy direction (e.g. taxation, trade policy, nationalisation/privatisation, industrial strategy) and international relations, which affect business confidence and investment. **Legal factors**: the legal environment sets the rules businesses must follow - contract law (enforceable agreements), employment law (minimum wage, working time, discrimination, unfair dismissal protections), consumer protection law (product safety, fair trading, consumer rights on faulty goods), health and safety law (a duty of care to employees, customers and the public), data protection law (how personal data may be collected, stored and used), and competition law (preventing anti-competitive practices such as cartels and abuse of a dominant position). Together, PEST(EL) factors are the **macro-environment** businesses cannot control but must monitor and respond to.

#### BT.A4 Macroeconomic factors
**Macroeconomic factors** are economy-wide conditions affecting all businesses in a country, largely outside any single firm's control: **economic growth** (rising GDP generally means rising demand); the **trade/business cycle** (boom, recession, slump, recovery - each phase changing demand, employment and confidence); **inflation** (a general, sustained rise in prices, eroding purchasing power and raising costs); **interest rates** (set by the central bank, affecting the cost of borrowing, consumer spending on credit, mortgage costs, and the exchange rate); **unemployment** (affecting both the labour supply available to employers and overall consumer demand); **exchange rates** (affecting the price of imports/exports and the competitiveness of exporters/importers); and **government fiscal and monetary policy** (taxation, government spending, interest rates and money supply, used to manage the economy, e.g. to control inflation or stimulate growth).
