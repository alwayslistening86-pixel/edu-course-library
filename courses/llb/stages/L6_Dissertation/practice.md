# L6_Dissertation - Practice: Dissertation — Independent legal research and sustained argument

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Turn the vague topic "problems with the law on economic duress" into a narrow, contestable research question with defined scope.
2. Take two sources that reach opposite conclusions on a topic covered elsewhere in this course (for example, whether Yam Seng-style good faith is genuinely settled) and write a short paragraph that synthesises rather than merely summarises them in sequence.
3. Justify, in a paragraph, why a doctrinal (rather than comparative or socio-legal) methodology suits a question about whether English contract law should recognise a general duty of good faith.
4. Identify the single strongest counter-argument to a position of the learner's choosing on any topic covered in this course, and write a paragraph addressing it fairly before responding.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
