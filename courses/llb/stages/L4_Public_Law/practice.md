# L4_Public_Law - Practice: Public Law — Constitutional principles and judicial review

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Explain whether a court could, consistently with parliamentary sovereignty, refuse to apply a validly enacted statute on the ground that it is grossly unjust.
2. Identify which GCHQ ground(s) apply where a licensing authority refuses a licence for a reason not listed in the empowering statute.
3. Apply legitimate expectation to a case where a local authority's published policy promised consultation before closing a service, and it closed the service without consulting.
4. Explain the remedy or remedies a claimant should seek where a decision was procedurally unfair but the underlying power to make it was lawful.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
