## Test scenario
A country's constitution guarantees "equality before the law," but for decades its courts interpreted this narrowly, upholding laws that excluded women from certain professions on the ground that the guarantee only required equal treatment of "similarly situated" people, and the courts considered men and women not similarly situated for this purpose. A new generation of judges wants to overturn this interpretation. Analyse this scenario from a Hart positivist perspective, a Dworkinian perspective, and a feminist legal theory perspective, explaining what each would say about whether the earlier interpretation was ever "the law," and about the legitimacy of the new judges departing from it.
