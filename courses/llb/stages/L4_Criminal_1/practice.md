# L4_Criminal_1 - Practice: Criminal Law I — General principles: actus reus, mens rea and homicide

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Apply actus reus to: (a) a lifeguard who watches a swimmer drown without intervening, and (b) a driver whose car rolls forward after a sudden, genuine seizure.
2. Apply R v Woollin to decide whether a defendant who plants a bomb with a warning, expecting evacuation, has the mens rea for murder if someone dies because the warning failed.
3. A defendant is subjected to prolonged domestic abuse and kills their abuser during a sudden confrontation. Analyse for loss of control.
4. A builder ignores an obvious, serious risk on site and a worker dies. Analyse for gross negligence manslaughter.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
