# L5_Commercial_Law - Practice: Commercial Law — Sale of goods, agency and international sale essentials

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Apply s.14(2) and s.14(3) SGA 1979 to the corroding-components scenario, identifying which implied term is breached and why.
2. A car dealer sells a car they hold on a voidable title (having paid with a bounced cheque) to an innocent third party before the true owner rescinds. Apply s.23 SGA and Caldwell.
3. An employee with no express authority to sign supplier contracts has always been allowed to do so by their manager without objection. Apply apparent authority to a new contract the employee signs.
4. Explain the practical difference to a buyer between an FOB and a CIF contract if the goods are damaged in transit through no fault of either party.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
