# L5_Commercial_Law - Lesson: Commercial Law — Sale of goods, agency and international sale essentials

## Goal
The learner applies the Sale of Goods Act 1979 implied terms and passing-of-property rules, the nemo dat exceptions, and the law of agency to a commercial problem.

## Syllabus items taught here
- L5-Commercial-1 - Sale of goods: implied terms: Apply ss.12-15 Sale of Goods Act 1979 to a defective-goods problem
- L5-Commercial-2 - Passing of property and risk: Apply ss.16-20 SGA 1979 to decide when property and risk pass
- L5-Commercial-3 - Nemo dat and its exceptions: Apply the nemo dat rule and its statutory exceptions to a disputed-title problem
- L5-Commercial-4 - Agency: creation and authority: Distinguish actual and apparent authority and apply Freeman & Lockyer
- L5-Commercial-5 - Agency: duties and liability: Apply an agent's fiduciary duties and the undisclosed principal doctrine
- L5-Commercial-6 - International sale essentials: Explain FOB and CIF trade terms and the role of the bill of lading

## How to teach this
Open with: a business buys 200 units of a specialist component "of merchantable quality, fit for use in outdoor installations" from a supplier's catalogue. On delivery, a third of the units corrode within weeks. Ask what the buyer can do, and against whom.

#### L5-Commercial-1 Sale of goods: implied terms
The **Sale of Goods Act 1979** (as amended, and largely mirrored for consumer sales by the Consumer Rights Act 2015) implies terms into contracts for the sale of goods even where the parties said nothing explicit: that the seller has the **right to sell** the goods (s.12), that goods sold by description **correspond with that description** (s.13), that goods are of **satisfactory quality** (s.14(2) — fit for all purposes such goods are commonly supplied for, free from minor defects, safe and durable, judged by what a reasonable person would regard as satisfactory given the price and description), and that goods are **reasonably fit for any particular purpose** the buyer makes known to the seller (s.14(3)). These are treated as **conditions** for most breaches (allowing rejection), though s.15A lets a non-consumer buyer be limited to a warranty-only remedy where the breach is so slight that rejection would be unreasonable.

#### L5-Commercial-2 Passing of property and risk
Under ss.16-18 SGA 1979, property (ownership) in **unascertained goods** does not pass until the goods are ascertained, and the parties' intention (assisted by default rules of thumb where intention is not expressed, ss.18 rules 1-5) governs when it passes; for **specific goods**, property generally passes when the parties intend it to, often on contract formation for unconditional sales of specific goods in a deliverable state. **Risk** generally passes with property (s.20) unless otherwise agreed, which matters for who bears loss if goods are damaged before delivery — commercial contracts frequently displace this default by express agreement or trade terms such as FOB or CIF.

#### L5-Commercial-3 Nemo dat and its exceptions
The default rule, *nemo dat quod non habet* (you cannot pass better title than you have), protects the true owner, but is subject to commercially important statutory exceptions protecting innocent third-party purchasers: sale by a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent (Factors Act 1889 s.2), sale under a **voidable title** before the seller's title is rescinded (SGA s.23; *Car & Universal Finance Co Ltd v Caldwell* [1965] 1 QB 525), and sale by a **buyer in possession** (SGA s.25) or a **seller in possession** after an earlier sale (SGA s.24). These exceptions exist to protect the security of commercial transactions at the expense of the original owner's strict property rights.

#### L5-Commercial-4 Agency: creation and authority
An **agent** acts on behalf of a **principal**, creating legal relations directly between the principal and a third party. **Actual authority** can be express or implied from the agent's position or conduct. **Apparent (ostensible) authority** can bind a principal even where actual authority was withheld or withdrawn, where the principal's own conduct represented to the third party that the agent had authority and the third party reasonably relied on that representation (*Freeman & Lockyer v Buckhurst Park Properties (Mangal) Ltd* [1964] 2 QB 480). An agent acting within authority generally is not personally liable on the contract made; one acting outside both actual and apparent authority may be personally liable for breach of warranty of authority.

#### L5-Commercial-5 Agency: duties and liability
An agent owes strict **fiduciary duties** to the principal: to act within authority, avoid conflicts of interest, not take a secret profit or bribe, and account for property and money received on the principal's behalf. The **undisclosed principal** doctrine, a distinctive feature of English commercial law, allows a third party who contracted with an agent not knowing of the principal's existence to nonetheless, in some circumstances, sue or be sued by the undisclosed principal directly, provided the agent had actual authority and the contract's terms do not exclude the possibility of an undisclosed principal.

#### L5-Commercial-6 International sale essentials
International sale contracts commonly use standard trade terms allocating cost, risk and responsibility between seller and buyer. Under **FOB (Free On Board)**, the buyer arranges and pays for carriage, and risk passes to the buyer on shipment. Under **CIF (Cost, Insurance, Freight)**, the seller arranges and pays for carriage and insurance to the named destination, but CIF is a "sale of documents": the seller performs by tendering the correct shipping documents (bill of lading, insurance policy, invoice), and risk still passes on shipment even though the seller organised carriage to destination. The **bill of lading** performs three functions: a receipt for the goods, evidence of the contract of carriage, and — crucially — a **document of title**, letting constructive possession of the goods be transferred by transferring the paper document.
