# S05_Macbeth_Themes_Characters_and_the_Exam_Question - Lesson: Macbeth: themes of ambition, kingship and guilt, the play's key characters, and the Paper 1 extract question

## Goal
The learner can discuss the play's major themes and characters with textual support and write a Paper 1 Section A Shakespeare response using an extract and whole-play evidence.

## Syllabus items taught here
- MAC.TH1 - Macbeth theme: ambition: track how ambition drives and destroys Macbeth across the play
- MAC.TH2 - Macbeth theme: kingship and power: contrast good and bad kingship as the play presents them
- MAC.TH3 - Macbeth theme: guilt and the supernatural: explain how guilt is dramatised through the supernatural and psychological breakdown
- MAC.CH1 - Macbeth character: Macbeth: trace Macbeth's change from soldier to tyrant with textual support
- MAC.CH2 - Macbeth character: Lady Macbeth: trace Lady Macbeth's change from driving force to broken figure
- MAC.CH3 - Macbeth character: Banquo, Macduff and minor characters: explain the dramatic function of the play's other key characters
- MAC.EX - Macbeth: the Paper 1 extract-based exam question and a model paragraph: answer an extract-plus-whole-play Shakespeare question in the AQA format

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to pick one word to describe Macbeth at the play's start and one word for the end (soldier/tyrant is a common pair), then ask what caused the change -- use their answer to launch into the character and theme content below.

#### MAC.TH1 Macbeth theme: ambition
Ambition is the engine of the whole tragedy: Macbeth's 'vaulting ambition' (his own phrase) exists before the witches speak, and their prophecy and Lady Macbeth's pressure only accelerate what is already present. The play presents ambition as corrupting when unchecked by moral restraint: each act of ambition-driven violence requires another to protect it, trapping Macbeth in an escalating cycle he cannot exit. Teach the argument that Shakespeare is not simply condemning ambition itself (Malcolm and Macduff are also ambitious for a just cause) but ambition pursued through illegitimate means, against the natural and moral order.

#### MAC.TH2 Macbeth theme: kingship and power
The play contrasts illegitimate kingship (Macbeth's, gained by murder and maintained by fear and further murder) with legitimate kingship (Duncan's and later Malcolm's, associated with order, generosity and divine sanction). Duncan is shown as a trusting, generous king; Macbeth's reign brings darkness, paranoia and tyranny, and Scotland itself is described as suffering under him. Malcolm's restoration at the end reasserts the idea that true kingship is granted, not seized, and that seized power is inherently unstable. Teach the contrast as a structural argument: the play's shape itself argues that illegitimate power collapses.

#### MAC.TH3 Macbeth theme: guilt and the supernatural
Guilt is dramatised through hallucination and the supernatural rather than simply stated: Macbeth's dagger vision, Banquo's ghost, and Lady Macbeth's sleepwalking and hand-washing all externalise psychological guilt as something almost physically real. The witches represent a supernatural force that tempts but does not compel -- Macbeth and Lady Macbeth choose to act on the prophecy, which keeps them morally responsible rather than mere puppets of fate. Teach the distinction between temptation (external, supernatural) and choice (internal, human) as central to how the play assigns moral responsibility.

#### MAC.CH1 Macbeth character: Macbeth
Macbeth begins as a genuinely brave, loyal soldier ('brave Macbeth', in the captain's report) and ends as a tyrant feared and abandoned by his own allies. His soliloquies reveal a conscience that never fully disappears -- he hesitates before Duncan's murder and is horrified by his own hand afterwards -- which makes him a tragic rather than simply villainous figure: someone destroyed partly by forces around him and partly by his own choices. Teach the learner to track this internal conflict (ambition versus conscience) as the throughline of every Macbeth-focused essay, rather than treating him as flatly evil from the start.

#### MAC.CH2 Macbeth character: Lady Macbeth
Lady Macbeth begins as the more ruthless and decisive partner, calling on spirits to strip her of feminine compassion and openly mocking Macbeth's hesitation, yet by Act 5 she is destroyed by the guilt she once dismissed, reduced to sleepwalking and obsessive hand-washing before her death. This arc subverts the audience's first impression of her as simply cruel, revealing instead a mind that suppressed conscience rather than lacked it. Teach the contrast between her forceful early language ('unsex me here') and her fragmented, broken speech in the sleepwalking scene as direct evidence of this psychological collapse.

#### MAC.CH3 Macbeth character: Banquo, Macduff and minor characters
Banquo is Macbeth's foil: also tempted by the witches' prophecy, he resists acting on it, which highlights Macbeth's choice to act on his own temptation as a moral failing rather than an inevitability. Macduff represents legitimate, righteous opposition; his family's murder gives him personal motivation alongside political duty, and he becomes the instrument of Macbeth's downfall (crucially, as a man 'not born' in the natural way, fulfilling the prophecy). Duncan, though he dies early, represents the legitimate, generous kingship the rest of the play measures Macbeth's tyranny against. Teach these figures as points of contrast that sharpen what the play says about Macbeth himself.

#### MAC.EX Macbeth: the Paper 1 extract-based exam question and a model paragraph
The question gives a printed extract and asks something like 'Starting with this extract, explore how Shakespeare presents [ambition/guilt/kingship] in Macbeth', requiring analysis of the extract first, then wider references to the rest of the play. Model opening: 'In this extract, Shakespeare presents Macbeth's ambition as already dangerously active before the witches even speak, shown through his brief, clipped response, "two truths are told" -- a line whose brevity suggests his mind is already racing ahead.' Then move outward: 'This mirrors his fuller admission later of "vaulting ambition", showing the trait as consistent rather than newly created by prophecy.' Teach the pattern: extract detail first, then a linked whole-play reference, repeated for each point, always closing with technical accuracy in mind for the 4 AO4 marks.
