# S10_An_Inspector_Calls_Themes_Characters_and_the_Exam_Question - Practice: An Inspector Calls: themes of responsibility, class and generational change, the play's key characters, and the Paper 2 exam question

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Write one sentence explaining why the Inspector's name might suggest 'ghoul' and how that connects to his dramatic function.
2. Compare Sheila's and her parents' reactions once they believe the Inspector was not real.
3. Explain, in your own words (no quotation), what the shift in stage-direction lighting from soft to hard represents.
4. Write the opening two sentences of an essay on 'How does Priestley present responsibility in An Inspector Calls?', analysing a short invented extract line in the play's style.
5. List three pieces of evidence (close paraphrase only, no quotation) you would use for an essay on class, one from each act.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
