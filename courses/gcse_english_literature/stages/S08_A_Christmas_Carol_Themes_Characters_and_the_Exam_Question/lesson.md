# S08_A_Christmas_Carol_Themes_Characters_and_the_Exam_Question - Lesson: A Christmas Carol: themes of greed, poverty and family, the novella's key characters, and the Paper 1 extract question

## Goal
The learner can discuss the novella's major themes and characters with textual support and write a Paper 1 Section B response using an extract and whole-novella evidence.

## Syllabus items taught here
- ACC.TH1 - A Christmas Carol theme: greed and redemption: track Scrooge's moral journey with textual support
- ACC.TH2 - A Christmas Carol theme: poverty and social responsibility: explain Dickens's social message using the Cratchits and the two children Ignorance and Want
- ACC.TH3 - A Christmas Carol theme: family and the spirit of Christmas: explain how family and Christmas are used as moral contrasts to Scrooge
- ACC.CH1 - A Christmas Carol character: Scrooge: trace Scrooge's change from miser to reformed man with textual support
- ACC.CH2 - A Christmas Carol character: the Cratchits, especially Bob and Tiny Tim: explain the Cratchits' dramatic and thematic function
- ACC.CH3 - A Christmas Carol character: the three spirits and Marley: explain the dramatic function of each supernatural visitor
- ACC.EX - A Christmas Carol: the Paper 1 extract-based exam question and a model paragraph: answer an extract-plus-whole-novel 19th-century novel question in the AQA format

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: is Scrooge a villain, or someone who has simply forgotten how to feel? Their answer opens directly into the theme and character work below.

#### ACC.TH1 A Christmas Carol theme: greed and redemption
Scrooge's greed is presented not as simple evil but as a chosen, learned response to earlier disappointment (Belle's departure, fear of poverty), which is precisely why the novella insists redemption is possible: if greed can be learned, it can also be unlearned. The structure supports this argument directly, moving Scrooge from miser to generous benefactor through forced confrontation with his own past, present and possible future. Teach the argument that Dickens presents redemption as requiring both feeling (Scrooge's genuine distress and joy) and action (his concrete generosity in Stave 5) -- feeling alone would not be enough.

#### ACC.TH2 A Christmas Carol theme: poverty and social responsibility
Dickens contrasts the Cratchits, materially poor but rich in love and gratitude, with wealthy but joyless Scrooge, arguing that moral worth and material wealth are not the same thing, while also insisting that the wealthy have a real, urgent responsibility to the poor -- most directly through Ignorance and Want, and the charity collectors' plea. The novella does not romanticise poverty as noble suffering; it presents specific, urgent harms (Tiny Tim's fragile health, the risk of his death) that only social and individual action can prevent. Teach this as Dickens using an entertaining ghost story to deliver a direct social campaigning message aimed at his wealthier readers.

#### ACC.TH3 A Christmas Carol theme: family and the spirit of Christmas
The Cratchit family and Fred's household represent warmth, forgiveness and togetherness despite hardship or being mocked, standing in deliberate contrast to Scrooge's isolated, family-less existence by choice. Christmas itself functions as a symbol throughout for generosity, community and renewal, which is why Scrooge's rejection of it in Stave 1 marks him as morally and socially isolated, and his full embrace of it in Stave 5 marks his complete reintegration into human community. Teach the learner that 'family' in this novella is not only about blood relations (Scrooge becomes 'a second father' to Tiny Tim) but about chosen generosity and connection.

#### ACC.CH1 A Christmas Carol character: Scrooge
Scrooge is introduced through relentless, list-like negative description (grasping, squeezing, cold as described through weather imagery associated with him) and is shown choosing isolation repeatedly -- rejecting his nephew, charity and Christmas itself. His transformation is neither instant nor unearned: the ghosts force him to genuinely confront the emotional cost of his choices before he changes, and the change is proven through sustained action, not a single moment of feeling. Teach the learner to track the shift from cold, hard imagery associated with Scrooge early on to warm, generous imagery associated with him by Stave 5, as concrete textual evidence of change.

#### ACC.CH2 A Christmas Carol character: the Cratchits, especially Bob and Tiny Tim
Bob Cratchit is presented as a loyal, uncomplaining employee and loving father despite poverty and Scrooge's meagre wage, embodying dignity without material comfort. Tiny Tim, physically frail and reliant on a crutch, functions partly as a symbol of vulnerable innocence and of the real human cost of poverty and inadequate charity -- his possible death in Stave 4's vision is the emotional low point of the whole book. Teach the learner that the Cratchits' function is partly realistic (a genuine depiction of the working poor) and partly symbolic (proof that poverty does not diminish moral worth), and that both readings matter for a full analysis.

#### ACC.CH3 A Christmas Carol character: the three spirits and Marley
Marley's ghost, weighed down by chains of cashboxes and ledgers forged through his own greed, functions as a direct warning of Scrooge's likely fate and establishes the supernatural mechanism of the whole plot. The three spirits are structured to escalate: Christmas Past is gentle and personal, Christmas Present is generous, abundant and morally direct (Ignorance and Want), and Christmas Yet to Come is silent, frightening and final, matching the increasing urgency Scrooge needs to feel. Teach the learner that each spirit's manner (gentle, generous, terrifying) is deliberately matched to the stage of persuasion Scrooge needs at that point in his transformation.

#### ACC.EX A Christmas Carol: the Paper 1 extract-based exam question and a model paragraph
The question gives a printed extract and asks something like 'Starting with this extract, explore how Dickens presents [Scrooge/poverty/redemption] in A Christmas Carol', requiring analysis of the extract first, then wider references across the whole novella. Model opening: 'In this extract, Dickens presents Scrooge's isolation through the narrator's ironic, distancing description of him as "solitary as an oyster" -- a simile suggesting he has sealed himself away from human connection.' Then move outward: 'This isolation is later directly challenged by the warmth of the Cratchits' household in Stave 3, whose poverty-stricken but loving Christmas exposes exactly what Scrooge's wealth has cost him.' Teach the pattern: extract detail first, then a linked whole-novella reference, repeated for each point.
