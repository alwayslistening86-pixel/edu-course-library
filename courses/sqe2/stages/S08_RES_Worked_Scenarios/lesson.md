# S08_RES_Worked_Scenarios - Lesson: Legal research across the five SQE2 practice-area contexts

## Goal
The learner applies the research method (identify, find, apply, reference) to a realistic research instruction in each of the five SQE2 practice-area contexts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-RES-CRI-1 - Worked scenario: sentencing guideline research: apply research skill to a Criminal Practice question
- SQE2-RES-DR-1 - Worked scenario: limitation period research: apply research skill to a Dispute Resolution question
- SQE2-RES-PROP-1 - Worked scenario: restrictive covenant research: apply research skill to a Property Practice question
- SQE2-RES-WILLS-1 - Worked scenario: intestacy entitlement research: apply research skill to a Wills and Probate question
- SQE2-RES-BUS-1 - Worked scenario: directors' duties research: apply research skill to a Business Organisations question

## How to teach this
Point out that the real SQE2 written sitting tests legal research in a subset of the five contexts per half-day (for example Property Practice and Wills and Intestacy on one sitting day), but the same identify-find-apply-reference method is used everywhere; what differs is the TYPE of source typical to that area (sentencing guidelines in Criminal Practice, land registry practice guides in Property Practice, and so on).

#### SQE2-RES-CRI-1 Worked scenario: sentencing guideline research
Scenario: research question - "What is the likely sentencing range for a first-time offender convicted of this specific offence, given these aggravating and mitigating factors?" Sources typically used: the relevant sentencing guideline (culpability and harm categories, starting points and ranges), and any statutory maximum. Apply by categorising the offence facts against the guideline's culpability/harm factors, then applying listed aggravating and mitigating features to move within the range. Reference the specific guideline and category used, and conclude with a stated likely range, not just "it depends."

#### SQE2-RES-DR-1 Worked scenario: limitation period research
Scenario: research question - "Has the client's claim for breach of contract become time-barred?" Sources typically used: the relevant limitation statute provision and, where relevant, case law on when time starts running (date of breach versus date of knowledge). Apply by identifying the trigger date on the facts and counting forward the statutory period, addressing any provision that could extend time (e.g. deliberate concealment). Reference the specific statutory section and conclude clearly whether the claim is or is not time-barred on the facts given.

#### SQE2-RES-PROP-1 Worked scenario: restrictive covenant research
Scenario: research question - "Can the client build an extension despite a covenant restricting 'alterations to the property' without the neighbour's consent?" Sources typically used: the covenant wording itself, and guidance/case law on interpreting restrictive covenants and remedies for breach (injunction, damages, or insurance as a practical alternative). Apply by construing the specific covenant wording against the proposed extension, and against practical options if it likely does breach. Reference the source of the covenant interpretation principle and conclude with a clear, practical recommendation.

#### SQE2-RES-WILLS-1 Worked scenario: intestacy entitlement research
Scenario: research question - "Who is entitled to the estate where the deceased died without a will, survived by a spouse and children?" Sources typically used: the statutory intestacy rules setting out the statutory legacy and division of the remainder between spouse and issue. Apply by identifying which family members survive and applying the statutory formula precisely to the facts given (value of estate, number of children). Reference the specific statutory provision and conclude with the actual entitlement figures or shares, not just the general rule.

#### SQE2-RES-BUS-1 Worked scenario: directors' duties research
Scenario: research question - "Has a director breached their duties by awarding a contract to a company in which they hold an undisclosed interest?" Sources typically used: the statutory directors' duties provisions (duty to avoid conflicts of interest, duty to declare interest in a proposed transaction) and any relevant procedural requirement in the articles. Apply by matching the facts (undisclosed interest, contract awarded) against each element of the relevant duty. Reference the specific statutory sections and conclude on whether a breach is established and what remedy might follow.
