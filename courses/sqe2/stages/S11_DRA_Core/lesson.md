# S11_DRA_Core - Lesson: Legal drafting: principles, drafting clauses and simple documents to instructions, and checking a draft

## Goal
The learner drafts clauses and short documents precisely to instructions, in plain English, and checks a draft for ambiguity and consistency.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-DRA-1 - Drafting principles: learn and apply: plain English, precision and following instructions exactly
- SQE2-DRA-2 - Drafting clauses to instructions: learn and apply: turning client instructions into effective, unambiguous clause wording
- SQE2-DRA-3 - Drafting simple documents to instructions: learn and apply: assembling a complete short document from instructions
- SQE2-DRA-4 - Checking and amending a draft: learn and apply: proofing a draft for ambiguity, inconsistency and defined-term errors

## How to teach this
Give the learner an instruction with a deliberate gap ("draft a clause requiring the tenant to keep the property in good repair" - but do not say by when, or to what standard) and ask them to draft it anyway. Use the result to show the single most common drafting failure: filling gaps with invented terms instead of drafting exactly to the instructions given and flagging what is missing - which is exactly what the real 45-minute SQE2 drafting task rewards.

#### SQE2-DRA-1 Drafting principles
Draft in plain English wherever the document type allows it, avoiding archaic legal filler ("hereinafter," "the said," "witnesseth") that adds no legal effect. Follow the instructions given exactly - do not add terms the client did not ask for, and do not omit terms they did, even if you think a different term would be better (flag your concern separately, do not silently substitute your own view). Define defined terms clearly and use them consistently throughout (if you define "the Property," use "the Property" every time, not "the premises" halfway through).

#### SQE2-DRA-2 Drafting clauses to instructions
Turn an instruction into effective wording by identifying exactly what the clause must achieve (an obligation, a right, a condition, an exclusion) and drafting the simplest wording that achieves it unambiguously. State who must do what, by when, and what happens if they do not, where the instruction implies consequences. Avoid ambiguous connectors ("and/or," vague "reasonable" without a standard where precision was instructed) unless the instruction specifically calls for flexibility. Read the clause back as a stranger would, checking it cannot be read two different ways.

#### SQE2-DRA-3 Drafting simple documents to instructions
Assemble a complete short document (a notice, a simple agreement, a resolution, a will clause) from instructions by first listing every element the document type requires (parties, date, operative provisions, signature block, as applicable) before drafting any wording, so nothing is missed. Draft in the correct order and format for that document type, and keep the structure clean: short numbered clauses or paragraphs are easier to check and amend than dense unbroken prose, and are what a supervising solicitor expects to see.

#### SQE2-DRA-4 Checking and amending a draft
Before finishing, check the draft against the instructions line by line to confirm nothing was missed or added, check every defined term is used consistently and every cross-reference (to another clause or schedule) is correct, and check for ambiguity by reading each clause as if you were the other side looking for a loophole. Common errors to catch: inconsistent numbering, a defined term used before it is defined, "shall" and "will" mixed inconsistently for obligations, and a condition with no stated consequence if unmet.
