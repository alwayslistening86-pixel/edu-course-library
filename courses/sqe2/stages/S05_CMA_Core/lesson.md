# S05_CMA_Core - Lesson: Case and matter analysis: extracting issues and facts from a bundle, spotting gaps, and producing a client-focused analysis

## Goal
The learner extracts the legal issues and relevant facts from an unfamiliar bundle of documents, identifies missing information, and writes a structured, client-focused analysis with a recommendation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-CMA-1 - Extracting the legal issues: learn and apply: spotting the causes of action, offences or issues in a bundle
- SQE2-CMA-2 - Extracting the relevant facts: learn and apply: separating relevant from irrelevant facts and building a chronology
- SQE2-CMA-3 - Identifying gaps and further evidence needed: learn and apply: spotting missing information and outstanding questions in a bundle
- SQE2-CMA-4 - Producing a client-focused analysis: learn and apply: structuring an analysis into issues, application, options and recommendation

## How to teach this
Give the learner an unfamiliar three-document bundle (an email chain, a letter, and a short attendance note) on any everyday dispute and ask: "In five minutes, tell me what this is actually about - not everything in it, just the two or three things that matter." This mirrors the real 60-minute computer-based task: skim first for the shape of the problem, then read closely only for what bears on the issues you have spotted.

#### SQE2-CMA-1 Extracting the legal issues
Read the bundle once quickly to identify its type (contract dispute, criminal allegation, conveyancing query, probate matter, company issue) and the parties' positions, then read again to pull out the specific legal issues raised by the facts (was there a valid offer and acceptance? was notice properly served? does the will meet formality requirements?). List issues as questions, not conclusions, at this stage. Distinguish the central issues from side issues that do not affect the outcome, since the SQE2 task rewards focus on what matters, not exhaustive coverage of everything mentioned.

#### SQE2-CMA-2 Extracting the relevant facts
Build a chronology of events with dates as you read, since bundles are rarely presented in date order and sequence often decides the outcome (which notice came first, which payment was late). Mark each fact as relevant (bears directly on an issue), background (context only) or irrelevant, and discard irrelevant material from your working notes so it does not clutter your analysis. Note where two documents conflict on the same fact (a letter says one date, an email says another) - conflicts are themselves relevant facts that must be flagged, not silently resolved by picking one.

#### SQE2-CMA-3 Identifying gaps and further evidence needed
No bundle is complete: identify what information would change your analysis if you had it (Is there a written contract? What does the missing invoice say? Has service of the notice been proved?) and state clearly what you assumed in its absence. Distinguish gaps that are merely inconvenient from gaps that are decisive - a decisive gap should be flagged prominently, near the top of your analysis, not buried, since a real solicitor cannot advise confidently around a decisive unknown without saying so.

#### SQE2-CMA-4 Producing a client-focused analysis
Structure the written analysis as: the issues identified, the relevant facts and any conflicts/gaps, application of the law to those facts (briefly - functioning legal knowledge is assumed, not tested at length here), the options available to the client, and a clear recommendation with reasons. Write for the client's decision-making, not as an academic essay: lead with what matters to them (risk, cost, likely outcome), and make the recommendation explicit rather than leaving the reader to infer it from a list of options.
