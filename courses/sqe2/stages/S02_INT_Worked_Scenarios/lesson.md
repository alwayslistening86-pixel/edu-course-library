# S02_INT_Worked_Scenarios - Lesson: Client interviewing across the five SQE2 practice-area contexts

## Goal
The learner adapts interview structure, questioning and objective-identification to a Criminal Practice, Dispute Resolution, Property Practice, Wills and Probate, and Business Organisations client, each with a realistic attendance note.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-INT-CRI-1 - Worked scenario: police station follow-up interview: apply interviewing skill to a Criminal Practice client
- SQE2-INT-DR-1 - Worked scenario: new litigation client take-on interview: apply interviewing skill to a Dispute Resolution client
- SQE2-INT-PROP-1 - Worked scenario: residential buyer take-on interview: apply interviewing skill to a Property Practice client
- SQE2-INT-WILLS-1 - Worked scenario: will instructions interview: apply interviewing skill to a Wills and Probate client
- SQE2-INT-BUS-1 - Worked scenario: company formation instructions interview: apply interviewing skill to a Business Organisations client

## How to teach this
Ask: "Does the interview structure from the last stage change when the client is in custody, or wants to buy a house, or has just lost a parent?" Draw out that the STRUCTURE stays fixed but the questions, sensitivity and likely objectives change with context - this is exactly what the real SQE2 oral sitting tests (interview and attendance note is examined live in two practice-area contexts per sitting, drawn from the five).

#### SQE2-INT-CRI-1 Worked scenario: police station follow-up interview
Scenario: Mr Douglas was interviewed under caution for shoplifting and has an appointment with you before his next police interview. Apply the structure: open by confirming what he already understands about his position; ask open questions about what happened in his own words before mentioning the evidence; be alert that he may minimise or over-disclose under stress. His likely objective is avoiding a criminal record or reducing the charge, not necessarily "getting off." Note advice given on next steps (e.g., obtaining disclosure, no comment strategy considerations) accurately and only what was actually said, since the note may later be scrutinised.

#### SQE2-INT-DR-1 Worked scenario: new litigation client take-on interview
Scenario: Ms Whitfield believes a supplier breached a contract and wants "to sue them." Use open questions to build the chronology of the contract and the alleged breach before any legal conclusion; clarify what she has already said to the other side (privilege and admissions matter here). Surface her real objective - often payment or performance, not litigation itself - and note the limitation date and any urgency (e.g., a looming deadline) as a flagged next step, since missing it would be a serious failure regardless of how good the interview was.

#### SQE2-INT-PROP-1 Worked scenario: residential buyer take-on interview
Scenario: Mr and Mrs Kaur are buying a house and want to know "what happens next." Confirm the parties, the property, price, any chain, mortgage offer status, and target completion date using open then closed questions; explain the conveyancing process briefly without overwhelming them with process the interview is not the place for a full lecture on title. Their real objective is usually certainty of timing and cost. The attendance note should record instructions to act, the funds position, and any special factors (leasehold, shared ownership) for the fee-earner who progresses the file.

#### SQE2-INT-WILLS-1 Worked scenario: will instructions interview
Scenario: Mrs Okonkwo, recently widowed, wants to make a new will. Use sensitive open questioning given the bereavement context; establish her family circumstances, assets, and wishes for executors, guardians (if relevant) and beneficiaries, checking capacity and freedom from undue influence are not in doubt. Her real objective may include protecting a vulnerable beneficiary, not just "leaving everything to the children." Record instructions precisely in the attendance note, including anyone who was present, since capacity and undue influence can later be challenged and the note may be the only contemporaneous record.

#### SQE2-INT-BUS-1 Worked scenario: company formation instructions interview
Scenario: Two founders, Mr Patel and Ms Byrne, want to set up a company together. Ask open questions about the business, their respective roles and contributions, and how decisions and disagreements will be handled, before moving to closed questions on share splits and director appointments. Their stated objective ("set up a company") often hides a real objective of protecting each founder if the relationship breaks down. The attendance note should record instructions on shareholding, director roles, and any shareholders' agreement discussion, flagging unresolved points for follow-up advice.
