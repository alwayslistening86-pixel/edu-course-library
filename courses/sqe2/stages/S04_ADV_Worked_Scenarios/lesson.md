# S04_ADV_Worked_Scenarios - Lesson: Advocacy across the five SQE2 practice-area contexts

## Goal
The learner adapts submission structure, witness-questioning and etiquette to a Criminal Practice, Dispute Resolution, Property Practice, Wills and Probate, and Business Organisations hearing.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-ADV-CRI-1 - Worked scenario: bail application: apply advocacy skill to a Criminal Practice hearing
- SQE2-ADV-DR-1 - Worked scenario: interim applications hearing: apply advocacy skill to a Dispute Resolution hearing
- SQE2-ADV-PROP-1 - Worked scenario: possession hearing: apply advocacy skill to a Property Practice hearing
- SQE2-ADV-WILLS-1 - Worked scenario: contested probate directions hearing: apply advocacy skill to a Wills and Probate hearing
- SQE2-ADV-BUS-1 - Worked scenario: interim injunction application: apply advocacy skill to a Business Organisations hearing

## How to teach this
Point out that in the real SQE2 oral sitting, advocacy is examined live in two of the five contexts per sitting (historically Dispute Resolution and Criminal Practice), but the skill itself - structure, questioning, etiquette - is identical across all five; only the tribunal, the applicable law and the tone differ. Ask: "What changes about your opening sentence if you're in a Magistrates' Court bail hearing versus a company's general meeting dispute?"

#### SQE2-ADV-CRI-1 Worked scenario: bail application
Scenario: you apply for bail for Mr Nkemelu, charged with an offence, opposed on grounds of flight risk. Structure: state you apply for bail, address the statutory presumption and the specific objection raised (flight risk), and propose concrete conditions (reporting, surety, residence, curfew) that answer that objection directly rather than simply asserting your client will not flee. Address the prosecution's strongest point head-on. Use correct Magistrates' Court address ("Sir/Madam" or "Your Worship" as directed) and be ready to answer questions on why the proposed conditions are sufficient.

#### SQE2-ADV-DR-1 Worked scenario: interim applications hearing
Scenario: you apply for summary judgment or a case management direction in a breach of contract claim. Structure: state the order sought, the test the court applies (e.g. no real prospect of success and no other compelling reason for trial), and apply the facts to that test point by point, with document references. Address the opponent's likely argument that there is a triable issue. Use correct civil court address and bundle discipline, since interim hearings are often paper-heavy and precise page references carry real weight with the tribunal.

#### SQE2-ADV-PROP-1 Worked scenario: possession hearing
Scenario: you apply for a possession order against a residential tenant in arrears. Structure: confirm the tenancy, the arrears figure with a clear breakdown, and that statutory notice requirements were met before addressing any defence raised (e.g. disrepair set-off). Be ready to address the court on proportionality and any human-rights or vulnerability factors raised by the tenant, since possession hearings often involve a personal, not purely commercial, dimension the tribunal will expect you to acknowledge sensitively while still pressing the application.

#### SQE2-ADV-WILLS-1 Worked scenario: contested probate directions hearing
Scenario: you seek directions in a contested probate matter where a sibling challenges the validity of a will on capacity grounds. Structure: state the direction sought (e.g. directions for expert medical evidence, or a timetable to trial), summarise the competing positions neutrally where appropriate, and address the strongest point on the other side (the medical evidence they rely on) rather than dismissing it. Directions hearings reward precision and proportionality over adversarial flourish, since the immediate question is case management, not who ultimately wins.

#### SQE2-ADV-BUS-1 Worked scenario: interim injunction application
Scenario: you apply for an interim injunction to restrain a departing director from using confidential client lists. Structure: address the American Cyanamid-style test (serious issue to be tried, adequacy of damages, balance of convenience) point by point, with a clear proposed order and, where relevant, an undertaking in damages. Address the respondent's strongest argument (that damages would adequately compensate your client) directly. Urgency and precision matter more than length in interim injunction advocacy, since the tribunal is deciding on limited evidence under time pressure.
