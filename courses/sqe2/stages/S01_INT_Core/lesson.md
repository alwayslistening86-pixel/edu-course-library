# S01_INT_Core - Lesson: Client interviewing: structure, questioning technique and identifying objectives, and the attendance note

## Goal
The learner runs a controlled client interview using open and closed questioning, distinguishes the client's stated request from their real objectives, and writes an accurate attendance note afterwards.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-INT-1 - Interview structure: learn and apply: a controlled opening, information-gathering, objective-setting, advice and closing structure for a client interview
- SQE2-INT-2 - Questioning technique: learn and apply: open and closed questioning, active listening and the funnel technique
- SQE2-INT-3 - Identifying the client's objectives: learn and apply: distinguishing stated wants from underlying commercial or personal objectives
- SQE2-INT-4 - The attendance note: learn and apply: producing an accurate, complete attendance note a colleague could act on

## How to teach this
Give the learner only one line: "Mrs Ferreira has come in about a problem with her landlord." Ask them to say out loud, before any facts are known, how they would open the interview and what they would NOT do (start giving advice, start asking closed questions). Use this to show that structure and technique come first, subject knowledge second - the skill is tested with an unseen client care letter and a short brief, in real time.

#### SQE2-INT-1 Interview structure
Run the interview in a fixed shape: (1) open with introductions, confirm time available, and explain confidentiality and what will happen at the end; (2) gather information using mostly open questions; (3) clarify and confirm the facts and the client's objectives; (4) give any advice the facts allow, flagging what needs further work; (5) close by agreeing next steps, who does what, and by when, and check the client has no further questions. In the real SQE2 assessment this is a 25-minute interview (10 minutes' prior reading time), so pace matters: spend the first two minutes on step (1) and do not let step (2) run past half the available time.

#### SQE2-INT-2 Questioning technique
Use open questions ("Tell me what happened when...", "What were you hoping to achieve?") to let the client tell their own story without your assumptions shaping it, then narrow with the funnel technique: open questions first, then more focused open questions, then closed questions only to pin down dates, figures or names. Reflect back what you have heard ("So you're saying the payment was due on the 1st but wasn't received until the 15th?") to check accuracy and show active listening. Avoid leading questions, jargon, and interrupting; use silence deliberately to let the client finish.

#### SQE2-INT-3 Identifying the client's objectives
The client's opening request ("I want to sue them") is rarely their real objective (getting paid, stopping the behaviour, preserving a relationship, minimising cost or publicity). Ask directly: "What would a good outcome look like for you?" and "Is there anything else driving this, for example cost, time, or your relationship with them?" Rank objectives where the client has more than one and flag where objectives conflict (wanting speed and wanting the cheapest option can conflict). Recording the client's real objective, not just their instruction, is what lets later advice be genuinely client-focused rather than technically correct but useless.

#### SQE2-INT-4 The attendance note
Write the attendance note immediately after the interview (25 minutes in the real assessment), in the firm's standard format: file reference, date, time, attendees, purpose, a chronological or thematic summary of what was said, the client's objectives, advice given, and agreed next steps with owners and deadlines. Write in the third person, past tense, and only record what was actually said or agreed - do not add advice you did not give or facts you did not check. A colleague picking up the file cold should be able to act on the note alone.
