# S12_DRA_Worked_Scenarios - Lesson: Legal drafting across the five SQE2 practice-area contexts

## Goal
The learner drafts a clause or short document to instructions correctly in each of the five SQE2 practice-area contexts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SQE2-DRA-CRI-1 - Worked scenario: bail conditions notice: apply drafting skill to a Criminal Practice document
- SQE2-DRA-DR-1 - Worked scenario: particulars of claim extract: apply drafting skill to a Dispute Resolution document
- SQE2-DRA-PROP-1 - Worked scenario: sale contract special condition: apply drafting skill to a Property Practice document
- SQE2-DRA-WILLS-1 - Worked scenario: will clause: apply drafting skill to a Wills and Probate document
- SQE2-DRA-BUS-1 - Worked scenario: board resolution: apply drafting skill to a Business Organisations document

## How to teach this
Ask: "What does 'drafting to instructions' actually look like differently in a will clause versus a board resolution?" Use this to show the drafting METHOD (plain English, precise to instructions, checked) is constant, but document conventions (a will clause's formal language, a resolution's fixed structure) are specific to the document type typical of that practice area, and getting the convention right is itself part of "legally correct" drafting.

#### SQE2-DRA-CRI-1 Worked scenario: bail conditions notice
Scenario: instructions to draft the bail conditions proposed for Mr Nkemelu (from the advocacy stage): residence at a stated address, a curfew between stated hours, reporting to a police station on stated days, and a surety in a stated sum. Draft each condition as a short, separate, numbered item stating precisely what is required, by whom, and how compliance is checked (e.g. reporting "in person" at a named station). Avoid vague conditions ("behave appropriately") that cannot be objectively checked or enforced, since an unenforceable condition is a drafting failure even if well-intentioned.

#### SQE2-DRA-DR-1 Worked scenario: particulars of claim extract
Scenario: instructions to draft the paragraph of particulars of claim setting out the breach in Ms Whitfield's contract dispute: the contract date, the delivery term breached, the actual (late) delivery date, and the loss claimed. Draft numbered paragraphs stating facts plainly and in the order they occurred, pleading the term breached and the breach itself as distinct, clearly stated allegations, and stating the loss claimed with a figure or basis for calculation. Do not plead argument or evidence here - only the facts and the claim.

#### SQE2-DRA-PROP-1 Worked scenario: sale contract special condition
Scenario: instructions to draft a special condition for the Kaurs' sale contract dealing with a specific item (e.g. fixtures to be included, or a retention pending completion of specified remedial works). State precisely what is included/required, by when, and what happens if not satisfied by completion (e.g. a retention released only on proof of completion of works). Use the defined terms already established in the standard conditions of sale ("the Property," "Completion Date") consistently rather than inventing new labels for the same thing.

#### SQE2-DRA-WILLS-1 Worked scenario: will clause
Scenario: instructions to draft a specific gift clause for Mrs Okonkwo's will, leaving a named item to a named beneficiary, with a substitute beneficiary if the first does not survive her. Use conventional, formal will-drafting language (a legacy clause identifying the gift, the beneficiary, and a survivorship/substitution provision) while keeping it as plain as the convention allows. Precision matters more than brevity here: an ambiguous gift clause is a classic source of contested probate, exactly the situation seen in the case-analysis and advocacy stages.

#### SQE2-DRA-BUS-1 Worked scenario: board resolution
Scenario: instructions to draft a board resolution recording the directors' approval of a specified new contract, naming the counterparty, the key terms approved, and authorising a named director to sign. Use the standard resolution format (recital of what is proposed, the resolved wording itself, stated in the third person as a decision of the board) and keep the operative "IT WAS RESOLVED THAT" wording precise and limited to exactly what was instructed, with no wider authority granted than the instruction supports.

## Explicitly not here
This completes the six skills; the cumulative exam follows, covering all six skills across the five contexts.
