# S08_Experimental_and_Other_Methods - Test: Research methods: experimental design and other research methods

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Distinguish between a laboratory experiment and a field experiment, and give one strength of each. [4 marks]
2. Explain what a natural experiment is, and give one reason a researcher might choose to use one. [2 marks]
3. Explain why interobserver reliability is checked in observation studies, and what a high level of agreement between observers suggests. [2 marks]
4. A psychologist finds that, across 40 participants, hours of sleep the night before a test and score on a memory test are positively correlated. Explain why this correlation cannot, by itself, prove that more sleep causes better memory performance. [2 marks]
5. Which experimental design uses different participants in each condition, but matches them as closely as possible on relevant characteristics? Choose every correct option.
   A. matched pairs
   B. repeated measures
   C. independent groups
   D. opportunity sampling

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 laboratory experiment -- takes place in a controlled, artificial environment; B1 strength, e.g. high control over extraneous variables; B1 field experiment -- takes place in participants' natural environment, with the IV still deliberately manipulated; B1 strength, e.g. more natural behaviour than a lab setting.
2. [2] B1 studies the effect of an IV that varies naturally/anyway, without the researcher manipulating it, e.g. comparing before and after a real event/policy; B1 reasonable reason, e.g. allows study of a situation that could not ethically or practically be manipulated by a researcher.
3. [2] B1 it is checked to see how far two or more independent observers, using the same behaviour categories, agree in what they record; B1 high agreement suggests the behaviour categories are being applied consistently/objectively, increasing confidence in the data (reducing observer bias).
4. [2] B1 correlation shows only that two co-variables are associated, not that one causes the other; B1 a third, unmeasured variable (e.g. general health, or stress levels) could be causing both more sleep and better memory, rather than sleep causing the memory improvement directly.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
