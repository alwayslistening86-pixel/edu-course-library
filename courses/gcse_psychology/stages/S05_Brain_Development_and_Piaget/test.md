## Test items
1. Name, in order, the four neural structures involved in early brain development described in this course, and state one function of the cortex. [3 marks]
2. Explain the nature-nurture debate as it applies to brain development. [2 marks]
3. Distinguish between assimilation and accommodation in Piaget's theory, using an example of each. [4 marks]
4. Describe Hughes' 'policeman doll' study and explain what it suggested about Piaget's claims regarding egocentrism. [4 marks]
5. In Piaget's theory, a child who wrongly believes a tall, thin glass holds more liquid than a short, wide glass containing the same amount of liquid is failing a test of what? Choose every correct option.
   A. conservation
   B. object permanence
   C. assimilation
   D. accommodation

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 brain stem, thalamus, cerebellum, cortex, in that order; B1 correctly ordered; B1 one correct cortex function, e.g. higher-order cognition/thinking/reasoning/language.
2. [2] B1 nature refers to innate, genetically programmed biological maturation of brain structures; B1 nurture refers to the effect of experience/environment/upbringing on how the brain develops, with most psychologists seeing development as an interaction of both.
3. [4] B1 assimilation -- fitting new information into an existing schema without changing it; B1 correct example; B1 accommodation -- changing an existing schema, or forming a new one, because new information does not fit; B1 correct example.
4. [4] B1 children had to hide a doll from two toy policemen's lines of sight, rather than using Piaget's three mountains task; B1 children as young as three and a half years old could often complete this correctly; B1 suggesting egocentrism is not as fixed/early a limitation as Piaget claimed; B1 children can take another's viewpoint when a task is made meaningful to them.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
