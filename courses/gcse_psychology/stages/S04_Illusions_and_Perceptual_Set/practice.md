# S04_Illusions_and_Perceptual_Set - Practice: Visual illusions and factors affecting perception

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Name the visual illusion in which the arrangement of three 'Pac-Man'-like shapes creates the impression of a bright triangle with edges that are not really there, and state which type of explanation (ambiguity/misinterpreted depth cues/fiction/size constancy) this is. [2 marks]
2. Briefly outline what Gilchrist and Nesberg found, and what it demonstrated about perception. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 the Kanizsa triangle; B1 fiction (perceiving contours/shapes that are not physically present).
2. [2] B1 food-deprived (hungry) participants rated pictures of food as brighter than participants who had recently eaten; B1 demonstrating that motivation can bias/influence perception (perceptual set).

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
