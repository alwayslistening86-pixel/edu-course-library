# S14_Language_and_Thought - Test: Language and thought, and human/animal communication

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Compare Piaget's view and the Sapir-Whorf hypothesis on the relationship between language and thought. [4 marks]
2. Explain how cross-cultural studies of colour recognition have been used as evidence in the language-thought debate. [3 marks]
3. Explain two ways in which human communication differs from known animal communication systems. [4 marks]
4. Describe the honeybee waggle dance studied by Von Frisch, and explain what information it communicates. [3 marks]
5. According to Piaget's view of the language-thought relationship, which comes first? Choose every correct option.
   A. thought/cognitive development
   B. language
   C. neither -- they develop completely independently
   D. non-verbal communication

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 Piaget: language depends on thought -- cognitive development comes first, and language expresses concepts already understood; B1 Sapir-Whorf: thinking depends on/is shaped by language -- the language spoken influences how a person can think about/categorise the world; B1 both views correctly and specifically attributed; B1 a clear, correctly reasoned contrast drawn between the two directions of influence claimed.
2. [3] B1 languages differ in how many basic colour terms they have; B1 speakers of a language with fewer colour terms have sometimes been found slower/less accurate at distinguishing colours their language does not separately name; B1 used as evidence broadly supporting the Sapir-Whorf view that language shapes thought/perception (while noting people can often still perceive the difference, which is used as evidence against a very strong version of the hypothesis).
3. [4] B1 humans can plan ahead and discuss future/hypothetical events, not tied to the immediate present; B1 correctly explained; B1 human language can combine a finite set of words into an effectively unlimited number of new, meaningful sentences (generative), unlike the fixed signal sets typical of animal communication; B1 correctly explained.
4. [3] B1 a figure-of-eight dance performed by a returning worker bee on the honeycomb; B1 the angle of the dance (relative to the sun) communicates the direction of a food source; B1 the duration/vigour of the dance communicates the distance to the food source.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
