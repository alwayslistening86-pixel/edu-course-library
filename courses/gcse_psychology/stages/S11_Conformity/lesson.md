# S11_Conformity - Lesson: Social influence: conformity

## Goal
The learner explains social factors (group size, anonymity, task difficulty) and dispositional factors affecting conformity, and describes Asch's line-judgement study and its findings.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 5.1 - Conformity: social factors (group size, anonymity, task difficulty) and dispositional factors (Asch's study)

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if everyone else in a room confidently gave an answer you were sure was wrong, would you say what you really thought, or go along with the group? Asch actually tested this. Every named study or theory in this course (e.g. Murdock, Bartlett, Piaget, McGarrigle and Donaldson, Hughes, Dweck, Gibson, Gregory, Gilchrist and Nesberg, Bruner and Minturn, Asch, Milgram, Adorno, Piliavin, Von Frisch, Darwin, Hebb, Penfield, Tulving, Wiles, Kaij) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of psychology, checked against real psychological knowledge when this course was built -- present it as real research, never as illustrative fiction, and if the learner misremembers who did a study or what it found, correct this explicitly. AQA GCSE Psychology has a substantial Research Methods strand (3.1.4, including 3.1.4.1 Data Handling): sampling methods, experimental design, types of data, and descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, range) are examined with real calculations, not just definitions -- every numerical worked example in this course was computed and checked with Python when the course was built, and the learner should always be made to show their working for a calculation, since AQA awards credit for correct method as well as a correct final answer. Diagrams this course cannot draw (e.g. the multi-store model boxes, visual illusions, brain lobes, a neuron, a scatter diagram) are described precisely in words -- have the learner sketch or describe each one back, since being able to label a diagram accurately is itself examined.

#### 5.1 Conformity: social factors (group size, anonymity, task difficulty) and dispositional factors (Asch's study)
**Conformity** is a change in a person's behaviour or opinion to match that of a majority group, even without direct pressure to do so. Several **social factors** affect how much people conform: **group size** (conformity generally increases as the size of the majority grows, up to a point, after which adding further members makes little extra difference); **anonymity** (people conform less when their individual response is public/identifiable, and more when they can respond privately, without their non-conformity being visible to the group); and **task difficulty** (conformity increases when a task is harder/more ambiguous, since people are less confident in their own judgement and rely more on the group for guidance). **Dispositional factors** (relating to an individual's own personality/characteristics) also matter, such as **personality** (e.g. people lower in self-esteem or higher in a need for social approval tend to conform more) and **expertise** (someone with genuine relevant expertise or confidence in the task is less likely to conform against their own judgement). The classic study of conformity is **Asch's (1951)** line-judgement experiment: a genuine participant sat with several confederates (actors working for the researcher) and was asked, out loud, to judge which of three comparison lines matched a standard line in length -- an unambiguous, easy task. On certain trials, the confederates unanimously and deliberately gave the same wrong answer before the participant responded. A substantial proportion of participants conformed to the incorrect majority on at least one such trial, despite the task's answer being objectively obvious when done alone, demonstrating the real power of majority group pressure on conformity, even where there was no threat of punishment for disagreeing.
