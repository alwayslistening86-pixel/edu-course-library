# S11_Conformity - Test: Social influence: conformity

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Define conformity. [2 marks]
2. Explain how group size and anonymity each affect conformity. [4 marks]
3. Describe Asch's line-judgement study and explain what it demonstrated about conformity. [4 marks]
4. Explain how a dispositional factor, such as low self-esteem, might make a person more likely to conform. [2 marks]
5. In Asch's line-judgement study, what was manipulated to test conformity? Choose every correct option.
   A. confederates giving a deliberately wrong answer before the genuine participant responded
   B. the amount of electric shock a confederate appeared to receive
   C. whether a bystander was alone or in a group
   D. the order in which a list of words was presented

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 a change in a person's behaviour or opinion; B1 to match that of a majority group, without direct pressure/instruction to do so.
2. [4] B1 group size -- conformity generally increases as the majority grows larger, up to a point; B1 correctly explained/qualified; B1 anonymity -- conformity is lower when a response is private/unidentifiable, and higher when public; B1 correctly explained.
3. [4] B1 participants judged line length alongside confederates who deliberately gave a wrong answer on certain trials; B1 a substantial proportion of participants conformed to the incorrect majority on at least one such trial; B1 despite the correct answer being objectively obvious when judged alone; B1 demonstrating the real power of majority group pressure, even with no threat of punishment for disagreeing.
4. [2] B1 lower self-esteem is linked to a greater need for social approval/acceptance from the group; B1 making the person more likely to go along with the majority rather than risk standing out/disapproval.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
