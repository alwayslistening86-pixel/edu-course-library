# S02_Reconstructive_Memory - Test: Memory as an active, reconstructive process

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Outline the theory of reconstructive memory. [3 marks]
2. Describe Bartlett's 'War of the Ghosts' study and what it showed about memory. [4 marks]
3. Two eyewitnesses are asked about a car crash they both saw. One is asked 'how fast was the car going when it smashed into the other car?' and the other is asked 'how fast was the car going when it hit the other car?'. Using the theory of reconstructive memory, explain why the two witnesses might give different speed estimates and different later memories of the event. [4 marks]
4. Which of these best describes a 'schema' in the theory of reconstructive memory? Choose every correct option.
   A. an organised packet of knowledge and expectation built from past experience
   B. a brief, detailed sensory trace lasting under a second
   C. the physical connection between two neurons
   D. a list of items recalled in a memory test

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 memory is not a passive, accurate recording but is actively reconstructed at recall; B1 using schemas -- organised packets of existing knowledge/expectation; B1 with 'effort after meaning', distorting details to fit those schemas.
2. [4] B1 British participants read an unfamiliar Native American folk tale and recalled it repeatedly over time; B1 recollections became shorter/more conventional and were distorted to fit British cultural schemas; B1 e.g. unfamiliar details were changed into more familiar ones; B1 supporting reconstructive memory rather than memory as a faithful recording.
3. [4] B1 the wording of the question acts as post-event/misleading information; B1 'smashed' implies a more severe collision than 'hit', which can be incorporated into the witness's reconstruction of the memory; B1 the witness given 'smashed' is likely to estimate a higher speed and may later even falsely recall broken glass or other severe-crash details; B1 correctly shows understanding that recall is reconstructed at the point of retrieval, not simply replayed.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
