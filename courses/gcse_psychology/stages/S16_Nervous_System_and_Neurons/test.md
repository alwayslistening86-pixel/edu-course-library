## Test items
1. Distinguish between the central nervous system and the peripheral nervous system. [2 marks]
2. Explain what happens in the body during the fight-or-flight response. [3 marks]
3. Explain the James-Lange theory of emotion, and why its proposed order of events is often described as counter-intuitive. [3 marks]
4. Describe synaptic transmission, and explain how Hebb's theory links repeated neural activity to learning. [4 marks]
5. Which type of neuron carries information from a sensory receptor towards the central nervous system? Choose every correct option.
   A. a sensory neuron
   B. a relay neuron
   C. a motor neuron
   D. an autonomic neuron

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 the central nervous system is the brain and spinal cord, processing information and coordinating a response; B1 the peripheral nervous system is all the nerves outside the CNS, connecting it to the rest of the body.
2. [3] B1 the autonomic nervous system rapidly prepares the body for action when a threat is perceived; B1 heart rate and breathing rate increase, and blood flow is diverted to the muscles; B1 adrenaline is released, readying the body to confront ('fight') or escape ('flight') the threat.
3. [3] B1 the physiological (bodily) response happens first, and the brain's interpretation of that bodily arousal is the emotion itself; B1 e.g. a racing heart is interpreted as fear, rather than fear causing the racing heart; B1 counter-intuitive because most people assume the emotion comes first and causes the bodily reaction, not the reverse.
4. [4] B1 an electrical impulse reaching the end of a neuron triggers release of a neurotransmitter into the synapse; B1 the neurotransmitter crosses the gap and binds to receptors on the next neuron, triggering a new impulse there; B1 Hebb proposed that when two connected neurons are repeatedly activated together, the synaptic connection between them is strengthened; B1 making it more likely one will trigger the other in future -- the proposed biological basis of learning/memory.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
