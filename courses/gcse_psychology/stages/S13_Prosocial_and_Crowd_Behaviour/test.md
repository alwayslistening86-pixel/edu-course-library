# S13_Prosocial_and_Crowd_Behaviour - Test: Prosocial behaviour and crowd/collective behaviour

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how the cost of helping can affect whether a bystander intervenes in an emergency. [2 marks]
2. Describe Piliavin et al.'s subway study, including its method and findings. [4 marks]
3. Explain deindividuation, and how it can make antisocial crowd behaviour more likely. [3 marks]
4. Explain how a dispositional factor could make one member of a rioting crowd less likely to join in the antisocial behaviour than another member. [2 marks]
5. Which term describes individuals exerting less effort on a task when working as part of a group than when working alone? Choose every correct option.
   A. social loafing
   B. deindividuation
   C. diffusion of responsibility
   D. conformity

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 a higher cost of helping (e.g. physical risk, unpleasantness, embarrassment, time) reduces the likelihood a bystander will intervene; B1 correctly reasoned/exemplified.
2. [4] B1 an actor staged a collapse on New York subway trains, appearing either ill (with a cane) or drunk; B1 real passengers' helping behaviour was observed as a field study; B1 helping was faster/more frequent for the apparently ill victim than the drunk victim; B1 helping was also reduced when the cost of helping was higher (e.g. the victim appearing to bleed).
3. [3] B1 being part of a large, often anonymous crowd can reduce a person's sense of individual identity and personal responsibility; B1 this can weaken normal restraints on behaviour; B1 making antisocial acts a lone individual would not commit more likely.
4. [2] B1 a personal characteristic such as strong personal morality (a firm sense of right and wrong) could make someone resist the pressure to join in; B1 correctly and specifically reasoned, e.g. holding to their own values even under group/deindividuation pressure.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
