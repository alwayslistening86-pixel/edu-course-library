# S13_Prosocial_and_Crowd_Behaviour - Practice: Prosocial behaviour and crowd/collective behaviour

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question (including full working for any calculation) before seeing the model answer. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Explain what is meant by diffusion of responsibility, and how it affects bystander intervention. [2 marks]
2. Briefly describe what Piliavin et al.'s subway study found about the type of victim and helping behaviour. [2 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [2] B1 responsibility for helping is spread across everyone present, so each individual bystander feels less personally obliged to act; B1 this reduces the likelihood that any one bystander intervenes when others are present.
2. [2] B1 helping was faster/more frequent for the apparently ill victim than the apparently drunk victim; B1 correctly attributed to Piliavin et al.'s (1969) field study.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
