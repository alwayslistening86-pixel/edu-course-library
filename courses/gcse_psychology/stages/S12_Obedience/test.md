# S12_Obedience - Test: Social influence: obedience

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Define obedience, and explain how it differs from conformity. [2 marks]
2. Explain, using Milgram's agency theory, how the authority of the person giving instructions affects obedience. [3 marks]
3. Outline Adorno's authoritarian personality theory. [4 marks]
4. A participant in a study is told by a researcher in a white coat to continue giving increasingly strong 'shocks' to another person, despite hearing cries of pain. Using Milgram's agency theory, explain why the participant might continue to obey. [3 marks]
5. According to Adorno, what did the F-scale measure? Choose every correct option.
   A. a personality type linked to obedience and prejudice
   B. the accuracy of eyewitness memory
   C. the strength of a visual illusion
   D. levels of conformity to a majority group

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 obedience is complying with a direct instruction from a figure of authority; B1 conformity is changing behaviour/opinion to match a majority group, without a direct instruction/order being given.
2. [3] B1 legitimate, socially recognised authority (e.g. a scientist, or someone in an official role) increases obedience; B1 because it makes a shift into the agentic state (seeing oneself as merely carrying out the authority's wishes) more likely; B1 correctly explained/linked to reduced personal responsibility.
3. [4] B1 some people have a personality type that makes them especially likely to be obedient towards authority; B1 and hostile/prejudiced towards those seen as lower status; B1 often linked to a strict, harshly disciplinarian upbringing; B1 measured using an attitude scale (the F-scale), reflecting a general disposition rather than a one-off situational response.
4. [3] B1 the researcher represents a legitimate authority figure, making a shift into the agentic state more likely; B1 in the agentic state, the participant sees themselves as merely carrying out the researcher's instructions, and attributes responsibility for any harm to the researcher, not themselves; B1 correctly and specifically applied to the given scenario.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
