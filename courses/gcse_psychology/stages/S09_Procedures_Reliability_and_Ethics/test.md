# S09_Procedures_Reliability_and_Ethics - Test: Research methods: procedures, reliability, validity and ethics

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain what a standardised procedure is, and why using one improves a study. [2 marks]
2. A researcher uses random allocation to place participants into two conditions of an independent groups experiment. Explain what random allocation is, and why it is used. [2 marks]
3. A researcher deceives participants about the true purpose of a study on obedience, without their informed consent. Using BPS ethical guidelines, explain two ways the researcher should address this after the study. [4 marks]
4. Explain the difference between a study being reliable and a study being valid, using an example of a study that could be reliable but not valid. [3 marks]
5. Which BPS ethical consideration specifically means participants can leave a study at any point without being penalised? Choose every correct option.
   A. the right to withdraw
   B. informed consent
   C. confidentiality
   D. debriefing

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 giving every participant the same instructions, materials, environment and timing as far as possible; B1 so any difference in the DV can be more confidently attributed to the IV, rather than to inconsistencies in how the study was run.
2. [2] B1 participants are assigned to conditions using a chance/random procedure; B1 to spread individual differences evenly across the two conditions, reducing the risk they explain any difference in the DV instead of the IV.
3. [4] B1 full debriefing, explaining the true aims and nature of the study once it has finished; B1 correctly explained; B1 offering support to deal with any distress caused by the deception/the study itself; B1 correctly explained (also credit reaffirming the right to withdraw their data retrospectively, with correct explanation).
4. [3] B1 reliability -- consistency, would the same result be obtained if repeated; B1 validity -- whether the study genuinely measures/reflects what it claims to; B1 correct example, e.g. a highly controlled laboratory study of aggression might reliably produce consistent scores each time, but lack validity if the artificial setting means participants do not behave as they would in real life.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
