# S09_Procedures_Reliability_and_Ethics - Lesson: Research methods: procedures, reliability, validity and ethics

## Goal
The learner explains standardised procedures and the control of extraneous variables (randomisation, allocation, counterbalancing), distinguishes reliability from validity, and applies the British Psychological Society's ethical guidelines to a research scenario.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.5 - Research procedures and quality: standardisation, control of extraneous variables, reliability, validity, and BPS ethical guidelines

## How to teach this
Ask the learner why it would be unfair to test one group of participants in a warm, quiet room and another group in a cold, noisy corridor -- standardised procedures exist to prevent exactly that kind of unfairness. Every named study or theory in this course (e.g. Murdock, Bartlett, Piaget, McGarrigle and Donaldson, Hughes, Dweck, Gibson, Gregory, Gilchrist and Nesberg, Bruner and Minturn, Asch, Milgram, Adorno, Piliavin, Von Frisch, Darwin, Hebb, Penfield, Tulving, Wiles, Kaij) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of psychology, checked against real psychological knowledge when this course was built -- present it as real research, never as illustrative fiction, and if the learner misremembers who did a study or what it found, correct this explicitly. AQA GCSE Psychology has a substantial Research Methods strand (3.1.4, including 3.1.4.1 Data Handling): sampling methods, experimental design, types of data, and descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, range) are examined with real calculations, not just definitions -- every numerical worked example in this course was computed and checked with Python when the course was built, and the learner should always be made to show their working for a calculation, since AQA awards credit for correct method as well as a correct final answer. Diagrams this course cannot draw (e.g. the multi-store model boxes, visual illusions, brain lobes, a neuron, a scatter diagram) are described precisely in words -- have the learner sketch or describe each one back, since being able to label a diagram accurately is itself examined.

#### 4.5 Research procedures and quality: standardisation, control of extraneous variables, reliability, validity, and BPS ethical guidelines
Good research planning requires **standardised procedures** -- giving every participant the same instructions, materials, environment and timing, as far as possible -- so that any difference found in the DV can be more confidently attributed to the IV rather than to inconsistencies in how the study was run. Extraneous variables are further controlled through: **randomisation** (using chance to decide something, e.g. the order materials are presented in, to prevent researcher bias from influencing the outcome); **allocation** (how participants are assigned to conditions, e.g. random allocation, to spread individual differences evenly across conditions in an independent groups design); and **counterbalancing** (in a repeated measures design, half the participants complete the conditions in one order and half in the reverse order, to cancel out order effects such as practice or fatigue across the group as a whole). Two further qualities matter when judging research: **reliability** is the consistency of a measure or finding -- would the same result be obtained if the study, or the measurement, were repeated? (e.g. two observers agreeing closely, or a questionnaire giving similar scores if the same person completes it twice); **validity** is whether a study or measure genuinely measures what it claims to measure, and whether its findings can be trusted/generalised -- a highly controlled laboratory study might be reliable (consistent) but lower in validity if the artificial setting means the behaviour observed does not reflect how people would really behave. Finally, psychological research must follow **ethical considerations**, based on the **British Psychological Society (BPS)** guidelines, including: informed consent (participants agree to take part, understanding what is involved, with special care for anyone unable to give full consent, e.g. children); the right to withdraw at any time, without penalty; protection from physical and psychological harm; confidentiality of participants' data and identity; and full debriefing after the study, including an explanation of the study's true aims where any deception was necessary, and support to deal with any distress caused.
