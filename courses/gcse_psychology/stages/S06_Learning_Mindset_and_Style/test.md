## Test items
1. Explain how the type of praise a child receives can influence which mindset (fixed or growth) they develop. [3 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by self-efficacy, and how a growth mindset can affect it. [2 marks]
3. Describe the idea of 'learning styles' (e.g. verbalisers and visualisers), and explain Willingham's evidence-based criticism of the idea that teaching should be matched to an individual's learning style. [4 marks]
4. According to Dweck's mindset theory, a person who avoids challenging tasks for fear that failing will reveal a lack of ability most likely holds which mindset? Choose every correct option.
   A. a fixed mindset
   B. a growth mindset
   C. an egocentric mindset
   D. a constructivist mindset

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 praising ability/outcome (e.g. 'you're so clever') tends to encourage a fixed mindset; B1 praising effort/process (e.g. 'you worked really hard') tends to encourage a growth mindset; B1 correct reasoning for why, e.g. ability-praise can make a child fear losing that label and avoid harder challenges, while effort-praise frames difficulty as part of learning.
2. [2] B1 self-efficacy is a person's belief in their own capability to succeed at a task; B1 a growth mindset (seeing effort/strategy as improving ability) tends to strengthen self-efficacy, especially after setbacks.
3. [4] B1 learning styles proposes individuals have a preferred way of taking in information, e.g. verbalisers (words/text) vs visualisers (images/diagrams); B1 the claim is that matching teaching to this preference should improve learning; B1 Willingham's review found this specific matching claim is not well supported by controlled evidence; B1 instead, teaching method should be matched to the content itself (e.g. diagrams for spatial content), not the learner's stated preference.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
