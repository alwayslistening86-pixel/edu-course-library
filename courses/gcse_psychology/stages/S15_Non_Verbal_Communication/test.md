# S15_Non_Verbal_Communication - Test: Non-verbal communication and its explanations

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: multiple-choice, short-answer, calculation and extended-writing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, before any marking. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain what postural echo is, and what it generally signals. [2 marks]
2. Explain how personal space can vary with culture, and why standing too close for the expected personal space can cause discomfort. [3 marks]
3. Explain, using evidence from neonates and congenitally blind individuals, why Darwin argued basic facial expressions of emotion are innate. [4 marks]
4. Describe Yuki et al.'s emoticon study and explain what it suggests about non-verbal behaviour. [4 marks]
5. Which piece of evidence would most directly support the claim that a basic facial expression of emotion is innate rather than learned? Choose every correct option.
   A. the expression is also shown by congenitally blind individuals who could not have learned it by watching others
   B. the expression differs between cultures
   C. the expression only appears after years of social interaction
   D. the expression is unique to one specific culture

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 unconsciously mirroring another person's posture; B1 generally signals rapport/agreement/that a conversation is going well.
2. [3] B1 different cultures typically maintain different preferred conversational distances; B1 correctly explained/exemplified; B1 standing closer than the distance expected in that culture/relationship can feel like an intrusion, causing discomfort.
3. [4] B1 Darwin argued basic emotional expressions are evolved, biologically inherited and universal, rather than learned by each culture separately; B1 neonates show recognisable basic expressions despite having had essentially no opportunity to learn them by watching others; B1 congenitally blind individuals also show recognisable basic expressions despite being unable to have learned them visually; B1 both pieces of evidence correctly used to support an innate, rather than learned, explanation.
4. [4] B1 Japanese participants focused more on the eyes, and American participants more on the mouth, when interpreting an emoticon's emotion; B1 consistent with culturally different emoticon styles/display rules in each country; B1 correctly attributed to Yuki et al.; B1 suggesting that at least some non-verbal interpretation is learned/culturally specific, rather than purely innate/universal.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
