# GCSE Psychology (AQA 8182) - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
Paper 1 covers 3.1.1-3.1.4 (S01-S10): four 25-mark sections, one per topic (Memory, Perception, Development, Research methods), each mixing multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions. Paper 2 covers 3.2.1-3.2.4 (S11-S19): the same four-section structure (Social influence, Language/thought/communication, Brain and neuropsychology, Psychological problems). Questions are original; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests, keeping each paper to its real topic split and AQA's real question styles (including diagram-based questions, described precisely in words). The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest.

## 10 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. [Paper 1] A participant is given a list of 16 unrelated words and asked to recall them immediately. Explain, using the multi-store model, why words from the start and end of the list are usually recalled better than words from the middle. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 1] Outline Bartlett's theory of reconstructive memory, and explain one way this differs from the multi-store model's view of memory. [4 marks]
3. [Paper 1] A researcher shows participants a photo containing ambiguous depth information. Compare briefly how Gibson and Gregory would explain how the participant perceives depth in the photo. [4 marks]
4. In Piaget's stage theory, at what age range does a child typically enter the concrete operational stage and begin to master conservation? Choose every correct option.
   A. around 7 to 11 years
   B. around 0 to 2 years
   C. around 2 to 7 years
   D. around 11 years and over
5. [Paper 1] A psychologist wants to find out whether listening to music while studying affects the number of words recalled from a list. State a suitable null hypothesis and a suitable (directional or non-directional) alternative hypothesis for this study. [3 marks]
6. [Paper 1] Eight participants recall the following numbers of words in a memory test: 4, 9, 6, 9, 5, 7, 8, 9. Calculate the mean, median and mode, showing your working. [4 marks]
7. [Paper 2] Explain, using Milgram's agency theory, why a participant in a study on obedience might obey an instruction they personally believe is wrong. [4 marks]
8. Which sampling method involves selecting every nth person from a list of the target population? Choose every correct option.
   A. systematic sampling
   B. random sampling
   C. opportunity sampling
   D. stratified sampling
9. [Paper 2] A psychologist records the number of bystanders present and whether a person stopped to help someone who dropped their shopping, across 10 trials. Explain how this method (a correlation) differs from an experiment, and give one weakness of using correlation here. [3 marks]
10. [Paper 2] Discuss the biological and psychological explanations of addiction, and evaluate the evidence for each. [8 marks]

[4] B1 memories are not accurate recordings but are actively reconstructed at the point of recall; B1 using schemas/existing knowledge, with 'effort after meaning' -- distorting the memory to fit what is expected/familiar; B1 the multi-store model treats memory as a more passive, faithful store/flow through stages; B1 clear contrast drawn between an active/constructive process and a passive storage process.
3. [4] B1 Gibson (direct theory): the visual environment itself provides enough information (e.g. motion parallax, texture gradient) for perception to happen directly, with no need for inference; B1 Gregory (constructivist theory): perception is an active, indirect process, using past experience and expectation to construct/infer a best guess at what is seen; B1 Gregory's theory better explains why the same ambiguous image or illusion can be perceived differently/incorrectly; B1 clear, correctly attributed contrast between the two named theorists.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B1 null hypothesis: there is no difference in the number of words recalled between those who study with music and those who study in silence (any difference is due to chance); B1 alternative hypothesis correctly states a difference (or a direction, e.g. fewer words recalled with music) is predicted; B1 both hypotheses clearly and correctly worded, referring to the actual IV and DV of the study.
6. [4] M1 mean: (4+9+6+9+5+7+8+9)/8 = 57/8; A1 mean = 7.125 (accept 7.1 or 7.13); M1 median: sort to 4,5,6,7,8,9,9,9, average the two middle values (7+8)/2; A1 median = 7.5, and mode = 9 (the most frequent value).
7. [4] B1 Milgram's agency theory: a person shifts from an 'autonomous state' (acting on their own conscience) to an 'agentic state' (seeing themselves as an agent carrying out another person's wishes, and so not personally responsible); B1 obedience is more likely with a legitimate authority figure; B1 proximity to the authority figure/victim, and cultural upbringing (respect for authority), affect how far this obedience goes; B1 correctly and specifically applied to the scenario, not just a general definition.

[3] B1 a correlation measures the association/relationship between two naturally occurring co-variables, with no variable deliberately manipulated by the researcher, unlike an experiment which manipulates an IV; B1 correctly names the two co-variables (number of bystanders; whether help was given); B1 weakness: correlation cannot establish cause and effect, e.g. a third, unmeasured factor might explain the relationship.

## Grading
Mark short answers and multiple-choice against the mark schemes; mark calculations with method shown (a correct final answer with no valid method shown earns only what the mark scheme allows for a bare answer); mark extended-writing questions by levels. A pass needs at least 50% of the 200 marks overall and at least 40% on each paper. Report the total, the percentage per paper and the weakest topic areas. AQA's grade boundaries change every series, so the course does not claim a predicted grade.
