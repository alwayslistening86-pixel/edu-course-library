# S19_R035_Planning_a_Campaign - Lesson: R035 Topic Area 3: planning a health promotion campaign

## Goal
The learner can plan a health promotion campaign covering aims related to PIES, timescales, resources, safety, communication, appropriateness, engagement methods and feedback methods.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R035-3.1a - Planning a campaign: aims related to PIES: write clear aims for a target audience
- R035-3.1b - Planning a campaign: timescales, resources, safety, communication and appropriateness: plan the practical detail of delivery
- R035-3.1c - Planning a campaign: methods to engage the target audience: choose and justify engagement methods
- R035-3.1d - Planning a campaign: feedback methods: choose ways to collect feedback on own performance

## How to teach this
Give the learner a public health challenge (for example, encouraging Year 10 students to drink more water) and build a plan live, strand by strand, asking 'what would you actually need to decide here?' before revealing the spec's own list. This models the thinking process the learner will need to do independently for their own Task 2 (T2).

#### R035-3.1a Planning a campaign: aims related to PIES
A campaign's aims state what it wants to change, improve or educate people about, and should be written so they are clear and specific rather than vague (for example, 'to increase Year 10 students' daily water intake to reduce dehydration-related tiredness' rather than 'to make students healthier'). Aims should be related to PIES: which physical, intellectual, emotional or social benefit is the campaign ultimately working towards? A strong aim names the target audience, the specific behaviour or knowledge to change, and the PIES benefit expected as a result, making it possible to judge afterwards whether the campaign succeeded.

#### R035-3.1b Planning a campaign: timescales, resources, safety, communication and appropriateness
Timescales set out when planning, preparation and delivery will happen, with realistic deadlines for each stage. Resources needed cover materials (leaflets, posters, props), equipment, budget and people (helpers, a venue). Safety considerations address risks in the delivery, for example checking a room for trip hazards, having any food-related activity comply with allergy rules, or ensuring information given is medically accurate and not harmful. Communication methods are the channels chosen to reach the audience (talk, poster, leaflet, video, interactive activity) and should suit the audience's age and preferences. Appropriateness to individuals means checking the campaign content, language and activities suit the specific target audience's age, culture, ability and needs.

#### R035-3.1c Planning a campaign: methods to engage the target audience
Engagement methods are the specific techniques used to hold the audience's attention and encourage participation, such as interactive quizzes, demonstrations, props, group discussion, incentives (stickers, small prizes) or storytelling. The method chosen should be justified by reference to the target audience: a quiz with prizes might suit younger children, while a discussion-based session might suit adults who want to ask questions. Learners should be able to choose at least two engagement methods for a given audience and explain why each is likely to work, linking the method's features to what is known about that audience's interests and attention span.

#### R035-3.1d Planning a campaign: feedback methods
Feedback methods are ways to collect the audience's views on the campaign's delivery, used later in the evaluation stage. Options include a short written questionnaire, verbal feedback collected during or after the session, a show of hands or simple rating scale, or an observation checklist completed by the deliverer. The method chosen should suit the audience (a simple smiley-face rating scale for young children; a written questionnaire with open questions for older audiences) and should ask questions that will actually help the learner evaluate their own performance later, such as 'what did you learn?' and 'what could be better next time?'.

## Explicitly not here
Actually delivering the campaign and evaluating performance (next stage). Writing the full Task 2 (T2) plan for assessment (a later stage covers the evidence requirements directly).
