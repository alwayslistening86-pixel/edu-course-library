# S07_Safeguarding - Lesson: Safeguarding service users

## Goal
The learner can explain what safeguarding means, who needs it, the impacts of a lack of it, the procedures and staff training that provide it, and how DBS checks work.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R032-4.1a - Service users who need safeguarding: know what safeguarding means and why specific groups need it
- R032-4.1b - Impacts of a lack of safeguarding: explain physical, intellectual, emotional and social impacts
- R032-4.1c - Safeguarding procedures: policy and Designated Safeguarding Lead: explain the role of the policy and the DSL
- R032-4.1d - Safeguarding training for all staff: explain what training gives staff the ability to do
- R032-4.1e - Disclosure and Barring Service checks: distinguish standard checks, enhanced checks and the barred list and give reasons for DBS checks

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Why might a nursery worker need a background check but a shop assistant might not?' Take answers, then define safeguarding. Handle the topic calmly and factually; the exam asks for procedures and impacts, not graphic detail. Note the spec's 'does not include' rule: details of an individual setting's own safeguarding policy are not needed.

#### R032-4.1a Service users who need safeguarding
Safeguarding means protecting people's health, wellbeing and human rights, and enabling them to live free from harm, abuse and neglect. The spec lists who may need it and why: vulnerable groups such as homeless people; children; people with physical and learning disabilities; people with mental health conditions; older adults in residential care settings; people with a sensory impairment such as sight loss or hearing loss; and people in residential care who are dependent on carers (children and older adults). Reasons include that they may be unable to protect themselves, may not recognise or report abuse, may depend on others for personal care, may be isolated, or may be less believed. Have the learner give a reason specific to each group.

#### R032-4.1b Impacts of a lack of safeguarding
If safeguarding fails, the service user can be harmed in every PIES area. Physical: injuries, malnutrition, pain, illness untreated. Intellectual: loss of skills, poor concentration, lack of development or education. Emotional: fear, anxiety, depression, low self-esteem. Social: withdrawal, isolation, loss of trust in others and difficulty forming relationships. For example, a resident left in soiled clothes may get infections and sores (physical), feel humiliated (emotional) and avoid others (social). Use a linked chain and apply to the group in the question. Impacts may be long term, not only immediate, for example a child neglected may fall behind at school.

#### R032-4.1c Safeguarding procedures: policy and Designated Safeguarding Lead
Care settings have a safeguarding policy: a written document setting out how the setting protects service users, what to do if abuse is suspected or disclosed, and who to tell. It gives staff clear steps, so action is quick and consistent. Each setting has a Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL): the person with responsibility for safeguarding. They are the point of contact for concerns, decide whether to refer to other agencies such as social services or the police, keep records and make sure staff are trained. Explain the benefit: service users are protected, staff know their duty and concerns are not ignored. Do not learn a specific setting's policy; explain the role of policies and the DSL in general terms.

#### R032-4.1d Safeguarding training for all staff
All staff, not just managers, must have safeguarding training so that they can do four things. Be aware of their duty to report a serious concern. Know the setting's procedures for reporting a disclosure of abuse or serious concern (do not promise secrecy, listen, record, report to the DSL). Recognise possible signs of abuse or harm: unexplained bruises, changes in behaviour, becoming withdrawn, weight loss, poor hygiene, fear of certain people. And know who to report to, usually the DSL or line manager. Benefit: abuse is spotted earlier, the right people are informed and service users are protected, creating a culture where concerns are taken seriously.

#### R032-4.1e Disclosure and Barring Service checks
The DBS provides criminal record checks so that employers can make safer recruitment decisions and prevent unsuitable people working with vulnerable groups. Three parts are named. A standard check shows spent and unspent convictions, cautions, reprimands and final warnings. An enhanced check shows this and can also include relevant information held by local police forces, and may include a check of the barred list; it is used for roles involving regular work with children or vulnerable adults such as care workers and teachers. The barred list contains people banned by law from working with children or vulnerable adults in certain roles. Reasons for DBS checks for all staff: to protect service users, keep unsuitable people out, reassure service users and families, meet legal requirements and protect the setting's reputation. Note: a DBS check does not guarantee safety; it only shows past record, so training and supervision are still needed.
