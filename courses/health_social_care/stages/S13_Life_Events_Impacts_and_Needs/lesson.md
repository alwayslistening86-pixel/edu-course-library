# S13_Life_Events_Impacts_and_Needs - Lesson: R033 life events, their impacts and the needs they create

## Goal
The learner can classify expected and unexpected life events, explain the five kinds of impact, and turn impacts into specific needs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R033-2.1a - Expected and unexpected life events: classify events as physical, relationship or life-circumstance and expected or unexpected
- R033-2.1b - Impacts of life events: physical, intellectual, emotional, social, financial: explain the five kinds of impact with chains of reasoning
- R033-2.1c - Identifying an individual's needs from the impacts: turn each impact into a specific need

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Name a change in someone's life that they planned and one that came out of nowhere. How did each change what they needed?' Then build the three-step chain: event, impact, need. Use fictional people. Link to R032: rights, person-centred values and communication all shape how needs are met.

#### R033-2.1a Expected and unexpected life events
Life events are changes that affect a person's life. Expected events are anticipated or normal for the life stage (starting school, puberty, retirement, menopause, moving out). Unexpected events are not planned or foreseen (accident, sudden illness, redundancy, bereavement of a young person). The spec groups events into three: physical events (accident or injury, ill health, genetic disorders, puberty, menopause); relationship changes (starting or ending relationships, divorce or separation, parenthood, bereavement); and life circumstances (school starting, changing or exclusion, redundancy, imprisonment, retirement, bankruptcy). Note that one event can be either: a pregnancy may be expected or a surprise; a death may be expected in illness or sudden. The learner should be able to say why an event is classed one way for a given individual.

#### R033-2.1b Impacts of life events: physical, intellectual, emotional, social, financial
The spec asks for five kinds of impact. Physical: illness or tiredness, pain, weight loss or gain, mobility, appearance. Intellectual: adapting to change, learning new skills, learning impairment. Emotional: mental health, grief, anxiety, stress, depression, self-esteem and self-image. Social: lifestyle choices, personal relationships with friends and family. Financial: change in income, increased costs, change in wealth. Example, redundancy: financial (lost income, bills), emotional (stress, lowered self-esteem), social (less contact with colleagues, cannot afford outings), physical (poor sleep, comfort eating), intellectual (needs to learn new skills or adapt to job hunting). Explain, do not list: say why the event causes each impact. The spec notes that these impacts differ by individual and can be positive as well as negative (parenthood can bring joy and fulfilment as well as tiredness and increased costs).

#### R033-2.1c Identifying an individual's needs based on the impacts
A need is something the person requires to cope with, adapt or recover. Each need should come directly from an impact. Spec examples: weight gain leads to dietary advice and support; stress or anxiety leads to coping mechanisms, someone to talk to and mental health support; loss of income leads to financial advice and support; learning impairment leads to specialist support, independent living and equipment. More: reduced mobility needs physiotherapy, aids or adaptations; loneliness after bereavement needs social contact and emotional support; a new baby's tiredness needs practical help and rest. Teach the sentence pattern: 'Because [event] led to [impact], [name] needs [need], so that [purpose].' Keep needs specific and personal, and remember the person-centred value of individuality: two people with the same event may need different things.

## Explicitly not here
The sources of support that meet needs (later stage), and the interview and evidence skills for Task 2a (next stage).
