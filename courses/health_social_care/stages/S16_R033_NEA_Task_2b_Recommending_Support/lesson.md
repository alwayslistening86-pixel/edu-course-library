# S16_R033_NEA_Task_2b_Recommending_Support - Lesson: R033 NEA Task 2b: research and recommending personalised support

## Goal
The learner can research sources of support with a method, record and reference them, and knows what justification and person-centred application look like at MB3.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R033-3.1e - Researching and recommending personalised support: match, coordinate, justify and apply person-centred values
- R033-SKRES - NEA skill: research as an investigation, and referencing: search with a method, record sources and use own words
- R033-T2b1 - Task 2b evidence: researched information on formal, informal and charity support: know what researched information means and how to record it
- R033-T2b2 - Task 2b evidence: justifying support and applying person-centred values: know how justification and application differ from description

## How to teach this
Ask: 'What is the difference between googling a service and researching it?' Use the spec's definition: research is searching carefully, with a method, to answer a question; it is wider than finding a fact and more focused than reading widely. Then practise the skill on a fictional individual and a different topic from the live assignment. The tutor must not write, model or correct the learner's recommendation; the learner must produce it independently. The tutor may explain what the criteria words mean.

#### R033-3.1e Researching and recommending personalised support
Recommending support means researching what exists for this individual's needs, choosing what fits, and explaining why. The spec's four parts: match support provision to specific individual needs (consider whether provision is statutory or private, location, availability, costs); offer coordinated care and treatment (different services working together so that gaps and duplication are avoided, for example GP referring to a physiotherapist while a charity offers emotional support); justify choices made (give reasons why this support meets that need and why it suits this person better than alternatives); and apply person-centred values (chosen from those listed in R032: individuality, choice, rights, independence, privacy, dignity, respect, partnership, encouraging decision making). Teach the logic chain: need, support, how it meets the need, and which value is respected. Example of person-centred application: choice: the person may prefer group or one-to-one counselling, so both are offered.

#### R033-SKRES NEA skill: research as an investigation, and referencing
Research is more than picking the first five search hits or collecting a pile of leaflets. Steps for a learner: define the question (what does this person need?); plan where to look (NHS and local council websites, charity sites, local directories, phone or visit where safe); search using specific terms including the location; compare sources, checking who runs them and how recent they are; note what is relevant (services, eligibility, cost, opening hours, how to contact); record each source as you go, so referencing is easy; and use your own words. Use a research log with source, date accessed, what it offered and how relevant it is. Do not copy text; where you quote, use quote marks. The teacher may refer you to the teaching content but must not direct you to specific research tasks. Different learners may choose similar sources, but identical work in a cohort would be highly unusual.

#### R033-T2b1 Task 2b evidence: researched information on formal, informal and charity support
Criterion (12 marks): information, based on research, about the support available for formal, informal and charity sources. MB1 (1 to 4): brief. MB2 (5 to 8): sound. MB3 (9 to 12): comprehensive. To reach MB3, the information must come from research (visible through sources or a research log), cover all three source types, and be comprehensive: specific services, what they provide, practical details such as location, cost, availability and eligibility. Brief information names a service with a line on what it does. Ask the learner to use the mark-band table as a checklist of their own; three source types, each with enough breadth and depth. The learner records sources properly.

#### R033-T2b2 Task 2b evidence: justifying support and applying person-centred values
Criterion (9 marks) has two strands. Justification of how support will meet the needs of the individual: MB1 (1 to 3) basic; MB2 (4 to 6) sound; MB3 (7 to 9) comprehensive. Application of person-centred values in recommending personalised support: basic, sound, effective. Justify means the reasons for doing something are explained in full: not 'the GP will help' but 'because Ravi's blood pressure needs monitoring, the GP practice within walking distance can provide regular checks, which meets his medical need without transport costs'. Apply values means showing the value in action in the recommendation (for example choice by offering options, individuality by tailoring support to his job loss, partnership by involving his wife). The values must be chosen from R032's list. To evidence it, the learner links every recommendation to a need from Task 2a and to a value, in their own words.
