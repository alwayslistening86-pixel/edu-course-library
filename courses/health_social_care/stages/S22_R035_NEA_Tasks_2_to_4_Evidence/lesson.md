# S22_R035_NEA_Tasks_2_to_4_Evidence - Lesson: R035 Task 2 to 4 evidence: planning, delivering and evaluating the campaign for assessment

## Goal
The learner knows what MB1 to MB3 evidence looks like for the campaign plan, for the introduction and delivery of the campaign, and for the written self-evaluation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R035-T2 - Task 2 evidence: a comprehensive campaign plan: cover all seven planning strands with understanding
- R035-T3a - Task 3 evidence: introduction and communication skills: know what an effective introduction and communication look like
- R035-T3b - Task 3 evidence: delivering a simple, sound or complex campaign: know what complex means and how observation is recorded
- R035-T4a - Task 4 evidence: evaluating planning, communication and engagement: write a comprehensive evaluation of strengths and weaknesses
- R035-T4b - Task 4 evidence: suggestions for improvement and use of feedback: write detailed suggestions and make full use of feedback

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to imagine grading two campaign plans, one that lists 'talk to a group about smoking' and one that specifies every planning strand in detail, and to guess which reaches which band. Use their instinct to introduce the mark bands precisely, then repeat for delivery and evaluation. This stage is entirely about knowing what the evidence needs to show; the learner produces the actual plan, delivery and evaluation independently in the live set assignment.

#### R035-T2 Task 2 evidence: a comprehensive campaign plan
Marking criterion (12 marks): production of a plan and demonstration of understanding of the aims (related to PIES), timescales, resources, safety, communication, methods (to engage the target audience) and feedback methods. MB1 (1 to 4): a basic plan showing limited understanding of these strands. MB2 (5 to 8): a sound plan showing partial understanding. MB3 (9 to 12): a comprehensive plan showing full understanding. Reaching MB3 means every one of the seven strands from Topic Area 3 is present, specific to the learner's own chosen challenge and target audience, and shows clear reasoning for each decision rather than a bare list of headings with one line each.

#### R035-T3a Task 3 evidence: introduction and communication skills
Marking criterion (6 marks): the effectiveness of the introduction to the campaign and communication skills demonstrated. MB1 (1 to 2): the introduction is brief and communication skills are basic. MB2 (3 to 4): the introduction is adequate and communication skills are sound. MB3 (5 to 6): the introduction is effective and communication skills are effective. Effectiveness at MB3 depends on the audience actually being welcomed and settled, the purpose being made clear, and communication (pace, clarity, body language, engagement with questions) holding the audience's attention throughout, judged from the teacher's direct observation during delivery.

#### R035-T3b Task 3 evidence: delivering a simple, sound or complex campaign
Marking criterion (6 marks): the complexity of the health promotion campaign delivered. MB1 (1 to 2): delivers a simple campaign. MB2 (3 to 4): delivers a sound campaign. MB3 (5 to 6): delivers a complex campaign. 'Complex' in this context means a campaign that addresses its aims thoroughly, uses well-chosen engagement methods suited to the audience, adapts to how the audience responds, and covers the topic with depth rather than a single simple message. This is judged by the teacher's observation, recorded on the individualised Teacher Observation Record Form required by OCR's guidance, not by anything the learner writes afterwards.

#### R035-T4a Task 4 evidence: evaluating planning, communication and engagement
This criterion is marked together with the improvement-suggestions criterion below as one 12-mark judgement. For the evaluation part, MB1 (basic) shows limited evaluation of strengths and weaknesses; MB2 (sound) shows adequate coverage; MB3 (comprehensive) shows a full evaluation of strengths and weaknesses across all three areas: planning, communication skills and how individuals were engaged. A comprehensive evaluation uses specific evidence (feedback comments, what was observed) for each strength and weakness claimed, across all three areas, rather than a general comment about how the session 'went'.

#### R035-T4b Task 4 evidence: suggestions for improvement and use of feedback
The second half of the same 12-mark criterion. MB1: limited suggestions for improvement and limited use of feedback. MB2: adequate suggestions and partial use of feedback. MB3: detailed suggestions for improvement and full use of feedback. Reaching MB3 means every suggestion is specific (what exactly would change), justified (why it would help) and clearly traceable to real feedback collected from the audience, addressing weaknesses identified across planning, communication and engagement, not just one area. OCR's guidance stresses this must be the learner's own written work, produced without being led through the process.
