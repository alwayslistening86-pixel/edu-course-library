# S18_R035_Factors_Influencing_Health - Lesson: R035 Topic Area 2: factors influencing health, leading a healthy lifestyle and barriers

## Goal
The learner can explain the four groups of factors that influence health, describe what individuals can do to lead a healthy lifestyle and the PIES benefits of doing so, and explain barriers that stop people leading a healthy lifestyle.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R035-2.1a - Factors influencing health: lifestyle choices: explain how alcohol, diet, exercise, sexual behaviour, smoking and self-help matter
- R035-2.1b - Factors influencing health: physical and mental health: explain illness, stress, anxiety and genetics as influences
- R035-2.1c - Factors influencing health: education and socio-economic: explain income, employment, literacy, qualifications and culture
- R035-2.1d - Factors influencing health: access to health services: explain location, opening times, local resources and availability
- R035-2.2a - What individuals can do to lead a healthy lifestyle: describe the healthy choices, eating, hygiene, mental health and activity
- R035-2.2b - Benefits of a healthy lifestyle in PIES terms: explain physical, intellectual, emotional and social benefits
- R035-2.3 - Barriers to leading a healthy lifestyle: explain advertising, peer pressure, lack of support and cost

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to list everything that affects whether a teenager eats well and exercises. Sort their answers into the four groups (lifestyle, physical/mental health, education/socio-economic, access to services) to introduce the structure. This links directly to Task 1c and 1d evidence in a later stage, where the learner explains these factors and barriers for a real target audience, so accuracy here matters.

#### R035-2.1a Factors influencing health: lifestyle choices
Lifestyle choices that influence health include alcohol consumption (heavy or regular drinking raises the risk of liver disease and some cancers), diet (a diet high in fat, sugar or salt contributes to obesity and heart disease), exercise (lack of physical activity is linked to obesity, poor mental health and weaker muscles and bones), sexual behaviour (unprotected sex raises the risk of sexually transmitted infections and unplanned pregnancy) and smoking (linked to lung disease, cancer and heart disease). 'Self-help' covers what individuals can do for themselves, such as choosing to stop smoking or reduce alcohol intake. For each, be able to name the choice and one specific health consequence.

#### R035-2.1b Factors influencing health: physical and mental health
Existing physical illness (for example a long-term condition such as diabetes or arthritis) can limit activity and increase risk from other health issues. Mental health conditions such as depression or anxiety affect motivation, sleep, appetite and the ability to engage in healthy behaviours, and stress raises blood pressure and can lead to unhealthy coping behaviours such as comfort eating or drinking. Genetics also influences health: some conditions (for example certain cancers or heart conditions) run in families, meaning a person may be at higher risk regardless of lifestyle. Learners should be able to explain how one physical and one mental health factor each affect a person's overall health.

#### R035-2.1c Factors influencing health: education and socio-economic factors
Income affects the ability to afford healthy food, gym membership, private healthcare or safe housing. Employment status affects both income and access to workplace benefits such as health insurance or an active job versus a sedentary one; unemployment is also linked to poorer mental health. Literacy affects a person's ability to understand health information, medicine labels or NHS letters. Qualifications affect the type of job available and therefore income and working conditions. Culture affects beliefs about health, diet, and willingness to seek help (for example, some cultures may see mental health support as stigmatised). Explain how at least two of these interact, for example low income limiting access to healthy food.

#### R035-2.1d Factors influencing health: access to health services
Access depends on location (rural areas may have fewer GP surgeries or longer travel times to hospital), opening times (services only open during the working day are hard for some people to attend), local resources (whether a community has leisure centres, health clinics or specialist services nearby) and general availability (waiting times, whether a service takes new patients). Poor access to health services means health problems can go undiagnosed or untreated for longer, worsening outcomes. Learners should give an example of how poor access in one of these four areas leads to a worse health outcome, for example long GP waiting times delaying diagnosis of a serious condition.

#### R035-2.2a What individuals can do to lead a healthy lifestyle
The specification lists practical choices: not smoking, protecting skin from the sun, practising safe sex, eating a balanced diet, drinking alcohol in moderation, maintaining good personal hygiene, keeping the mind active (reading, puzzles, learning), and taking regular exercise. Learners should be able to describe each choice concretely, for example a balanced diet means eating the recommended portions from each food group and limiting processed food, and regular exercise means at least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week for adults. These choices map directly onto avoiding many of the risks studied in the lifestyle-factors item, so the two should be taught together.

#### R035-2.2b Benefits of a healthy lifestyle in PIES terms
Using the PIES framework already met in earlier stages: physical benefits include a stronger immune system, healthy weight and lower disease risk; intellectual benefits include better concentration and cognitive function, partly from good diet and exercise improving blood flow to the brain; emotional benefits include improved mood, self-esteem and reduced anxiety, partly through exercise releasing endorphins; social benefits include more energy and confidence to take part in social and community activities, such as team sports or clubs. A full answer covers all four PIES areas rather than only physical, and links each benefit to a specific healthy choice, for example: regular exercise improves physical fitness and also boosts mood (emotional).

#### R035-2.3 Barriers to leading a healthy lifestyle
Barriers include advertising and media (junk food and alcohol advertising can encourage unhealthy choices, and unrealistic body images in media can harm self-esteem), peer pressure (friends encouraging smoking, drinking or unhealthy eating to fit in), lack of support (no friends, family or role models encouraging healthy behaviour, making change harder to sustain) and cost (healthy food, gym membership or sports equipment can be more expensive than less healthy alternatives, and this particularly affects lower-income individuals). Learners should be able to explain how each barrier makes healthy living harder for a specific type of person, not just list the barrier's name.
