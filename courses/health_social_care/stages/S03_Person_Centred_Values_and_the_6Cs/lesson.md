# S03_Person_Centred_Values_and_the_6Cs - Lesson: Person-centred values and the 6Cs

## Goal
The learner can define the nine person-centred values and the 6Cs and give a specific example of a provider applying each in a named care setting.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R032-2.1a - Person-centred values and how providers apply them: define the nine person-centred values and give applied examples
- R032-2.1b - The 6Cs: qualities of a service practitioner: define the 6Cs and show how they deliver person-centred values

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Two nurses give you the same injection. One says nothing and walks off. The other introduces herself, asks if you are ready, explains what she is doing and asks how it felt. What is different, and which values does that show?' Let the learner name the values in their own words before matching to the spec's terms. Remember the spec says the values and 6Cs must be defined (know) and applied with examples (understand).

#### R032-2.1a Person-centred values and how providers apply them
Person-centred care puts the individual at the centre and treats them as a whole person, not a condition. The spec lists: individuality (treat each person as unique, with their own history, culture, preferences and needs; for example a care plan built around what matters to this resident), choice (offering options, such as what to wear or eat), rights (making sure the five rights are upheld), independence (letting people do what they can for themselves, for example providing a walking frame rather than a wheelchair to someone who can still walk), privacy (closing the curtains or door during personal care, knocking before entering), dignity (treating with respect and protecting self-worth, for example covering the person during a wash and speaking to them as an adult), respect (listening, using their preferred name, valuing their beliefs), partnership (working with the service user, family and other professionals as a team), and encouraging decision making of the service user (giving the information and time for them to decide). In exams, give a concrete action by a named person in a named setting.

#### R032-2.1b The 6Cs: qualities of a service practitioner
The 6Cs describe the qualities practitioners need to deliver person-centred values. Care: doing the job well for the person's wellbeing, being consistent and kind. Compassion: noticing distress and responding with kindness and empathy, for example sitting with a frightened patient. Competence: having the knowledge, skills and training to do the job properly, such as correct hand washing or safe moving and handling. Communication: listening, explaining clearly and sharing information with the team, essential to individuality and partnership. Courage: doing the right thing, for example reporting a concern about poor care or a safeguarding worry even if it is uncomfortable. Commitment: dedication to improving the experience of service users and to keep learning. Teach the connection: a competent nurse using clear communication respects choice; a courageous carer who reports abuse upholds protection from harm. Ask the learner to link each C to one value.

## Explicitly not here
Benefits of applying the values, and the effects of not applying them (next stage).
