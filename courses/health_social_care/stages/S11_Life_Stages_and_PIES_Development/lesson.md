# S11_Life_Stages_and_PIES_Development - Lesson: R033 life stages, milestones and PIES development

## Goal
The learner can describe key milestones and physical, intellectual, emotional and social development in each of the five life stages, and knows what Task 1 asks them to evidence about one individual's stage.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R033-1.1a - Life stages and key milestones: know the five age groups and milestone examples
- R033-1.1b - PIES development across the life stages: describe physical, intellectual, emotional and social development at each stage
- R033-T1a - Task 1 evidence: describing growth and development through a life stage using PIES: know what MB1 to MB3 look like for this criterion and how to evidence it

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Think of someone you know at each of five ages. What could they do at each age that they could not do earlier?' Draw a timeline together and add PIES headings. This is a NEA unit: the learner will later choose an individual, so teach content in general and use fictional people in practice. Do not create templates for the learner; the aim is that they can structure a description themselves.

#### R033-1.1a Life stages and key milestones
The spec uses five life stages: 4 to 10 years (childhood), 11 to 18 years (adolescence), 19 to 45 years (young adulthood), 46 to 65 years (middle adulthood) and 65+ years (older adulthood). Milestones are typical points of growth and development, and people reach them at different times. Childhood: learning to read and write, riding a bike, forming friendships, starting school. Adolescence: puberty, secondary school and exams, forming identity and independence, first romantic relationships. Young adulthood: leaving home, starting work or higher education, long-term partnerships, parenthood, physical peak. Middle adulthood: children leaving home, career peak or change, menopause, first signs of ageing. Older adulthood: retirement, grandparenthood, reduced mobility, bereavement of partner or friends. Stress that milestones are guidelines, not tests, and that an individual may be described as developing differently.

#### R033-1.1b PIES development across the life stages
PIES stands for Physical, Intellectual, Emotional and Social. The spec's headings: Physical: fine motor skills (small movements such as writing) and gross motor skills (large movements such as running), mobility, characteristic body changes, sexual characteristics, puberty, menopause and ageing characteristics. Intellectual: language development, sentence construction, logical thinking, problem solving, decision making and the deterioration of mental abilities. Emotional: bonding, different attachments, independence, self-confidence, self-image, self-esteem, love and affection. Social: relationships, social skills and responsibilities. Examples by stage: 4 to 10, fine motor skills improve so handwriting becomes neat, sentences get longer, friendships start; 11 to 18, puberty, abstract thinking, self-image concerns, peer groups; 19 to 45, physical peak, decision making about career and partners, long-term attachments, responsibilities; 46 to 65, menopause, mental abilities are steady but memory may slow, confidence may be high, roles shift; 65+, reduced mobility and strength, slower recall or dementia in some, bereavement, changing social circle.

#### R033-T1a Task 1 evidence: describing growth and development through a life stage using PIES
Task 1 has two criteria. The first, worth 6 marks, is a description of the growth and development of the individual through the life stage, using PIES. MB1 (1 to 2 marks): a brief description. MB2 (3 to 4): a sound description. MB3 (5 to 6): a comprehensive description, meaning complete, with depth and breadth. To evidence it: choose an individual (famous or known, anonymised with names changed or redacted) with enough information to cover the life stage; organise the description under each of the four PIES headings; give actual milestones and dates or ages for that individual, not general statements about all people; and use your own words. The teacher must check the individual is suitable, and the teacher must not create templates. Coaching point: 'brief' means a few facts with no detail; 'comprehensive' means every PIES area has specific, detailed information about this person. The tutor can rehearse the skill on a fictional person, but never on the live set assignment.

## Explicitly not here
The factors that affect development and how to evidence them (next stage), life events (later stage).
