# S01_How_The_Qualification_Works - Lesson: How the Cambridge National in Health and Social Care works

## Goal
The learner can say what the three units are, how each is assessed and graded, what the four performance objectives reward, how to read a mark band, and how R032 knowledge feeds the coursework units.

## Syllabus items taught here
- J835-STRUCT - Qualification structure, units, weightings and terminal rule: know the three units, how each is assessed, and the terminal assessment rule
- J835-PO - Performance Objectives PO1 to PO4 and their weightings: explain what each performance objective rewards
- J835-GRADE - Grading: raw marks, UMS, unit and qualification grade boundaries: work out how unit marks combine into an overall grade
- J835-DESC - NEA descriptor words and performance descriptors: read a mark band using the OCR descriptor words
- J835-SYN - Synoptic links between R032 and the NEA units: use R032 knowledge inside R033 and R035 evidence

## How to teach this
Open with: 'You will be graded on one exam and two pieces of coursework. Which do you think is worth more, and why might the exam be treated differently?' Let the learner guess, then reveal the numbers. Keep this stage short and practical: its job is to make the rest of the course feel navigable. Return to it before the NEA units so that the mark-band words are familiar.

#### J835-STRUCT Qualification structure, units, weightings and terminal rule
The qualification is the OCR Level 1/Level 2 Cambridge National in Health and Social Care (J835), a Technical Award for 14 to 16 year olds, 120 guided learning hours. The learner completes three units. R032 Principles of care in health and social care settings is the exam: 1 hour 15 minutes, 70 marks, six compulsory questions, set and marked by OCR, worth 40 per cent. R033 Supporting individuals through life events is coursework (NEA, non-examined assessment) worth 60 marks, marked by the teacher and moderated by OCR. The third unit is one optional unit; this course covers R035 Health promotion campaigns (60 marks) rather than R034 Creative and therapeutic activities. The two NEA units together are 60 per cent. The terminal rule means the exam must be sat in the final assessment series before certification, so an earlier sitting is only practice. NEA work is done from an OCR-set assignment that is live for a year and released each 1 June. Exam series are January and June.

#### J835-PO Performance Objectives PO1 to PO4 and their weightings
There are four performance objectives. PO1 is recall of knowledge and showing understanding (exam only). PO2 is applying knowledge and understanding, for example to a scenario or a chosen individual. PO3 is analysing and evaluating knowledge, understanding and performance. PO4 is demonstrating and applying skills and processes, such as researching, planning and delivering a campaign (NEA only). Overall weightings are PO1 16.5 to 20 per cent, PO2 35.5 to 38.5 per cent, PO3 24 to 26.5 per cent and PO4 19.5 per cent. The message for the learner: only about a fifth of the whole qualification is plain recall. Most marks reward using what you know on a person, a situation or your own campaign. In the exam the 8-mark extended question always assesses PO3, so it needs discussion or evaluation rather than description.

#### J835-GRADE Grading: raw marks, UMS, unit and qualification grade boundaries
Raw marks are R032 out of 70 and each NEA unit out of 60. OCR converts them to a uniform mark scale (UMS): R032 is worth 80 UMS and each NEA unit 60 UMS, so the qualification totals 200 UMS. Raw grade boundaries move each series, but the UMS boundaries never change. For a 60 UMS NEA unit: Distinction* at Level 2 is 54, Distinction 48, Merit 42, Pass 36, and at Level 1 Distinction 30, Merit 24, Pass 18. For the 80 UMS exam: 72, 64, 56, 48, then 40, 32, 24. Overall totals out of 200: Distinction* L2 180, Distinction L2 160, Merit L2 140, Pass L2 120, Distinction L1 100, Merit L1 80, Pass L1 60; below 60 is unclassified. Worked example: exam 60 UMS plus NEA 46 plus NEA 44 gives 150, which is Merit at Level 2. Have the learner compute two such totals and say what one more grade would need.

#### J835-DESC NEA descriptor words and performance descriptors
NEA is marked in three mark bands, MB1 to MB3, task by task. If nothing meets MB1 the task scores zero. OCR defines the words. MB1 words: basic (simplistic, undeveloped), brief (few facts, no detail or examples), limited (only some of what is needed), simple (a few parts not related to each other). MB2 words: adequate, partial, some (over half of what is expected), sound (valid and logical, mostly secure but not fully developed). MB3 words: comprehensive (complete, shows depth and breadth), detailed (point by point on all key information), effective (achieves the intended result), full, justified (reasons explained in full), clear, and consistently. The performance descriptors say a Distinction at Level 2 candidate uses detailed knowledge, a wide range of terminology, makes reasoned judgements and shows effective planning and perceptive evaluation. Exam command words (Appendix B) are taught later in R032-X2.

#### J835-SYN Synoptic links between R032 and the NEA units
Synoptic means using knowledge from across the qualification together. The spec names the links. In R033, Topic Area 2 (impacts of life events) draws on R032 rights, person-centred values and communication; Topic Area 3 (sources of support) draws on R032 person-centred values, and Task 2b requires the person-centred values to be chosen from those listed in R032. In R035, planning and delivering a campaign (Topic Areas 3 and 4) draw on R032 effective communication. Practical consequence: learn R032 first and properly. When the learner writes about support for an individual, the words individuality, choice, dignity or partnership are marks, not decoration. When delivering a campaign, the verbal and non-verbal skills of R032 are the criteria against which delivery is judged.
