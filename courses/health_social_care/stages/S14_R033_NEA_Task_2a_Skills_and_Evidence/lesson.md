# S14_R033_NEA_Task_2a_Skills_and_Evidence - Lesson: R033 NEA skills for Task 2a: interviewing, authenticity and evidencing life events

## Goal
The learner knows how to plan and record a consented interview, what counts as their own authentic and anonymised work, and what evidence each Task 2a criterion needs at MB3.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R033-SKAUTH - NEA rules: authenticity, anonymity, data protection, AI and feedback: know what counts as the learner's own work
- R033-SKINT - NEA skill: interviewing an individual with consent: plan, permission, conduct and record an interview
- R033-T2a1 - Task 2a evidence: describing two life events and the life stage they occurred in: know what the criterion asks for and how to evidence it
- R033-T2a2 - Task 2a evidence: explaining the impacts of the life event on five areas: know what MB3 needs across all five areas
- R033-T2a3 - Task 2a evidence: explaining the individual's needs based on the impacts: link each need back to a stated impact

## How to teach this
Ask: 'If a teacher did half your coursework for you, what would the marks show?' Use the answer to introduce authenticity. Stress that this stage teaches skills and rules and never supplies content or a template. The tutor can help the learner plan questions and check what the criteria mean, but the learner must write the assessed work themselves and the tutor must not write, draft, model or correct it. Practise interviews on a fictional role-play or family member on a different topic, not with the live task.

#### R033-SKAUTH NEA rules: authenticity, anonymity, data protection, AI and feedback
The rules in section 6 protect fairness. The learner's work must be their own, produced independently under teacher supervision, in their own words. Plagiarism is submitting someone else's work as your own or failing to acknowledge a source, including web text and AI. Quoting, even when referenced, does not show understanding; the learner must do something with the information. Sources must be referenced: quote marks, website address and date accessed, book or article title and year, and for AI the name of the tool and the date. Personal details of the individual must not be included: names can be redacted, no addresses, phone numbers or full-face images. Information about the individual must be stored confidentially and comply with the Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR. Feedback rules: teachers may give general reminders and factual feedback, but may not point out specific gaps, give model answers, headings or templates, or coach. Students must have completed the whole assignment before it is marked, and one resubmission after moderation is allowed. OCR-set assignments must not be used for practice.

#### R033-SKINT NEA skill: interviewing an individual with consent
Task 2a requires the learner to interview an individual who agrees to talk about their life events and the impact on them. The individual can be a family member, friend or peer. Before the interview, the centre obtains permission using a consent form, signed and dated, and follows its safeguarding, health and safety policies. Skill steps for the learner: plan open questions (what happened, how did it affect you, what did you need, who helped) organised by PIES and financial; choose a suitable time and place; explain the purpose and that the person can decline any question; listen actively and take notes; do not press for distressing detail; thank them. After the interview, the learner completes the Interview Authentication Form (in the set assignment), and the teacher must see evidence such as photographs or audio or contact with the person. A record of redacted notes accompanies the report. Personal details must be anonymised before submission, and information should be destroyed once post-results services have passed or within six months, whichever is sooner.

#### R033-T2a1 Task 2a evidence: describing two life events and the life stage they occurred in
Criterion (6 marks): a description of two life events and the life stage they occurred in for the individual chosen. MB1 (1 to 2): brief. MB2 (3 to 4): sound. MB3 (5 to 6): comprehensive. To evidence this, the learner needs two distinct events from the individual's interview, each identified (what happened and when), placed within the correct life stage from R033 Topic Area 1 (with age), and described with enough detail: circumstances, expected or unexpected, who was involved. The classification (physical event, relationship change or life circumstance) helps show understanding. Check: are there two events, is each in a stated life stage, and does the description include depth and breadth rather than a single sentence? The individual must have events that are appropriate to allow access to all criteria; the teacher checks this.

#### R033-T2a2 Task 2a evidence: explaining the impacts of the life event on five areas
Criterion (12 marks): an explanation of the impacts of the life event on physical, intellectual, emotional, social and financial aspects. MB1 (1 to 4): limited. MB2 (5 to 8): adequate. MB3 (9 to 12): comprehensive. The criterion is about all five areas, so evidence needs an explanation for each, drawn from what the individual said, with reasons why the event caused each impact. A limited answer covers a few areas with little reasoning; a comprehensive answer covers all five, uses specific details from the interview, shows how impacts link (for example loss of income leading to stress and reduced social life), and uses the vocabulary of R033. If an area truly did not apply, the learner should explain why. The individual chosen must have experienced an event that impacts all five areas, which is why the teacher checks suitability.

#### R033-T2a3 Task 2a evidence: explaining the individual's needs based on the impacts
Criterion (6 marks): explanation of the needs of the individual, based on the impacts of the life event. MB1 (1 to 2): limited. MB2 (3 to 4): adequate. MB3 (5 to 6): comprehensive. Needs must be tied to the impacts already explained, so they should be specific, personal and each traceable to an impact; a general list of needs that people commonly have is not enough. Evidence should include the need, the impact it responds to, and why the individual needs it (what would happen without it). The learner should also consider that needs may change over time and use the individual's own words from the interview. These needs feed directly into Task 2b, where support is researched, so accuracy here matters.
