# S17_R035_Public_Health_Issues - Lesson: R035 Topic Area 1: current public health issues and why a healthy society matters

## Goal
The learner can explain why a healthy society matters, describe current public health challenges and the organisations tackling them, and describe a real health promotion campaign, its benefits and its target audience.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R035-1.1 - The importance of a healthy society: give four reasons a healthy society matters
- R035-1.2a - Current public health challenges: explain what each challenge is, who it affects and its impact on society
- R035-1.2b - Organisations promoting public health: know the roles of charities, the NHS, government agencies and WHO
- R035-1.3a - Current health promotion campaigns: describe examples of campaigns and what they set out to do
- R035-1.3b - Benefits of a health promotion campaign to individuals and society: explain behaviour change, education, empowerment and quality of life
- R035-1.3c - Target audience of a campaign: identify who a campaign is aimed at and why

## How to teach this
Open with: 'If nobody in the country ever got healthier, what would happen to hospitals, workplaces and family life?' Build the four reasons from the answer, then move into named challenges the learner can see in the news or at school (obesity, vaping and smoking, mental health). This stage is knowledge for the optional R035 unit only, building on nothing from R032 or R033 directly, though PIES and person-centred ideas from earlier stages will resurface.

#### R035-1.1 The importance of a healthy society
OCR's specification gives four reasons a healthy society matters: control of communicable diseases (fewer outbreaks spreading through a population), decreased cost of care (less spent treating preventable illness, freeing NHS and social care resources), decreased sickness and dependency (fewer people needing time off work or relying on others for daily tasks), and increased life expectancy (people living longer, healthier lives). Learners should be able to state all four and give a brief reason for each, for example: fewer people smoking means fewer cases of lung disease, which reduces hospital admissions and treatment costs. Link each reason to both the individual and society to show the wider impact, not just personal benefit.

#### R035-1.2a Current public health challenges
The specification lists challenge areas including obesity, flu and other viruses, alcohol, heart disease and stroke, sexual health, cancer, physical activity levels, mental health, smoking, and child dental health. For each challenge studied, the learner must be able to state what the challenge is (for example, obesity is having an unhealthy amount of body fat that increases health risks), who is affected (age groups, regions, or groups more at risk) and how it affects individuals and society (individual: reduced quality of life, higher risk of related illness; society: strain on NHS resources, lost productivity). Choose two or three challenges to study in depth rather than skimming all ten, and use current statistics or news examples to make them concrete.

#### R035-1.2b Organisations promoting public health
Public health challenges are tackled by a range of organisations: national charities (for example the British Heart Foundation, Cancer Research UK, Mind), the NHS (screening programmes, vaccination campaigns, GP-led advice), government agencies (the UK Health Security Agency and Department of Health and Social Care, which set policy and run national campaigns), and the World Health Organization (WHO, which coordinates global public health responses and sets international guidance). For each organisation type, learners should know one example of what it actually does, for example: the NHS runs the flu vaccination programme each autumn targeting over-65s and at-risk groups; WHO monitors and responds to disease outbreaks worldwide.

#### R035-1.3a Current health promotion campaigns
A health promotion campaign is an organised effort to inform or persuade people to change behaviour for better health. Well-known UK examples include Change4Life (healthy eating and activity for families), Drinkaware (responsible alcohol use) and Every Mind Matters (mental health and wellbeing, run by the NHS). Learners should be able to describe what a chosen campaign sets out to do: its central message, the medium it uses (television, social media, posters, school programmes) and the behaviour it wants to change. Describing a campaign accurately means naming it, stating its aim in one sentence, and giving one concrete example of its materials or activities.

#### R035-1.3b Benefits of a health promotion campaign to individuals and society
Campaigns benefit individuals and society in linked ways: behaviour change (people adopting healthier habits, such as eating five portions of fruit and vegetables a day), education (raising awareness of risks or services people did not know about), empowerment (giving people the knowledge and confidence to make their own healthy choices rather than being told what to do) and improved quality of life (better physical and mental health, longer life, more independence). A strong answer links a specific campaign to a specific benefit, for example: Change4Life's Sugar Swaps material educates parents about hidden sugar, which empowers them to make small swaps, leading to better long-term diet and quality of life for the family.

#### R035-1.3c Target audience of a campaign
Every campaign is designed for a particular target audience, defined by factors such as age, gender, health condition, lifestyle or location. Identifying the target audience means asking who the campaign's message, images, language and channel are aimed at, and why that audience was chosen (because they are most at risk, hardest to reach, or most able to act on the message). For example, Every Mind Matters targets the general adult population through digital and social media because mental health affects a wide cross-section of people and online channels reach them where they already spend time. Learners should practise stating the target audience and justifying the choice for two or three real campaigns.

## Explicitly not here
Factors that influence an individual's own health, and barriers to a healthy lifestyle (next stage). Planning and delivering the learner's own campaign (later stages).
