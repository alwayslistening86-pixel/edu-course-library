# S21_R035_NEA_Skills_and_Task1_Evidence - Lesson: R035 NEA skills and Task 1 evidence: research, independence rules and the public health challenge

## Goal
The learner knows the research method and independence rules for the R035 set assignment, and knows what MB1 to MB3 evidence looks like for choosing and explaining a public health challenge and its target audience's factors and benefits.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R035-SKRES - NEA skill: research for a campaign, and referencing: search with a method and record sources
- R035-SKOWN - NEA rules: independence, templates, observation and feedback: know what help is allowed and what makes the work your own
- R035-T1a - Task 1 evidence: reasons for choosing the public health challenge: know what MB1 to MB3 look like for this criterion
- R035-T1b - Task 1 evidence: why addressing the challenge matters to a healthy society: know what full understanding looks like
- R035-T1c - Task 1 evidence: factors influencing the target audience's health and wellbeing: explain factors for a named target audience
- R035-T1d - Task 1 evidence: barriers to a healthy lifestyle for the target audience: explain barriers specific to the audience
- R035-T1e - Task 1 evidence: benefits of following the campaign advice in PIES terms: explain benefits across all four PIES areas

## How to teach this
Ask: 'You Google "obesity" and copy the first result's opening paragraph. Is that research?' Use the answer to introduce OCR's definition of research as an active, methodical process, not a single search. As with R033, the tutor may explain what the criteria require and help the learner plan their own research and thinking, but must never write, draft or model any part of the assessed evidence, and must not direct the learner's planning decisions.

#### R035-SKRES NEA skill: research for a campaign, and referencing
OCR's guidance is explicit: research means searching carefully with a method, examining a subject from different points of view; a single trip to the library or picking the first search-engine result is not sufficient. A proper method for R035 might be: identify what is already known and what gaps need filling, use at least two different source types (an NHS or charity page, a government statistics page, a news article), record what each source adds, and cross-check facts that appear in only one place. Every source used must be referenced: the web address and date accessed, or the book, article or organisation name and date, and any AI tool used must be named with the date of use, exactly as required across the NEA units.

#### R035-SKOWN NEA rules: independence, templates, observation and feedback
For R035, OCR's guidance states that teachers must not direct students to complete specific planning; students must work on their own when planning the health promotion campaign, applying what they have learnt, and must not be led through the process. The teacher must observe each student's delivery of their campaign and complete an individualised Teacher Observation Record Form; delivery is suggested to take between 10 and 20 minutes. The evaluation is a written task that students must produce based on feedback and self-reflection, and must not be led through a process of evaluating. As with all NEA units, work must be the student's own, plagiarism (including uncredited AI use) is not allowed, and OCR-set assignments must not be used for practice.

#### R035-T1a Task 1 evidence: reasons for choosing the public health challenge
Marking criterion: an explanation of the reasons for choosing the public health challenge. MB1 (1 to 2): brief explanation. MB2 (3 to 4): sound explanation. MB3 (5 to 6): comprehensive explanation. To reach MB3, the learner needs several distinct, well-reasoned reasons for their choice (for example, personal relevance, how common the challenge is locally or nationally, or its seriousness), not just 'because it interests me'. Reasons should connect to real evidence, such as a statistic or a local observation, and should be written in the learner's own words based on their own research, not copied from a source.

#### R035-T1b Task 1 evidence: why addressing the challenge matters to a healthy society
Marking criterion: understanding of why addressing this public health challenge is important. OCR's descriptors move from limited, through partial, to full understanding. A full-understanding answer draws on the reasons for a healthy society covered earlier in this course (control of disease, cost of care, sickness and dependency, life expectancy) and applies them specifically to the chosen challenge, showing genuine reasoning about cause and effect (for example, explaining specifically how reducing this challenge would lower NHS costs or increase life expectancy), rather than restating the general reasons without connecting them to the challenge.

#### R035-T1c Task 1 evidence: factors influencing the target audience's health and wellbeing
This criterion sits alongside the barriers criterion and is marked together as one banded judgement covering both. For factors, the learner explains, for their own chosen and researched target audience, which of the lifestyle, physical/mental health, education/socio-economic and access-to-services factors are most relevant and why, using specific detail about that audience rather than generic statements. A brief answer names one or two factors without explanation; a comprehensive answer explains several factors with clear reasoning tied to the specific target audience's real circumstances, drawing on the learner's own research into that audience.

#### R035-T1d Task 1 evidence: barriers to a healthy lifestyle for the target audience
Paired with the factors criterion above. The learner explains barriers (advertising, peer pressure, lack of support, cost) as they specifically apply to their researched target audience, again with specific reasoning rather than a generic list. A comprehensive explanation shows why each barrier is particularly relevant to this audience (for example, cost being a bigger barrier for a lower-income target group) and, ideally, how barriers interact with each other. Evidence should be traceable to research the learner carried out into their chosen target audience, not assumptions.

#### R035-T1e Task 1 evidence: benefits of following the campaign advice in PIES terms
Marking criterion: an explanation of the benefits of following the campaign's advice in terms of PIES. MB1: brief. MB2: sound. MB3: comprehensive. All four PIES areas should be addressed for a comprehensive answer, each explained specifically in relation to the campaign topic and target audience (not generic 'exercise is good for you' statements). For example, for a campaign on reducing screen time before bed: physical (better sleep quality), intellectual (improved concentration the next day), emotional (reduced anxiety from less late-night scrolling), social (more energy for daytime social activities).

## Explicitly not here
Planning, delivering and evaluating the campaign for assessment (next stage covers Tasks 2 to 4 evidence). No sample content for the live set assignment is given.
