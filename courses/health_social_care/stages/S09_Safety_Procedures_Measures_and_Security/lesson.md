# S09_Safety_Procedures_Measures_and_Security - Lesson: Safety procedures, safety measures and security

## Goal
The learner can distinguish procedures from measures, explain the safety procedures and equipment considerations, and explain how each security measure protects service users and staff.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R032-4.3a - Safety procedures for reducing risk and promoting good practice: explain first aid policy, risk assessment, training, emergency procedures and equipment checks
- R032-4.3b - Safety measures and the difference between a procedure and a measure: tell a set process from a single action and give examples of each
- R032-4.4 - Security measures that protect service users and staff: explain the seven security measures and how each protects people

## How to teach this
Take a tour of your own home or school in imagination: 'Where are the fire exits, what would happen if a stranger walked in, and what stops a toddler falling out of a window?' Then organise the answers into procedures (set processes), measures (single actions) and security. The spec says not to teach full risk-assessment details or first aid practice; teach why and how they protect.

#### R032-4.3a Safety procedures for reducing risk and promoting good practice
A safety procedure is a set process that is followed. The spec lists: a first aid policy (who is trained, where kits are, how accidents are recorded); risk assessments (identifying hazards, who might be harmed and how, and controlling them; for example checking a room before a wheelchair user visits); staff training programmes for equipment use, moving and handling techniques (using hoists and slide sheets to avoid injury to staff and service users) and first aid; emergency procedures such as a fire drill and evacuation (alarm, leaving by exits, assembly point, roll call, not using lifts); and equipment considerations: it must be fit for purpose, safety checked, risk assessed, and there must be a reporting system for damage (for example a broken wheelchair brake is reported and taken out of use). Each protects service users (fewer accidents, faster help) and providers (fewer injuries, meeting health and safety law).

#### R032-4.3b Safety measures and the difference between a procedure and a measure
A procedure is a set process that is followed, such as a fire drill or carrying out a risk assessment. A measure is a particular action, such as putting up a wet floor sign. The spec's measures include displaying a fire safety notice and using warning signs such as a wet floor sign or a no entry sign. Measures give an immediate visual warning, prevent slips or entry into unsafe areas and support the procedure (a fire notice tells people the exit route). Teach the test: could you tick it off in one action? A measure. Does it have stages and a person responsible? A procedure. Examiners often ask for the difference, so give a one-line definition for each and one example of each. Link to settings: a wet floor sign in a hospital corridor, a no entry sign on a store room in a day centre.

#### R032-4.4 Security measures that protect service users and staff
Security protects people from harm by others and keeps vulnerable people from leaving unsafely. Spec measures: identifying staff with ID lanyards and uniform, so visitors and service users know who is authorised; monitoring keys, which limits the number of people with access and keeps a list of keyholders, so it is known who has them; receiving and monitoring visitors, where staff on duty at the entrance monitor access, a signing in and out book shows who is present (essential in a fire), and visitor badges are issued; reporting concerns to line managers so senior staff can act; external doors that restrict access, such as electronic swipe cards, buzzers or security pads with pin codes; and window locks and restraints, which keep vulnerable service users safe by preventing them falling out or strangers entering. For each say who it protects. Example: a buzzer entry on a children's day centre prevents strangers coming in and children leaving.

## Explicitly not here
Exam technique (next stage) and full risk assessment or first aid practice (excluded by the specification).
