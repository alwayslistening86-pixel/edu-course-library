# S12_Factors_Affecting_Growth_and_Development - Lesson: R033 factors affecting growth and development

## Goal
The learner can identify six types of factor with examples, explain how they positively and negatively affect development, and knows how to evidence the Task 1 explanation criterion.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R033-1.1c - Factors affecting growth and development: the six types: identify physical, social, emotional, economic, cultural and environmental factors with examples
- R033-1.1d - How the factors affect an individual's growth and development: explain positive and negative effects of each factor with reasoning
- R033-T1b - Task 1 evidence: explaining how two factors of each type affected development: know what MB1 to MB3 look like for this criterion and how to evidence it

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Two children start school on the same day. What could make one thrive and the other struggle, apart from ability?' Sort responses under six headings. Then teach explanation, not just listing: factor, effect, PIES area, consequence. This unit is NEA, so the learner practises on fictional cases and never on the live assignment.

#### R033-1.1c Factors affecting growth and development: the six types
The spec names six types of factor with examples. Physical: diet and nutrition, activities, lifestyle choices such as alcohol and smoking, genetics, physical and mental health, disability and sensory impairment. Social: positive and negative relationships, social inclusion or exclusion, opportunities, discrimination and bullying. Emotional: anxiety, fear, sadness or happiness, grief, attachment and family security. Economic: family income, employment, debts, bills, wealth, education, private or public health providers (services). Cultural: community, religion, race, gender identity and sexual orientation. Environmental: housing needs and conditions, pollution (air, noise, light), neighbourhood, home environment (neglect, conflict) and access to services. Most examples can be positive or negative; for example a supportive friendship group is positive, bullying negative. Have the learner give each factor type an example of a positive and negative influence.

#### R033-1.1d How the factors affect an individual's growth and development
To explain, connect a named factor to a named area of PIES and say why. Structure: the factor, the effect on development, the reason, and the consequence. Example: 'Living in damp, overcrowded housing (environmental) increases the chance of respiratory illness, which means the child misses school; missed school slows intellectual development such as reading skills, and being away from friends reduces social development.' Positive example: 'Regular sport (physical) strengthens muscles and bones and builds teamwork, raising self-esteem.' A strong explanation covers both positive and negative effects, shows how factors interact (low income may lead to poor diet and stress), and applies to the person and life stage. Avoid one-line examples such as 'money affects development'. The criterion asks for two factors of each type.

#### R033-T1b Task 1 evidence: explaining how two factors of each type affected development
The second Task 1 criterion is worth 9 marks: an explanation of how the growth and development of the individual has been affected by two of each of the specified factors (so twelve factors across physical, social, emotional, economic, cultural and environmental). MB1 (1 to 3 marks): limited explanation. MB2 (4 to 6): adequate explanation. MB3 (7 to 9): comprehensive explanation. To evidence this, check you have exactly what the criterion says: two of each type, all six types, about the individual, explaining the effect on their growth and development rather than describing the factor. Practical advice: keep a checklist of the twelve boxes as a planning tool of your own; explain each with a cause and effect sentence; and review whether each explanation names the developmental area affected. Teachers may prompt learners generally to check that they have covered all parts but may not point out specific gaps or supply headings or templates.

## Explicitly not here
Life events and support (later stages). The tutor should not draft the learner's Task 1 explanation.
