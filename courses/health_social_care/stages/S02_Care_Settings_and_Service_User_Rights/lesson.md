# S02_Care_Settings_and_Service_User_Rights - Lesson: Care settings and the rights of service users

## Goal
The learner can sort settings into health and social care, explain the five rights with examples of how each is met, and link maintained rights to service users' health and wellbeing.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R032-1.1 - Types of care settings: give examples of health care and social care settings
- R032-1.2 - The rights of service users: explain each of the five rights with examples of how they are met
- R032-1.3 - Benefits to health and wellbeing when rights are maintained: link maintained rights to empowerment, self-esteem, needs met and trust

## How to teach this
Open with: 'You are in hospital for three days. List five things you would want to decide for yourself, and one thing you would never want other patients to know.' Use the answers to introduce rights and let the learner name each right before you name it. Then sort a list of settings into health and social care together.

#### R032-1.1 Types of care settings
The spec sorts settings into two types. Health care settings: dental practice, GP surgery, health centre, hospital, nursing home, opticians, pharmacy, walk-in centre. Social care settings: community centre, day centre, foodbank, homeless shelter, residential home, retirement home, social services department, support group. Learners must know examples of each type. A useful test: health care mainly treats illness or promotes health (a nurse, a dentist); social care supports daily living, independence and social needs (a residential home, a day centre). Nursing homes are listed under health care because they provide nursing, while residential homes are social care because they provide accommodation and personal care. Exam questions often put a person in one setting, so be ready to say what the setting is and who would use it. Ask the learner to give an example of a service user, a practitioner and one risk for each of three settings.

#### R032-1.2 The rights of service users
A service user is a person who receives care or support. Service users are entitled to five rights, and providers must meet them. The right to choice: being offered options, such as a menu that allows for vegetarian, halal or gluten-free needs, or a choice of male or female practitioner. The right to confidentiality: personal information is only shared with those who need it, for example records stored securely and not discussed in a corridor. The right to consultation: being asked for their opinions and involved in decisions about care, for example being asked about treatment options or their care plan review. The right to equal and fair treatment: not being discriminated against, for example an interpreter for a patient who does not speak English. The right to protection from abuse and harm: being kept safe, for example DBS-checked staff and safe moving and handling. For each, the exam wants an example of how the right is met, not just the name of the right.

#### R032-1.3 Benefits to service users' health and wellbeing when their rights are maintained
The spec lists five benefits. Empowerment: independence, feeling in control of their life, having choice. High self-esteem: feeling valued and respected, positive mental health. Needs are met: appropriate care or treatment, such as a mobility aid or dietary needs met, leading to good or improving physical or mental health. Trust: reassurance that providers will not harm them, confidence that providers have the service user's best interests in mind and confidence in the care received. Skill to teach: linking. Do not just say 'choice gives empowerment'; write 'because Mrs Ali is offered a choice of when to wash, she keeps control of her routine, which makes her feel empowered and less anxious'. Practise chains: right, action, benefit, effect on health or wellbeing. Confidentiality can be linked to trust; consultation to feeling valued and respected; protection to trust and physical health.

## Explicitly not here
Person-centred values and the 6Cs (next stage), communication and safeguarding procedures (later stages).
