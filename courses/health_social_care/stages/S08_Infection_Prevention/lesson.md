# S08_Infection_Prevention - Lesson: Infection prevention: cleanliness, personal hygiene and PPE

## Goal
The learner can explain general cleanliness, personal hygiene and PPE measures and say how each protects service users and providers in different care settings.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R032-4.2a - Infection prevention: general cleanliness: explain cleanliness measures and why they protect people
- R032-4.2b - Infection prevention: personal hygiene measures: explain personal hygiene measures for staff
- R032-4.2c - Infection prevention: PPE: match items of PPE to tasks and settings

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Name three ways germs can get from a hospital bed to a visitor's toddler.' Then group the measures. The spec requires reasons for infection prevention in different types of care settings and how the measures protect both service users and providers, so vary the setting: dental practice, nursery-type day centre, nursing home, foodbank.

#### R032-4.2a Infection prevention: general cleanliness
Measures named in the spec: use anti-bacterial sprays on surfaces; clean toys and play equipment regularly (in a children's setting, where toys are put in mouths); mop floors and vacuum carpets daily; clean and disinfect toilets frequently; correct disposal of hazardous waste in health and care settings (clinical waste, sharps, soiled dressings, in the right bins). Reasons: to stop bacteria and viruses spreading, particularly to people with weakened immunity such as older adults, babies and people on chemotherapy, and to protect staff from picking up infection and passing it on. Result: fewer infections, fewer sick days, quicker recovery. Setting example: a residential home has norovirus outbreaks if toilets are not disinfected, spreading quickly among residents who share facilities.

#### R032-4.2b Infection prevention: personal hygiene measures
Staff measures in the spec: hair tied back or covered; open wounds covered with waterproof dressings; no jewellery (it traps germs and can catch on a person); no nail polish (chips can hide bacteria or fall into food or wounds); correct hand washing routine (palms, backs, between fingers, thumbs, fingertips, wrists, with soap and water for at least 20 seconds, dry with a disposable towel); regular showering and hair washing; regular brushing of teeth; and appropriate use and disposal of tissues, antiseptic wipes and sanitiser. Why: staff touch many people and surfaces, so they can carry infection from one service user to another (cross-infection). Protecting providers: covering wounds stops staff catching infection too. Encourage the learner to say who is protected and how.

#### R032-4.2c Infection prevention: PPE
PPE is personal protective equipment, worn to act as a barrier between staff and service user. The spec lists disposable aprons, disposable gloves, rubber gloves (reusable, for cleaning), face masks, hairnets or hygiene hats, overalls, overshoes and surgical garments or scrubs. Match to tasks: disposable gloves and apron for personal care or dressing wounds (change between service users); rubber gloves for cleaning; face masks in surgery or when someone has a respiratory infection; hairnets in a kitchen; overshoes and scrubs in theatre or clean areas. Explain protection both ways: it protects the service user from the practitioner's germs and the practitioner from body fluids and infectious agents. Common error: wearing the same gloves for two service users. Disposal of used PPE is part of infection control.
