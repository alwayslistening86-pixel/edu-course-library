## Test items
1. Explain how select committees hold the Executive to account. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 2, Section A style, source] Source (an extract describing a government bill being significantly delayed by repeated Lords amendments, with ministers accusing unelected peers of frustrating the elected government's mandate, and peers responding that they were improving flawed legislation). Using the source and your own knowledge, evaluate the effectiveness of the House of Lords in scrutinising government legislation. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 one committee per government department (plus cross-cutting committees) scrutinises policy, spending and administration; B1 can summon ministers, officials and outside experts to give evidence; B1 publish reports that government must formally respond to; B1 chairs elected by the whole House since 2010, increasing independence from party whips; B1 cannot force a change of policy (a limitation on their power); B1 named example (e.g. Public Accounts Committee, Treasury Select Committee) strengthens the answer.
2. [30] Mark out of 30: must use the source's own detail plus the learner's own knowledge to answer the question, not just paraphrase the source. Mark using the same generic levels as ESSAY30, with credit specifically for engaging with what the source says/shows and testing it against wider knowledge. Credit: effective - expertise, less whipped/more independent scrutiny, real power to delay/amend and force reconsideration, Parliament Acts as backstop showing ultimate Commons primacy is preserved; limited effectiveness - cannot block money bills or indefinitely obstruct manifesto bills (Salisbury Convention), lacks democratic legitimacy to face down a determined Commons majority; use of the source's specific example integrated with own knowledge.
