Give the whole test at once, with no hints, under rough time pressure (allow about 1.5 minutes per mark, matching the real papers). ## Test items
1. Explain why a Prime Minister's dominance over the Cabinet can vary over the course of their time in office. [9 marks]
2. [Paper 2, Section A style, non-source] Evaluate the extent to which the UK Executive has become 'presidential'. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [9] Level 3 (7-9): explains factors such as size of Commons majority, personal popularity/media standing, unity or division within the governing party, and specific events (scandals, policy failures, economic crises) that can strengthen or weaken a PM's authority; uses a named PM (e.g. Thatcher's fall in 1990, Johnson's fall in 2022, or another) as evidence that dominance is not fixed but can collapse; balanced range of factors. Level 2 (4-6): partial. Level 1 (1-3): basic.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: for - personal mandates/media focus on the PM, dominance of special advisers and inner circles over full Cabinet, sofa government examples (e.g. Blair/Iraq); against - PM remains dependent on Commons confidence and party support unlike a fixed-term president, collective Cabinet responsibility and resignations can still constrain a PM, examples of PMs forced out by their own party (Thatcher 1990, Johnson 2022, Truss 2022) show weaker structural insulation than a US president; balanced judgement with named examples.
