## Test items
1. Explain why the UK's use of direct democracy remains limited compared with representative democracy. [9 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section A style] Evaluate the extent to which pressure groups enhance UK democracy. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [9] Level 3 (7-9): explains legal position (referendums are advisory, Parliament stays sovereign); explains practical concerns (complexity of most issues, cost, voter fatigue, risk of manipulation by simplistic campaigns); explains political caution after divisive referendums (e.g. 2016 EU referendum's aftermath); balanced range of factors. Level 2 (4-6): partial explanation. Level 1 (1-3): basic/undeveloped.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: enhance - representation between elections, expertise fed into policy, education of public opinion, outlet for participation beyond voting, protection of minority interests; undermine - unequal access/resources favour wealthy insider groups, unaccountable/unelected influence, direct action can be disruptive or undemocratic, 'hyperpluralism' can produce policy gridlock; balanced judgement using named case-study groups.
