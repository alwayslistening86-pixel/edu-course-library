## Test items
1. Explain why formal constitutional amendment is so rare in the USA. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which the US Constitution remains an effective framework for democratic government today. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 Article V requires two-thirds of both houses of Congress; B1 plus ratification by three-quarters (38) of state legislatures/conventions; B1 an extremely high bar designed to force broad, lasting consensus rather than transient majority support; B1 example: the Equal Rights Amendment passed Congress in 1972 but failed ratification; B1 as a result most practical constitutional change happens through Supreme Court interpretation rather than formal amendment; B1 only 27 amendments total, the last substantive one in 1971.

Credit: effective - stable, entrenched protection of rights/structure, checks and balances still meaningfully constrain each branch, has adapted through Supreme Court interpretation without needing constant amendment; ineffective - counter-majoritarian features (Electoral College, Senate malapportionment, life-tenured justices), gridlock from separated powers worsened by polarisation, near-impossible formal amendment leaving some issues unresolved (e.g. gun control, ERA); balanced judgement with specific named examples.
