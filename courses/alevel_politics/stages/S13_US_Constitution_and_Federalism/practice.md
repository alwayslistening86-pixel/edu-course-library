# S13_US_Constitution_and_Federalism - Practice: The nature of the US Constitution; its key features; US federalism; interpretations and debates

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Explain the difference between an entrenched and an unentrenched constitution, using the US Constitution as your example. [3 marks]
2. Give two checks the branches of US government have over one another. [4 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [3] B1 entrenched: difficult to change, requiring a special process beyond an ordinary legislative majority; B1 the US Constitution requires two-thirds of both houses of Congress plus ratification by three-quarters of states (Article V); B1 contrast: the UK constitution is unentrenched, changeable by ordinary Act of Parliament.
2. [4] B2 presidential veto of legislation (Congress can override by 2/3); B2 Senate confirmation of nominees/ratification of treaties; B2 judicial review of laws/executive actions; B2 Congress's power of the purse/impeachment, each briefly explained -- any 2 for full marks.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish an attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
