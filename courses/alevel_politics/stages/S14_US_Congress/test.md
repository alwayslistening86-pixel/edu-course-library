## Test items
1. Explain how the Senate filibuster affects the legislative process. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which Congress is an effective check on the presidency. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 the filibuster allows extended debate in the Senate that can only be ended by a 60-vote cloture motion; B1 this means most legislation effectively needs 60 votes (a supermajority), not just a simple majority, to pass the Senate; B1 gives the minority party significant power to block or delay legislation even when it lacks a majority; B1 contributes to legislative gridlock, especially under divided or narrowly-controlled government; B1 some legislation (e.g. budget reconciliation bills) can bypass the filibuster under special rules, limiting its scope; B1 debates exist over reforming or abolishing it.
2. Credit: effective - power of the purse, Senate confirmation power, oversight hearings/investigations (especially under divided government), impeachment power, override of vetoes; ineffective - same-party Congress often reluctant to check 'its own' president, delegation of broad authority to executive agencies over time, deference on war/national-emergency powers, high bar for veto override/impeachment conviction rarely met; use of named recent examples; balanced judgement.
