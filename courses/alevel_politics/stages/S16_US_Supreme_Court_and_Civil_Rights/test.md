## Test items
1. Explain the difference between judicial activism and judicial restraint. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which the US Supreme Court has become a political rather than a judicial institution. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 judicial activism: judges willing to interpret the Constitution to address contemporary needs, more ready to overturn precedent or strike down laws; B1 judicial restraint: judges defer heavily to the elected branches and existing precedent (stare decisis), intervening only in the clearest cases; B1 critics of activism: unelected judges substituting their own policy preferences for democracy; B1 critics of excessive restraint: can leave real injustices unaddressed; B1 links to originalism (often associated with restraint) versus Living Constitution (often associated with activism), though the link is not absolute; B1 named example of a ruling illustrating one approach.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: political - predictable ideological voting patterns aligned with appointing presidents' parties, increasingly partisan/politicised confirmation battles, landmark rulings reshaping contested policy areas (abortion, affirmative action, campaign finance); judicial - justices apply genuine legal reasoning and precedent (stare decisis), originalism/Living Constitution represent legitimate interpretive philosophies rather than raw politics, life tenure designed to insulate from immediate political pressure; balanced judgement with named cases.
