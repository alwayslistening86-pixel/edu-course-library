## Test items
1. Explain why modern liberals support a greater role for the state than classical liberals. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section B style] Evaluate the extent to which liberals are united in their view of the state. [24 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 classical liberals see the state as a threat to negative freedom, wanting it minimal; B1 modern liberals argue unregulated capitalism left many individuals practically unfree (poverty, poor education/health); B1 modern liberals support positive freedom, the capacity to fulfil potential; B1 this requires state provision (welfare, education, healthcare); B1 influenced by Keynesian economic intervention; B1 both traditions retain a commitment to individual rights and limited, accountable government, differing over degree not principle.
2. [24] Mark out of 24 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-5) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (6-10) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, mostly descriptive; Level 3 (11-15) accurate knowledge, developed analysis of similarities/differences/tensions, some use of named thinkers, but uneven depth; Level 4 (16-20) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis using thinkers effectively, evaluation reaching a judgement; Level 5 (21-24) excellent, precise knowledge, sophisticated analysis integrating thinkers throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement. Credit: united - all liberals see the state as necessary but potentially threatening to liberty, favour limited/constitutional/accountable government, support the rule of law and rights protections; divided - classical liberals (Locke, Nozick-adjacent minimal state) want a minimal 'night-watchman' state, modern liberals (Rawls) want redistributive intervention to secure positive freedom; balanced judgement using named thinkers.
