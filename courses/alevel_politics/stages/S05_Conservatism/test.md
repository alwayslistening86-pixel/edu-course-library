## Test items
1. Explain how Edmund Burke's ideas exemplify traditional conservatism's approach to change. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section B style] Evaluate the extent to which the New Right represents a break from traditional conservative ideas. [24 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 Burke argued change should be approached with great caution, organically; B1 built on existing institutions/practices rather than sweeping them away; B1 respect for tradition and empiricism (accumulated, tested wisdom); B1 rejected abstract rationalist schemes for remaking society (his critique of the French Revolution); B1 links to core idea of human imperfection (distrust of pure reason to redesign society); B1 links to organic society (change should respect society as an interconnected whole).
2. [24] Mark out of 24 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-5) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (6-10) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, mostly descriptive; Level 3 (11-15) accurate knowledge, developed analysis of similarities/differences/tensions, some use of named thinkers, but uneven depth; Level 4 (16-20) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis using thinkers effectively, evaluation reaching a judgement; Level 5 (21-24) excellent, precise knowledge, sophisticated analysis integrating thinkers throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement. Credit: break - neo-liberal individualism/atomism rejects organic society and paternalism, minimal-state economics contrasts with paternalist welfare, Rand/Nozick's libertarianism prioritises individual liberty over hierarchy; continuity - neo-conservative concern for order, tradition, authority and social cohesion still present, both reject utopian/revolutionary change, pragmatic willingness to adapt in office (e.g. Thatcherism's gradual implementation); balanced judgement using named thinkers.
