# S15_US_Presidency - Practice: Formal and informal presidential power; relationships and limitations; interpretations and debates

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Give two informal sources of presidential power. [4 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by 'divided government' and why it matters for a president. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 electoral mandate; B2 executive orders; B2 national events; B2 the cabinet; B2 powers of persuasion; B2 the Executive Office of the President (NSC/OMB/WHO), each briefly explained -- any 2 for full marks.
2. [3] B1 divided government: different parties control the presidency and one or both chambers of Congress; B1 makes passing the president's legislative agenda far harder, increasing reliance on veto threats, negotiation or executive action instead; B1 contrasted with unified government, where the president's party controls both chambers.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish an attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
