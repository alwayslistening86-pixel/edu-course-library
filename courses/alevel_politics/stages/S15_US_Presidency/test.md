## Test items
1. Explain why a president's ability to achieve their aims typically changes over the course of a term in office. [9 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which the modern US presidency has become 'imperial'. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [9] Level 3 (7-9): explains the 'honeymoon period' of higher initial popularity/political capital; explains the typical impact of midterm elections in reducing a president's congressional support; explains 'lame duck' status reducing leverage once re-election is no longer possible/in a final term; explains how unforeseen events (crises, scandals, economic conditions) can shift a president's standing at any point; balanced range of factors, ideally with reference to a specific president since 2000. Level 2 (4-6): partial. Level 1 (1-3): basic.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: imperial - expansive use of executive orders and emergency powers, unilateral military action, extensive executive-privilege claims, EXOP's institutional capacity independent of Congress; imperilled/constrained - divided government limiting legislative success, courts ruling against presidential actions, Congress's power of the purse/oversight/confirmation, media and public opinion constraints, midterm losses weakening later-term authority; use of specific presidents/examples since 2000; balanced judgement.
