## Test items
1. Explain why the Supreme Court cannot strike down an Act of Parliament in the way the US Supreme Court can strike down a law. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 2, Section A style, non-source] Evaluate the extent to which sovereignty has shifted away from Westminster since 1997. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 the UK constitution is built on parliamentary sovereignty - Parliament can make or unmake any law; B1 the UK Supreme Court can rule executive/secondary-legislation action unlawful (judicial review, ultra vires) but not invalidate primary legislation itself; B1 contrast with the codified, entrenched US Constitution which gives the US Supreme Court a textual basis (judicial review since Marbury v Madison) to strike down statutes; B1 UK courts instead interpret legislation and can declare it incompatible with the Human Rights Act, but this does not automatically strike it down - Parliament must choose to amend it; B1 reflects the difference between the UK's uncodified/unentrenched and the USA's codified/entrenched constitutional systems; B1 named UK example (e.g. Miller II) shows the real, but bounded, extent of judicial power.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: shifted - devolution (Scotland/Wales/NI), EU membership 1997-2020 pooling legal sovereignty, referendums constraining Parliament's practical freedom, judicial review's growing assertiveness (Miller cases); not shifted - Parliament retains ultimate legal sovereignty (could in theory revoke devolution/never entrenched EU law), Brexit itself is proof Parliament/the electorate could reclaim sovereignty pooled with the EU, devolved bodies remain legally subordinate; balanced judgement distinguishing legal from political sovereignty.
