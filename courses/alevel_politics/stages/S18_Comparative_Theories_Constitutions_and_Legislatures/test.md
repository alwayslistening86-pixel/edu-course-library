## Test items
1. [Paper 3A, Section B style, compulsory] Using a structural approach, evaluate why the UK Parliament is less able to block government legislation than the US Congress. [12 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which the US Congress is a more powerful legislature than the UK Parliament. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [12] Mark out of 12 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-3) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (4-6) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis; Level 3 (7-9) accurate knowledge with developed analysis; Level 4 (10-12) accurate, precise knowledge with sustained analysis and (where the question asks) evaluation reaching a judgement, appropriate to the time available for a 12-mark answer. Credit: fusion of powers in the UK - government sits within and normally controls the Commons via its majority and party whipping, so blocking 'its own' legislation is politically costly for backbenchers; separation of powers in the USA - Congress is institutionally independent of the presidency, does not depend on the President's confidence, and often faces divided government, giving it structural leverage the whipped UK Commons usually lacks; the Parliament Acts/Salisbury Convention further limit the unelected Lords' ability to block manifesto legislation, reinforcing UK executive dominance; a structural (institutional-design) explanation, distinguished from cultural or rational-choice explanations.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: more powerful - constitutional independence from the executive, genuine ability to block a president's agenda especially under divided government, near-equal bicameral powers, exclusive powers (impeachment, confirmation, treaty ratification); less/equally powerful in some respects - UK Parliament is sovereign and can in theory do anything by statute (unlike Congress, bound by a codified constitution and judicial review), Lords' independence and select committees provide real scrutiny, government's Commons dominance is itself contingent on majority size and can weaken (hung parliaments); comparative theories (rational/cultural/structural) used to explain the differences; balanced judgement.
