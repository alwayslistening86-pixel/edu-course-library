## Test items
1. Explain why the Scottish Parliament and the Northern Ireland Assembly use different electoral systems from Westminster. [9 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section A style, non-source] Evaluate the extent to which first-past-the-post remains the best electoral system for UK general elections. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [9] Level 3 (7-9): explains AMS's aim of proportionality while keeping a constituency link for Scotland; explains STV's aim of power-sharing/proportional representation for divided Northern Ireland; explains that devolved systems were designed to avoid the single-party dominance FPTP can produce, relevant to consensus government; balanced range. Level 2 (4-6): partial. Level 1 (1-3): basic.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: for FPTP - simplicity, strong constituency link, tendency to produce decisive, accountable single-party government; against - disproportionality, wasted votes, under-representation of smaller/spread-out parties, growing mismatch between vote share and seats as party support fragments (citing recent elections); alternatives (AMS/STV) and their trade-offs; balanced judgement.
