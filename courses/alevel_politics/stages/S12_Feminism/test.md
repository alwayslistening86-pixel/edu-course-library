## Test items
1. Explain how radical feminism differs from liberal feminism in its analysis of gender inequality. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 2, Section B style] Evaluate the extent to which feminists agree that 'the personal is political'. [24 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 liberal feminism: individualism as the basis of equality, seeks equal legal/political rights and opportunity within existing structures; B1 liberal feminism works through reform (legislation, equal opportunity policy); B1 radical feminism: patriarchy is the most fundamental social division, predating/underlying other oppressions including class; B1 radical feminism: 'the personal is political' - private relationships and the family themselves are sites of oppression requiring fundamental change; B1 radical feminism therefore seeks deeper cultural/social transformation, not just legal equality; B1 named thinker (e.g. Millett on the family as patriarchy's chief institution) illustrates the radical position.
2. [24] Mark out of 24 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-5) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (6-10) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, mostly descriptive; Level 3 (11-15) accurate knowledge, developed analysis of similarities/differences/tensions, some use of named thinkers, but uneven depth; Level 4 (16-20) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis using thinkers effectively, evaluation reaching a judgement; Level 5 (21-24) excellent, precise knowledge, sophisticated analysis integrating thinkers throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement. Credit: agree - most feminist traditions accept private relationships/the family are shaped by power and so are a legitimate object of political analysis (Millett, Rowbotham); disagree/vary in emphasis - liberal feminists focus more on public/legal equality and are more cautious about extending 'political' fully into private life, post-modern/intersectional feminists (hooks) stress that the personal is political differently for different women depending on race/class; balanced judgement using named thinkers.
