## Test items
1. Explain how the Electoral College can produce a result that differs from the national popular vote. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which money determines the outcome of US elections. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 the Electoral College allocates 538 electoral votes by state (roughly reflecting population, with a small-state bias from the fixed +2 Senate seats each state gets); B1 all but two states award their electoral votes winner-takes-all; B1 a candidate can therefore win narrowly in enough large/swing states to win the Electoral College while losing the popular vote overall (winning big margins in states they lose nationally is 'wasted'); B1 this happened in 2000 and 2016; B1 concentrates campaign effort on competitive swing states rather than the whole country; B1 reform (e.g. abolition, or a National Popular Vote Interstate Compact) faces the high bar of Article V amendment or requires enough states to voluntarily join a compact.
