# S19_Comparing_Executives_Judiciaries_and_Democracy - Practice: Comparing UK/US executives, supreme courts and civil rights, and democracy and participation

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Give one difference between how the UK Prime Minister and the US President can be removed from office. [3 marks]
2. Explain one reason UK parties are generally more internally disciplined than US parties. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [3] B1 the UK PM can be removed at any time by losing a Commons confidence vote or their own party's support, with no fixed term; B2 the US President serves a fixed four-year term and can only be removed early via impeachment (House) and conviction (two-thirds Senate), a very high bar rarely met.
2. [3] B1 UK's fusion of powers means government depends on maintaining Commons confidence, so MPs' career advancement (and often re-selection) depends heavily on party loyalty via the whip; B2 US members of Congress are not dependent on their party for the executive's survival (fixed terms, separation of powers), giving them more room to defy leadership without bringing down a government.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish an attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
