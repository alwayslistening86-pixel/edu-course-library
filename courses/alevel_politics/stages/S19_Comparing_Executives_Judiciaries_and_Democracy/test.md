## Test items
1. [Paper 3A, Section A style] Evaluate the extent to which the US Supreme Court has more power than the UK Supreme Court. [12 marks]
2. [Paper 3A, Section C style] Evaluate the extent to which the US political system offers voters more effective democratic participation than the UK system. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [12] Mark out of 12 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-3) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (4-6) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis; Level 3 (7-9) accurate knowledge with developed analysis; Level 4 (10-12) accurate, precise knowledge with sustained analysis and (where the question asks) evaluation reaching a judgement, appropriate to the time available for a 12-mark answer. Credit: more power - can strike down primary legislation via judicial review (Marbury v Madison), life tenure insulates justices further, entrenched Bill of Rights gives rulings lasting constitutional force; qualifications - UK Supreme Court still meaningfully constrains executive action (ultra vires, Miller cases) and can prompt legislative change via HRA incompatibility declarations, so the UK Court is not powerless, just narrower in formal scope; brief but balanced judgement appropriate to 12 marks.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: more effective - primaries give voters more direct control over candidate selection than UK party selection processes, more frequent elections (House every two years) and more directly-elected offices (president, many judges/local officials) increase participation opportunities, extensive interest-group/litigation avenues for participation; less effective - low and unequal turnout, the Electoral College can override the popular vote, campaign-finance influence of wealthy donors, entrenched two-party system limits real choice compared with the UK's increasingly multi-party, more proportionally-elected devolved politics; comparative theories used to explain the differences; balanced judgement.
