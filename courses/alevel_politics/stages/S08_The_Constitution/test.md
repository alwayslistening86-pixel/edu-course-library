# S08_The_Constitution - Test: Nature and sources of the UK constitution; change since 1997; devolution; debates on further reform

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in Edexcel's style: short explain questions and levels-marked essay questions at the tariffs used in the real papers (12, 24 or 30 marks), with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints, under rough time pressure (allow about 1.5 minutes per mark, matching the real papers). Then mark against the mark schemes below (Edexcel's generic levels-based approach for extended writing) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`. ## Test items
1. Explain three ways the UK constitution changed under the Labour governments of 1997-2010. [9 marks]
2. [Paper 2, Section A style, non-source] Evaluate the extent to which the UK constitution should be codified and entrenched. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [9] Level 3 (7-9): identifies and explains three of - Lords reform (removal of most hereditary peers), devolution to Scotland/Wales/NI, the Human Rights Act 1998, creation of the Supreme Court, electoral reform for devolved bodies - each with accurate detail. Level 2 (4-6): two changes explained, or three named with limited detail. Level 1 (1-3): basic/undeveloped.
2. [30] Mark out of 30 using Edexcel's generic levels for extended political writing: Level 1 (1-6) basic, undeveloped; Level 2 (7-12) some accurate knowledge, limited analysis, largely descriptive; Level 3 (13-18) accurate, relevant knowledge with developed analysis of similarities, differences and connections, some evaluation; Level 4 (19-24) accurate, wide-ranging knowledge, sustained analysis and evaluation with a supported judgement, good use of named examples; Level 5 (25-30) excellent, precise, wide-ranging knowledge, sophisticated sustained analysis and evaluation throughout, and a fully substantiated, well-weighed judgement using precise up-to-date examples. Credit: for codification - clearer, more accessible, stronger protection against erosion by a Commons majority, resistant to hasty change; against - loses valuable flexibility/adaptability, threatens parliamentary sovereignty and democratic accountability by shifting power to unelected judges, difficult to agree content/entrenchment mechanism in practice; use of examples (Brexit, Human Rights Act debates, comparison with the US Constitution); balanced judgement.
