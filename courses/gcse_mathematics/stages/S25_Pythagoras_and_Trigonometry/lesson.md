# S25_Pythagoras_and_Trigonometry - Lesson: Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry

## Goal
The learner applies Pythagoras' theorem in two and three dimensions, uses sine, cosine and tangent in right-angled triangles, knows exact trigonometric values, and uses the sine rule, cosine rule and area formula in any triangle.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 10.05a - Pythagoras' theorem in 2D and 3D: find missing sides in right-angled triangles
- 10.05b - Trigonometric ratios in right-angled triangles, 2D and 3D: use sin, cos, tan for sides and angles
- 10.05c - Exact trigonometric values for 0, 30, 45, 60, 90 degrees: recall exact trig values
- 10.05d - Sine rule: use the sine rule in any triangle
- 10.05e - Cosine rule: use the cosine rule in any triangle

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A ladder 5 m long leans against a wall with its foot 1.5 m from the wall. How high does it reach, and what angle does it make with the ground?' The two questions lead to Pythagoras and to trigonometry.

#### 10.05a Pythagoras' theorem in 2D and 3D
In a right-angled triangle a^2 + b^2 = c^2, where c is the hypotenuse (the longest side, opposite the right angle). To find a shorter side subtract: b = sqrt(c^2 - a^2). Check the answer is smaller than the hypotenuse. In 3D, the space diagonal of a cuboid l by w by h is sqrt(l^2 + w^2 + h^2): for 6, 8 and 24 it is sqrt(36 + 64 + 576) = 26. Higher: derive and prove Pythagoras' theorem using areas.

#### 10.05b Trigonometric ratios in right-angled triangles
Label the sides relative to the angle: hypotenuse, opposite, adjacent. sin = opposite / hypotenuse, cos = adjacent / hypotenuse, tan = opposite / adjacent (SOH CAH TOA). To find a side, substitute and rearrange: with hypotenuse 12 and angle 35 degrees, opposite = 12 sin 35 = 6.88. To find an angle use the inverse function: opposite 5, hypotenuse 13 gives angle = sin^-1(5/13) = 22.6 degrees. Angles of elevation are measured above the horizontal and angles of depression below. In 3D, find the angle between a line and a plane by making a right-angled triangle. Check the calculator is in degrees mode.

#### 10.05c Exact trigonometric values
Recall: sin 0 = 0, sin 30 = 1/2, sin 45 = sqrt(2)/2, sin 60 = sqrt(3)/2, sin 90 = 1; cos is the same list in reverse; tan 0 = 0, tan 30 = sqrt(3)/3, tan 45 = 1, tan 60 = sqrt(3), and tan 90 is not defined. Derive them from an equilateral triangle of side 2 (halved to give 30, 60, 90 with sides 1, sqrt(3), 2) and a right-angled isosceles triangle with sides 1, 1, sqrt(2). Use them to give exact answers without a calculator.

#### 10.05d The sine rule
For any triangle with sides a, b, c opposite angles A, B, C: a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C. Use it when you know two angles and a side (find another side), or two sides and a non-included angle (find another angle, using sin A / a = sin B / b to find an angle). Example: A = 50, B = 70, a = 8: b = 8 sin 70 / sin 50 = 9.81. Be aware that finding an angle from a sine can give an acute or obtuse value; use the diagram to decide.

#### 10.05e The cosine rule
a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc cos A. Use it when you know two sides and the included angle (find the third side) or all three sides (find an angle): cos A = (b^2 + c^2 - a^2)/(2bc). With b = 7, c = 9, A = 40: a^2 = 49 + 81 - 126 cos 40 = 33.5, a = 5.79. Choose between sine and cosine rules by the given information: cosine for SAS or SSS, sine when an angle and its opposite side are both known.
