# S01_Integers_Primes_and_Order - Lesson: Integers, primes, factors and order of operations

## Goal
The learner calculates accurately with positive and negative integers, uses number-property vocabulary precisely, writes numbers as products of primes, finds HCF and LCM, and evaluates mixed expressions in the correct order.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 1.01a - Four operations with positive and negative integers: add, subtract, multiply and divide integers of either sign accurately
- 1.02a - Vocabulary: odd, even, prime, factor, multiple, square, cube, root: use the standard number-property words precisely
- 1.02b - Primes and prime factorisation (index notation at Higher): write any whole number as a product of primes
- 1.02c - HCF and LCM (from prime factorisations at Higher): find highest common factors and lowest common multiples
- 1.03a - Conventional order of operations: evaluate mixed expressions in the right order
- 1.04a - Inverse operations and checking: undo a calculation to check an answer

## How to teach this
Open with: 'What is -3 - (-5), and how do you know?' Let the learner reason with a number line or a debt picture before stating any sign rules. Ask them to explain any rule in their own words before moving on.

#### 1.01a Four operations with positive and negative integers
Adding a negative moves left on the number line only if you are subtracting a positive; the reliable rule is that subtracting a negative is the same as adding the positive (5 - (-3) = 8). For multiplying and dividing, same signs give a positive answer and different signs give a negative (-4 x -3 = 12, -12 / 3 = -4). Most errors are sign-tracking slips, so have the learner write each sign change on its own line. Long multiplication and short/long division must be done confidently without a calculator; check with an estimate.

#### 1.02a Number vocabulary
The learner must use the words correctly: a factor divides exactly into a number (factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12), a multiple is in its times table (multiples of 12: 12, 24, 36...), a prime has exactly two factors (1 and itself; so 1 is not prime and 2 is the only even prime), a square number is n x n, a cube number is n x n x n, and a root undoes a power. Practise by asking 'Is 51 prime?' (no: 3 x 17) and 'Which square number is a multiple of 7 between 30 and 60?' (49). Recall of squares to 15 x 15 and cubes of 1 to 5, 10 is expected.

#### 1.02b Primes and prime factorisation
Use a factor tree or repeated division by primes until every branch ends in a prime. 84 = 2 x 2 x 3 x 7. Foundation answers can be written as a product; Higher answers should use index notation: 84 = 2^2 x 3 x 7. Stress that every whole number above 1 has exactly one prime factorisation, so different trees must give the same answer. Check by multiplying back.

#### 1.02c HCF and LCM
Foundation: list the factors (or multiples) of each number and pick the greatest common one (or the smallest common multiple). Higher: use prime factorisations. Write 36 = 2^2 x 3^2 and 60 = 2^2 x 3 x 5. HCF takes the lowest power of each shared prime: 2^2 x 3 = 12. LCM takes the highest power of every prime that appears: 2^2 x 3^2 x 5 = 180. A quick check: HCF x LCM = product of the two numbers (12 x 180 = 2160 = 36 x 60). Word problems ('two buses leave every 12 and 18 minutes') are LCM; sharing into equal groups is HCF.

#### 1.03a Order of operations
The order is brackets, then powers and roots (indices), then multiplication and division from left to right, then addition and subtraction from left to right (BIDMAS/BODMAS). Fraction bars and roots act as brackets: in (12 + 6) / 3, 12 + 6 is done first; in 12 + 6 / 3 the division goes first (= 14). Have the learner predict their calculator's output for 6 + 4 x 2^2 and then check (22, not 40).

#### 1.04a Inverse operations and checking
Addition and subtraction are inverses, as are multiplication and division, and squaring and square rooting. Use the inverse to check: if 437 x 24 = 10 488 then 10 488 / 24 should return 437. Inverses also underpin solving equations later, so name the inverse explicitly whenever it is used.

## Explicitly not here
Fractions and decimals (next stage) and algebra (later): keep every example numerical.
