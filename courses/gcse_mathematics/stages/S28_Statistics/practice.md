# S28_Statistics - Practice: Sampling, charts, averages, scatter graphs and data quality

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. A survey of school lunches asks only pupils in the canteen queue on Friday. Explain two ways the sample may be biased and suggest a better method.
2. Draw a pie chart for these votes: A 12, B 18, C 6, D 24 (give angles). Higher: a school of 600 has 220 in Year 7; how many Year 7 pupils are needed in a stratified sample of 60?
3. Find the mean, median and range of 8, 9, 5, 4, 10, 32. Comment on which average best represents the data.
4. Higher: estimate the median and interquartile range from a cumulative frequency table (provided) and compare two classes using one average and one measure of spread. Draw a scatter graph for the paired data (3, 12), (5, 18), (8, 25), (10, 31) and estimate the y-value at x = 7.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
