# S19_Circle_Theorems - Lesson: Circle terminology and circle theorems

## Goal
The learner names the parts of a circle, states and applies the standard circle theorems with correct reasons, and proves them.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 8.05a - Circle terminology (Higher): name the parts of a circle
- 8.05b - Circle theorem: angle at centre is twice angle at circumference (Higher): apply and prove the centre theorem
- 8.05c - Circle theorem: angle in a semicircle (Higher): apply and prove the semicircle theorem
- 8.05d - Circle theorem: angles in the same segment (Higher): apply and prove the same-segment theorem
- 8.05e - Circle theorem: radius and chord (Higher): apply and prove the chord theorem
- 8.05f - Circle theorem: radius and tangent (Higher): apply and prove the tangent theorem
- 8.05g - Circle theorem: tangent-chord (alternate segment) (Higher): apply and prove the alternate-segment theorem
- 8.05h - Circle theorem: cyclic quadrilateral (Higher): apply and prove the cyclic-quadrilateral theorem

## How to teach this
All content in this stage is Higher tier. Start by asking the learner to draw an angle at the centre and an angle at the circumference standing on the same arc and measure both, then discuss what they notice.

#### 8.05a Circle terminology
Centre; radius (centre to the circumference); diameter (through the centre, twice the radius); circumference (the perimeter); chord (a line joining two points on the circumference); tangent (a line touching the circle at exactly one point); arc (part of the circumference); sector (region between two radii and an arc); segment (region between a chord and an arc). Every radius has the same length, so triangles formed by two radii and a chord are isosceles.

#### 8.05b Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle at any point on the remaining circumference. If the angle at the circumference is 35 degrees, the angle at the centre is 70 degrees. Proof idea: join the circumference point to the centre. This makes two isosceles triangles (two radii each); the exterior angle of each equals the sum of its two equal base angles, so the central angle is double the angle at the circumference.

#### 8.05c The angle in a semicircle is a right angle
If AB is a diameter and C is on the circumference, angle ACB = 90 degrees. This is the case of the previous theorem where the centre angle is 180 degrees. If angle ABC = 32 degrees then angle BAC = 58 degrees. Reason in full: 'the angle in a semicircle is a right angle' followed by 'angles in a triangle sum to 180 degrees'.

#### 8.05d Angles in the same segment are equal
Angles at the circumference standing on the same chord or arc, on the same side of it, are equal. If two such angles are marked and one is 47 degrees the other is 47 degrees. Proof: each is half the angle at the centre subtending the same arc.

#### 8.05e A radius perpendicular to a chord bisects it
The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, and a radius that bisects a chord is perpendicular to it. For a chord of length 16 cm in a circle of radius 10 cm, the perpendicular distance from the centre is sqrt(100 - 64) = 6 cm (half the chord is 8 cm). Proof: the two triangles formed by the radii and the perpendicular are congruent (right angle, hypotenuse and a shared side).

#### 8.05f A radius is perpendicular to the tangent
A tangent is at right angles to the radius at the point of contact. Two tangents from the same external point are equal in length. If P is 13 cm from the centre O of a circle of radius 5 cm, the tangent length is sqrt(169 - 25) = 12 cm. Reasoning: 'the angle between a tangent and a radius is 90 degrees'.

#### 8.05g Tangent-chord (alternate segment) theorem
The angle between a tangent and a chord at the point of contact is equal to the angle in the alternate segment (the segment on the other side of the chord). If a tangent at A makes an angle of 62 degrees with chord AB, then angle ACB for C in the alternate segment is 62 degrees. State it as 'angle between tangent and chord equals angle in the alternate segment'.

#### 8.05h Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral
The opposite angles of a quadrilateral with all four vertices on a circle sum to 180 degrees. If one angle is 105 degrees, the opposite angle is 75 degrees. An exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle. Use it in multi-step problems with other angle facts and always name the theorem.
