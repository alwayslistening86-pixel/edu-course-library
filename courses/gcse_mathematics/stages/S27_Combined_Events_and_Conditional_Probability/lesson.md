# S27_Combined_Events_and_Conditional_Probability - Lesson: Venn diagrams, tree diagrams and conditional probability

## Goal
The learner uses Venn diagrams and tree diagrams, applies the addition law for mutually exclusive events and the complement rule, and (Higher) handles independent, dependent and conditional probability.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 11.02c - Two-circle Venn diagrams and probability: use Venn diagrams for probability
- 11.02d - Tree diagrams for successive events: build and use tree diagrams
- 11.02e - Addition law for mutually exclusive events; complement rule: combine probabilities of exclusive events
- 11.02f - Independent and dependent events; conditional probability (Higher): handle dependence and conditional probability

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A bag holds 3 red and 2 blue counters. You take one, keep it, and take a second. Is the chance of blue the same both times?' It exposes the difference between with and without replacement.

#### 11.02c Two-circle Venn diagrams
A Venn diagram shows how sets overlap. In a class of 30, 18 study French (F), 12 study Spanish (S) and 5 study both. Fill the overlap first (5), then F only (13), S only (7), and the outside (30 - 25 = 5 study neither). Use the diagram to find probabilities such as P(F) = 18/30, P(F and S) = 5/30 and P(F or S) = 25/30. Note that P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). Higher: use set notation for union, intersection and complement.

#### 11.02d Tree diagrams for successive events
Draw a branch for each outcome at each stage, with probabilities on branches summing to 1 at each split. Multiply along branches to find the probability of a combined outcome and add the probabilities of the branches giving the required outcome. If P(rain) = 0.3 on each of two days (independent): P(rain both days) = 0.09; P(exactly one wet day) = 0.3 x 0.7 + 0.7 x 0.3 = 0.42. Use the fact that 'at least one' = 1 - P(none).

#### 11.02e Mutually exclusive events, the addition law and the complement rule
Mutually exclusive events cannot happen together, so P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). The probabilities of all mutually exclusive, exhaustive outcomes sum to 1, so P(not A) = 1 - P(A). For a spinner with P(red) = 0.2, P(blue) = 0.35, P(green) = 0.25, P(yellow) is 1 - 0.8 = 0.2. When events are not mutually exclusive, subtract the overlap.

#### 11.02f Independent and dependent events; conditional probability
Independent events do not affect each other, so P(A and B) = P(A) x P(B) (successive coin flips). Dependent events change the probabilities on later branches: a bag of 3 red and 2 blue counters, taking two without replacement, has P(red, red) = 3/5 x 2/4 = 3/10, and on the second pick the denominator has reduced. Conditional probability is a probability given that something has happened: P(B given A) = P(A and B) / P(A). In a Venn diagram, the probability of being in S given that you are in F is 5/18 (5 of the 18 in F). Higher only.
