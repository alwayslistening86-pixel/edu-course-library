# S22_Congruence_Similarity_and_Enlargement - Lesson: Congruence, similarity and enlargement

## Goal
The learner proves and uses congruence and similarity, enlarges shapes by any scale factor, and uses length, area and volume scale factors.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 9.02a - Congruent triangles and proofs (SSS, ASA, SAS, RHS): recognise and prove congruence
- 9.02b - Using congruence in calculations and proofs: use congruence to prove facts about shapes
- 9.04a - Similar triangles and proofs: recognise and prove similarity
- 9.04b - Enlargement, including fractional and negative scale factors: enlarge shapes from a centre
- 9.04c - Length, area and volume ratios for similar shapes: scale lengths, areas and volumes correctly

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Two triangles have all three angles the same. Must they be the same size? Must they be the same shape?' Let the learner sketch counter-examples.

#### 9.02a Congruent triangles and congruence conditions
Congruent shapes are identical in shape and size (allowing reflection or rotation). Triangles are congruent if, and only if, one of four conditions holds: three sides equal (SSS), two sides and the included angle (SAS), two angles and a corresponding side (ASA or AAS), or a right angle, hypotenuse and one other side (RHS). Three equal angles (AAA) or two sides and a non-included angle (SSA) are not enough. A proof states each pair of equal parts with a reason, names the condition and concludes 'triangles ABC and DEF are congruent'.

#### 9.02b Using congruence in calculations and proofs
Once triangles are congruent, all corresponding parts are equal. Proof example: in a kite ABCD with AB = AD and CB = CD, triangle ABC and triangle ADC share AC, so by SSS they are congruent, so angle ABC = angle ADC. Use congruence to prove the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal or that the diagonals of a rectangle are equal. Say 'corresponding parts of congruent triangles are equal' when using it.

#### 9.04a Similar triangles and similarity proofs
Similar shapes have the same shape but different size: corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio. For triangles, two equal angles are enough. If a line DE is parallel to BC in triangle ABC (D on AB, E on AC), then angle ADE equals angle ABC (corresponding angles) and angle A is common, so triangles ADE and ABC are similar. Sides: AD/AB = AE/AC = DE/BC. Use the ratio to find missing sides: if AD = 4, AB = 10 and DE = 6, then BC = 15.

#### 9.04b Enlargement with positive, fractional and negative scale factors
An enlargement needs a centre and a scale factor. Multiply each vertex's distance from the centre by the scale factor along the same line. A scale factor above 1 makes the shape larger; between 0 and 1 makes it smaller (a fractional scale factor); a negative scale factor puts the image on the opposite side of the centre, inverted. Describe fully with 'enlargement, scale factor ..., centre (...)'. Use the image and object to find the scale factor as a ratio of corresponding lengths.

#### 9.04c Length, area and volume ratios for similar shapes
If lengths are in ratio 1 : k, then areas are in ratio 1 : k^2 and volumes in ratio 1 : k^3. Two similar cones with heights 5 cm and 15 cm have a length scale factor of 3, so the larger's surface area is 9 times and volume 27 times. If the smaller has volume 40 cm^3 the larger has 1080 cm^3. To go backwards, take the square or cube root of the area or volume ratio: areas 4 : 25 give lengths 2 : 5.
