# S04_Rounding_Estimation_and_Bounds - Lesson: Rounding, estimation and bounds

## Goal
The learner rounds to a stated accuracy, estimates answers without a calculator, and (Higher) states error intervals and calculates with upper and lower bounds.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.01a - Rounding: tens, hundreds, significant figures, decimal places: round to a stated degree of accuracy
- 4.01b - Estimating calculations by approximation: estimate answers without a calculator using rounded values
- 4.01c - Error intervals; upper and lower bounds (Higher): express the range a rounded value could take and calculate with bounds

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to estimate 38.7 x 21.3 / 4.9 with no calculator and then check. Discuss which roundings made the answer closer.

#### 4.01a Rounding to tens, hundreds, significant figures and decimal places
Decimal places count digits after the point; significant figures count from the first non-zero digit. 0.004 587 to 2 significant figures is 0.0046, to 3 decimal places is 0.005. Look at the next digit: 5 or more rounds up. When rounding a whole number to a significant figure, keep the size with zeros (48 762 to 2 s.f. = 49 000). Never round in the middle of a multi-step calculation; round only the final answer.

#### 4.01b Estimating calculations
Round every number to 1 significant figure (or a friendly number) before calculating. 38.7 x 21.3 / 4.9 becomes 40 x 20 / 5 = 160 (real answer 168.2). For square roots, use nearby squares: sqrt(50) is a little over 7 because 7^2 = 49. Write the approximate symbol and show the rounded values. Higher: estimates may include roots and longer expressions; decide whether the estimate is an over- or under-estimate depending on which way each number was rounded.

#### 4.01c Error intervals and bounds
A number rounded to a stated accuracy lies in an interval. If x = 4.6 to 1 decimal place, then 4.55 <= x < 4.65. Note the inequality: the lower bound is included, the upper bound is not. Higher: for calculations, use the bound that gives the extreme value: the upper bound of a sum uses both upper bounds, but the upper bound of a difference a - b uses the upper bound of a and the lower bound of b; the upper bound of a quotient a / b uses upper a and lower b. For a length of 12 cm (to the nearest cm) and a time of 3.0 s (nearest 0.1 s), the maximum speed is 12.5 / 2.95 = 4.24 cm/s. Truncation errors have intervals running from the truncated value up to the next step (3.7 truncated to 1 d.p. means 3.7 <= x < 3.8).
