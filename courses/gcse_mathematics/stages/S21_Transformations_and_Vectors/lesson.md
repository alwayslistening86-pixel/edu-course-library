# S21_Transformations_and_Vectors - Lesson: Transformations and vectors

## Goal
The learner reflects, rotates and translates shapes, describes single and combined transformations fully, and uses column vectors and vector arithmetic including simple vector proofs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 9.01a - Reflect shapes in mirror lines: reflect shapes in horizontal, vertical and diagonal lines
- 9.01b - Rotate shapes about a centre: rotate shapes through given angles
- 9.01c - Column vectors for translations: translate shapes using column vectors
- 9.01d - Sequences of transformations; describing the result: combine and describe transformations fully
- 9.03a - Vector addition, subtraction, scalar multiplication; vector proofs: combine vectors and use them in proofs
- 9.03b - Column-vector representation on coordinate grids: draw and read vectors on a grid

## How to teach this
Give a shape on a grid and ask: 'How could you slide, flip or turn it to land on this other shape, and what information would you need to give someone so they could do exactly the same?'

#### 9.01a Reflections
Reflect in a mirror line by moving each vertex the same perpendicular distance to the other side. Lines to recognise: x = a (vertical), y = b (horizontal), y = x and y = -x (diagonal). To describe, give the type (reflection) and the equation of the mirror line. The image is congruent to the original, but with reverse orientation.

#### 9.01b Rotations
A rotation needs a centre, an angle and a direction (clockwise or anticlockwise). Rotate by 90, 180 or 270 degrees using tracing paper or by turning the vector from the centre to each vertex. To describe a rotation, give all three pieces of information. To find the centre, join two pairs of corresponding points and draw perpendicular bisectors; they meet at the centre.

#### 9.01c Translations and column vectors
A translation slides every point by the same vector, written as a column (3 over -2) meaning 3 right and 2 down. Describe it as 'translation by the vector (3, -2)', never as 'move by 3, -2'. The image has the same size, shape and orientation. The reverse translation uses the negative vector.

#### 9.01d Combined transformations
Perform each transformation in order and describe the overall single transformation if there is one. Reflecting in x = 1 and then in x = 4 gives a translation of 6 to the right (twice the distance between the mirror lines). Two reflections in intersecting lines give a rotation. Give full descriptions and note that combining transformations in a different order can give a different result.

#### 9.03b Column vectors on grids
A vector has magnitude and direction. On a grid, a vector from A to B is written AB and its column shows the horizontal and vertical steps. Vectors are equal if they have the same magnitude and direction, wherever they are drawn. Higher: the magnitude of the vector (a, b) is sqrt(a^2 + b^2), so the magnitude of (5, 12) is 13.

#### 9.03a Vector arithmetic and proofs
Higher only. Add vectors by adding components, subtract by subtracting, and multiply by a scalar by scaling each component. In a diagram, AB = -a + b if OA = a and OB = b (go from A back to O, then to B). The midpoint M of AB has OM = a + 1/2 (b - a) = 1/2 (a + b). Two vectors are parallel if one is a scalar multiple of the other, so if OP = 3a + 6b and OQ = a + 2b then OP = 3 OQ, so O, P and Q are on a straight line. Write vector paths carefully, going from one point to another by known vectors.
