# S15_Curves_and_Graph_Transformations - Lesson: Curves, trigonometric and exponential graphs, circles and graph transformations

## Goal
The learner recognises and sketches quadratic, cubic, reciprocal, exponential and trigonometric graphs and circle equations, and describes translations and reflections of graphs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 7.01c - Recognise and sketch linear, quadratic, cubic and reciprocal graphs (Higher): recognise standard graph shapes and their key features
- 7.01d - Exponential graphs (Higher): recognise and sketch y = k^x
- 7.01e - Sine, cosine and tangent graphs (Higher): recognise and sketch the trig graphs
- 7.01f - Equations of circles centred on the origin (Higher): use x^2 + y^2 = r^2
- 7.03a - Translations and reflections of graphs: describe graph transformations

## How to teach this
Show four curves without equations and ask the learner to say what each one is likely to be and why (turning points, asymptotes, symmetry) before naming them.

#### 7.01c Recognising and sketching linear, quadratic, cubic and reciprocal graphs
Linear: straight line. Quadratic: parabola, a U shape when the x^2 coefficient is positive and an inverted U when negative, with one turning point. Sketch y = x^2 - 4x + 3: roots at x = 1 and 3, y-intercept 3, turning point (2, -1). Cubic: an S-shaped curve for x^3 (going from bottom-left to top-right when the coefficient is positive), up to two turning points. Reciprocal y = 1/x: two separate branches (hyperbola) that approach the axes but never touch them, so x = 0 and y = 0 are asymptotes; it is symmetrical about y = x. State key features on the sketch: intercepts, turning points, asymptotes.

#### 7.01d Exponential graphs
An exponential y = k^x (k > 0, k different from 1) passes through (0, 1). For k > 1, such as y = 2^x, it grows rapidly to the right and approaches the x-axis (asymptote y = 0) to the left. For 0 < k < 1, such as y = (1/2)^x, it decays. Use a table of values to plot it and to model growth and decay contexts such as bacteria doubling every hour.

#### 7.01e Sine, cosine and tangent graphs
y = sin x and y = cos x have period 360 degrees and range from -1 to 1. Sine starts at 0, peaks at 90 degrees, crosses zero at 180 degrees, with minimum -1 at 270 degrees. Cosine starts at 1 and is a sine curve shifted left by 90 degrees. y = tan x has period 180 degrees, is undefined at 90 and 270 degrees (vertical asymptotes) and passes through 0 and 180 degrees. Use the graph and its symmetry to solve equations: sin x = 0.5 for 0 <= x <= 360 gives x = 30 and x = 150 degrees.

#### 7.01f Equations of circles centred on the origin
x^2 + y^2 = r^2 is a circle with centre (0, 0) and radius r. x^2 + y^2 = 25 has radius 5 and passes through (3, 4), (-5, 0) and (0, -5). Test whether a point is on the circle by substituting. Use the equation with a straight line to find intersection points, and with a radius gradient to find a tangent (see the previous stage).

#### 7.03a Translations and reflections of graphs
Starting from y = f(x): y = f(x) + a translates the graph up by a; y = f(x + a) translates it left by a (opposite to the sign); y = -f(x) reflects it in the x-axis; y = f(-x) reflects it in the y-axis. From y = x^2, the graph y = (x - 2)^2 + 1 has its turning point at (2, 1). Describe a transformation by naming the type and giving the vector or the mirror line.
