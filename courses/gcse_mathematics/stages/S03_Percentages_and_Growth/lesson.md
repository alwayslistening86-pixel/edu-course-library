# S03_Percentages_and_Growth - Lesson: Percentages: change, reverse and repeated change

## Goal
The learner converts between fractions, decimals and percentages, calculates percentages of amounts, applies increases and decreases with multipliers, reverses a percentage change, and models simple and compound interest and depreciation.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 2.03a - Convert between fractions, decimals and percentages: switch freely between the three forms
- 2.03b - Percentage as parts per hundred; calculating percentages: find a percentage of an amount and express one amount as a percentage of another
- 2.03c - Percentage increase and decrease; simple interest; multipliers and reverse percentages (Higher): apply percentage change with a single multiplier and reverse it
- 5.03a - Simple and compound interest, depreciation; exponential growth and decay (Higher): model repeated percentage change

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A jacket is reduced by 20%, then by another 20%. Is it 40% off?' Let the learner test it with a real price before teaching multipliers.

#### 2.03a Convert between fractions, decimals and percentages
Percent means per hundred, so 37% = 37/100 = 0.37. From a fraction, divide (3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%); from a decimal, multiply by 100 (0.06 = 6%). Know the common set: 1/2 = 0.5 = 50%, 1/4 = 25%, 3/4 = 75%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/10 = 10%, 1/3 = 33.3...%, 2/3 = 66.6...%.

#### 2.03b Percentage as parts per hundred, and calculating percentages
Non-calculator: find 10% by dividing by 10, then build other percentages (35% of 240: 10% = 24, so 30% = 72 and 5% = 12, total 84). Calculator: multiply by the decimal (0.35 x 240). To express one amount as a percentage of another, divide and multiply by 100 (18 out of 40 = 18/40 = 0.45 = 45%).

#### 2.03c Percentage change, simple interest, multipliers and reverse percentages
A percentage increase of p% has multiplier 1 + p/100 (an increase of 15% is x 1.15); a decrease has 1 - p/100 (a decrease of 8% is x 0.92). Simple interest is the same amount every year: 5% of 800 = 40 per year, so 3 years gives 120. Percentage change is (change / original) x 100: a price rising from 40 to 46 is 6/40 = 15%. Higher: reverse percentages divide by the multiplier. If a price after a 20% increase is 90, the original is 90 / 1.2 = 75 (not 90 less 20%). Emphasise that the percentage is always of the original amount.

#### 5.03a Interest, depreciation, growth and decay
Compound interest applies the multiplier repeatedly: 1200 at 3% for 4 years is 1200 x 1.03^4 = 1350.61 (to the penny). Depreciation is the same with a multiplier below 1: a car worth 14 000 losing 12% a year is worth 14 000 x 0.88^3 = 9541 after 3 years. Higher: write the model generally as final = original x multiplier^n, recognise exponential growth (multiplier above 1) and decay (below 1), and interpret what the multiplier means in context. Compare with simple interest to show why compound growth accelerates.
