# S06_Ratio_and_Proportion - Lesson: Ratio and proportion

## Goal
The learner writes, simplifies and shares in ratios, links ratio to fractions, solves direct and inverse proportion problems, and (Higher) writes proportion as equations of the form y = kx and y = k/x.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 5.01a - Ratios as a:b and 1:n; simplifying: write, simplify and compare ratios
- 5.01b - Splitting a quantity in a ratio (three or more parts at Higher): share an amount in a given ratio
- 5.01c - Ratio as a fraction of the whole: link ratio parts to fractions of the total
- 5.01d - Simple ratio and proportion problems; ratio and linear functions (Higher): solve scaling problems and connect ratio to y = mx
- 5.02a - Direct proportion; y = kx (Higher): solve direct-proportion problems, formally with y = kx at Higher
- 5.02b - Inverse proportion; y = k/x (Higher): solve inverse-proportion problems, formally with y = k/x at Higher

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to share 30 sweets between three friends in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5 before teaching a method, and let them describe how they did it.

#### 5.01a Writing and simplifying ratios
A ratio compares quantities in the same units: 45 cm to 1.5 m is 45 : 150 = 3 : 10. Simplify by dividing every part by the HCF. Write a ratio in the form 1 : n by dividing by the left number (4 : 10 = 1 : 2.5). Compare ratios by writing them as 1 : n so they are on the same footing.

#### 5.01b Dividing a quantity in a ratio
Add the parts to find the total number of shares, divide the amount by that total to find one share, then multiply back up. 240 shared 3 : 5 gives 8 shares, one share = 30, so the parts are 90 and 150. Check that the parts sum to the total. Higher: three or more parts (2 : 3 : 5), and problems that give one part and ask for the other or for the total ('the smaller share is 36').

#### 5.01c Ratio as a fraction of the whole
In a ratio a : b the first quantity is a/(a + b) of the total. If boys : girls = 3 : 4 then boys are 3/7 of the class. Use this to move between ratio and fraction/percentage language, and to solve problems such as 'the fraction of the mixture that is water'.

#### 5.01d Ratio and proportion problems, and ratio and linear functions
Use the unitary method: if 5 pens cost 3.50 then 1 pen costs 0.70 and 8 pens cost 5.60. Scale recipes and maps with the same idea. Higher: a ratio y : x that is constant means y = kx, a straight line through the origin with gradient k, connecting ratio to graphs.

#### 5.02a Direct proportion
Two quantities are in direct proportion if one is a fixed multiple of the other: doubling one doubles the other. Test with the unitary method or by checking that the ratio stays constant. Higher: write y proportional to x as y = kx, find k from given values (y = 12 when x = 3 gives k = 4), then use it. The same approach handles y = kx^2 (area from radius) and y = k sqrt(x): find k, then substitute.

#### 5.02b Inverse proportion
If one quantity goes up as the other goes down so that the product stays constant, they are in inverse proportion: 4 workers take 12 days, so 6 workers take 8 days (4 x 12 = 6 x 8 = 48). Higher: write y = k/x, find k, then use it. If y is inversely proportional to x^2, y = k/x^2. Always say in words which type of proportion applies before calculating.
