# S14_Coordinates_and_Straight_Line_Graphs - Lesson: Coordinates, tables of values and straight-line graphs

## Goal
The learner plots and reads coordinates, draws graphs from tables of values, finds and uses the gradient and intercept of a straight line, uses parallel and perpendicular gradients, and reads solutions to equations from graphs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 7.01a - Coordinates in all four quadrants: plot and read coordinates
- 7.01b - Plot graphs from tables of values: draw graphs from a table of values
- 7.02a - Gradient and intercept; y = mx + c: read and write straight-line equations
- 7.02b - Parallel and perpendicular lines; tangent to a circle (Higher): use gradient relationships between lines
- 6.03d - Approximate solutions from graphs: read solutions off graphs

## How to teach this
Show a line on a grid and ask: 'What single rule connects every x with its y?' Let the learner spot the pattern before introducing y = mx + c.

#### 7.01a Coordinates in all four quadrants
Coordinates are (x, y): across first, then up or down. The axes divide the plane into four quadrants; check the signs to identify the quadrant. The midpoint of A(x1, y1) and B(x2, y2) is ((x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2): the midpoint of (-2, 5) and (6, 1) is (2, 3). Choose axis scales carefully so the points fit.

#### 7.01b Drawing graphs from tables of values
Substitute each x into the rule, plot the points, and join with a ruler for straight lines or a smooth curve for curves. For y = x^2 - 2x - 1 with x from -2 to 4 the y-values are 7, 2, -1, -2, -1, 2, 7. If a plotted point is out of line, recheck the substitution before redrawing. Label the axes and the graph with its equation.

#### 7.02a Gradient, intercept and y = mx + c
Gradient = change in y / change in x. A line through (1, 3) and (3, 11) has m = 8/2 = 4, and substituting a point gives 3 = 4(1) + c, so c = -1 and y = 4x - 1. In y = mx + c, m is the gradient and c is where the line crosses the y-axis. Rearrange first when needed: 2y + 6x = 10 becomes y = -3x + 5. Sketch a line quickly by marking the intercept and using the gradient to find a second point.

#### 7.02b Parallel and perpendicular lines; tangents to circles
Higher only. Parallel lines have equal gradients. Perpendicular lines have gradients whose product is -1: m2 = -1/m1. The line through (4, 3) perpendicular to y = 2x + 1 has gradient -1/2, so 3 = -2 + c, c = 5, giving y = -x/2 + 5. The tangent to x^2 + y^2 = 25 at (3, 4): the radius has gradient 4/3, so the tangent has gradient -3/4 and y - 4 = -3/4 (x - 3), or 4y = -3x + 25.

#### 6.03d Approximate solutions from graphs
To solve an equation using a graph, draw the graph of one side and read where it meets the other. Solving x^2 - 2x - 1 = 0 means reading where y = x^2 - 2x - 1 crosses y = 0 (the x-axis); solving x^2 - 2x - 1 = 2 needs the line y = 2. Two graphs' intersection points solve the corresponding simultaneous equations. Read values to a sensible accuracy, usually 1 decimal place, and give x-values only when the question asks for solutions of an equation in x.
