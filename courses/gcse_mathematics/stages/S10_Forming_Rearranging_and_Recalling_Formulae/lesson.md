# S10_Forming_Rearranging_and_Recalling_Formulae - Lesson: Forming, rearranging and recalling formulae

## Goal
The learner forms formulae from contexts, rearranges them, recalls the formulae the exam does not give, and uses the constant-acceleration formulae.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 6.02a - Form formulae and expressions from contexts: turn a worded situation into a formula
- 6.02c - Rearrange formulae, including repeated subject (Higher): change the subject of a formula
- 6.02d - Recall standard formulae (circle, Pythagoras, trigonometry, quadratic, sine/cosine rules): know the formulae that are not given in the exam
- 6.02e - Constant-acceleration (kinematics) formulae: use v = u + at and related formulae

## How to teach this
Ask: 'A plumber charges 40 to visit plus 25 an hour. Write a rule for the cost and then use it backwards to find the time for a 165 bill.' The second part introduces rearranging.

#### 6.02a Forming formulae and expressions from contexts
Name the letters, then translate each phrase. A plumber's cost C for h hours is C = 40 + 25h. The perimeter of a rectangle with length (x + 3) and width x is P = 2(x + 3) + 2x = 4x + 6. Say what each letter stands for, and check the formula with one easy number.

#### 6.02c Rearranging formulae
Treat it like solving an equation: whatever you do to one side, do to the other, using inverse operations in reverse order. v = u + at gives t = (v - u)/a. A = pi r^2 gives r = sqrt(A/pi). For C = 5(F - 32)/9, multiply by 9, divide by 5, add 32: F = 9C/5 + 32. Higher: when the subject appears more than once, collect those terms on one side and factorise. From y = (3x + 1)/(x - 2): y(x - 2) = 3x + 1, xy - 2y = 3x + 1, xy - 3x = 2y + 1, x(y - 3) = 2y + 1, so x = (2y + 1)/(y - 3).

#### 6.02d Recalling standard formulae
Recall without being given them: circumference C = 2 pi r and area A = pi r^2 of a circle, Pythagoras' theorem a^2 + b^2 = c^2, the trigonometric ratios (sin = opposite/hypotenuse, cos = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan = opposite/adjacent), and, at Higher, the quadratic formula x = (-b +/- sqrt(b^2 - 4ac))/(2a), the sine rule a/sin A = b/sin B, the cosine rule a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc cos A and the triangle area 1/2 ab sin C. Encourage regular low-stakes recall practice: write the formula, then use it once.

#### 6.02e Constant-acceleration (kinematics) formulae
With initial speed u, final speed v, acceleration a, time t and distance s: v = u + at, s = ut + 1/2 a t^2 and v^2 = u^2 + 2as. Choose the formula that contains the three known quantities and the one wanted. A car starts from rest (u = 0), accelerates at 2 m/s^2 for 5 s: v = 10 m/s and s = 25 m. Keep units consistent (m, s, m/s, m/s^2).
