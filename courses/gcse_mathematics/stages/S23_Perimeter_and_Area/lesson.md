# S23_Perimeter_and_Area - Lesson: Perimeter and area

## Goal
The learner finds perimeters and areas of rectilinear, triangular, quadrilateral and circular shapes, including arcs, sectors and composite shapes.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 10.02a - Perimeter of rectilinear shapes: find perimeters of straight-sided shapes
- 10.02b - Circumference and arc length: calculate circumference and arc length
- 10.02c - Perimeter of composite shapes: find perimeters of shapes built from parts
- 10.03a - Area of triangles, including 1/2 ab sin C: find triangle areas including by trigonometry
- 10.03b - Area of parallelograms: find parallelogram areas
- 10.03c - Area of trapezia: find trapezium areas
- 10.03d - Area of circles and sectors: find circle and sector areas
- 10.03e - Area of composite shapes: find areas of shapes built from parts

## How to teach this
Give an L-shaped floor plan and ask: 'How could you work out how much carpet and how much skirting board is needed?' It separates area from perimeter naturally.

#### 10.02a Perimeter of rectilinear shapes
Perimeter is the total distance round the edge, in length units. For a rectilinear shape, work out the missing sides from the given ones first: a 10 by 6 rectangle with a 3 by 2 notch removed from a corner still has perimeter 32 (notches do not change the perimeter of a corner cut-out). Check that every edge is included once.

#### 10.02b Circumference and arc length
Circumference C = 2 pi r or pi d. An arc is a fraction of the circumference: arc length = (theta/360) x 2 pi r. A 60-degree arc of a circle of radius 9 cm has length 1/6 x 18 pi = 3 pi cm = 9.42 cm to 2 decimal places. A semicircle's perimeter includes the diameter.

#### 10.02c Perimeter of composite shapes
Break the outline into straight edges and arcs and add only the parts that form the boundary. A running track made of a rectangle 100 m by 40 m with a semicircle on each 40 m end has perimeter 2 x 100 + 2 pi x 20 = 200 + 40 pi = 325.7 m to 1 decimal place. Take care not to include internal lines.

#### 10.03a Area of triangles
Area = 1/2 x base x perpendicular height. Use the height at right angles to the chosen base, not the slanted side. Higher: when two sides and the included angle are known, area = 1/2 ab sin C. A triangle with sides 7 and 9 and included angle 40 degrees has area 1/2 x 7 x 9 x sin 40 = 20.2 to 3 significant figures.

#### 10.03b Area of parallelograms
Area = base x perpendicular height, not base times slanted side. A parallelogram with base 12 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm has area 60 cm^2. It can be cut and re-arranged into a rectangle to show why.

#### 10.03c Area of trapezia
Area = 1/2 (a + b) h, where a and b are the parallel sides and h is the distance between them. Parallel sides of 8 and 14 with height 5 give 1/2 x 22 x 5 = 55. Split into rectangle and triangle to check.

#### 10.03d Area of circles and sectors
Area of a circle = pi r^2 (not 2 pi r). A sector is a fraction of a circle: area = (theta/360) x pi r^2. A 90-degree sector of radius 8 cm has area 1/4 x 64 pi = 16 pi = 50.3 cm^2. Higher: the area of a segment is the sector area minus the triangle area: for angle 90 degrees and r = 8, 16 pi - 32 = 18.3 cm^2.

#### 10.03e Area of composite shapes
Split into standard shapes, or subtract a cut-out from an enclosing shape, and write the area of each part before combining. A window in the shape of a rectangle 60 cm by 90 cm with a semicircle of diameter 60 cm on top has area 5400 + 1/2 x pi x 30^2 = 5400 + 450 pi = 6813.7 cm^2. Give exact answers in terms of pi when asked (5400 + 450 pi).
