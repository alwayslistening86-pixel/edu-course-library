# S17_Angles_Polygons_and_Symmetry - Lesson: Geometry vocabulary, angle facts, polygons and symmetry

## Goal
The learner uses geometric vocabulary and notation precisely, applies angle facts with stated reasons, finds interior and exterior angles of polygons, and uses the properties and symmetries of triangles and quadrilaterals.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 8.01a - Vocabulary: points, lines, planes, parallel, perpendicular: use geometric terminology correctly
- 8.01b - Angle types; labelling conventions for triangles: name angles and sides using standard notation
- 8.01c - Polygon terminology: name and classify polygons
- 8.03a - Angles at a point; angle proofs: use angles at a point and justify with reasons
- 8.03b - Angles on a straight line: use the 180-degree fact
- 8.03c - Angles in parallel lines: use alternate, corresponding and co-interior angles
- 8.03d - Angle sum of triangles and polygons: find interior and exterior angles
- 8.04a - Properties of triangles: use triangle properties in reasoning
- 8.04b - Properties of quadrilaterals: use quadrilateral properties in reasoning
- 8.04c - Reflection and rotation symmetry of polygons: identify lines and orders of symmetry

## How to teach this
Give an angle diagram description with a missing angle and ask: 'What is x, and which fact tells you so?' Require a stated reason for every angle, not just the number.

#### 8.01a Points, lines, planes, parallel and perpendicular
A point has position only; a line segment has two end points; a line extends without end; a plane is a flat surface. Parallel lines never meet and are marked with matching arrows. Perpendicular lines meet at 90 degrees, marked with a small square. Vertices are corners and edges join them.

#### 8.01b Angle types and naming conventions
Acute is under 90 degrees, right is exactly 90, obtuse is between 90 and 180, straight is 180, reflex is between 180 and 360. Angle ABC means the angle at B between BA and BC. In a triangle, the side opposite vertex A is written a. Equal sides are ticked and equal angles are arced. Use three-letter notation to avoid ambiguity.

#### 8.01c Polygon terminology
A polygon is a closed shape with straight sides. Name by number of sides: triangle 3, quadrilateral 4, pentagon 5, hexagon 6, heptagon 7, octagon 8, nonagon 9, decagon 10. A regular polygon has all sides and all angles equal. Triangles: equilateral (three equal sides), isosceles (two), scalene (none), right-angled (one 90 degree angle).

#### 8.03a Angles at a point and angle proofs
Angles round a point sum to 360 degrees. Vertically opposite angles, formed when two straight lines cross, are equal. To justify an angle, write it as a chain: 'angle ABC = 62 degrees because vertically opposite angles are equal'. Higher: build short proofs, for example prove the angles at a point on two intersecting lines are 360 degrees using the fact that angles on a line sum to 180 degrees.

#### 8.03b Angles on a straight line
Angles on a straight line sum to 180 degrees. If one angle is 118 degrees, the adjacent angle is 62 degrees. Use this to find missing angles in several linked steps: state the fact used each time.

#### 8.03c Angles in parallel lines
Alternate angles (Z shape) are equal, corresponding angles (F shape) are equal, and co-interior angles (C shape) add to 180 degrees. If AB is parallel to CD and a transversal makes an angle of 65 degrees with AB, the alternate angle with CD is 65 degrees and the co-interior angle is 115 degrees. Always name the fact fully, never 'Z angles' alone.

#### 8.03d Angle sums of triangles and polygons
Angles in a triangle sum to 180 degrees; in a quadrilateral, 360 degrees. Interior angle sum of an n-sided polygon = (n - 2) x 180 degrees. Exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360 degrees, so each exterior angle of a regular n-gon is 360/n and each interior angle is 180 - 360/n. A regular decagon has exterior angle 36 degrees and interior angle 144 degrees. Higher: derive the (n - 2) x 180 formula by splitting a polygon into triangles and prove the triangle angle sum using a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side.

#### 8.04a Properties of triangles
Isosceles: two equal sides and equal base angles. Equilateral: all angles 60 degrees. The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles. Use properties to justify: 'triangle ABC is isosceles so angle ACB = angle ABC'. Higher: write a full reasoned proof of a triangle result.

#### 8.04b Properties of quadrilaterals
Square: four equal sides and angles, diagonals equal and bisecting at 90 degrees. Rectangle: opposite sides equal, four right angles, diagonals equal. Parallelogram: opposite sides parallel and equal, opposite angles equal, diagonals bisect each other. Rhombus: four equal sides, diagonals bisect at 90 degrees. Kite: two pairs of adjacent equal sides, one pair of equal angles. Trapezium: one pair of parallel sides. Higher: use these properties in short proofs, e.g. the angles of a parallelogram.

#### 8.04c Symmetry of polygons
A line of symmetry is a mirror line; the order of rotational symmetry is the number of times a shape looks the same in one full turn. A regular n-gon has n lines and rotational order n. A rectangle has 2 lines and order 2. A parallelogram has no lines and order 2. A kite has 1 line and order 1. A rhombus has 2 lines and order 2.
