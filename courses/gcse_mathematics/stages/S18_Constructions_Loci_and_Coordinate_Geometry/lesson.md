# S18_Constructions_Loci_and_Coordinate_Geometry - Lesson: Drawing, constructions, loci and coordinates in geometry

## Goal
The learner draws diagrams from descriptions, constructs triangles and standard constructions accurately, draws loci, and uses coordinates to solve geometric problems.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 8.01e - Draw diagrams from written descriptions: turn a description into an accurate sketch
- 8.01f - Constructions with ruler, protractor, compasses: construct triangles and angles accurately
- 8.01g - Coordinates in plane geometry: use coordinates to solve geometry problems
- 8.02a - Perpendicular bisector and midpoint constructions: construct perpendicular bisectors
- 8.02b - Angle bisector construction: bisect an angle with compasses
- 8.02c - Perpendicular from a point to a line (Higher): construct the shortest distance from a point to a line
- 8.02d - Loci from constructions (Higher): draw and use loci to solve region problems

## How to teach this
Give a written description ('triangle ABC has AB = 7 cm, angle at A is 50 degrees and AC = 5 cm') and ask the learner to sketch it before measuring, so the description is understood before the drawing is done accurately.

#### 8.01e Drawing diagrams from written descriptions
Read the description slowly, mark each piece of given information as you sketch, and label vertices in the order given. Sketch first, with lengths and angles marked, then draw accurately if asked. A sketch need not be to scale but must show the correct shape and relationships (parallel, equal, right angles).

#### 8.01f Accurate constructions with ruler, protractor and compasses
Use a sharp pencil. Measure lengths to the nearest millimetre and angles to 1 degree. SAS: draw the base, measure the angle, mark the second length. SSS: draw the base, then arcs from each end with the two remaining lengths; their crossing gives the third vertex. Leave construction arcs visible. Marks typically allow small tolerance (about 2 mm and 2 degrees).

#### 8.01g Coordinates in plane geometry
Use coordinates to work with shapes. The distance between (x1, y1) and (x2, y2) is sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2) by Pythagoras. The midpoint is the average of the coordinates. Show that A(1, 2), B(4, 3), C(5, 6), D(2, 5) is a parallelogram: AB has gradient 1/3 and DC has gradient 1/3 (parallel), and AD has gradient 3 and BC has gradient 3 (parallel). Use gradient to show parallel or perpendicular sides, and lengths to show equal sides.

#### 8.02a Perpendicular bisector and midpoint
With compasses set to more than half the length of AB, draw arcs from A above and below the line, then repeat from B with the same setting. Join the arc crossing points: this line passes through the midpoint of AB at 90 degrees. Every point on it is the same distance from A and B.

#### 8.02b Angle bisector
Place the compass point at the vertex and draw an arc cutting both arms. From each intersection draw arcs of equal radius that cross inside the angle. The line from the vertex through the crossing point splits the angle in two equal parts. Every point on the bisector is equidistant from the two arms.

#### 8.02c Perpendicular from a point to a line
Higher only. From the point P draw an arc that cuts the line at two points, A and B. From A and from B draw arcs of the same radius that cross on the far side of the line at Q. The line PQ is perpendicular to the line and gives the shortest distance from P to the line. If P is on the line, draw arcs either side of P on the line first.

#### 8.02d Loci
Higher only. A locus is the set of points satisfying a rule: at a fixed distance from a point is a circle; at a fixed distance from a line is a pair of parallel lines with semicircular ends; equidistant from two points is their perpendicular bisector; equidistant from two lines is the angle bisector. To solve a region problem, draw each locus with construction marks and shade the region satisfying all the conditions. Example: 'a shrub must be closer to A than to B and less than 4 cm from C' means the side of the perpendicular bisector of AB nearest A, and inside a circle of radius 4 cm centred at C.
