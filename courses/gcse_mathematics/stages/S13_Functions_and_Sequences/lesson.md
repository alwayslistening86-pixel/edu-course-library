# S13_Functions_and_Sequences - Lesson: Functions and sequences

## Goal
The learner uses function notation, finds inverse and composite functions, generates sequences from rules, and finds nth-term formulae and special sequences.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 6.05a - Functions: input/output, inverse and composite functions: use function notation, inverses and composites
- 6.06a - Sequences: position-to-term rules and nth term (Higher): generate sequences and find nth-term rules
- 6.06b - Special sequences and other nth-term sequences (Higher): recognise special sequences and quadratic nth terms

## How to teach this
Present a number machine: 'input x, multiply by 3, subtract 2'. Ask for outputs, then for the input that gives 13, before writing f(x) = 3x - 2.

#### 6.05a Functions, inverse and composite functions
f(x) = 3x - 2 means 'input x, output 3x - 2'. f(4) = 10. The inverse function undoes the machine in reverse order: swap the letters and rearrange, so from y = 3x - 2 we get f^-1(x) = (x + 2)/3. A composite function applies one function after another, working from the inside: with g(x) = x^2, fg(x) = f(g(x)) = 3x^2 - 2 and gf(x) = (3x - 2)^2, which are different. Higher: solve equations such as fg(x) = 25 by forming the composite first, and check that f f^-1(x) = x.

#### 6.06a Sequences: term-to-term and position-to-term rules, and the nth term
A term-to-term rule gives each term from the previous one (start at 3, add 4: 3, 7, 11, ...). The nth term gives the term from its position n. For a linear sequence the common difference d is the coefficient of n: 3, 7, 11 has d = 4, so nth term = 4n + (3 - 4) = 4n - 1. To test whether 100 is in the sequence 5n + 2, solve 5n + 2 = 100 and see whether n is a whole number (19.6, so no). Higher: quadratic sequences. For 2, 7, 14, 23 the first differences are 5, 7, 9 and the second differences are constant at 2, so the nth term begins with n^2 (half the second difference). Subtract n^2 to get 1, 3, 5, 7 = 2n - 1, giving n^2 + 2n - 1.

#### 6.06b Special sequences
Recognise square numbers (1, 4, 9, 16), cube numbers (1, 8, 27), triangular numbers (1, 3, 6, 10, 15, nth term n(n + 1)/2), the Fibonacci sequence (each term is the sum of the previous two), powers of 2 and 10. Distinguish arithmetic (constant difference) from geometric sequences (constant multiplier r): 3, 6, 12, 24 has nth term 3 x 2^(n - 1). Higher: use a nth term with n^2 or a power of a number to generate any term, and recognise a quadratic-shaped rule from constant second differences.
