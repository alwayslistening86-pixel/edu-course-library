# S02_Fractions_and_Decimals - Lesson: Fractions and decimals: equivalence, arithmetic and ordering

## Goal
The learner works fluently with fractions, mixed numbers and decimals, converts between them, calculates by hand, and orders and compares numbers written in different forms.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 2.01a - Equivalent fractions and mixed numbers: move between equivalent fractions, mixed numbers and improper fractions
- 2.01b - Fraction arithmetic including mixed numbers (improper fractions at Higher): add, subtract, multiply and divide fractions
- 2.01c - Fractions of quantities; one quantity as a fraction of another: find a fraction of an amount and write one amount as a fraction of another
- 2.02a - Fractions to decimals; place value; recurring decimals to fractions (Higher): convert between fractions and decimals, including recurring
- 2.02b - Decimal arithmetic including negatives, without a calculator: add, subtract and multiply decimals by hand
- 2.02c - Dividing decimals without a calculator: divide by whole numbers and (Higher) by decimals by hand
- 2.04a - Order integers, fractions, decimals and percentages: put mixed forms in order by converting to a common form
- 2.04b - Comparison symbols: use <, >, <=, >= correctly

## How to teach this
Ask: 'Which is bigger, 5/8 or 0.6, and how could you prove it?' before any rules. Let the learner find their own method (common denominators, converting to decimals) and then name it.

#### 2.01a Equivalent fractions and mixed numbers
Multiplying or dividing numerator and denominator by the same number gives an equivalent fraction (3/4 = 9/12 = 15/20). Simplify by dividing by the HCF so 18/24 = 3/4. A mixed number like 2 3/5 becomes the improper fraction 13/5 (2 x 5 + 3 over 5), and back again by dividing: 13 / 5 = 2 remainder 3.

#### 2.01b Fraction arithmetic
Add and subtract by rewriting over a common denominator and only then combining the numerators: 2/3 + 3/4 = 8/12 + 9/12 = 17/12 = 1 5/12. Multiply straight across (2/3 x 3/4 = 6/12 = 1/2), cancelling first when possible. Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal (2/3 / 4/5 = 2/3 x 5/4 = 10/12 = 5/6). With mixed numbers, convert to improper fractions first. At Higher, expect longer multi-step calculations mixing improper fractions and negatives.

#### 2.01c Fractions of quantities and one quantity as a fraction of another
To find 3/5 of 60: divide by the denominator (60 / 5 = 12) then multiply by the numerator (12 x 3 = 36). To write 35 minutes as a fraction of 2 hours, use the same units first (35/120 = 7/24). For fractions greater than 1, 5/4 of 80 is 100. Reverse problems ('3/5 of a number is 36') divide by the numerator then multiply by the denominator.

#### 2.02a Fractions to decimals, place value and recurring decimals
Convert by dividing the numerator by the denominator (3/8 = 0.375). Place value: in 4.507 the 5 is worth 5 tenths and the 7 is worth 7 thousandths. Fractions with denominators containing only the factors 2 and 5 terminate; others recur (1/3 = 0.333...). Higher: turn a recurring decimal into a fraction. Let x = 0.454545...; then 100x = 45.4545..., so 99x = 45, x = 45/99 = 5/11.

#### 2.02b Decimal arithmetic without a calculator
Add and subtract by lining up the decimal points. To multiply 3.2 x 0.4, calculate 32 x 4 = 128 and place the decimal point so there are as many decimal places in the answer as in the question in total (two here): 1.28. Negative decimals follow the same sign rules as integers. Estimate first to check where the point belongs.

#### 2.02c Dividing decimals without a calculator
Dividing a decimal by a whole number uses short or long division with the point carried straight up (7.5 / 5 = 1.5). Higher: dividing by a decimal, multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 to make the divisor whole (4.8 / 0.06 becomes 480 / 6 = 80).

#### 2.04a Ordering integers, fractions, decimals and percentages
Convert everything to the same form before comparing, usually decimals. To order 0.6, 5/8, 59% and 3/5, convert each to a decimal: 0.6, 0.625, 0.59 and 0.6. That gives 59% < 0.6 = 3/5 < 5/8. Write out the converted values as evidence rather than just the final order.

#### 2.04b Comparison symbols
< means less than, > greater than, <= less than or equal to, >= greater than or equal to. The open end of the symbol faces the larger number. Practise reading and writing statements such as 0.6 < 5/8 and 3/5 >= 60%.
