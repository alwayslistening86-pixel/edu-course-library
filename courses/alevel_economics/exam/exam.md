# A-level Economics (AQA 7136) - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
Paper 1 covers microeconomics (S01-S14): one data-response context (questions of 2, 4, 9 and 25 marks) and one essay (a 15-mark 'explain' question and a 25-mark 'evaluate' question). Paper 2 covers macroeconomics (S15-S22) in the same format. Paper 3 covers everything: 30 multiple-choice questions and a case study with several extended questions. Questions are original; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests. The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest.

## 12 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. [Paper 1] Define price elasticity of supply. [2 marks]
2. [Paper 1] A cinema raises ticket prices from £10 to £12 and attendance falls from 5,000 to 4,500 a week. Calculate the PED and the change in weekly revenue. [4 marks]
3. [Paper 1] With the aid of a diagram, explain why a monopsony employer may pay lower wages and employ fewer workers than a competitive labour market. [9 marks]
4. [Paper 1] Essay: 'Indirect taxes are the best way to correct the market failure caused by demerit goods.' Evaluate this view. [25 marks]
5. [Paper 2] Nominal wages rose by 6% while CPI inflation was 4%. Calculate the approximate change in real wages and explain its significance. [4 marks]
6. [Paper 2] Explain how an appreciation of the pound is likely to affect UK inflation and the current account. [9 marks]
7. [Paper 2] Essay: 'Monetary policy is more effective than fiscal policy in managing aggregate demand.' Evaluate this view. [25 marks]
8. [Paper 2] The marginal propensity to consume domestic output is 0.6. Investment falls by £12bn. Calculate the change in national income. [2 marks]
9. [Paper 3] A good has YED of -0.4. It is: Choose every correct option.
   A. an inferior good
   B. a normal necessity
   C. a luxury
   D. a complement
10. [Paper 3] Which would shift the long-run aggregate supply curve to the right? Choose every correct option.
   A. an improvement in labour productivity
   B. a cut in interest rates
   C. net inward migration of working-age people
   D. an increase in consumer confidence
11. [Paper 3] Which condition is essential for a market to be perfectly contestable? Choose every correct option.
   A. low sunk costs
   B. a single firm
   C. product differentiation
   D. high advertising spending
12. [Paper 3] Case study: a government is considering nationalising the rail industry. Evaluate the case for public ownership of railways. [15 marks]

## Grading
Mark short answers against the mark schemes; mark 9-, 15- and 25-mark answers by levels (knowledge, application and analysis; 25-mark answers also need evaluation leading to a supported judgement). A pass needs at least 50% of the 240 marks overall and at least 40% on each paper. Report the total, the percentage per paper and the weakest topic areas. AQA's grade boundaries change every series, so the course does not claim a predicted grade.
