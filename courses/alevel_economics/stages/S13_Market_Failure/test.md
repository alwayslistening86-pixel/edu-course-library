# S13_Market_Failure - Test: The market mechanism and market failure

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: short calculations and explanations, a data-response or essay question, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below (levels-style for longer answers) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain, using a diagram described in words, why a free market overproduces a good whose production creates pollution. [6 marks]
2. Explain why public goods will be underprovided by the free market. [3 marks]
3. Explain, using an example, why a merit good is underconsumed in a free market. [4 marks]
4. Explain why a road can be described as a quasi-public good. [2 marks]
5. Explain how geographical immobility of labour can cause market failure. [3 marks]
6. Which are true of a negative production externality? Choose every correct option.
   A. marginal social cost exceeds marginal private cost
   B. the market price is too high
   C. the market output is too high
   D. a welfare loss results

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 MPC (supply) and MSC curves, with MSC above MPC by the external cost; B1 MPB = MSB (demand); B1 market output where MPC = MPB; B1 socially optimal output where MSC = MSB is lower; B1 so the market overproduces (price too low); B1 welfare loss triangle between MSC and MSB over the excess output.
2. [3] B1 non-excludable, so consumers can benefit without paying (free-rider problem); B1 firms can't charge enough to make a profit; B1 so the market fails to provide them (missing market).
3. [4] B1 e.g. education; B1 positive externalities (a more productive workforce) aren't considered by individuals; B1 imperfect information: people underestimate the private benefits; B1 so consumption is below the socially optimal level.
4. [2] B1 usually non-excludable/non-rival when uncongested; B1 but becomes rival when congested, and can be made excludable with tolls.
5. [3] B1 workers can't move to areas with job vacancies (housing costs, family ties); B1 so unemployment coexists with labour shortages; B1 resources are misallocated/underused.
6. Correct: A, C, D (exactly these options, no others)
