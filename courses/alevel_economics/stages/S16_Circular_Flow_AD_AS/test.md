## Test items
1. Government spending rises by £20bn. The marginal propensity to consume domestic output (after tax) is 0.6. Calculate the final change in national income and explain the multiplier process. [4 marks]
2. Explain the accelerator process and why it makes investment volatile. [3 marks]
3. Using AD/AS analysis (described in words), explain the effects of a large rise in world oil prices on the UK economy. [6 marks]
4. Compare the Keynesian and classical views of the long-run aggregate supply curve and their policy implications. [6 marks]
5. Explain two reasons why the AD curve slopes downwards. [4 marks]
6. Which are injections into the circular flow of income? Choose every correct option.
   A. investment
   B. savings
   C. exports
   D. government spending

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 k = 1/(1 - 0.6) = 2.5; A1 £50bn; B1 the initial spending becomes income for others; B1 who spend a proportion of it, creating further rounds of spending that diminish because of withdrawals.
2. [3] B1 investment depends on the rate of change of output/demand; B1 when growth speeds up, firms need more capital, so investment rises sharply; B1 a slowdown in growth (even without a fall in output) can cause investment to fall.
3. [6] B1 higher costs of production; B1 SRAS shifts left; B1 the price level rises (cost-push inflation); B1 real output falls (and unemployment may rise): stagflation; B1 AD may also fall as real incomes are squeezed; B1 size depends on dependence on oil and the response of wages/policy.
4. [6] B1 classical: LRAS vertical at full-employment output; B1 markets self-correct; B1 so demand management affects only prices in the long run; B1 Keynesian: horizontal at low output, then rising, vertical at full capacity; B1 economies can be stuck below full employment; B1 so increasing AD can raise output where there is spare capacity.
5. [4] B1 wealth effect: a lower price level raises the real value of wealth; B1 increasing consumption; B1 trade effect: lower UK prices make exports more competitive and imports less attractive; B1 increasing net exports (or interest rate effect).
6. Correct: A, C, D (exactly these options, no others)
