## Test items
1. Explain how the principle of diminishing marginal utility helps explain why demand curves slope downwards. [3 marks]
2. Using examples, explain two biases in consumer decision making identified by behavioural economists. [4 marks]
3. Explain how asymmetric information can lead to market failure in the market for used cars. [3 marks]
4. Explain how nudges could be used by the government to reduce obesity. [9 marks]
5. Explain what is meant by bounded rationality. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 each extra unit consumed gives less additional satisfaction; B1 so consumers are only willing to pay less for additional units; B1 hence quantity demanded rises only as price falls.
2. [4] B1 anchoring: decisions influenced by an initial reference value; B1 e.g. a 'was £100, now £60' price; B1 availability bias: judging likelihood by how easily examples come to mind; B1 e.g. overestimating the risk of flying after a crash is in the news (or rules of thumb, with an example).
3. [3] B1 sellers know more about the car's quality than buyers; B1 buyers may be unwilling to pay high prices for fear of 'lemons', so good cars are withdrawn (adverse selection); B1 resources misallocated: fewer mutually beneficial trades occur.
4. [9] Level 3 (7-9): clear chains of analysis of how nudges change choice architecture (calorie labels, healthy options at eye level, smaller default portions) and address bounded self-control and biases, applied to food choices, with relevant examples. Level 2 (4-6): some relevant analysis with limited development or application. Level 1 (1-3): mostly descriptive knowledge.
5. [2] B1 people try to make rational decisions but are limited by information, cognitive ability and time; B1 so they make satisfactory rather than optimal choices (satisficing).
