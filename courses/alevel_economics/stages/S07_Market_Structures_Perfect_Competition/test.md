## Test items
1. Explain, using diagrams described in words, how supernormal profits in the short run are eliminated in the long run in perfect competition. [6 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by the principal-agent problem in large firms. [3 marks]
3. Explain why a firm might aim to maximise sales revenue rather than profit. [3 marks]
4. Market shares of the five largest firms are 31%, 24%, 12%, 8% and 5%. Calculate the three-firm and five-firm concentration ratios. [2 marks]
5. Explain when a perfectly competitive firm will shut down in the short run. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 short run: market price above AC at the profit-maximising output (MC = MR); B1 supernormal profit shown by the area (P - AC) x Q; B1 no barriers, and perfect information, so new firms enter; B1 market supply shifts right, lowering price; B1 until price equals minimum AC; B1 only normal profit remains (long-run equilibrium, P = MC = min AC).
2. [3] B1 ownership (shareholders: principals) is separated from control (managers: agents); B1 managers may pursue their own objectives (e.g. sales, salaries, perks); B1 rather than maximising profit for shareholders.
3. [3] B1 managers' pay/status may be linked to sales or firm size; B1 larger market share can deter entry and gain economies of scale; B1 long-run profit may be higher (or to build a brand).
4. [2] B1 3-firm 67%; B1 5-firm 80%.
5. [2] B1 if price falls below average variable cost; B1 since it can't cover its variable costs, producing increases losses above the fixed costs.
