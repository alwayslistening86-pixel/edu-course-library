# S03_Demand_Supply_Elasticity - Lesson: Demand, supply and elasticities

## Goal
The learner explains the determinants of demand and supply, distinguishes movements along from shifts of the curves, calculates and interprets price, income and cross elasticities of demand and price elasticity of supply, and relates elasticity to total revenue.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.1.3.1 - The determinants of the demand for goods and services
- 4.1.3.2 - Price, income and cross elasticities of demand
- 4.1.3.3 - The determinants of the supply of goods and services
- 4.1.3.4 - Price elasticity of supply
- QS2 - Calculate, use and understand percentages and percentage changes
- QS8 - Make calculations of elasticity and interpret the result

## How to teach this
Ask why a cinema raises prices on Saturday nights but a supermarket doesn't raise the price of bread when it rains. Teach each topic as a chain of reasoning: define, explain with a diagram (described in words, then sketched by the learner), apply to a real context, then evaluate (it depends on...). Numerical examples were computed when the course was built. For current figures (Bank Rate, the National Living Wage, inflation, tax rates), check the latest official sources rather than relying on the course text.

#### 4.1.3.1 The determinants of the demand for goods and services
Demand is the quantity consumers are willing and able to buy at each price in a given period. A change in price causes a movement along the demand curve (extension or contraction); a change in any other determinant shifts it: income, prices of substitutes and complements, tastes and preferences, advertising, population and its structure, expectations. Normal goods: demand rises with income; inferior goods: demand falls as income rises.

#### 4.1.3.2 Price, income and cross elasticities of demand
Price elasticity of demand (PED) = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price: elastic if |PED| > 1, inelastic if < 1, unitary if 1; perfectly elastic or inelastic at the extremes. Determinants: availability of substitutes, proportion of income spent, necessity vs luxury (and addictiveness), time period, breadth of definition. PED and total revenue: if demand is price inelastic, a price rise increases total revenue; if elastic, it reduces it; revenue is maximised where PED is unitary. Income elasticity (YED) = % change in Qd ÷ % change in income: positive for normal goods (> 1 luxuries), negative for inferior goods. Cross elasticity (XED) = % change in Qd of good A ÷ % change in price of good B: positive for substitutes, negative for complements, zero if unrelated. Uses for firms and government (pricing, tax incidence). *Example:* price rises from £4.00 to £4.40 (+10%) and quantity falls from 500 to 460 (-8%): PED = -0.8 (inelastic); revenue rises from £2,000 to £2,024.

#### 4.1.3.3 The determinants of the supply of goods and services
Supply is the quantity producers are willing and able to sell at each price. Price changes cause movements along; shifts come from costs of production (wages, raw materials, energy), productivity, technology, indirect taxes and subsidies, the number of firms, weather (agriculture), and expectations. Higher prices increase profitability and encourage firms to supply more.

#### 4.1.3.4 Price elasticity of supply
Price elasticity of supply (PES) = % change in quantity supplied ÷ % change in price. Supply is more elastic with spare capacity, stocks, easy factor mobility and in the long run (the time period is key; agricultural supply is inelastic in the short run). *Example:* price rises 20% and quantity supplied rises 5%: PES = 0.25 (inelastic).

#### QS2 Calculate, use and understand percentages and percentage changes
Percentages and percentage change = (new - old) ÷ old x 100. *Example:* from 250 to 280 is 12%; from 280 back to 250 is -10.7%. Percentage points differ from percentages: an interest rate rising from 4% to 5% rises by 1 percentage point, or 25%.

#### QS8 Make calculations of elasticity and interpret the result
Elasticity calculations: always compute percentage changes first, keep the sign (PED is normally negative; comment on magnitude), and interpret: e.g. YED of +2.5 means a luxury (income elastic); XED of -0.6 means complements.
