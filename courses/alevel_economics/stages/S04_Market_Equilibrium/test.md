# S04_Market_Equilibrium - Test: Equilibrium prices and interrelated markets

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: short calculations and explanations, a data-response or essay question, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below (levels-style for longer answers) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain, using a demand and supply diagram (described in words), the effect of a poor coffee harvest on the market for coffee. [4 marks]
2. Explain the signalling and incentive functions of prices, using the example of rising demand for electric cars. [4 marks]
3. Explain, with an example, what is meant by joint supply, and how a rise in demand for one of the goods affects the other. [3 marks]
4. Qd = 300 - 5P and Qs = 60 + 7P. Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity, then the new equilibrium if demand rises to Qd = 360 - 5P. [4 marks]
5. Explain what is meant by derived demand, with an example. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 supply shifts left; B1 at the old price there is excess demand; B1 price rises, rationing the reduced supply; B1 new equilibrium: higher price, lower quantity (size depends on PED).
2. [4] B1 signalling: a rising price signals to producers that consumers want more; B1 incentive: higher profits encourage firms to produce more/enter the market; B1 resources are reallocated towards electric car production; B1 supply extends/increases.
3. [3] B1 two goods produced together from the same process; B1 e.g. beef and leather; B1 more demand for beef increases cattle slaughter, increasing the supply of leather and lowering its price.
4. [4] M1 300 - 5P = 60 + 7P; A1 P = 20, Q = 200; M1 360 - 5P = 60 + 7P; A1 P = 25, Q = 235.
5. [2] B1 demand for a good or factor that arises from demand for another product; B1 e.g. demand for construction workers derives from demand for new houses.
