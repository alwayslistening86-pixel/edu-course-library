# S23_Exam_Skills - Lesson: Data response, essays, case studies and quantitative skills

## Goal
The learner answers AQA data-response, essay, case-study and multiple-choice questions: interprets data in extracts (tables, graphs, text), constructs and uses diagrams, applies ratios and fractions, structures 9- and 15-mark explanations (knowledge, application, analysis) and 25-mark evaluations and reaches supported judgements.

## Syllabus items taught here
- QS1 - Calculate, use and understand ratios and fractions
- QS4 - Construct and interpret a range of standard graphical forms
- QS9 - Interpret, apply and analyse information in written, graphical and numerical forms

## How to teach this
Give the learner a short news extract with a chart and ask for three facts it shows, before any analysis. Teach each topic as a chain of reasoning: define, explain with a diagram (described in words, then sketched by the learner), apply to a real context, then evaluate (it depends on...). Numerical examples were computed when the course was built. For current figures (Bank Rate, the National Living Wage, inflation, tax rates), check the latest official sources rather than relying on the course text.

#### QS1 Calculate, use and understand ratios and fractions
Ratios and fractions. *Example:* a weighted price index with weights (out of 100) and item indices (Food: weight 18, index 104; Energy: weight 12, index 120; Housing: weight 30, index 103; Other: weight 40, index 102): weighted index = Σ(weight x index)/100 = 104.8, so the average price level rose 4.8%. The debt-to-GDP ratio: debt of £2,700bn and GDP of £2,800bn gives 96.4%.

#### QS4 Construct and interpret a range of standard graphical forms
Construct and interpret graphs: supply and demand, cost and revenue curves, AD/AS, PPFs, Lorenz curves, Phillips curves, and time-series charts from data. Diagrams need labelled axes, labelled curves, equilibrium points, and arrows showing shifts; refer to them in the text.

#### QS9 Interpret, apply and analyse information in written, graphical and numerical forms
Interpret, apply and analyse information in written, graphical and numerical forms: quote precise data (with units and dates), compare trends (not just single points), distinguish levels from rates of change (e.g. slowing growth is still growth), and use extract evidence to support analysis and evaluation. Structure: 2-mark definition; 4-mark data interpretation (two supported points); 9-mark and 15-mark 'explain' questions (knowledge, application to the context, developed chains of analysis, often with a diagram; no evaluation needed); 25-mark 'evaluate' answers add evaluation: weigh arguments, consider magnitude, time period, assumptions and 'it depends on', and conclude with a supported judgement.
