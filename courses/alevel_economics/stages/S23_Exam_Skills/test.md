# S23_Exam_Skills - Test: Data response, essays, case studies and quantitative skills

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: short calculations and explanations, a data-response or essay question, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below (levels-style for longer answers) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Weights (out of 100) and price indices for four categories are: Food: weight 20, index 106; Transport: weight 15, index 98; Housing: weight 35, index 104; Other: weight 30, index 101. Calculate the weighted average price index and the percentage rise in the price level. [2 marks]
2. Unemployment rose from 1.3 million to 1.5 million while the labour force stayed at 34 million. Calculate the change in the unemployment rate in percentage points. [2 marks]
3. Explain what distinguishes a 25-mark essay answer in the top level from one in the middle level. [4 marks]
4. Using the extract statement 'house prices rose 10% while wages rose 4%', explain the likely effect on housing affordability and consumption. [9 marks]
5. A country's exports rose by £5bn and imports rose by £8bn. What happened to its trade balance? Choose every correct option.
   A. It worsened by £3bn
   B. It improved by £3bn
   C. It worsened by £13bn
   D. It is impossible to tell

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 Σ(weight x index)/100; A1 102.6, a rise of 2.6%.
2. [2] M1 rates 3.8% and 4.4%; A1 a rise of 0.6 percentage points.
3. [4] B1 top: precise knowledge and well-developed chains of analysis; B1 applied to the context with relevant evidence/diagrams; B1 balanced evaluation weighing arguments (magnitude, time, assumptions); B1 a supported final judgement, whereas middle-level answers are less developed or one-sided.
4. [9] Level 3 (7-9): uses the data (the house-price-to-earnings ratio rose about 5.8%), analyses reduced affordability for first-time buyers (larger deposits and mortgages), the wealth effect raising homeowners' consumption, and distributional effects, with a relevant diagram (housing market or AD). Level 2 (4-6): some analysis with limited use of data. Level 1 (1-3): generic points.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)

## Grading
Apply `rubric.json`'s `stage_rubrics.S23_Exam_Skills` exactly. Mark each answer against its mark scheme (method marks need the method shown; a multiple-choice item is worth 1 mark and needs every correct option and nothing else). 18 marks in all; a pass needs at least 11 (60%, rounded up). Record `pass` or `fail` in `syllabus_status`, with the mark and a short honest note on what was missed.

## If not passed
Name the specific misconceptions the wrong answers show, run remediation on those, then re-test with fresh items of the same types (never this exact set).

Record the pass. Every stage is now passed, so the cumulative exam becomes available.
