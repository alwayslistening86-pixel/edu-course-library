## Test items
1. The pound depreciates against the dollar from $1.40 to $1.25. Calculate the new dollar price of a UK export priced at £200, and the percentage depreciation. [3 marks]
2. Explain the J-curve effect. [4 marks]
3. Evaluate the policies a government could use to reduce a persistent current account deficit. [25 marks]
4. Distinguish between economic growth and economic development, and give one indicator of development other than GDP per capita. [3 marks]
5. Explain two barriers to economic development in low-income countries. [4 marks]
6. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of a fixed exchange rate. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 old price $280; B1 new price $250; B1 depreciation of 10.7%.
2. [4] B1 after a depreciation, the current account may worsen at first; B1 because demand for exports and imports is price inelastic in the short run (contracts, habits); B1 import spending rises in domestic currency; B1 over time, as demand becomes more elastic (Marshall-Lerner satisfied), the current account improves.
3. [25] Level 5 (21-25): analysis of expenditure-switching (depreciation, protectionism, supply-side policies to raise competitiveness) and expenditure-reducing policies (higher interest rates, tighter fiscal policy); balanced evaluation (conflicts with growth and employment, retaliation and WTO rules, Marshall-Lerner and J-curve, whether a deficit financed by FDI is a problem) reaching a supported judgement. Level 4 (16-20): good analysis, some evaluation. Level 3 (11-15): some analysis, limited evaluation. Levels 1-2 (1-10): mainly knowledge, little analysis.
4. [3] B1 growth: an increase in real GDP/output; B1 development: broader improvement in living standards and welfare (health, education, freedoms); B1 e.g. the Human Development Index (life expectancy, education and income).
5. [4] B1 corruption/poor governance; B1 diverts resources and discourages investment; B1 primary product dependency; B1 volatile commodity prices make export revenue and growth unstable (or poor infrastructure, low education, debt, conflict).
6. [2] B1 advantage: certainty for traders and investors (encourages trade and FDI); B1 disadvantage: loss of independent monetary policy/need for reserves to defend the rate.
