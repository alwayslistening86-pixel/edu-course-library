## Test items
1. Nominal GDP was £2,300bn in year 1 and £2,450bn in year 2. The GDP deflator rose from 100 to 104. Calculate the real growth rate. [3 marks]
2. Explain two limitations of using GDP per capita to compare living standards between countries. [4 marks]
3. Distinguish between the claimant count and the Labour Force Survey measure of unemployment. [3 marks]
4. Explain what a weighted price index is and why weights are needed. [3 marks]
5. Explain why the relative importance of macroeconomic objectives has changed over time. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 real GDP in year 2 = 2,450/1.04 = £2,356bn; M1 change relative to 2,300; A1 2.4%.
2. [4] B1 doesn't show distribution of income; B1 average may hide wide inequality; B1 differences in price levels/exchange rates; B1 so PPP adjustments are needed (or hidden economy, non-marketed output, environment, leisure, with explanation).
3. [3] B1 claimant count: people claiming unemployment-related benefits; B1 LFS (ILO): a survey of people without a job, who want one, have looked in the past four weeks and can start within two weeks; B1 LFS is usually higher, as it includes those not eligible for/not claiming benefits.
4. [3] B1 each item's price change is multiplied by its share of household spending; B1 because items take different proportions of spending; B1 so a change in the price of a major item (e.g. energy) affects the cost of living more.
5. [3] B1 e.g. full employment was prioritised after the 1930s/Keynesian era; B1 high inflation in the 1970s led to a focus on price stability (inflation targeting); B1 more recently, sustainability/financial stability objectives gained importance.
