# S21_Globalisation_and_Trade - Test: Globalisation and trade

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: short calculations and explanations, a data-response or essay question, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below (levels-style for longer answers) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. With the same resources, Country X can make 60 units of cloth or 30 units of food; Country Y can make 40 cloth or 10 food. Identify which country has an absolute advantage in each good and which has a comparative advantage in each, showing your working. [4 marks]
2. Explain, using a diagram described in words, the effects of imposing a tariff on imported steel. [6 marks]
3. Evaluate the costs and benefits of globalisation for developing countries. [25 marks]
4. Explain two arguments for protectionism. [4 marks]
5. Distinguish between a free trade area and a customs union. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 X has an absolute advantage in both; M1 opportunity cost of 1 food: X 2 cloth, Y 4 cloth; A1 X has comparative advantage in food; A1 Y in cloth (1 cloth costs Y 0.25 food vs X 0.50 food).
2. [6] B1 world supply at the world price; tariff raises the domestic price by the tariff; B1 domestic production rises; B1 domestic consumption falls; B1 imports fall; B1 government gains tariff revenue (tariff x imports); B1 consumer surplus falls, with deadweight welfare losses (production and consumption triangles).
3. [25] Level 5 (21-25): analysis of benefits (export markets, FDI and MNC investment, technology transfer, jobs, growth, cheaper imports) and costs (exploitation, environmental damage, primary product dependency, repatriated profits, inequality); balanced evaluation (institutions, infrastructure, terms of trade, government policy) reaching a supported judgement. Level 4 (16-20): good analysis, some evaluation. Level 3 (11-15): some analysis, limited evaluation. Levels 1-2 (1-10): mainly knowledge, little analysis.
4. [4] B1 infant industry: new industries need protection to grow; B1 until they achieve economies of scale to compete; B1 anti-dumping: foreign firms selling below cost; B1 protects domestic firms from unfair competition (or strategic industries, jobs).
5. [2] B1 free trade area: no tariffs between members, but each sets its own tariffs with non-members; B1 customs union: free trade plus a common external tariff.
