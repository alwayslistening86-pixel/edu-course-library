## Test items
1. Explain the difference between demand-pull and cost-push inflation, with a cause of each. [4 marks]
2. Explain why deflation caused by falling aggregate demand can be harmful. [4 marks]
3. Explain the short-run and long-run Phillips curves and what they imply for policy. [6 marks]
4. Explain one conflict between economic growth and a balanced current account. [3 marks]
5. Explain how inflation redistributes income between savers and borrowers. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 demand-pull: AD increases faster than AS (excess demand); B1 e.g. a consumer boom/rapid credit growth; B1 cost-push: rising costs of production shift SRAS left; B1 e.g. higher energy prices or wages.
2. [4] B1 consumers delay purchases expecting lower prices; B1 reducing AD further (a deflationary spiral); B1 the real value of debt rises, so borrowers cut spending; B1 firms' profits fall, leading to lower investment and higher unemployment.
3. [6] B1 short-run: inverse relationship between unemployment and inflation; B1 governments could lower unemployment at the cost of higher inflation; B1 long-run: vertical at the natural rate of unemployment/NAIRU; B1 because expectations of inflation adjust (workers demand higher wages); B1 so demand management can't permanently lower unemployment below the natural rate; B1 supply-side policies are needed to reduce the natural rate.
4. [3] B1 faster growth raises incomes; B1 and spending on imports (UK has a high marginal propensity to import); B1 so the current account deficit widens.
5. [2] B1 if inflation exceeds the interest rate, the real value of savings falls; B1 while the real value of debts falls, benefiting borrowers.
