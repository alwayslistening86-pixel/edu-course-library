## Test items
1. Distinguish between income and wealth, and explain why wealth is usually more unequally distributed than income. [4 marks]
2. Explain what a Gini coefficient of 0.35 indicates, and how it is calculated from a Lorenz curve. [3 marks]
3. Explain how a progressive income tax can reduce inequality in the distribution of income. [9 marks]
4. Explain what is meant by the poverty trap. [2 marks]
5. Seven incomes (£000) are 14, 18, 18, 25, 31, 44, 80. Calculate the mean and median, and explain why they differ. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 income is a flow of money over time; B1 wealth is a stock of assets at a point in time; B1 wealth accumulates and is inherited; B1 and assets generate further income/capital gains, so inequality compounds.
2. [3] B1 moderate inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality); B1 ratio of the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve; B1 to the total area under the line of equality.
3. [9] Level 3 (7-9): clear analysis: higher marginal (and average) rates at higher incomes take a larger share from high earners, narrowing post-tax income gaps (Lorenz curve moves towards the line of equality, Gini falls), revenue can fund benefits and services for low-income households; applied with examples. Level 2 (4-6): some relevant analysis with limited development or application. Level 1 (1-3): mostly descriptive knowledge.
4. [2] B1 when a person's income rises, means-tested benefits are withdrawn and taxes paid; B1 so the net gain is small (a high effective marginal tax rate), reducing the incentive to work more.
5. [3] B1 mean £32.9k; B1 median £25k; B1 the high income of 80 raises the mean (positively skewed distribution).
