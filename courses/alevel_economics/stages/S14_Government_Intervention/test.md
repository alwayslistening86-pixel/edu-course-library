## Test items
1. A specific tax of £2 per unit is imposed on a good. The price rises from £10 to £11.50 and the quantity falls from 1,000 to 800. Calculate the tax revenue, and the shares of the tax paid by consumers and producers. [3 marks]
2. Explain how tradable pollution permits can reduce pollution at the least cost. [4 marks]
3. With the help of a diagram, explain the likely effects of a maximum price (rent control) set below the equilibrium in the housing market. [9 marks]
4. Explain two causes of government failure. [4 marks]
5. Explain the role of the Competition and Markets Authority in relation to mergers. [3 marks]
6. Explain one argument for and one against privatisation. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 revenue = 2 x 800 = £1,600; B1 consumers pay £1.50 per unit (75%), £1,200 in total; B1 producers pay £0.50 per unit (25%), £400.
2. [4] B1 government sets a cap on total emissions and issues permits; B1 firms that can cut emissions cheaply do so and sell spare permits; B1 firms with high abatement costs buy permits; B1 so the reduction happens where it's cheapest (and the cap can be lowered over time).
3. [9] Level 3 (7-9): accurate diagram: ceiling below equilibrium, excess demand; analysis of lower rents for tenants who find housing, contraction in supply (landlords leave, less maintenance), queues, black markets or key money; effect depends on the elasticity of supply. Level 2 (4-6): some relevant analysis with limited development or application. Level 1 (1-3): mostly descriptive knowledge.
4. [4] B1 information gaps: government may not know the true size of an externality; B1 so a tax may be set at the wrong level; B1 unintended consequences: e.g. a high tax on cigarettes encourages smuggling; B1 undermining the policy (or regulatory capture, administrative costs, political short-termism).
5. [3] B1 investigates mergers that may substantially lessen competition; B1 can block them or impose conditions (e.g. selling parts of the business); B1 to protect consumers from higher prices/less choice.
6. [2] B1 for: profit motive and competition increase efficiency (or raise revenue); B1 against: a private monopoly may exploit consumers or underinvest, so needs regulation.
