# S19_Financial_Markets_Monetary_Policy - Test: Financial markets, banks, monetary policy and financial regulation

## Test items
1. Explain why there is an inverse relationship between bond prices and market interest rates. [3 marks]
2. Explain how an increase in Bank Rate is transmitted to a fall in inflation. [6 marks]
3. Explain the conflict between liquidity and profitability for a commercial bank. [3 marks]
4. Explain how quantitative easing is intended to increase aggregate demand. [4 marks]
5. Explain what is meant by moral hazard in the banking system. [2 marks]
6. Distinguish between the roles of the PRA and the FCA. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 bonds pay a fixed coupon; B1 if market interest rates rise, new bonds/deposits offer higher returns, so demand for existing bonds falls; B1 their price falls until their yield (coupon/price) matches market rates.
2. [6] B1 commercial banks raise their lending and saving rates; B1 borrowing becomes more expensive and saving more attractive, so consumption falls; B1 investment falls as the cost of borrowing rises; B1 asset prices (houses, shares) fall, reducing wealth and confidence; B1 the pound appreciates, reducing net exports and lowering import prices; B1 so AD falls and inflationary pressure eases (with a time lag).
3. [3] B1 liquid assets (cash, reserves) earn little or no return; B1 profitable assets (long-term loans) are less liquid and riskier; B1 a bank must balance holding enough liquidity to meet withdrawals against earning profits.
4. [4] B1 the central bank creates money to buy assets (mainly government bonds); B1 bond prices rise and yields/long-term interest rates fall; B1 cheaper borrowing encourages investment and consumption; B1 higher asset prices increase wealth and confidence (portfolio rebalancing).
5. [2] B1 banks take excessive risks; B1 because they expect to be bailed out by the government/central bank if they fail ('too big to fail').
6. [2] B1 PRA: prudential regulation of individual banks, building societies and insurers (safety and soundness); B1 FCA: regulates conduct of financial firms and protects consumers.
