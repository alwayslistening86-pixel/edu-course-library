# S01_Economic_Problem - Test: Economic methodology and the economic problem

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: short calculations and explanations, a data-response or essay question, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below (levels-style for longer answers) and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Define opportunity cost and give an example of a choice a government faces. [2 marks]
2. Explain, with the aid of a production possibility diagram (described in words), the effect of an increase in the size of the labour force. [4 marks]
3. Explain why a production possibility frontier is usually drawn concave to the origin. [2 marks]
4. Distinguish between renewable and non-renewable resources, with an example of each. [2 marks]
5. Explain why economics is described as a social science. [3 marks]
6. Which would cause an outward shift of an economy's PPF? Choose every correct option.
   A. an improvement in technology
   B. a fall in unemployment
   C. an increase in the capital stock
   D. a movement of resources from producing one good to another

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 the next best alternative forgone when a choice is made; B1 e.g. spending more on defence means less on health (any valid example).
2. [4] B1 diagram: two goods on the axes, a concave PPF; B1 the PPF shifts outwards; B1 more resources mean more of both goods can be produced; B1 a movement from a point on the old curve to the new shows economic growth (potential output rises).
3. [2] B1 resources are not equally suited to producing both goods; B1 so as more of one is produced, increasingly more of the other must be given up (rising opportunity cost).
4. [2] B1 renewable: can be replenished (e.g. forests, solar energy); B1 non-renewable: finite, used up (e.g. oil, coal).
5. [3] B1 it studies human behaviour/social interactions; B1 uses a scientific method: models and evidence to test hypotheses; B1 but controlled experiments are difficult, and behaviour varies, so predictions are less certain than in natural sciences.
6. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
