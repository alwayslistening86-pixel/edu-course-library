## Test items
1. A monopolist faces P = 120 - 2Q with constant MC = AC = 40. Calculate the profit-maximising price, output and profit, and the deadweight loss compared with a competitive outcome. [5 marks]
2. Explain why a natural monopoly may be in the public interest. [3 marks]
3. With the help of a diagram, explain how train companies use third-degree price discrimination and how it affects consumers. [9 marks]
4. Explain two barriers to entry that protect a monopoly. [4 marks]
5. Explain what is meant by consumer surplus. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] M1 MR = 120 - 4Q = 40; A1 Q = 20, P = 80; A1 profit = (80 - 40) x 20 = 800; M1 competitive Q where P = MC: 120 - 2Q = 40, Q = 40; A1 DWL = 0.5 x (80 - 40) x (40 - 20) = 400.
2. [3] B1 very large economies of scale/high fixed costs (e.g. water networks); B1 one firm can supply the whole market at a lower average cost than several; B1 duplication of infrastructure would waste resources (though regulation may be needed to control prices).
3. [9] Level 3 (7-9): accurate two-market diagram: higher price where demand is less elastic (peak commuters) and lower where more elastic (off-peak leisure), MC = MR in each market; analysis of transfer of consumer surplus to producers and extra consumers served off-peak. Level 2 (4-6): some relevant analysis with limited development or application. Level 1 (1-3): mostly descriptive knowledge.
4. [4] B1 legal barriers such as patents; B1 prevent rivals copying the product for a period; B1 economies of scale; B1 new entrants would face higher average costs (or sunk costs, brand loyalty, control of resources).
5. [2] B1 the difference between the maximum consumers are willing to pay and the price actually paid; B1 shown by the area under the demand curve above the price.
