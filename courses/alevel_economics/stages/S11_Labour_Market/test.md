## Test items
1. Explain, using a diagram described in words, how the introduction of a minimum wage can increase employment in a monopsony labour market. [6 marks]
2. Explain three factors that influence the elasticity of demand for labour. [3 marks]
3. Explain two reasons why a surgeon earns more than a care worker. [4 marks]
4. With the help of a diagram, explain the possible effects of a large increase in the National Living Wage on employment in low-wage labour markets. [9 marks]
5. Explain how discrimination against a group of workers affects their wages and employment. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 monopsonist faces an upward-sloping supply of labour (AC of labour) with MCL above it; B1 employs where MRP = MCL, paying the wage read from the supply curve (below MRP); B1 a minimum wage set between this wage and the MRP makes MCL horizontal at the minimum wage up to the supply curve; B1 the firm now employs where the minimum wage meets supply/MRP; B1 so employment and wages both rise; B1 up to the competitive level (if set too high, employment falls).
2. [3] B1 proportion of labour costs in total costs (higher → more elastic); B1 ease of substituting capital for labour; B1 PED for the final product (more elastic → labour demand more elastic) (or time period).
3. [4] B1 higher MRP: the surgeon's output is highly valued; B1 so demand for surgeons' labour is high; B1 supply is limited: long training and high qualifications; B1 so supply is inelastic/low (care workers' supply is larger with fewer barriers).
4. [9] Level 3 (7-9): accurate diagram(s): a wage floor above equilibrium in a competitive market causing excess supply of labour (lower employment where demand for labour is elastic) and/or a monopsony where it can raise both wages and employment; analysis of productivity, incentives and firms costs. Level 2 (4-6): some relevant analysis with limited development or application. Level 1 (1-3): mostly descriptive knowledge.
5. [3] B1 employers value the group's labour below its true MRP; B1 demand for their labour is lower; B1 so they receive lower wages and/or fewer are employed (and labour is misallocated).
