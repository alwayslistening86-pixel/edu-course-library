# S10_Contestability_and_Efficiency - Lesson: Competitive dynamics, contestable markets and efficiency

## Goal
The learner explains the dynamics of competition (creative destruction, how firms compete), contestable and non-contestable markets (sunk costs, hit-and-run entry), and static efficiency (allocative, productive), dynamic efficiency and X-inefficiency, comparing market structures.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.1.5.8 - The dynamics of competition and competitive market processes
- 4.1.5.9 - Contestable and non-contestable markets
- 4.1.5.10 - Market structure, static efficiency, dynamic efficiency and resource allocation

## How to teach this
Ask whether a market with only one airline on a route must be uncompetitive. Teach each topic as a chain of reasoning: define, explain with a diagram (described in words, then sketched by the learner), apply to a real context, then evaluate (it depends on...). Numerical examples were computed when the course was built. For current figures (Bank Rate, the National Living Wage, inflation, tax rates), check the latest official sources rather than relying on the course text.

#### 4.1.5.8 The dynamics of competition and competitive market processes
Competition is a process: firms compete on price, quality, innovation and service; new entrants and technologies challenge incumbents (creative destruction). Competitive markets tend to benefit consumers (lower prices, more choice, innovation) and improve efficiency; but firms may try to reduce competition (mergers, predatory pricing).

#### 4.1.5.9 Contestable and non-contestable markets
A contestable market has low barriers to entry and exit, especially no sunk costs (costs that can't be recovered on exit), and perfect information about technology. The threat of hit-and-run entry makes incumbents behave competitively (charging prices close to average cost, earning only normal profit) even if there are few firms. Non-contestable markets have high sunk costs and barriers. Market structure alone may not predict behaviour; contestability matters.

#### 4.1.5.10 Market structure, static efficiency, dynamic efficiency and resource allocation
Static efficiency: at a point in time. Allocative efficiency: P = MC, resources allocated to maximise welfare. Productive efficiency: producing at minimum average cost. Dynamic efficiency: improvements over time through innovation, investment and new products, needing profits and incentives. X-inefficiency: costs higher than necessary because of slack management (organisational slack), often with little competition. Perfect competition achieves static efficiency; monopoly may achieve dynamic efficiency and economies of scale but tends to be allocatively and X-inefficient.
