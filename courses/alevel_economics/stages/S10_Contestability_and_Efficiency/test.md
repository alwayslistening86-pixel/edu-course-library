## Test items
1. Explain how the threat of entry can make a market with only one firm behave competitively. [4 marks]
2. Distinguish between allocative, productive and dynamic efficiency. [3 marks]
3. Evaluate the view that monopoly is always less efficient than perfect competition. [25 marks]
4. Explain what is meant by X-inefficiency and why it is more likely in monopoly. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 if the market is contestable (low barriers, no sunk costs); B1 supernormal profit would attract hit-and-run entry; B1 so the incumbent keeps prices near average cost; B1 earning only normal profit (and being efficient) to deter entry.
2. [3] B1 allocative: P = MC, resources used to produce what consumers value most; B1 productive: output at minimum average cost; B1 dynamic: improvements over time through innovation and investment.
3. [25] Level 5 (21-25): well-developed analysis of static inefficiencies (P > MC, not at minimum AC, X-inefficiency, deadweight loss) against economies of scale (natural monopoly) and dynamic efficiency (supernormal profits funding R&D); balanced evaluation (contestability, regulation, scale economies in the industry) reaching a supported judgement. Level 4 (16-20): good analysis, some evaluation. Level 3 (11-15): some analysis, limited evaluation. Levels 1-2 (1-10): mainly knowledge, little analysis.
4. [3] B1 costs higher than the minimum possible for the output produced (organisational slack); B1 a monopolist faces little competitive pressure; B1 and supernormal profits mean it can survive with slack management.
