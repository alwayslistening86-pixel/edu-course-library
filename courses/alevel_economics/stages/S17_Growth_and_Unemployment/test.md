## Test items
1. Explain what is meant by a negative output gap and its likely effects on unemployment and inflation. [4 marks]
2. Evaluate whether economic growth is always desirable. [25 marks]
3. Explain two consequences of long-term unemployment for the economy. [4 marks]
4. Explain how net migration of working-age people could affect the UK labour market. [3 marks]
5. Define a recession. [1 mark]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 actual output below potential output; B1 resources (labour) are underused; B1 so cyclical unemployment is high; B1 and inflationary pressure is low.
2. [25] Level 5 (21-25): analysis of benefits (higher incomes and living standards, lower unemployment, more tax revenue for public services) and costs (pollution, climate change, resource depletion, inequality, inflation if above trend); balanced evaluation (sustainable growth, type and distribution of growth, short vs long run) reaching a supported judgement. Level 4 (16-20): good analysis, some evaluation. Level 3 (11-15): some analysis, limited evaluation. Levels 1-2 (1-10): mainly knowledge, little analysis.
3. [4] B1 lost output: the economy operates inside its PPF; B1 lower living standards/wasted resources; B1 hysteresis: skills erode, making workers less employable; B1 so structural unemployment rises and potential output falls (or fiscal costs).
4. [3] B1 increases labour supply; B1 filling skills shortages, raising potential output; B1 may reduce wages in some low-skilled sectors (evidence suggests small effects).
5. [1] B1 two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth.
