# S17_Growth_and_Unemployment - Lesson: Economic growth, the economic cycle and unemployment

## Goal
The learner distinguishes short-run and long-run growth, explains output gaps and the economic cycle, the costs and benefits of growth (including sustainability), and the types, causes and consequences of unemployment, including employment trends and migration.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.2.3.1 - Economic growth and the economic cycle
- 4.2.3.2 - Employment and unemployment

## How to teach this
Ask why unemployment can stay high for years after a recession ends. Teach each topic as a chain of reasoning: define, explain with a diagram (described in words, then sketched by the learner), apply to a real context, then evaluate (it depends on...). Numerical examples were computed when the course was built. For current figures (Bank Rate, the National Living Wage, inflation, tax rates), check the latest official sources rather than relying on the course text.

#### 4.2.3.1 Economic growth and the economic cycle
Short-run growth: rising real output using spare capacity (increase in AD); long-run (trend) growth: increase in productive potential (LRAS/PPF shifts out). Output gap: the difference between actual and potential output: negative (below potential, spare capacity, unemployment) or positive (above potential, inflationary pressure); difficult to measure. The economic cycle: boom, downturn, recession (two consecutive quarters of falling real GDP), recovery. Demand-side and supply-side shocks cause fluctuations; causes of cyclical instability include swings in credit growth, asset-price bubbles and speculation, and herd behaviour/'animal spirits' in business and consumer confidence. Benefits of growth: higher living standards, lower unemployment, more tax revenue; costs: environmental damage, depletion of resources, inequality, inflation. Sustainable growth meets present needs without compromising future generations.

#### 4.2.3.2 Employment and unemployment
Employment, unemployment and inactivity. Types of unemployment: cyclical (demand-deficient), frictional (between jobs), structural (mismatch from changes in the economy, occupational and geographical immobility), seasonal. Real-wage (classical) unemployment: wages above the market-clearing level. Voluntary vs involuntary unemployment. Consequences for individuals (loss of income, skills, health), firms, the government (benefits, lost tax) and the economy (lost output, hysteresis). Migration affects the size and skills of the labour force, and may affect wages and employment.
