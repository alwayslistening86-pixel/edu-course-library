# S10_Transport_in_Plants - Test: Mass transport in plants

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how water moves up the xylem according to the cohesion-tension theory. [4 marks]
2. Describe the mass flow hypothesis for translocation. [5 marks]
3. Give one piece of evidence supporting and one against the mass flow hypothesis. [2 marks]
4. Explain why the diameter of a tree trunk is smallest around midday. [2 marks]
5. Describe how a radioactive tracer experiment shows that sugars are transported in phloem. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 water evaporates from the leaves (transpiration); B1 creating tension (negative pressure) in the xylem, pulling water up; B1 hydrogen bonds between water molecules (cohesion) form a continuous column; B1 adhesion to xylem walls.
2. [5] B1 sucrose is actively transported into sieve tubes at the source (via companion cells); B1 lowering water potential, so water enters from the xylem by osmosis; B1 increasing hydrostatic pressure; B1 at the sink sucrose is removed (used/stored), water leaves, pressure lowers; B1 the pressure gradient moves the solution from source to sink.
3. [2] B1 for: sap exudes under pressure from cut phloem/aphid stylets (or ringing, tracer experiments); B1 against: sucrose moves to different sinks at different rates (or sieve plates would resist flow).
4. [2] B1 transpiration is highest; B1 so tension in the xylem is greatest, pulling the walls inwards.
5. [3] B1 supply 14CO2 to a leaf; B1 it is incorporated into sugars by photosynthesis; B1 autoradiography shows radioactivity in the phloem (moving to sinks).
