# S10_Transport_in_Plants - Lesson: Mass transport in plants

## Goal
The learner explains the movement of water through xylem by the cohesion-tension theory and of organic substances through phloem by the mass flow hypothesis, and evaluates the evidence from ringing and tracer experiments.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.4.2 - Mass transport in plants

## How to teach this
Ask how a 100-metre redwood lifts water to its top leaves with no pump. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. Use AQA's precise terms (e.g. 'water potential', 'complementary', 'tertiary structure') and insist on them in answers. Every numerical answer and statistic here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.3.4.2 Mass transport in plants
Xylem moves water and mineral ions. Cohesion-tension: water evaporates from leaves (transpiration), lowering the water potential in the mesophyll cells; water is drawn out of the xylem, creating tension; water molecules form hydrogen bonds with one another (cohesion) so a continuous column is pulled up; adhesion to xylem walls helps. Evidence: tree trunk diameter decreases when transpiration is high (tension); if xylem is broken, air enters and water no longer moves up (and it doesn't leak out). Phloem transports organic substances (e.g. sucrose) by translocation from sources to sinks. Mass flow: sucrose is actively loaded into phloem sieve tubes at the source (via companion cells, co-transport with H+), lowering water potential, so water enters from the xylem by osmosis, increasing hydrostatic pressure; at the sink, sucrose is removed (used in respiration or stored), water leaves, and pressure falls; the pressure gradient drives mass flow. Evidence: ringing experiments (removing a ring of bark and phloem causes swelling above the ring as sugars accumulate), radioactive tracers (14CO2 fixed in photosynthesis; autoradiography shows labelled sugars in phloem), aphid mouthparts (sap under pressure). Evidence against: sugars travel to many sinks at different rates; sieve plates would slow flow.
