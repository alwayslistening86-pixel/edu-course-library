# S17_Stimuli_Receptors_Heart_Rate - Lesson: Survival and response, receptors and control of heart rate

## Goal
The learner explains taxes, kineses and tropisms (IAA in flowering plants), the reflex arc, the Pacinian corpuscle and generator potentials, rods and cones (sensitivity and acuity), myogenic control of the heart and its nervous control via receptors, and carries out required practical 10.

#### 3.6.1.1 Survival and response (A-level only)
Organisms increase their chance of survival by responding to changes in their environment. Taxis: a directional movement towards or away from a stimulus (e.g. woodlice move away from light); kinesis: a non-directional change in the rate of movement or turning in response to the intensity of a stimulus (e.g. woodlice move faster and turn less in dry conditions, making it more likely they reach damp areas). Tropisms in flowering plants: growth responses to directional stimuli controlled by indoleacetic acid (IAA), a growth factor: in shoots, IAA moves to the shaded side and stimulates cell elongation, so the shoot bends towards light (positive phototropism); in roots, IAA accumulates on the lower side and inhibits elongation, so roots grow downwards (gravitropism). Reflex arc: receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone (spinal cord) → motor neurone → effector; rapid, involuntary, protective.

#### 3.6.1.2 Receptors (A-level only)
Receptors respond to specific stimuli. The Pacinian corpuscle (in the skin) responds to mechanical pressure: pressure deforms the lamellae and stretch-mediated sodium ion channels in the neurone's membrane, letting Na+ in and producing a generator potential; if it reaches threshold, an action potential is produced. The retina: rods (rhodopsin; several rods connect to one bipolar neurone: spatial summation, high sensitivity to low light, low visual acuity, black and white) and cones (iodopsin, three types for red, green and blue light; each cone has its own bipolar neurone: high visual acuity, colour vision, need bright light). Cones are concentrated at the fovea.

#### 3.6.1.3 Control of heart rate (A-level only)
Cardiac muscle is myogenic: the sinoatrial node (SAN) initiates a wave of electrical activity across the atria, causing them to contract; non-conducting tissue delays it; the atrioventricular node (AVN) passes the wave after a short delay (allowing atria to empty) down the bundle of His to the Purkyne tissue in the ventricle walls, so the ventricles contract from the base up. Heart rate is modified by the medulla oblongata: chemoreceptors (in the aorta and carotid arteries, detecting CO2/pH) and pressure receptors (baroreceptors) send impulses; the sympathetic nervous system increases the rate of SAN discharge (via the cardioacceleratory centre), and the parasympathetic system (vagus nerve) decreases it.

#### RP10 Required practical 10: the effect of an environmental variable on the movement of an animal using a choice chamber or maze
**Method (choice chamber, e.g. woodlice):** divide the chamber into regions of different conditions (e.g. damp/dry with water or a drying agent, light/dark with covers); place a set number of animals in the centre; after a set time, count the number in each region; repeat, and analyse with a chi-squared test. Maze: record the direction taken at each junction. Ethics: handle animals carefully, minimise time, return them to their habitat.
