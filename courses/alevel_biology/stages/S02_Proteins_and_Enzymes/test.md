# S02_Proteins_and_Enzymes - Test: Proteins and enzymes

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Use the induced-fit model to explain how an enzyme catalyses a reaction. [3 marks]
2. Explain why the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction falls above the optimum temperature. [3 marks]
3. Explain how a competitive inhibitor reduces the rate of a reaction, and why its effect decreases at high substrate concentration. [3 marks]
4. In required practical 1 using catalase, 36 cm^3 of oxygen was collected in the first 2 minutes. Calculate the mean rate in cm^3 min^-1 and give two variables that must be controlled. [3 marks]
5. Describe the biuret test for protein. [2 marks]
6. Describe the difference between the secondary and tertiary structure of a protein. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 substrate binds to the active site, which changes shape slightly to fit around it; B1 forming an enzyme-substrate complex; B1 which puts strain on bonds (or brings molecules together), lowering the activation energy.
2. [3] B1 hydrogen (and ionic) bonds in the tertiary structure break; B1 the active site changes shape (denatures); B1 substrate no longer fits/fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form.
3. [3] B1 similar shape to the substrate, binds to the active site; B1 preventing enzyme-substrate complexes forming; B1 more substrate molecules out-compete the inhibitor for active sites.
4. [3] B1 18 cm^3 min^-1; B1 B1 two of: pH (buffer), concentration/volume of hydrogen peroxide, amount/concentration of catalase, temperature.
5. [2] B1 add biuret reagent (sodium hydroxide and copper(II) sulfate solution); B1 purple/lilac colour shows protein.
6. [2] B1 secondary: folding of the chain into α-helix or β-pleated sheet held by hydrogen bonds between N-H and C=O of the backbone; B1 tertiary: overall 3D folding held by bonds between R groups (hydrogen, ionic, disulfide).
