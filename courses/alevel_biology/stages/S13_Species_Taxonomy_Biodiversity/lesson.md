# S13_Species_Taxonomy_Biodiversity - Lesson: Species, taxonomy, biodiversity and investigating diversity

Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. #### 3.4.5 Species and taxonomy
A species is a group of organisms that can breed to produce fertile offspring. Courtship behaviour is species-specific and helps recognise members of the same species and of the opposite sex, identify a mate who is able to breed, form a pair bond and synchronise mating. Classification: a hierarchy of taxa (domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species), with no overlap; binomial names (genus and species, e.g. Homo sapiens). Phylogenetic classification groups organisms by evolutionary relationships; advances in immunology and genome sequencing are clarifying these relationships.

#### 3.4.6 Biodiversity within a community
Biodiversity can relate to a range of habitats, from a small local habitat to the Earth. Species richness is the number of different species in a community. The index of diversity d = N(N - 1)/Σn(n - 1), where N is the total number of organisms of all species and n the number of each species; it takes account of both richness and evenness. Farming reduces biodiversity (hedgerow removal, monoculture, pesticides, wetland drainage); conservation schemes balance it with food production. *Example:* five species with counts 12, 10, 8, 6, 4 give d = 4.88; counts 30, 5, 2, 2, 1 (same richness) give d = 1.74, showing lower evenness.

#### 3.4.7 Investigating diversity
Genetic diversity within, or between, species can be investigated by comparing the base sequence of DNA, the base sequence of mRNA, or the amino acid sequence of proteins (older methods compared frequencies of measurable or observable characteristics). Gene technology has changed methods: DNA sequencing and comparison is quicker and more precise than observing characteristics. Quantitative investigations need careful sampling: random, large samples; calculate a mean and standard deviation for each.

#### MS 1.6 Understand the terms mean, median and mode
Mean, median and mode. *Example:* for leaf lengths 42, 45, 45, 47, 51 mm: mean 46 mm, median 45 mm, mode 45 mm.

#### MS 1.10 Understand measures of dispersion, including standard deviation and range
Dispersion: the range and the standard deviation (spread around the mean; about 68% of normally distributed values lie within ±1 SD). *Example:* 42, 45, 45, 47, 51 mm: range 9 mm; sample standard deviation 3.32 mm. Overlapping mean ± SD suggests the difference between two samples may not be significant.
