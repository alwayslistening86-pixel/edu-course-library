# S13_Species_Taxonomy_Biodiversity - Test: Species, taxonomy, biodiversity and investigating diversity

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why an index of diversity is more useful than species richness for comparing two habitats. [2 marks]
2. A meadow sample contained species A 25, B 10, C 8, D 5, E 2. Calculate the index of diversity. [2 marks]
3. Explain how courtship behaviour helps ensure successful reproduction. [3 marks]
4. Explain why comparing the base sequences of DNA gives more reliable evidence of relationships than comparing observable characteristics. [2 marks]
5. Calculate the mean and standard deviation of these shell lengths (mm): 14.2, 15.1, 13.8, 14.9, 15.5, 14.0. [2 marks]
6. Describe two ways farming reduces biodiversity. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 it takes account of the number of individuals of each species as well as the number of species; B1 so a community dominated by one species scores lower (evenness).
2. [2] M1 N = 50, 50 x 49/Σn(n - 1); A1 3.19.
3. [3] B1 recognises members of the same species (so offspring are fertile); B1 identifies a mate of the opposite sex that is able to breed; B1 forms a pair bond/synchronises mating.
4. [2] B1 observable characteristics may be influenced by the environment or by many genes; B1 DNA sequences are compared directly and precisely, and more differences mean a more distant relationship.
5. [2] B1 mean 14.58 mm; B1 sample SD 0.68 mm.
6. [2] B1 B1 two of: hedgerow removal; monoculture; use of pesticides/herbicides; draining wetlands/filling ponds; overgrazing.
