# S16_Ecosystems_and_Nutrient_Cycles - Test: Energy and ecosystems, and nutrient cycles

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. The NPP of a grassland is 8500 kJ m^-2 year^-1 and the net production of herbivores is 360 kJ m^-2 year^-1. Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer and give one reason it is low. [3 marks]
2. Describe the process of eutrophication following the leaching of fertiliser into a lake. [5 marks]
3. Explain the roles of bacteria in converting nitrogen in dead organisms into a form plants can absorb. [3 marks]
4. Explain two ways farmers reduce respiratory losses in livestock to increase productivity. [2 marks]
5. Explain how mycorrhizae help plants. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 360/8500 x 100; A1 4.2%; B1 not all plant material is eaten/some is indigestible and lost in faeces (or energy lost in respiration/as heat).
2. [5] B1 nitrates increase algal growth (algal bloom); B1 blocking light from plants below, which die; B1 saprobionts decompose dead organisms; B1 their aerobic respiration uses up dissolved oxygen; B1 fish and other aerobic organisms die.
3. [3] B1 saprobionts (ammonification) release ammonium ions from proteins/nitrogen compounds; B1 nitrifying bacteria oxidise ammonium to nitrite; B1 then to nitrate, absorbed by roots.
4. [2] B1 restricting movement (less energy used in muscle contraction); B1 keeping animals warm indoors (less energy used to maintain body temperature).
5. [2] B1 fungal hyphae increase the surface area of the root system; B1 improving uptake of water and mineral ions (e.g. phosphate).
