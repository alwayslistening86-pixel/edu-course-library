# S26_Practical_Skills_Data_and_Essay - Lesson: Practical skills, data analysis, statistics and the synoptic essay

## Goal
The learner plans and evaluates experiments, identifies variables, presents and processes data (means, frequency tables, histograms, scatter graphs), selects and applies statistical tests (t-test, Spearman's rank), handles uncertainties, uses equations and graphs, and plans and writes a 25-mark synoptic essay.

## How to teach this
Give the learner two sets of leaf lengths from sun and shade and ask how they'd decide whether the difference is real. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. Use AQA's precise terms (e.g. 'water potential', 'complementary', 'tertiary structure') and insist on them in answers. Every numerical answer and statistic here was computed when the course was built.

#### PS1.1 Solve problems set in practical contexts
Solve problems set in practical contexts: e.g. from an unfamiliar investigation, identify what was measured, calculate a rate or percentage change, and interpret the result. **The essay (Paper 3)** is also a problem-solving task: 25 marks, one title from a choice of two, drawing on several areas of the specification. Plan for 5-10 minutes: list relevant topics across the course, choose the best, and link each explicitly to the title. Use A-level detail (not GCSE), accurate terminology and a clear structure. AQA marks it by levels (1-5 up to 21-25 marks), judged on the range of relevant topics covered, the depth and accuracy of A-level content, how well each is linked to the title, and freedom from errors and irrelevant material.

#### PS1.2 Apply scientific knowledge to practical contexts
Apply knowledge to practical contexts: e.g. explain why a buffer is used in an enzyme experiment, or why a plant is kept in the dark before a starch test.

#### PS2.1 Comment on experimental design and evaluate scientific methods
Comment on design and evaluate methods: validity (does it test the hypothesis?), controls (a control experiment without the independent variable), repeats, sample size, and possible improvements.

#### PS2.2 Present data in appropriate ways
Present data: tables with headings and units; line graphs for continuous data; bar charts for categoric data; histograms for frequency data grouped in continuous classes; scatter graphs for correlation.

#### PS2.3 Evaluate results and draw conclusions with reference to measurement uncertainties and errors
Evaluate results and conclusions: consider the size of error bars or standard deviations (overlap suggests no significant difference), statistical test results, and uncertainties; don't claim more than the data show; correlation is not causation. *Student's t-test example:* sun leaves [12.1, 13.4, 11.8, 12.9, 13.0, 12.5, 11.9, 12.7] and shade leaves [13.6, 14.2, 13.1, 14.8, 13.9, 14.4, 13.5, 14.0] (mm): t = 5.05 with 14 degrees of freedom; the critical value at p = 0.05 is 2.14, so the difference in means is significant (p = 1.8 x 10^-4).

#### PS2.4 Identify variables including those that must be controlled
Variables: independent, dependent and control variables, and how each control is kept constant (e.g. water bath for temperature, buffer for pH).

#### PS3.1 Plot and interpret graphs
Plot and interpret graphs: sensible scales, labelled axes with units, best-fit lines or curves, error bars (± 1 SD or 95% confidence intervals).

#### PS3.2 Process and analyse data using appropriate mathematical skills
Process data: percentage change, rates, means, standard deviations, statistical tests.

#### PS3.3 Consider margins of error, accuracy and precision of data
Margins of error: uncertainty of a measuring instrument = ± half the smallest division (for a single reading); percentage uncertainty = uncertainty/value x 100%. *Example:* a 10 cm^3 measuring cylinder read to ±0.1 cm^3 measuring 5.0 cm^3: 2%.

#### PS4.1 Know and understand how to use a wide range of experimental and practical instruments, equipment and techniques
Instruments and techniques: microscopes and graticules, colorimeters, potometers, respirometers, data loggers, pipettes and serial dilutions, chromatography and electrophoresis, aseptic technique, dissection tools, quadrats and transects; know the purpose and correct use of each.

#### MS 1.1 Use an appropriate number of significant figures
Significant figures: give answers to the same number of significant figures as the least precise data.

#### MS 1.2 Find arithmetic means
Arithmetic mean: sum ÷ number of values; exclude anomalies with a reason.

#### MS 1.3 Construct and interpret frequency tables and diagrams, bar charts and histograms
Frequency tables, bar charts and histograms: in a histogram, the bars touch and class widths are continuous.

#### MS 1.7 Use a scatter diagram to identify a correlation between two variables
Scatter diagrams show correlation (positive, negative or none); a correlation coefficient measures its strength. *Example:* for light intensity [2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16] and plant cover [31, 26, 27, 20, 18, 15, 16, 9], Spearman's rank r_s = -0.952; for n = 8 the critical value at p = 0.05 is 0.738 (two-tailed), so there is a significant negative correlation.

#### MS 1.11 Identify uncertainties in measurements and use simple techniques to determine uncertainty when data are combined
Uncertainties combine: when values are added or subtracted, add the absolute uncertainties; when multiplied or divided, add the percentage uncertainties.

#### MS 2.1 Understand and use the symbols =, <, <<, >>, >, ∝, ~
Symbols: <, >, <<, >>, ∝, ~ (e.g. rate ∝ enzyme concentration when substrate is in excess).

#### MS 2.2 Change the subject of an equation
Change the subject: magnification = image/actual → actual = image/magnification; N = MC/R rearranges to find any term.

#### MS 3.1 Translate information between graphical, numerical and algebraic forms
Translate between graphs, numbers and equations: e.g. read a rate from a graph and express the relationship in words or as an equation.

#### MS 3.2 Plot two variables from experimental or other data
Plot two variables: independent on the x-axis, dependent on the y-axis, points plotted accurately, line of best fit.

#### MS 3.3 Understand that y = mx + c represents a linear relationship
y = mx + c: a straight line; the gradient m is the rate of change and c the intercept, e.g. a calibration line of absorbance against concentration.
