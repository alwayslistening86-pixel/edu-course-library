## How to teach this
Ask why a slice of potato goes floppy in salty water but stiff in pure water. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. Use AQA's precise terms (e.g. 'water potential', 'complementary', 'tertiary structure') and insist on them in answers. #### 3.2.3 Transport across cell membranes
Fluid-mosaic model: a phospholipid bilayer with proteins (channel and carrier proteins), glycoproteins, glycolipids and cholesterol (restricts the movement of other molecules, making the membrane less fluid). Simple diffusion: net movement of small, non-polar or lipid-soluble molecules down a concentration gradient through the bilayer. Facilitated diffusion: passive movement of larger or charged molecules through channel or carrier proteins. Osmosis: net movement of water from higher to lower water potential (ψ) across a partially permeable membrane; pure water has ψ = 0 kPa, and solutions have negative ψ. Active transport: movement against a concentration gradient using carrier proteins and ATP. Co-transport: e.g. glucose absorption in the ileum: sodium ions are actively pumped out of epithelial cells into the blood, creating a gradient; sodium ions then diffuse into the cell from the lumen with glucose through a co-transporter protein; glucose then moves into the blood by facilitated diffusion. Rate depends on the concentration gradient, surface area, number of channel/carrier proteins and (for diffusion) thickness; cells adapted for rapid transport have more carrier proteins or a larger surface area (microvilli).

#### RP3 Required practical 3: a dilution series and calibration curve to find the water potential of plant tissue
**Method:** make a dilution series of sucrose solutions (e.g. 0.0 to 1.0 mol dm^-3) from a stock solution; cut equal cylinders of potato, blot and weigh; immerse in each solution for a fixed time at constant temperature; blot and reweigh; calculate percentage change in mass (allows for different starting masses). Plot percentage change against concentration; where the line crosses zero (no net water movement), the solution's water potential equals that of the tissue; read ψ from a calibration table or curve.

#### RP4 Required practical 4: the effect of a named variable on the permeability of cell-surface membranes
**Method (beetroot):** cut equal pieces, wash to remove pigment from damaged cells; place in water at different temperatures (or in different concentrations of ethanol) for a fixed time; remove the pieces and measure the absorbance (or % transmission) of the surrounding solution with a colorimeter (blue-green filter), calibrated with distilled water. Higher absorbance means more betalain pigment has leaked: the membrane is more permeable (high temperature denatures membrane proteins and increases phospholipid fluidity; ethanol dissolves lipids).

#### MS 3.4 Determine the intercept of a graph
Read an intercept from a graph. *Example:* percentage mass change is +8% at 0.2 mol dm^-3 and -4% at 0.4 mol dm^-3; assuming a straight line between them, it crosses zero at 0.2 + 0.2 x 8/12 = 0.33 mol dm^-3.
