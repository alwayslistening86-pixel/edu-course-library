# S06_Membrane_Transport - Test: Membrane structure and transport across membranes

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how glucose is absorbed from the lumen of the ileum into the blood by co-transport. [4 marks]
2. In required practical 3, percentage changes in mass were: 0.1 mol dm^-3 +12.0%, 0.3 +4.5%, 0.5 -3.0%, 0.7 -9.5%. Estimate the sucrose concentration with the same water potential as the tissue, and explain why percentage change is used. [3 marks]
3. Explain why raising the temperature above about 45 °C increases the leakage of pigment from beetroot cells. [3 marks]
4. Give two differences between facilitated diffusion and active transport. [2 marks]
5. Describe the fluid-mosaic model of membrane structure. [3 marks]
6. Which substances cross membranes mainly by simple diffusion through the phospholipid bilayer? Choose every correct option.
   A. oxygen
   B. glucose
   C. carbon dioxide
   D. sodium ions

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 Na+ actively transported out of the epithelial cell into the blood (using ATP); B1 creating a lower Na+ concentration inside the cell; B1 Na+ diffuses into the cell with glucose via a co-transporter protein (glucose moves against its gradient); B1 glucose moves into the blood by facilitated diffusion.
2. [3] M1 zero lies between 0.3 and 0.5; A1 0.3 + 0.2 x 4.5/7.5 = 0.42 mol dm^-3; B1 cylinders had different starting masses.
3. [3] B1 membrane proteins denature; B1 creating gaps in the membrane; B1 phospholipids gain kinetic energy and become more fluid, increasing permeability.
4. [2] B1 active transport needs ATP (facilitated diffusion is passive); B1 active transport moves substances against a concentration gradient (only carrier proteins, not channels).
5. [3] B1 phospholipid bilayer; B1 proteins scattered (embedded/partly embedded) like a mosaic; B1 molecules can move (fluid), with cholesterol, glycoproteins and glycolipids.
6. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
