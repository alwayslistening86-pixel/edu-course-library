# S11_Genes_and_Protein_Synthesis - Lesson: DNA, genes, chromosomes and protein synthesis

## Goal
The learner describes DNA in prokaryotes and eukaryotes, genes, the genetic code, introns and exons, homologous chromosomes and alleles, and explains transcription (with splicing) and translation, including the roles of mRNA, tRNA and ribosomes.

## How to teach this
Give the learner an mRNA sequence and ask them to predict the amino acids before explaining how the cell does it. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. #### 3.4.1 DNA, genes and chromosomes
In eukaryotes, nuclear DNA is long, linear and associated with histones (together, chromosomes); mitochondria and chloroplasts contain short, circular DNA not associated with proteins, as in prokaryotes. A gene is a base sequence of DNA at a fixed locus coding for the amino acid sequence of a polypeptide or a functional RNA (rRNA, tRNA). The genetic code: a triplet of bases codes for an amino acid; it is universal (the same in almost all organisms), non-overlapping (each base read once) and degenerate (more than one triplet per amino acid); start and stop codons. In eukaryotes much nuclear DNA doesn't code: multiple repeats between genes, and introns within genes (exons code). The genome is the complete set of genes in a cell; the proteome is the full range of proteins it can produce. Homologous chromosomes carry the same genes (loci) but may have different alleles.

#### 3.4.2 DNA and protein synthesis
mRNA: a single strand, codons, made in transcription; tRNA: a single strand folded into a clover-leaf by hydrogen bonds, with an anticodon and an amino acid binding site. Transcription: RNA polymerase joins RNA nucleotides complementary to the template strand of a gene (U pairs with A); in eukaryotes this makes pre-mRNA, which is spliced (introns removed) to form mRNA; in prokaryotes mRNA is produced directly. Translation: the mRNA attaches to a ribosome; tRNA molecules with complementary anticodons bring specific amino acids; the ribosome catalyses peptide bonds between adjacent amino acids (ATP provides energy for tRNA to pick up specific amino acids); the ribosome moves along to the next codon until a stop codon. The polypeptide then folds (and may be modified in the Golgi).
