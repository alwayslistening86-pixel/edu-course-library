# S14_Photosynthesis - Lesson: Photosynthesis

## Goal
The learner explains the light-dependent reaction (photoionisation, chemiosmosis, photolysis) and the light-independent reaction (Calvin cycle), limiting factors and their agricultural use, and carries out required practicals 7 and 8.

## How to teach this
Ask where the oxygen released by plants comes from: carbon dioxide or water, and how anyone found out. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. Use AQA's precise terms (e.g. 'water potential', 'complementary', 'tertiary structure') and insist on them in answers. Every numerical answer and statistic here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.5.1 Photosynthesis (A-level only)
Light-dependent reaction (thylakoid membranes): chlorophyll absorbs light, exciting electrons, which leave it (photoionisation); some energy from the electrons is used to make ATP from ADP and Pi, and to reduce NADP to reduced NADP. Electrons pass along an electron transfer chain in the thylakoid membrane, releasing energy that pumps protons into the thylakoid space; protons diffuse back through ATP synthase, producing ATP (chemiosmotic theory). Photolysis of water: 2H2O → 4H+ + 4e- + O2, replacing the electrons lost from chlorophyll; oxygen is released. Light-independent reaction (Calvin cycle, stroma): CO2 reacts with ribulose bisphosphate (RuBP, 5C), catalysed by rubisco, forming two molecules of glycerate 3-phosphate (GP, 3C); GP is reduced to triose phosphate (TP) using reduced NADP and ATP; some TP makes useful organic substances (glucose, amino acids, lipids); most regenerates RuBP, using ATP. Limiting factors: light intensity, CO2 concentration and temperature; growers raise these in glasshouses (e.g. burning fuels for CO2 and heat, artificial lighting) where the extra yield is worth the cost.

#### RP7 Required practical 7: chromatography of photosynthetic pigments from the leaves of different plants
**Method:** grind leaves with sand and a solvent (propanone); spot the extract repeatedly on a pencil origin line of chromatography paper or a TLC plate, drying between spots; stand in a solvent (below the origin) in a sealed container; remove when the solvent front nears the top and mark it. Calculate Rf = distance moved by pigment ÷ distance moved by solvent, and identify pigments (e.g. carotene near the front, chlorophylls lower). Compare leaves from shade-tolerant and shade-intolerant plants. Hazards: propanone is flammable and an irritant.

#### RP8 Required practical 8: the effect of a named factor on the rate of dehydrogenase activity in extracts of chloroplasts
**Method:** isolate chloroplasts by blending leaves in an ice-cold, isotonic buffer and filtering/centrifuging; add DCPIP, a blue redox indicator that is reduced (turns colourless) by the electrons that would normally reduce NADP (so it measures the activity of dehydrogenase in the light-dependent reaction); vary a named factor (light intensity, distance from a lamp, or temperature); measure the time to decolourise or the absorbance with a colorimeter. Controls: tubes kept in the dark and without chloroplasts.
