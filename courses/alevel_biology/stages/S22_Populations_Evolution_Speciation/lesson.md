#### 3.7.2 Populations (A-level only)
Species exist as one or more populations. A population is all the organisms of one species in a habitat, which can interbreed; the gene pool is all the alleles of all the genes in a population; allele frequency is the proportion of a gene pool made up of an allele. Hardy-Weinberg: p + q = 1 and p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1 (p = dominant allele frequency, q = recessive; p^2, 2pq and q^2 are the genotype frequencies). It predicts constant allele frequencies if: the population is large, mating is random, and there is no selection, mutation or migration. *Example:* cystic fibrosis in 1 in 2500: q^2 = 0.0004, q = 0.02, p = 0.98, carriers 2pq = 0.0392 (about 1 in 26).

#### 3.7.3 Evolution may lead to speciation (A-level only)
Individuals within a population vary in phenotype because of genetic factors (mutation, meiosis, random fertilisation) and environmental factors. Predation, disease and competition for the means of survival result in differential survival and reproduction: natural selection changes allele frequencies (directional, stabilising, and disruptive selection, which favours both extremes). Speciation: populations become reproductively isolated, so allele frequencies change independently (natural selection and genetic drift, which is random change and more important in small populations) until they can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring. Allopatric speciation: geographical separation. Sympatric speciation: reproductive isolation within the same area (e.g. mutations affecting courtship behaviour, flowering times or gamete compatibility). Evolution is a change in the allele frequencies in a population.

#### MS 2.3 Substitute numerical values into algebraic equations using appropriate units for physical quantities
Substitute values into equations: from q^2, q = root(q^2); then p = 1 - q; then 2pq.

#### MS 2.4 Solve algebraic equations
Solve equations: if 36% of a population shows the dominant phenotype, q^2 = 0.64, so q = 0.8, p = 0.2, and heterozygotes 2pq = 0.32.
