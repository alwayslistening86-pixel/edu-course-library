## Goal
The learner describes antagonistic skeletal muscles, the ultrastructure of a myofibril and the sliding filament theory, the roles of calcium ions and ATP, phosphocreatine, and the structure and properties of slow and fast skeletal muscle fibres.

#### 3.6.3 Skeletal muscles are stimulated to contract by nerves and act as effectors (A-level only)
Skeletal muscles act in antagonistic pairs against an incompressible skeleton. Gross and microscopic structure: muscle made of fibres (multinucleate), containing myofibrils made of sarcomeres: thick filaments of myosin and thin filaments of actin; bands: A band (length of myosin), I band (actin only), H zone (myosin only), Z line (sarcomere ends). Sliding filament theory: an action potential arrives at the neuromuscular junction and spreads through T-tubules; Ca2+ is released from the sarcoplasmic reticulum; Ca2+ causes tropomyosin to move away from the binding sites on actin (it does so by binding to troponin, which is extra detail not required by the specification); myosin heads (with ADP) bind actin, forming actomyosin cross-bridges; the heads change angle (power stroke), pulling actin towards the centre and releasing ADP; ATP binds, detaching the head; ATP hydrolase (activated by Ca2+) hydrolyses ATP, returning the head to its original position; repeat. The sarcomere, I band and H zone shorten; the A band stays the same length. Phosphocreatine regenerates ATP (phosphorylates ADP) anaerobically for short bursts. Slow-twitch fibres: contract slowly, for sustained contraction and posture, rely on aerobic respiration: many mitochondria, much myoglobin (red), a good blood supply. Fast-twitch fibres: rapid, powerful contractions for short periods, rely on anaerobic respiration: more phosphocreatine and glycogen, fewer mitochondria.
