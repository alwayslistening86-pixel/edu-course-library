# S09_Digestion_and_Transport_in_Animals - Lesson: Digestion, absorption and mass transport in animals

## Goal
The learner explains digestion of carbohydrates, lipids and proteins by named enzymes and absorption of the products, and explains haemoglobin and oxygen dissociation curves, the circulatory system, the cardiac cycle, blood vessels, tissue fluid, and cardiovascular disease risk data, and carries out required practical 5.

## How to teach this
Ask why a llama living at high altitude has haemoglobin with a curve shifted to the left. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. Use AQA's precise terms (e.g. 'water potential', 'complementary', 'tertiary structure') and insist on them in answers. #### 3.3.3 Digestion and absorption
Digestion: large biological molecules are hydrolysed to smaller ones that can be absorbed. Carbohydrates: amylase (salivary and pancreatic) hydrolyses starch to maltose; membrane-bound disaccharidases (maltase, sucrase, lactase) on the ileum epithelium hydrolyse disaccharides to monosaccharides. Lipids: lipase hydrolyses triglycerides to monoglycerides and fatty acids; bile salts emulsify lipids into small droplets (larger surface area for lipase) and form micelles. Proteins: endopeptidases hydrolyse bonds within polypeptides, exopeptidases hydrolyse terminal bonds, and membrane-bound dipeptidases hydrolyse dipeptides. Absorption: monosaccharides and amino acids by co-transport with sodium ions (S06); micelles carry monoglycerides and fatty acids to the epithelium, where they diffuse in (lipid-soluble), are reformed into triglycerides in the ER and Golgi, packaged into chylomicrons and released by exocytosis into lacteals (lymph).

#### 3.3.4.1 Mass transport in animals
Haemoglobins are a group of chemically similar proteins found in many organisms, with a quaternary structure of four polypeptides, each with a haem group containing Fe^2+ that binds oxygen. Loading in the lungs (high pO2), unloading in respiring tissues. Oxygen dissociation curve (sigmoid): binding of the first oxygen changes the shape, making it easier for more to bind (cooperative binding). The Bohr effect: higher CO2 lowers the affinity (curve shifts right), unloading more oxygen to respiring tissues. Organisms in low-oxygen environments (or small, active animals) have haemoglobin with different affinities. Circulation: a closed, double circulatory system in mammals; the heart: four chambers (right and left atria and ventricles), the septum separating the two sides, a thicker muscular wall in the left ventricle (pumping blood round the whole body at higher pressure), atrioventricular valves (between atria and ventricles) and semilunar valves (in the aorta and pulmonary artery), with the vena cava and pulmonary vein entering the atria and the pulmonary artery and aorta leaving the ventricles; coronary arteries; pulmonary and systemic circuits with named vessels (vena cava, aorta, pulmonary artery and vein, renal artery and vein). Cardiac cycle: atrial systole, ventricular systole, diastole; pressure and volume changes and valve movements (atrioventricular valves close when ventricular pressure exceeds atrial; semilunar valves open when it exceeds arterial). Cardiac output = stroke volume x heart rate. Vessels: arteries (thick muscular and elastic walls, maintain pressure), arterioles (muscle to control flow), veins (thin walls, valves, low pressure), capillaries (one-cell-thick endothelium, narrow, short diffusion). Tissue fluid forms at the arteriole end from high hydrostatic pressure; returns at the venule end by osmosis because of the lower water potential from plasma proteins; excess drains into lymph. Interpret data on risk factors for cardiovascular disease.

#### RP5 Required practical 5: dissection of an animal or plant gas exchange or mass transport system, or of an organ within one
**Method (e.g. dissecting a mammalian heart, fish gills or insect tracheae):** use a sharp scalpel on a dissecting board, cutting away from the body; identify structures and make an annotated scientific drawing. Ethical considerations: use material from food sources, dispose of it properly; safety: sharp instruments, wash hands, clean surfaces.
