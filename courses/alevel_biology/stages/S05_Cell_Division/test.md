# S05_Cell_Division - Test: The cell cycle, mitosis and binary fission

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe the behaviour of chromosomes in metaphase and anaphase of mitosis. [4 marks]
2. In a root-tip squash, 45 cells were in prophase, 12 in metaphase, 8 in anaphase, 10 in telophase and 425 in interphase. Calculate the mitotic index, and the time spent in metaphase if the cycle takes 24 hours. [3 marks]
3. In required practical 2, explain why the root tip is placed in hydrochloric acid and why it is squashed. [2 marks]
4. Explain why drugs that prevent spindle fibres forming can be used to treat cancer, and why they have side effects. [3 marks]
5. Describe how a bacterium divides by binary fission. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 metaphase: chromosomes line up at the equator; B1 attached to spindle fibres by centromeres; B1 anaphase: centromeres divide; B1 chromatids pulled to opposite poles by the spindle.
2. [3] M1 (45 + 12 + 8 + 10)/500; A1 0.15; A1 metaphase 34.6 minutes (= 12/500 x 24 h = 0.576 h).
3. [2] B1 acid breaks down the material holding cells together (and stops division) so cells separate; B1 squashing produces a single layer of cells so light passes through and cells are seen clearly.
4. [3] B1 chromosomes cannot be separated, so mitosis stops; B1 cancer cells divide rapidly/uncontrollably, so are most affected; B1 other rapidly dividing normal cells (hair, gut lining) are also affected.
5. [3] B1 replication of the circular DNA; B1 and of plasmids; B1 division of the cytoplasm to produce two daughter cells (each with one copy of the DNA).
