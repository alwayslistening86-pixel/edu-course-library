# S15_Respiration - Test: Respiration

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe the role of oxygen in aerobic respiration and explain why ATP production stops without it. [3 marks]
2. Explain why the production of lactate allows glycolysis to continue in muscle during intense exercise. [2 marks]
3. Describe the link reaction. [3 marks]
4. In required practical 9, explain why a control of boiled yeast is included, and what soda lime does in a respirometer. [2 marks]
5. Explain how fatty acids can be used as a respiratory substrate. [2 marks]
6. Which processes produce ATP by substrate-level phosphorylation? Choose every correct option.
   A. glycolysis
   B. the Krebs cycle
   C. the link reaction
   D. oxidative phosphorylation

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 final electron acceptor in the electron transfer chain (forms water with protons); B1 without it, electrons can't pass along the chain and protons aren't pumped; B1 so no proton gradient/chemiosmosis, and NAD/FAD aren't regenerated for the Krebs cycle.
2. [2] B1 pyruvate is reduced to lactate using reduced NAD; B1 regenerating NAD, which is needed to oxidise triose phosphate in glycolysis.
3. [3] B1 pyruvate is oxidised to acetate (loses CO2: decarboxylation); B1 producing reduced NAD; B1 acetate combines with coenzyme A to form acetylcoenzyme A.
4. [2] B1 shows any change is due to respiration by living yeast (enzymes denatured in the control); B1 soda lime absorbs CO2, so the movement shows oxygen uptake.
5. [2] B1 broken down into 2-carbon fragments; B1 which form acetylcoenzyme A and enter the Krebs cycle (glycerol converted to triose phosphate).
6. Correct: A, B (exactly these options, no others)
