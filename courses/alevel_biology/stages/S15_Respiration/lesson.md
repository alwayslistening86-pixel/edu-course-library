## Goal

## How to teach this
Ask why sprinters' muscles 'burn' and why bread rises. Teach from structure to function: what the molecule, cell or organ is like, how that lets it work, then an example or data to interpret. Use AQA's precise terms (e.g. 'water potential', 'complementary', 'tertiary structure') and insist on them in answers. #### 3.5.2 Respiration (A-level only)
Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose is phosphorylated (using 2 ATP) and split into two triose phosphate, which are oxidised to pyruvate, producing a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 reduced NAD. Anaerobic respiration: in plants and microorganisms, pyruvate → ethanol + CO2; in animals and some bacteria, pyruvate → lactate; both regenerate NAD so glycolysis can continue (only 2 ATP per glucose). Aerobic respiration: pyruvate enters the mitochondrial matrix by active transport; link reaction: pyruvate is oxidised to acetate (losing CO2 and producing reduced NAD); acetate combines with coenzyme A to form acetylcoenzyme A. Krebs cycle: acetylcoenzyme A (2C) combines with a 4C molecule to form a 6C molecule, which is converted back to 4C in a series of oxidation-reduction reactions, producing ATP (by substrate-level phosphorylation), reduced NAD, reduced FAD and CO2. Oxidative phosphorylation (inner membrane, cristae): electrons from reduced NAD and FAD pass along the electron transfer chain; energy released pumps protons into the intermembrane space; protons flow back through ATP synthase (chemiosmosis), producing ATP; oxygen is the final electron acceptor, combining with electrons and protons to form water. Other respiratory substrates: lipids (fatty acids broken into 2C fragments for acetylcoenzyme A; glycerol to triose phosphate) and proteins (amino acids deaminated, entering the Krebs cycle).

#### RP9 Required practical 9: the effect of a named variable on the rate of respiration of cultures of single-celled organisms
**Method (e.g. yeast):** use a respirometer, or measure CO2 production (gas syringe) or the rate of reduction of methylene blue (a redox indicator, blue to colourless) by yeast suspension in a glucose solution; vary a named variable (temperature, substrate, glucose concentration); equilibrate in a water bath; include a control with boiled yeast. In a respirometer with soda lime (absorbs CO2), movement of the coloured liquid measures oxygen uptake: volume = πr^2 x distance moved.
