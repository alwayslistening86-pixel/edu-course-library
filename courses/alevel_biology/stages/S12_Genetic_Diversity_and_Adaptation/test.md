# S12_Genetic_Diversity_and_Adaptation - Test: Mutation, meiosis, genetic diversity and natural selection

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how meiosis produces genetically varied gametes. [4 marks]
2. Explain why a substitution mutation may have no effect on the polypeptide, but a deletion usually has a large effect. [3 marks]
3. Explain how a population of bacteria becomes resistant to an antibiotic. [4 marks]
4. Describe stabilising selection, using human birth weight as an example. [2 marks]
5. Calculate the number of possible chromosome combinations in a zygote of an organism with 2n = 12, from independent segregation and random fertilisation. [2 marks]
6. Name the process that gives rise to Down's syndrome and describe what happens. [2 marks]
7. In required practical 6, give two aseptic techniques and explain why plates are incubated at 25 °C. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 independent segregation: homologous pairs line up randomly in meiosis I; B1 so any combination of maternal and paternal chromosomes can go to each gamete; B1 crossing over between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes; B1 exchanges alleles, producing new combinations.
2. [3] B1 the genetic code is degenerate, so the new triplet may code for the same amino acid; B1 a deletion causes a frame shift; B1 changing every triplet after it, and so many amino acids.
3. [4] B1 a random mutation produces an allele for resistance; B1 in the presence of the antibiotic, bacteria without it die; B1 resistant bacteria survive and reproduce; B1 passing on the allele, so its frequency increases (directional selection).
4. [2] B1 babies of intermediate weight have the highest survival; B1 so the mean stays the same and the range/standard deviation decreases.
5. [2] M1 2^6 per gamete; A1 (2^6)^2 = 4096.
6. [2] B1 non-disjunction; B1 a pair of homologous chromosomes (21) fails to separate in meiosis, so a gamete has an extra copy.
7. [3] B1 B1 two of: flame the loop, work near a Bunsen flame, flame the neck of bottles, disinfect the bench, lift the lid only slightly; B1 25 °C reduces the chance of growing human pathogens.
