#### 3.4.3 Genetic diversity can arise as a result of mutation or during meiosis
Gene mutations: changes in the base sequence (substitution, deletion) during DNA replication; because of degeneracy, a substitution may not change the amino acid; a deletion causes a frame shift. Mutagenic agents increase the mutation rate. Chromosome mutations: non-disjunction changes the number of whole chromosomes (e.g. Down's syndrome) or sets. Meiosis: two divisions producing four genetically different haploid cells. Variation arises from independent segregation of homologous chromosomes (in meiosis I) and crossing over between chromatids of homologous chromosomes (exchanging alleles). Number of combinations from independent segregation = 2^n (n = number of homologous pairs); random fertilisation increases variation further: 2^n x 2^n. *Example:* humans (n = 23): 2^23 = 8,388,608 combinations per gamete. Distinguish mitosis and meiosis in diagrams (chromosome number, pairing of homologues).

#### 3.4.4 Genetic diversity and adaptation
Genetic diversity: the number of different alleles of genes in a population; it enables natural selection. Natural selection: random mutation produces new alleles; some give a selective advantage; individuals with them are more likely to survive, reproduce and pass them on; over many generations the new allele increases in frequency. Directional selection (e.g. antibiotic resistance in bacteria): the extreme phenotype is favoured and the mean shifts; stabilising selection (e.g. human birth weights): the modal phenotype is favoured and the range narrows. Adaptations are anatomical, physiological and behavioural.

#### RP6 Required practical 6: aseptic techniques to investigate the effect of antimicrobial substances on microbial growth
**Method:** use aseptic technique: disinfect the bench, work near a Bunsen flame (updraught carries microbes away), flame the neck of culture bottles, sterilise the inoculating loop by flaming; spread bacteria (broth culture) evenly on agar with a sterile spreader; place paper discs soaked in different antimicrobials (and a control disc of sterile water); tape the lid (not sealed all round, so aerobic conditions and no anaerobic pathogens) and incubate at 25 °C (not 37 °C, to avoid human pathogens); measure the diameter of the clear zone (inhibition zone) and calculate its area, πr^2.
