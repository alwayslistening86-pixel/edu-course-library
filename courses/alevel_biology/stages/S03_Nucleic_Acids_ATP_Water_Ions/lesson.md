# S03_Nucleic_Acids_ATP_Water_Ions - Lesson: DNA and RNA, replication, ATP, water and inorganic ions

#### 3.1.5.1 Structure of DNA and RNA
A nucleotide has a pentose sugar, a phosphate group and a nitrogen-containing organic base. DNA: deoxyribose; bases adenine, cytosine, guanine and thymine; RNA: ribose; bases A, C, G and uracil. Nucleotides join by condensation, forming phosphodiester bonds (a sugar-phosphate backbone). DNA is a double helix of two polynucleotide strands held by hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs (A-T, C-G). RNA is a relatively short single polynucleotide chain. The structure of DNA was accepted by scientists only gradually, as evidence accumulated; many doubted that such a simple molecule could carry genetic information.

#### 3.1.5.2 DNA replication
Semi-conservative replication: DNA helicase breaks the hydrogen bonds between bases, unwinding the helix; each strand acts as a template; free DNA nucleotides are attracted to their complementary bases; DNA polymerase joins adjacent nucleotides by condensation, forming phosphodiester bonds. Each new molecule has one original and one new strand, ensuring genetic continuity. Evidence: Meselson and Stahl grew bacteria in heavy nitrogen (15N), then transferred them to light 14N: after one generation all DNA was of intermediate density (ruling out conservative replication); after two, half intermediate and half light.

#### 3.1.6 ATP
ATP (adenosine triphosphate): adenine, ribose and three phosphate groups. Hydrolysis by ATP hydrolase: ATP + H2O → ADP + Pi, releasing energy for energy-requiring reactions; the inorganic phosphate can phosphorylate other compounds, often making them more reactive. ATP is resynthesised from ADP and Pi by condensation, catalysed by ATP synthase, in respiration and photosynthesis. It is an immediate energy source: small, soluble, releases energy in small manageable amounts in a single step.

#### 3.1.7 Water
Water is polar and forms hydrogen bonds. Properties: a metabolite (condensation and hydrolysis); an important solvent in which metabolic reactions occur; a relatively high heat capacity, buffering temperature changes; a relatively large latent heat of vaporisation, giving a cooling effect with little water loss (sweating); strong cohesion between molecules, supporting columns of water in xylem and producing surface tension where water meets air.

#### 3.1.8 Inorganic ions
Inorganic ions occur in solution in cytoplasm and body fluids: hydrogen ions (determine pH, and so enzyme activity), iron ions (a component of haemoglobin, binding oxygen), sodium ions (co-transport of glucose and amino acids), phosphate ions (in DNA, ATP and phospholipids).
