# S20_Homeostasis - Test: Homeostasis: blood glucose and water potential

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe the second messenger model of glucagon action. [4 marks]
2. Explain how the loop of Henle creates a low water potential in the medulla of the kidney. [3 marks]
3. Describe how ADH reduces water loss when the blood water potential falls. [4 marks]
4. Explain the difference between type I and type II diabetes and how each is treated. [4 marks]
5. In required practical 11, a calibration curve gives absorbance 0.62 for the unknown, which lies between the 4.0 mmol dm^-3 standard (0.70) and the 6.0 mmol dm^-3 standard (0.50). Assuming a straight line between them, estimate the glucose concentration and explain why absorbance falls as glucose rises. [3 marks]
6. Explain why negative feedback is involved in homeostasis. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 glucagon binds to a specific receptor on the liver cell membrane; B1 activates adenylate cyclase; B1 which converts ATP to cyclic AMP; B1 cAMP activates protein kinase, which activates enzymes that hydrolyse glycogen to glucose.
2. [3] B1 Na+ and Cl- actively transported out of the ascending limb; B1 which is impermeable to water; B1 so the ion concentration in the medulla rises, lowering its water potential (water then leaves the descending limb/collecting duct by osmosis).
3. [4] B1 osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect the fall; B1 posterior pituitary releases more ADH; B1 ADH increases the permeability of the collecting duct/DCT to water (aquaporins); B1 more water reabsorbed by osmosis into the blood, so a smaller volume of concentrated urine.
4. [4] B1 type I: no insulin produced (β cells destroyed); B1 treated with insulin injections; B1 type II: cells don't respond to insulin (receptors less responsive); B1 treated by diet and exercise (and drugs).
5. [3] M1 4.0 + 2.0 x (0.70 - 0.62)/(0.70 - 0.50); A1 4.8 mmol dm^-3; B1 more glucose reduces more blue Cu2+ to precipitate, leaving less blue colour to absorb light.
6. [2] B1 a change from the normal level is detected and the response reverses it; B1 returning the factor to the set point, keeping conditions within limits.
