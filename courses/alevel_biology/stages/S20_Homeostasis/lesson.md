#### 3.6.4.1 Principles of homeostasis and negative feedback (A-level only)
Homeostasis is the maintenance of a stable internal environment within restricted limits, by physiological control systems: temperature and pH (for enzyme activity) and blood glucose (for water potential and a reliable energy supply). Negative feedback restores systems to their original level; separate mechanisms for correcting departures in each direction give greater control. Positive feedback amplifies a change away from the normal level (e.g. during an action potential, Na+ entry opens more Na+ channels).

#### 3.6.4.2 Control of blood glucose concentration (A-level only)
Factors that influence blood glucose: diet, glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose), gluconeogenesis (glucose from non-carbohydrates), glycogenesis (glucose → glycogen). Insulin (from β cells of the islets of Langerhans) binds receptors on liver and muscle cells: more glucose transporter proteins (GLUT4) in membranes, activation of enzymes converting glucose to glycogen; blood glucose falls. Glucagon (α cells) binds receptors on liver cells: activates enzymes for glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis; blood glucose rises. Adrenaline also raises blood glucose. Second messenger model: adrenaline or glucagon binds a receptor, activating adenylate cyclase, which converts ATP to cyclic AMP (cAMP); cAMP activates protein kinase, which activates glycogenolysis. Type I diabetes: the body cannot produce insulin (autoimmune destruction of β cells): controlled by insulin injections. Type II: receptors lose responsiveness to insulin (often linked with obesity): controlled by diet, exercise and drugs. Evaluate positions of health advisers and the food industry on the rise of type II diabetes. Colorimetric methods measure glucose concentration.

#### 3.6.4.3 Control of blood water potential (A-level only)
Osmoregulation. The nephron: glomerulus and Bowman's capsule (ultrafiltration under high hydrostatic pressure, forming glomerular filtrate); proximal convoluted tubule (selective reabsorption of glucose, amino acids, many ions and water; microvilli, many mitochondria, co-transport); loop of Henle (the ascending limb actively transports Na+ and Cl- out into the medulla and is impermeable to water, creating a low water potential in the medulla; water leaves the descending limb and collecting duct by osmosis; a counter-current multiplier); distal convoluted tubule; collecting duct. Longer loops of Henle concentrate urine more (desert animals). ADH: osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect a fall in blood water potential; the posterior pituitary releases ADH; ADH makes the walls of the distal convoluted tubule and collecting duct more permeable to water (aquaporins inserted); more water is reabsorbed, less urine is produced.

#### RP11 Required practical 11: a glucose dilution series and colorimetric calibration curve to find the glucose concentration of an unknown 'urine' sample
**Method:** make a dilution series of glucose (e.g. 0 to 10 mmol dm^-3) from a stock solution; add the same volume of Benedict's reagent to each, heat in a boiling water bath for the same time; after filtering or letting the precipitate settle, measure the absorbance (or % transmission) of each with a colorimeter (red filter): the more glucose, the less blue remains. Plot a calibration curve; test the unknown 'urine' sample in the same way and read its concentration from the curve.
