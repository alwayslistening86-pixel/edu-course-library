# S23_Populations_in_Ecosystems - Test: Populations in ecosystems, sampling and succession

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. In a mark-release-recapture study, 64 beetles were marked. Later, 72 were caught, of which 9 were marked. Estimate the population and give two assumptions. [3 marks]
2. Describe how succession leads from bare rock to a climax community. [4 marks]
3. Explain how you would investigate the effect of light intensity on the distribution of a plant species across a woodland edge. [4 marks]
4. Explain the difference between interspecific and intraspecific competition, and their effects on population size. [3 marks]
5. A population of bacteria grows from 500 to 64 000 in 3.5 hours. How many generations have passed, and what is the generation time? [2 marks]
6. Explain why conservation of heathland may involve preventing succession. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 64 x 72/9; A1 512; B1 two of: marking doesn't affect survival/is not lost; marked mix randomly; no births, deaths or migration between samples.
2. [4] B1 pioneer species (e.g. lichens) colonise; B1 they change the abiotic conditions (weathering rock, dead organisms form soil); B1 new species colonise and out-compete earlier ones; B1 biodiversity increases until a stable climax community forms.
3. [4] B1 belt transect from woodland into open ground; B1 quadrats at regular intervals; B1 measure light intensity with a light meter and plant percentage cover in each quadrat; B1 repeat transects and use a correlation test (e.g. Spearman's rank).
4. [3] B1 interspecific: between different species for the same resources; B1 can reduce the population of one species (or exclude it); B1 intraspecific: within a species, limits population size around the carrying capacity.
5. [2] M1 2^n = 128, n = 7; A1 generation time = 210/7 = 30 minutes.
6. [2] B1 succession would lead to woodland, replacing heathland species; B1 grazing, cutting or burning keeps the community at an earlier stage (plagioclimax), preserving the habitat.
