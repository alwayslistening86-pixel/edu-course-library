# S24_Mutation_and_Gene_Expression - Test: Gene mutations, stem cells, regulation of gene expression and cancer

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how oestrogen stimulates transcription of a target gene. [4 marks]
2. Explain how increased methylation of a tumour suppressor gene could lead to cancer. [3 marks]
3. Describe how RNA interference prevents the expression of a gene. [3 marks]
4. Give two differences between benign and malignant tumours. [2 marks]
5. Explain the advantages of induced pluripotent stem cells over embryonic stem cells for treating disease. [2 marks]
6. Explain why a substitution mutation can change the tertiary structure of a protein. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 oestrogen is lipid-soluble and diffuses through the cell-surface membrane; B1 binds a receptor site on a transcription factor; B1 changing its shape so it is activated; B1 the transcription factor binds to the promoter, allowing RNA polymerase to start transcription.
2. [3] B1 methylation of the gene's DNA (promoter) prevents transcription; B1 so the tumour suppressor protein isn't made; B1 cell division is not slowed/apoptosis not triggered, so uncontrolled division.
3. [3] B1 siRNA (or microRNA) binds to a protein complex; B1 and to complementary mRNA; B1 which is cut up/hydrolysed, so it can't be translated.
4. [2] B1 B1 two of: malignant grow faster; malignant invade surrounding tissue; malignant spread by metastasis; benign are encapsulated/have a well-defined boundary.
5. [2] B1 no destruction of embryos (fewer ethical concerns); B1 made from the patient's own cells, so no rejection.
6. [3] B1 changes the base triplet, which may code for a different amino acid; B1 changing the primary structure; B1 so hydrogen/ionic/disulfide bonds form in different places, changing the tertiary structure.
