# S04_Cell_Structure_and_Microscopy - Test: Eukaryotic and prokaryotic cells, viruses and methods of studying cells

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why the homogenising solution in cell fractionation is cold, isotonic and buffered. [3 marks]
2. Compare transmission and scanning electron microscopes. [3 marks]
3. An eyepiece graticule division equals 2.5 μm at x400. A cell spans 18 divisions. Calculate its length in μm and in m (standard form). [2 marks]
4. Describe the role of the rough endoplasmic reticulum and Golgi apparatus in producing and secreting an extracellular enzyme. [4 marks]
5. Describe the structure of an HIV-like virus particle. [3 marks]
6. In ultracentrifugation of liver tissue, which organelle is in the first pellet, and why? [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 cold: reduces the activity of enzymes that break down organelles; B1 isotonic: prevents organelles bursting or shrinking by osmosis; B1 buffered: prevents proteins/enzymes denaturing through pH changes.
2. [3] B1 TEM has higher resolution; B1 TEM gives 2D images of thin sections showing internal structure; B1 SEM gives 3D images of surfaces (both need a vacuum, so living specimens can't be viewed).
3. [2] B1 45 μm; B1 4.5 x 10^-5 m.
4. [4] B1 ribosomes on the RER synthesise the protein; B1 RER transports it (in vesicles) to the Golgi; B1 Golgi modifies (e.g. adds carbohydrate) and packages it into vesicles; B1 vesicles fuse with the cell-surface membrane, releasing the enzyme by exocytosis.
5. [3] B1 genetic material (RNA); B1 capsid (protein coat); B1 attachment proteins (and lipid envelope, reverse transcriptase).
6. [2] B1 nuclei; B1 they are the largest/densest organelles, so sediment at the lowest speed.
