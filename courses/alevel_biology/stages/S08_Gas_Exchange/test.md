# S08_Gas_Exchange - Test: Surface area to volume ratio and gas exchange

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain how the counter-current system in fish gills increases the efficiency of gas exchange. [3 marks]
2. Describe the mechanism of inspiration in humans. [4 marks]
3. Explain how two adaptations of a xerophytic leaf reduce water loss. [4 marks]
4. Explain why insects close their spiracles when resting, and why this is a trade-off. [2 marks]
5. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a spherical cell of radius 5 μm and one of radius 20 μm, and explain the significance. [3 marks]
6. A study shows a correlation between air pollution levels and asthma rates. Explain why this does not prove that pollution causes asthma. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 blood and water flow in opposite directions; B1 so blood always meets water with a higher oxygen concentration; B1 a diffusion gradient is maintained along the whole length of the lamella.
2. [4] B1 external intercostal muscles contract (internal relax), ribs move up and out; B1 diaphragm contracts and flattens; B1 volume of the thorax increases; B1 pressure falls below atmospheric, so air moves in.
3. [4] B1 sunken stomata/hairs/rolled leaves; B1 trap moist air, reducing the water potential gradient between leaf and air; B1 thick waxy cuticle; B1 increases the diffusion distance/is waterproof, reducing evaporation.
4. [2] B1 reduces water loss by evaporation; B1 but limits gas exchange/oxygen supply (fine when respiration is slow).
5. [3] M1 SA:V = 3/r; A1 0.6 μm^-1 and 0.15 μm^-1; B1 the larger cell has a smaller SA:V, so diffusion is less able to meet its needs.
6. [2] B1 correlation doesn't show causation; B1 other factors may be involved (e.g. smoking, genetics, housing), so they must be controlled for.
