# S25_Genome_Projects_and_Gene_Technology - Test: Genome projects, recombinant DNA, genetic screening and fingerprinting

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe how a human gene can be inserted into a bacterial plasmid and how transformed bacteria are identified. [5 marks]
2. Explain why the genome of a human does not allow the proteome to be determined easily. [2 marks]
3. Explain how genetic fingerprinting can show whether a man is the father of a child. [3 marks]
4. Explain how a labelled DNA probe can detect a mutant allele. [3 marks]
5. After PCR, how many copies of a single DNA molecule are there after 30 cycles? Give your answer in standard form. [1 mark]
6. Give one argument for and one against the use of genetically modified crops. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] B1 cut the plasmid and gene with the same restriction endonuclease; B1 giving complementary sticky ends; B1 DNA ligase joins the gene into the plasmid; B1 bacteria take up the plasmid (calcium ions and heat shock); B1 marker genes (e.g. antibiotic resistance or fluorescence) identify bacteria that took up the recombinant plasmid.
2. [2] B1 much of the genome is non-coding DNA/introns; B1 regulatory genes and alternative splicing (and modification) mean one gene can give several proteins.
3. [3] B1 VNTR patterns are inherited: half of the child's bands come from each parent; B1 compare the child's bands with the mother's and alleged father's; B1 all the child's bands not from the mother must match the father's.
4. [3] B1 short single-stranded DNA complementary to the mutant allele; B1 hybridises (binds by base pairing) to the allele if present; B1 label (fluorescent/radioactive) shows where it has bound.
5. [1] B1 2^30 = 1.07 x 10^9.
6. [2] B1 for: higher yields/pest resistance/reduced pesticide use/improved nutrition; B1 against: reduced biodiversity/gene transfer to wild plants/farmers dependent on patented seed (cost).
