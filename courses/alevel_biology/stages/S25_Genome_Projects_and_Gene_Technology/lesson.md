# S25_Genome_Projects_and_Gene_Technology - Lesson: Genome projects, recombinant DNA, genetic screening and fingerprinting

## Goal

#### 3.8.3 Using genome projects (A-level only)
Sequencing projects have read the genomes of many organisms. Determining the genome of simpler organisms allows their proteome to be determined (e.g. identifying antigens for vaccines). In more complex organisms, non-coding DNA and regulatory genes mean that knowledge of the genome can't easily be translated into the proteome. Sequencing methods are continuously updated and have become automated, faster and cheaper.

#### 3.8.4.1 Recombinant DNA technology (A-level only)
Producing DNA fragments: reverse transcriptase makes cDNA from mRNA; restriction endonucleases cut DNA at specific recognition sequences, often leaving sticky ends; a gene machine synthesises a gene from a known protein sequence. In vitro amplification: the polymerase chain reaction (PCR): heat to 95 °C to separate strands; cool to about 55 °C for primers to anneal; 72 °C for Taq DNA polymerase to add free nucleotides; each cycle doubles the DNA. In vivo cloning: add promoter and terminator regions; insert the fragment into a vector (plasmid) using the same restriction enzyme and DNA ligase; transform host cells (e.g. with calcium ions and heat shock); identify transformed cells with marker genes (antibiotic resistance, fluorescence, enzymes). Recombinant DNA technology produces transformed (genetically modified) organisms, with benefits (drug production, pest-resistant crops) and issues (ethical, financial, social; e.g. herbicide-resistant crops and the patenting of seeds). Gene therapy: inserting functional alleles into cells (somatic, not germline) using vectors.

#### 3.8.4.2 Differences in DNA between individuals of the same species can be exploited for identification and diagnosis of heritable conditions (A-level only)
DNA probes: short, single-stranded DNA, labelled (radioactively or fluorescently), complementary to a target allele; they hybridise to the allele, which is then detected. Used to screen patients for heritable conditions, drug responses or health risks; genetic counselling helps people understand results and make decisions; personalised medicine tailors treatment to a person's genotype.

#### 3.8.4.3 Genetic fingerprinting (A-level only)
Genetic fingerprinting: an organism's genome contains many variable number tandem repeats (VNTRs); the probability of two individuals having the same VNTRs is very low. Method: extract DNA, amplify by PCR, cut with restriction enzymes, separate by gel electrophoresis (fragments move towards the positive electrode, smaller ones further), transfer and hybridise with labelled probes, and visualise the pattern. Uses: determining genetic relationships and variability, forensic science, medical diagnosis, animal and plant breeding. *Example:* after 25 PCR cycles one DNA molecule becomes 2^25 = 33,554,432 copies.
