#### MA2.3 Labour costing: direct vs indirect labour, and calculating labour cost including overtime premium
**Direct labour** is time that can be traced to a specific product/cost unit (e.g. a machine operator's time making a specific batch); **indirect labour** cannot be traced this way (e.g. a supervisor's or cleaner's time, or an operator's idle time). Labour cost is calculated as: basic pay (hours worked x basic rate) plus any **overtime premium** -- overtime is usually paid at a multiple of the basic rate (e.g. 'time and a half' = 1.5 x basic rate, 'double time' = 2 x basic rate) for hours worked beyond a standard number. *Worked example:* an employee's basic rate is £14.00/hour, with a standard 37-hour week; they work 42 hours, with hours beyond 37 paid at time and a half. Basic pay = 37 x £14.00 = **£518.00**; overtime hours = 42 - 37 = 5; overtime rate = £14.00 x 1.5 = £21.00/hour; overtime pay = 5 x £21.00 = **£105.00**; total gross pay = 518.00 + 105.00 = **£623.00**.

#### MA2.4 Overhead allocation and absorption: allocation vs apportionment, apportionment bases, calculating absorption rates, and accounting for under/over absorption
**Allocation** charges a whole overhead cost directly to the one cost centre that caused it (e.g. a production department's own supervisor's salary, allocated wholly to that department); **apportionment** shares an overhead across several cost centres, using a fair **basis** related to how each centre benefits or causes the cost (e.g. rent apportioned by floor area, machine insurance by the value of machinery in each centre, heating by volume). Once each production cost centre's total overhead (allocated plus apportioned) is known, an **overhead absorption rate (OAR)** is calculated to charge (absorb) that overhead into the cost of each unit produced: OAR = Budgeted overhead / Budgeted activity level (e.g. labour hours or machine hours). Because the OAR is set in advance using *budgeted* figures, the overhead actually **absorbed** into production (OAR x *actual* activity) will rarely exactly equal the overhead *actually incurred* -- the difference is **under-absorption** (absorbed less than incurred, meaning cost of sales was understated and needs a further charge to profit) or **over-absorption** (absorbed more than incurred, meaning cost of sales was overstated and profit needs increasing). *Worked example:* rent of £20,000 is apportioned between Production A (6,000 sq ft) and Production B (4,000 sq ft), total 10,000 sq ft: A's share = £20,000 x 6,000/10,000 = **£12,000**; B's share = £20,000 x 4,000/10,000 = **£8,000**. Production A's total budgeted overhead (after all allocation and apportionment) is £45,000, and budgeted labour hours are 9,000: OAR = 45,000/9,000 = **£5.00 per labour hour**. If actual labour hours worked were 9,300, overhead **absorbed** = 9,300 x £5.00 = £46,500; if actual overhead **incurred** was £47,000, the business has **under-absorbed** by 47,000 - 46,500 = **£500**.
