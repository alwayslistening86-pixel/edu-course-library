# S23_MA_Short_Term_Decision_Making - Test: Breakeven and CVP analysis, limiting factors and relevant costing

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry and financial-statement questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Selling price £55/unit, variable cost £33/unit, fixed costs £88,000. Calculate the breakeven point in units, and the margin of safety in units if budgeted sales are 5,000 units. [4 marks]
2. Selling price £20/unit, variable cost £12/unit, fixed costs £40,000. Calculate the sales volume (units) required for a target profit of £16,000. [3 marks]
3. Labour hours are limited to 1,000 hours. Product P: contribution £18/unit, 3 labour hours/unit, maximum demand 200 units. Product Q: contribution £22/unit, 2 labour hours/unit, maximum demand 300 units. Rank the products by contribution per labour hour and determine the optimal production plan. [5 marks]
4. A division earns contribution of £12,000 but is allocated £15,000 of shared head-office fixed costs, only £3,000 of which is genuinely avoidable if the division closes. Should the division be discontinued on relevant-cost grounds? Choose every correct option.
   A. No -- its contribution (£12,000) exceeds the avoidable fixed costs (£3,000)
   B. Yes -- its contribution is less than its total allocated fixed costs
   C. Yes -- all fixed costs are always relevant to this decision
   D. There is not enough information to decide
5. Explain why a sunk cost should be ignored in a make-or-buy decision. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 contribution = 55-33 = £22/unit; M1 breakeven = 88,000/22 = 4,000 units; A1 margin of safety = 5,000-4,000 = 1,000 units; B1 correctly identifying that margin of safety is budgeted sales less breakeven sales.
2. [3] M1 contribution = 20-12 = £8/unit; M1 required units = (40,000+16,000)/8; A1 = 7,000 units.
3. [5] M1 P: contribution/hour = 18/3 = £6; M1 Q: contribution/hour = 22/2 = £11; A1 Q ranks higher, so produce Q first: 300 units uses 600 hours, leaving 400 hours; B1 P: 400/3 = 133 units (rounding down to whole units), within its 200-unit demand limit; B1 correct reasoning that ranking by contribution per unit of limiting factor (not per unit alone) maximises total contribution.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [2] B1 a sunk cost has already been spent and cannot be recovered or changed by the decision being made now; B1 relevant costs must be future, incremental costs that change as a direct result of the decision -- a sunk cost fails this test, so including it would distort the decision.
