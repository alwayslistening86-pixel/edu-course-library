# S27_TAX_VAT_Calculations_and_Returns - Lesson: Calculating VAT and preparing/submitting the VAT return

## Goal
The learner calculates output tax, input tax and the VAT payable/recoverable for a period, and explains preparing/submitting the VAT return, deadlines and compliance requirements.

## Syllabus items taught here
- TAX3.1 - Calculating VAT: output tax and input tax, when input tax cannot be recovered, and adjusting for credit notes/prompt payment discounts
- TAX3.2 - Preparing and submitting the VAT return: the return's boxes, deadlines, records to keep, and the consequences of non-compliance

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: a business has more input VAT than output VAT for a period. Does it pay VAT to HMRC, or does HMRC repay it? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### TAX3.1 Calculating VAT: output tax and input tax, when input tax cannot be recovered, and adjusting for credit notes/prompt payment discounts
**Output tax** is the VAT a business charges on its own taxable sales; **input tax** is the VAT it is charged on its own purchases and expenses. A VAT-registered business pays over to HMRC the *net* of the two: VAT payable = output tax - input tax (or, if input tax exceeds output tax, the business is instead due a **VAT repayment** from HMRC). Not all input tax can be recovered: input tax is generally **not recoverable** on **business entertaining** costs (entertaining anyone who is not an employee, e.g. clients), on goods/services used for a **non-business purpose**, and on costs relating wholly to **making exempt supplies** (since no output VAT was charged on those sales to offset it against). **Credit notes** and **prompt payment discounts** reduce the VAT due: where a credit note is issued (e.g. for returned goods), the VAT on it is deducted from output tax for the period it is issued in; where a prompt payment discount is offered and taken, VAT is due only on the discounted amount actually paid, requiring a VAT adjustment if the original invoice was raised (and VAT accounted for) on the full amount. *Worked example:* in a VAT period, a business has standard-rated sales (VAT-exclusive) of £45,000, standard-rated purchases (VAT-exclusive, VAT recoverable) of £18,000, and separately incurs £600 (VAT-exclusive) of client entertaining costs (input VAT not recoverable). Output tax = £45,000 x 20% = **£9,000**. Input tax = £18,000 x 20% = **£3,600** (the £600 x 20% = £120 of VAT on entertaining is excluded, as it is not recoverable). VAT payable to HMRC = 9,000 - 3,600 = **£5,400**.

#### TAX3.2 Preparing and submitting the VAT return: the return's boxes, deadlines, records to keep, and the consequences of non-compliance
The **VAT return** is normally submitted for each **VAT period** (usually quarterly), summarising the period's output tax, input tax and the resulting VAT due/repayable, using the standard **9-box** structure: **Box 1** VAT due on sales (output tax); **Box 2** VAT due on acquisitions from EU member states; **Box 3** total VAT due (Box 1 + Box 2); **Box 4** VAT reclaimed on purchases (input tax); **Box 5** net VAT to pay or reclaim (the difference between Box 3 and Box 4); **Box 6** total value of sales excluding VAT; **Box 7** total value of purchases excluding VAT; **Box 8** and **Box 9** total value of goods supplied to/acquired from EU member states. Returns are submitted under **Making Tax Digital (MTD)**, using compatible software, keeping digital records. The submission and payment **deadline** is normally **one calendar month and 7 days** after the end of the VAT period. **Records that must be kept** for VAT purposes include VAT invoices issued and received, credit notes, and a VAT account summarising output and input tax -- normally for at least 6 years. **Non-compliance** (late submission, late payment, errors) can lead to: **interest** on VAT paid late, **penalties** (for late submission and/or late payment, and for careless or deliberate errors), and, for serious or repeated non-compliance, an **HMRC enquiry** into the business's wider VAT affairs.
