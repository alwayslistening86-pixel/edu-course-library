# S11_FA_Inventory_and_Period_End_Judgement - Lesson: Recording inventory under IAS 2, and the judgement needed for period end adjustments

## Goal
The learner records inventory under IAS 2 (lower of cost and net realisable value, item by item), and explains the professional judgement needed at period end.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA5.3 - Recording inventory under IAS 2: valuing at the lower of cost and net realisable value on an item-by-item basis, and the journal for closing inventory
- FA5.4 - Considerations when recording period end adjustments: their effect on reported results, and responding appropriately to time and reporting pressure

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if an item cost £500 to make but can now only be sold for £350, at what value should it appear in inventory? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### FA5.3 Recording inventory under IAS 2: valuing at the lower of cost and net realisable value on an item-by-item basis, and the journal for closing inventory
Accounting for inventory applies the **accruals basis** -- unsold inventory at the period end represents cost not yet matched against the income it will generate, so it is carried forward as an asset rather than expensed. Under **IAS 2**, inventory is valued at the **lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV)**, applied on an **item-by-item** (not total) basis -- so if some items are below cost and others above, the below-cost items are written down individually while the others stay at cost; they cannot be netted off against each other. **Cost** can include purchase price, import duties and directly attributable costs of bringing inventory to its present location/condition (e.g. carriage inwards), but excludes selling costs. Valuing inventory correctly matters because the closing inventory figure directly affects the period's **cost of sales** and therefore **gross/net profit** -- an overstated closing inventory overstates profit (and vice versa). Accounting software can automate recording, tracking and valuing inventory (e.g. under a perpetual FIFO/AVCO system); the closing inventory figure is entered by a **journal entry**: debit inventory (statement of financial position, an asset) and credit the closing inventory adjustment within cost of sales (increasing profit, since it recognises the value carried forward rather than expensed). *Worked example:* three inventory lines at the year end: Item A cost £5,000, NRV £6,200 -- valued at the lower, cost £5,000; Item B cost £3,200, NRV £2,800 -- valued at the lower, NRV £2,800; Item C cost £4,500, NRV £4,500 -- valued at £4,500 either way. Total closing inventory = £5,000 + £2,800 + £4,500 = **£12,300**.

#### FA5.4 Considerations when recording period end adjustments: their effect on reported results, and responding appropriately to time and reporting pressure
Period end adjustments (accruals, prepayments, depreciation, inventory, irrecoverable debts and allowances) can **significantly affect** the reported profit and asset values, since several involve estimates and judgement rather than a single objectively correct figure. Accounting software relies on **accurate date entry** to allocate transactions to the right period. Applying **professional scepticism, integrity and objectivity** matters especially here, because these judgement-heavy adjustments are exactly where profit could be manipulated -- e.g. understating an accrual, overstating inventory NRV, or under-providing an allowance for doubtful receivables, all overstate profit. The accountant should be alert to, and resist, **period end pressures**: time pressure to close the books quickly (risking rushed, poor-quality judgements), pressure to report a more favourable result than the evidence supports, or pressure from someone in authority to make a particular adjustment -- responding by applying the evidence and accounting standards objectively, documenting judgements made, and escalating or declining if pressured to misstate the figures.
