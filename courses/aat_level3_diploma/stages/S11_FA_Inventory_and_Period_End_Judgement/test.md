# S11_FA_Inventory_and_Period_End_Judgement - Test: Recording inventory under IAS 2, and the judgement needed for period end adjustments

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry and financial-statement questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Three inventory items at the year end: Item X cost £2,000, NRV £1,750; Item Y cost £3,400, NRV £3,900; Item Z cost £1,100, NRV £1,100. Calculate the total closing inventory value. [3 marks]
2. Explain the effect on profit of overstating closing inventory, and why this makes inventory valuation a judgement area accountants should apply professional scepticism to. [3 marks]
3. Give one example of a period end pressure an accountant might face, and explain the appropriate response. [2 marks]
4. A closing inventory journal entry debits inventory (an asset). What is credited? Choose every correct option.
   A. The closing inventory adjustment within cost of sales
   B. Accumulated depreciation
   C. The VAT control account
   D. The disposals account

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 Item X valued at lower, NRV £1,750; M1 Item Y valued at lower, cost £3,400; Item Z £1,100 either way; A1 total = 1,750 + 3,400 + 1,100 = £6,250.
2. [3] B1 overstated closing inventory reduces cost of sales (more is 'carried forward' rather than expensed), which overstates gross profit and net profit for the period; B1 inventory valuation involves judgement (estimating NRV, identifying obsolete/damaged stock), which creates scope for manipulation; B1 professional scepticism means critically assessing the evidence for inventory values rather than accepting management's figures at face value.
3. [2] B1 example, e.g. time pressure to close the books quickly, pressure to report a more favourable result, or pressure from a person in authority to make a particular adjustment; B1 appropriate response: apply the evidence and accounting standards objectively, document the judgement made, and escalate or decline rather than misstate the figures.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
