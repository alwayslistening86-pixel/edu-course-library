# S07_FA_Advanced_Double_Entry_and_Ledgers - Lesson: The accounting equation, ledger classification, books of prime entry and period end routines

## Goal
The learner uses the accounting equation, classifies ledger accounts, explains books of prime entry and ledger accounting including control accounts, and carries out period end routines.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA2.1 - The accounting equation: its importance, the effect of transactions on capital/assets/liabilities, and calculating any element from the other two
- FA2.2 - Classifying ledger accounts: non-current and current assets (tangible/intangible), non-current and current liabilities, equity, income and expenses
- FA2.3 - Books of prime entry and ledger accounting: the daybooks, the general ledger vs memorandum ledgers, and control accounts (receivables, payables, wages and salaries, VAT)
- FA2.4 - Financial period end routines: balancing off accounts by classification, verifying balances, and determining whether transactions are genuine and valid

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if a business's assets are £62,000 and its liabilities are £19,000, what is its capital, and which figure changed if it then takes out a further £5,000 bank loan? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### FA2.1 The accounting equation: its importance, the effect of transactions on capital/assets/liabilities, and calculating any element from the other two
The **accounting equation** -- Assets = Liabilities + Equity (capital) -- underpins double-entry bookkeeping: every transaction keeps it in balance. It matters because it is the structural basis for both the statement of financial position and for checking that double entry has been correctly applied. Any one element can be calculated from the other two: Equity = Assets - Liabilities; Assets = Liabilities + Equity; Liabilities = Assets - Equity. Every transaction affects at least two elements, keeping the equation balanced -- e.g. taking out a bank loan increases an asset (bank) and increases a liability (the loan) by the same amount; buying inventory for cash simply moves value between two assets (bank down, inventory up), leaving total assets, and the equation, unchanged. *Worked example:* a business has assets of £62,000 and liabilities of £19,000; its equity is £62,000 - £19,000 = **£43,000**. If it then takes out a £5,000 bank loan, assets rise to £67,000 (more cash) and liabilities rise to £24,000 (the loan); equity is unaffected, and £67,000 - £24,000 = £43,000 still.

#### FA2.2 Classifying ledger accounts: non-current and current assets (tangible/intangible), non-current and current liabilities, equity, income and expenses
Ledger accounts are classified as: **non-current assets** -- **tangible** (physical, e.g. property, plant, equipment, held for more than one accounting period) and **intangible** (no physical substance, e.g. goodwill, purchased software licences, held for more than one period); **current assets** (expected to be realised/used within one period, e.g. inventory, receivables, bank, cash); **non-current liabilities** (due for settlement more than one period ahead, e.g. a long-term bank loan); **current liabilities** (due within one period, e.g. payables, a bank overdraft, tax payable); **equity/capital** (the owner's residual interest); **income** (amounts earned, e.g. sales revenue, other income); **expenses** (amounts incurred running the business, e.g. rent, wages, depreciation). Correctly classifying an account matters for correctly preparing the financial statements -- a non-current asset misclassified as a current asset, for example, distorts working-capital ratios.

#### FA2.3 Books of prime entry and ledger accounting: the daybooks, the general ledger vs memorandum ledgers, and control accounts (receivables, payables, wages and salaries, VAT)
**Books of prime entry** (daybooks) are where transactions are first recorded from source documents before summarising into the ledgers: the **sales daybook**, **sales returns daybook**, **purchases daybook**, **purchases returns daybook**, **discounts allowed daybook**, **discounts received daybook**, the **cash book** and the **journal** (used for transactions with no other daybook, e.g. depreciation, correcting errors, opening entries). Each daybook records the figures already calculated on the source document (net, VAT, total), including the correct **VAT treatment** of each transaction. The **general ledger** holds the double-entry accounts for every asset, liability, income, expense and equity item, including the **control accounts**: the **receivables ledger control account** and **payables ledger control account** (summarising, in total, everything owed by/to all credit customers/suppliers), a **wages and salaries control account** (summarising gross pay, deductions and net pay for payroll postings) and the **VAT control account** (summarising output and input VAT). **Memorandum (subsidiary) ledgers** -- the individual receivables and payables ledgers -- hold one account per customer/supplier for reference and credit control, but are *not* part of the double-entry system; only the control accounts are. Accounting software usually automates the transfer of daybook totals into the control accounts.

#### FA2.4 Financial period end routines: balancing off accounts by classification, verifying balances, and determining whether transactions are genuine and valid
**Period end routines**: ledger accounts are balanced off differently depending on their classification -- income and expense accounts are closed off to the statement of profit or loss for the period (their balances do not carry forward); asset, liability and capital accounts are balanced off and the balance is **carried down** to start the next period. Accounting software usually automates this period end routine. Before relying on the figures, general ledger balances should be **verified** against relevant sources and reconciliations -- e.g. the bank account against the bank statement, the receivables control account against the receivables ledger. The accountant should also consider whether transactions recorded are **genuine and valid** for inclusion (e.g. not duplicated, not recorded in the wrong period, properly authorised) before the period is closed off.
