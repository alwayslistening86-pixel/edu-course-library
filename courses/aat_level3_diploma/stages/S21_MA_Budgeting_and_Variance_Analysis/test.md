## Test items
1. A fixed budget for 800 units shows variable cost £15/unit and fixed cost £6,000. Flex this budget to an actual output of 950 units, giving the flexed total cost. [3 marks]
2. Standard labour cost is 4 hours/unit at £11.00/hour. Actual production was 600 units, using 2,500 hours at a total cost of £26,250. Calculate the labour rate variance and the labour efficiency variance. [5 marks]
3. Standard selling price is £30/unit; budgeted sales are 1,000 units; standard profit is £6/unit. Actual sales were 1,050 units at an average price of £29.20/unit. Calculate the sales price variance and the sales volume variance. [4 marks]
4. A favourable material usage variance means: Choose every correct option.
   A. actual material used was less than the standard quantity allowed for the output achieved
   B. actual material used was more than the standard quantity allowed
   C. the material price paid was lower than standard
   D. the material price paid was higher than standard
5. Give one possible reason for an adverse labour efficiency variance, and one possible reason for a favourable material price variance. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 flexed variable cost = 950 x 15 = £14,250; M1 fixed cost unchanged = £6,000; A1 flexed total = £20,250.
2. [5] M1 actual rate = 26,250/2,500 = £10.50/hr; M1 rate variance = (11.00-10.50) x 2,500 = £1,250 favourable; M1 standard hours for actual output = 600 x 4 = 2,400 hours; M1 efficiency variance = (2,400-2,500) x 11.00 = £1,100 adverse; A1 both variances correctly labelled adverse/favourable with correct figures.
3. [4] M1 sales price variance = (29.20-30.00) x 1,050 = £840 adverse; M1 sales volume variance = (1,050-1,000) x 6.00 = £300 favourable; A1 both variances correctly calculated; B1 both correctly labelled adverse/favourable.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [2] B1 adverse labour efficiency, e.g. poorly trained/inexperienced staff, machine breakdowns, poor quality materials slowing work; B1 favourable material price, e.g. a bulk-purchase discount negotiated, or a fall in market prices.
