#### FA7.1 The sole trader's statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position: purpose, terminology and preparation (net assets presentation)
The **statement of profit or loss** shows a business's income and expenses over a period, arriving at the profit or loss for that period; the **statement of financial position** shows its assets, liabilities and capital at a single point in time. The two are linked through the **accounting equation**: the period's profit (or loss) increases (or decreases) capital, which is why the statement of profit or loss's bottom line feeds directly into the statement of financial position. Key terminology: **sales revenue** (income from the business's main trading activity), **net purchases** (purchases less purchases returns and any discount received treated as a reduction), **cost of sales** (opening inventory + net purchases - closing inventory -- the cost of the goods actually sold in the period). The statement of financial position is usually presented using the **net assets** format: total assets less total liabilities, which should equal total capital (the accounting equation rearranged). *Worked example:* Revenue £220,000; opening inventory £18,000, purchases £142,000, closing inventory £24,000, so cost of sales = 18,000 + 142,000 - 24,000 = **£136,000**; gross profit = 220,000 - 136,000 = **£84,000**. Expenses: rent £12,000, wages £38,000, other expenses £9,000, total £59,000; net profit = 84,000 - 59,000 = **£25,000**. Statement of financial position: non-current assets (carrying amount) £95,000; current assets: inventory £24,000, receivables £31,000, bank £6,000 = £61,000; total assets £156,000; current liabilities: payables £22,000; **net assets** = 156,000 - 22,000 = **£134,000**, which should equal closing capital.

#### FA7.2 The sole trader's capital account: accounting for drawings (cash, goods, services), capital injections and the period's profit or loss
The sole trader's **capital account** explains the movement in capital during the period: it starts with **opening capital**, adds any **capital introduced** (further funds/assets the owner puts into the business) and the period's **profit** (or deducts a **loss**), and deducts **drawings** -- cash, goods or services the owner has taken out of the business for personal use (goods taken for own use are valued at their cost to the business, not their selling price, and removed from purchases/cost of sales as well as being recorded as drawings). *Worked example:* opening capital £45,000; profit for the year £18,500; capital introduced £2,000; drawings: cash £9,000, goods taken for own use (at cost) £600. Closing capital = 45,000 + 18,500 + 2,000 - 9,000 - 600 = **£55,900**.
