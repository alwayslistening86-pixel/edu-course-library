# S13_FA_Sole_Trader_Financial_Statements - Test: Preparing the sole trader's statement of profit or loss, statement of financial position and capital account

## Test items
1. Revenue £96,000; opening inventory £6,000, purchases £58,000, closing inventory £7,500; total expenses (excluding cost of sales) £21,000. Calculate cost of sales, gross profit and net profit. [4 marks]
2. Non-current assets (carrying amount) £68,000; current assets: inventory £7,500, receivables £14,200, bank £3,100; current liabilities: payables £9,800. Calculate net assets. [3 marks]
3. Opening capital £52,000, capital introduced £4,000, profit for the year £19,300, drawings (cash) £11,000, drawings (goods at cost) £750. Calculate the closing capital. [3 marks]
4. On a sole trader's statement of financial position, net assets should equal which figure? Choose every correct option.
   A. Closing capital
   B. Gross profit
   C. Cost of sales
   D. Opening capital
5. Explain why goods taken by the owner for personal use are recorded as drawings at their cost, not their normal selling price. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 cost of sales = 6,000 + 58,000 - 7,500 = £56,500; A1 gross profit = 96,000 - 56,500 = £39,500; M1 net profit = 39,500 - 21,000; A1 = £18,500.
2. [3] M1 total assets = 68,000 + 7,500 + 14,200 + 3,100 = £92,800; M1 net assets = 92,800 - 9,800; A1 = £83,000.
3. [3] M1 52,000 + 4,000 + 19,300; M1 - 11,000 - 750; A1 = £63,550.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [2] B1 drawings reduce the business's resources by the value the business actually gave up, which is the cost of the goods, not the profit it would have made by selling them; B1 the cost is also removed from purchases/cost of sales, since those goods were not actually sold to a customer.
