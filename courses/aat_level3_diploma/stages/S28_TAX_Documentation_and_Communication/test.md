# S28_TAX_Documentation_and_Communication - Test: Using tax documentation and legislation, and communicating VAT/tax information

## How to run this

Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why applying tax legislation to a specific situation requires more than simply finding a rule that looks similar. [2 marks]
2. Explain the difference in communication style needed when reporting tax information to HMRC compared with explaining it to a client. [3 marks]
3. Which best explains why UK tax information should be rechecked regularly rather than relied on indefinitely? Choose every correct option.
   A. UK tax rates, thresholds and rules change regularly, at least annually
   B. UK tax law never changes once set
   C. Only VAT law changes; income tax law is fixed
   D. HMRC guidance is only updated once a decade
4. Give one consequence of communicating tax information to a client inaccurately, and one consequence of communicating it to HMRC late. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 tax rules can be very specific to particular facts (e.g. the exact type of supply, or a business's particular circumstances); B1 a rule that looks superficially similar but does not actually match the facts could give the wrong answer, so the specific circumstances must be checked against the actual rule, not just a general impression of it.
2. [3] B1 to HMRC: must follow HMRC's required format/channel (e.g. MTD-compatible software) and meet statutory deadlines, in the technical form required; B1 to a client: plain, accessible language suited to their level of tax knowledge; B1 both require accuracy, but the appropriate format and level of technical detail differ by audience.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 inaccurate communication to a client, e.g. the client makes a decision based on wrong information, or a return is filed incorrectly leading to penalties; B1 late communication to HMRC, e.g. a missed filing deadline creates a compliance failure (interest/penalties) regardless of how accurate the content eventually submitted is.
