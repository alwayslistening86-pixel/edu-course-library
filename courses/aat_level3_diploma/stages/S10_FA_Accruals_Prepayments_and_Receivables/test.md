# S10_FA_Accruals_Prepayments_and_Receivables - Test: Accruals, prepayments, irrecoverable debts and allowances for doubtful receivables

## Test items
1. A business's telephone bill of £750 for the quarter to 31 October was the last bill received before the 31 December year end. Calculate the accrual required, assuming the same monthly usage. [3 marks]
2. A business writes off a specific irrecoverable debt of £3,500. Receivables were £120,000 before the write-off, and a general allowance of 2.5% is then required on the remaining balance (opening allowance was £2,000). Calculate (a) the remaining receivables after the write-off, (b) the new allowance required, and (c) the total charge to profit this period. [5 marks]
3. A payment made in December covering January to March next year is an example of which adjustment, from this period's point of view? Choose every correct option.
   A. A prepayment
   B. An accrual
   C. An irrecoverable debt
   D. A general allowance
4. Explain the difference between a specific allowance and a general allowance for doubtful receivables. [2 marks]
5. Explain why only the change in the allowance for doubtful receivables, not its full closing balance, is charged to profit each period. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 monthly cost = 750/3 = £250; M1 2 further months (Nov, Dec) have been used but not billed; A1 accrual = 250 x 2 = £500.
2. [5] M1 (a) remaining receivables = 120,000 - 3,500 = £116,500; M1 (b) new allowance = 116,500 x 2.5% = £2,912.50; M1 increase in allowance = 2,912.50 - 2,000 = £912.50; A1 (c) total charge to profit = irrecoverable debts expense £3,500 + increase in allowance £912.50 = £4,412.50; B1 correct treatment: the write-off and the allowance are two separate, additive charges to profit.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 a specific allowance targets named, individually identified doubtful balances; B1 a general allowance applies a percentage to the remaining (not specifically doubtful) receivables balance as a broad estimate of risk.
5. [2] B1 the allowance is already partly provided for from prior periods (the opening balance); B1 charging the full closing balance again would double-count the amount already recognised in earlier periods -- only the increase (or decrease) this period represents this period's new charge (or credit).
