# S15_FA_Profitability_Ratios - Test: Calculating and interpreting profitability ratios

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry and financial-statement questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Revenue £240,000, gross profit £84,000, net profit £36,000. Calculate the gross profit margin and the net profit margin. [4 marks]
2. Net profit £45,000, total equity £180,000, non-current liabilities (long-term loan) £70,000. Calculate the ROCE. [3 marks]
3. A business's gross profit margin has fallen sharply while revenue has stayed the same. Which is the most likely explanation? Choose every correct option.
   A. Cost of sales has risen relative to revenue
   B. Non-operating expenses such as rent have risen
   C. Revenue has increased
   D. Capital employed has fallen
4. Explain two reasons a profitability ratio might change from one period to the next, other than a genuine change in trading performance. [2 marks]
5. Explain why comparing a business's ROCE to another business's ROCE should be done with caution. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 gross margin = 84,000/240,000 x 100; A1 = 35%; M1 net margin = 36,000/240,000 x 100; A1 = 15%.
2. [3] M1 capital employed = 180,000 + 70,000 = £250,000; M1 ROCE = 45,000/250,000 x 100; A1 = 18%.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 a change in accounting policy (e.g. a different depreciation or inventory valuation method), breaking consistency between periods; B1 a one-off or unusual item distorting a single period's figures.
5. [2] B1 comparability is weaker if the two businesses use different accounting policies, are of different sizes, or operate in different industries; B1 professional scepticism is needed -- a single ratio comparison in isolation, without considering this wider context, can mislead.
