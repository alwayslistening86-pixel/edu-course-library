# S25_TAX_UK_Tax_System_and_VAT_Principles - Test: The UK tax system and the principles of VAT

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry and financial-statement questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. State two of the main UK taxes and, for each, what it is charged on. [2 marks]
2. Explain HMRC's role in the UK tax system. [2 marks]
3. Which best describes the difference between primary and secondary tax legislation? Choose every correct option.
   A. Primary legislation is an Act of Parliament; secondary legislation is detailed regulations made under powers an Act grants
   B. Primary legislation only applies to VAT; secondary legislation only applies to income tax
   C. There is no meaningful difference between them
   D. Secondary legislation always overrides primary legislation
4. Explain why tax evasion is considered more serious than aggressive tax avoidance, even though both reduce the tax collected. [2 marks]
5. [TAX2.1] State the current UK standard rate of VAT, and give one example of a zero-rated supply. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 income tax (individuals' income); B1 corporation tax (companies' profits); B1 NIC (funds state benefits/pensions); B1 VAT (consumer spending); B1 capital gains tax (gains on disposing of assets) -- any 2 for full marks.
2. [2] B1 HMRC administers and collects UK taxes, and publishes guidance on how tax law applies; B1 HMRC also investigates non-compliance and enforces tax law (e.g. through penalties, enquiries).
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 evasion is illegal -- deliberately not paying tax that is legally due (e.g. concealing income) -- and is a criminal offence; B1 avoidance stays within the letter of the law, even if it exploits its intent, so while HMRC can still challenge and reverse it, it is not itself a criminal offence in the way evasion is.
5. [2] B1 standard rate 20%; B1 any reasonable zero-rated example, e.g. most food, children's clothing, books.
