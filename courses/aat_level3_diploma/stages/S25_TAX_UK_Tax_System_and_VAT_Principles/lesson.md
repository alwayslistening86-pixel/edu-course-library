## How to teach this
Ask the learner: what is the difference between a zero-rated supply and an exempt supply, if both mean no VAT is actually charged? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### TAX1.1 The UK tax system: the main taxes and their purposes, HMRC's role, how tax legislation is made and changed, and the impact of avoidance vs evasion
The UK has several main taxes, each serving a different purpose: **income tax** (on individuals' income), **corporation tax** (on companies' profits), **National Insurance contributions (NIC)** (funding state benefits/pensions, paid by employees, employers and the self-employed), **VAT** (a tax on consumer spending, collected by VAT-registered businesses on HMRC's behalf), and **capital gains tax** (on gains made disposing of assets). **HM Revenue and Customs (HMRC)** administers and collects UK taxes, sets and publishes guidance, investigates non-compliance, and enforces tax law. UK tax law is made through **primary legislation** (Acts of Parliament, chiefly an annual **Finance Act**, which enacts each year's Budget tax changes) and **secondary legislation** (detailed regulations made under powers granted by primary legislation, without needing a full new Act). **Tax avoidance** (legally arranging affairs to minimise tax, but in a way that exploits the law's intent rather than genuinely reducing economic activity) and **tax evasion** (illegally not paying tax that is legally due, e.g. concealing income) both reduce the tax collected to fund public services, though only evasion is a criminal offence -- aggressive avoidance can still be challenged and reversed by HMRC, and damages public trust in the fairness of the tax system. Accountants preparing tax information owe a duty of **confidentiality** to their client/employer regarding the tax information they handle, subject to the legal exceptions covered in Business Awareness (money laundering disclosure, S03).

#### TAX2.1 VAT principles: VAT as a consumption tax, taxable/exempt/zero-rated/outside-the-scope supplies, and the current VAT rates
**VAT** is a **consumption tax** -- ultimately borne by the final consumer, but collected and paid over to HMRC at each stage of the supply chain by VAT-registered businesses. Supplies fall into categories: **taxable supplies** (standard-rated, reduced-rated or zero-rated -- VAT applies, even if the rate charged is 0%, and input VAT on related costs can generally be reclaimed); **exempt supplies** (outside the VAT system entirely -- no output VAT is charged, but input VAT on related costs generally *cannot* be reclaimed either, e.g. most financial services, insurance, education); **outside-the-scope supplies** (not supplies of goods/services for VAT purposes at all, e.g. wages paid to employees, or transactions outside the UK's VAT jurisdiction). The **current VAT rates** are: **standard rate 20%** (most goods/services), **reduced rate 5%** (e.g. domestic fuel/power) and **zero rate 0%** (e.g. most food, children's clothing, books -- still a taxable supply, just charged at 0%). The type of supply directly determines the VAT treatment: whether output VAT is charged at all (and at what rate), and whether related input VAT can be recovered.
