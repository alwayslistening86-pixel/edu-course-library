## Test items
1. Explain the difference between a product cost and a period cost, giving one example of each. [3 marks]
2. A machine operator's wages, directly traceable to the units they produce, are an example of which type of cost? Choose every correct option.
   A. A direct cost
   B. An indirect cost
   C. A fixed cost only
   D. A period cost only
3. A delivery van's running costs include a fixed annual insurance premium plus fuel that varies with mileage driven. Classify this cost by behaviour, and explain why. [2 marks]
4. Explain why accurate cost information matters for a business, giving one example of a poor decision that could follow from inaccurate costing. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 a product cost is included in the cost of the product and only affects profit when the product is sold, e.g. direct materials or direct labour; B1 a period cost is expensed in the period incurred regardless of sales, e.g. selling and administrative expenses; B1 correct examples given for each.
2. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
3. [2] B1 a semi-variable cost; B1 because it has both a fixed element (insurance, unaffected by mileage) and a variable element (fuel, which rises with mileage/activity).
4. [2] B1 inaccurate cost information can lead to poor pricing, production or investment decisions since management relies on it for planning, control and decision-making; B1 example, e.g. underpricing a product because its true cost was understated, or continuing to produce an unprofitable product because its cost was understated.
