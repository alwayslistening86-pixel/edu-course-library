#### MA1.1 The purpose of costing, budgeting and internal reporting, and the importance of providing accurate information
**Costing** determines how much it costs to produce a product or deliver a service; **budgeting** plans income and expenditure for a future period; **internal reporting** provides management with the information they need to plan, control and make decisions -- none of which the general-purpose financial statements built for external users (Financial Accounting: Preparing Financial Statements) are detailed or timely enough for. Management accounting information does not have to follow external financial reporting standards, since it is used only inside the organisation, but it must still be **accurate** -- inaccurate costing information can lead directly to the wrong price being set, an unprofitable product being kept in production, or a budget that fails to control spending, so the same rigour that applies to external reporting still matters internally.

#### MA2.1 Classifying costs: fixed, variable and semi-variable by behaviour; direct and indirect by element; product and period costs
Costs are classified by **behaviour**: a **fixed cost** stays the same in total regardless of activity level within a relevant range (e.g. rent); a **variable cost** changes in direct proportion to activity level (e.g. raw materials, £ per unit produced); a **semi-variable cost** has both a fixed and a variable element (e.g. a phone bill with a fixed line rental plus a per-minute charge). Costs are also classified by **element**: **direct costs** can be traced specifically to a single cost unit (e.g. the materials that go into one particular product); **indirect costs** (overheads) cannot be traced to a single cost unit and are shared across many (e.g. factory rent, supervisors' salaries). Finally, costs are classified by **timing**: a **product cost** is included in the cost of the product itself and only hits profit when that product is sold (e.g. direct materials, direct labour, production overhead); a **period cost** is expensed in the period it is incurred regardless of what is sold (e.g. selling and administrative expenses). *Worked example:* factory rent (£3,000/month regardless of output) is a **fixed**, **indirect**, **period** cost; raw material used in a product (£4/unit produced) is a **variable**, **direct**, **product** cost.
