# S06_FA_Principles_and_Qualities_of_Information - Lesson: The framework of accounting and the qualities of useful financial information

## Goal
The learner names the primary users of final accounts, explains the accounting principles underlying final accounts preparation, and explains the qualities of useful financial information.

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA1.1 - The primary users of final accounts: existing and potential investors, lenders, and other creditors, and how each uses the accounts
- FA1.2 - The framework of accounting: the accruals, going concern, business entity, materiality, consistency, prudence and money measurement principles
- FA1.3 - Qualities of useful financial information: the fundamental characteristics (relevance, faithful representation) and enhancing characteristics (comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability)

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: why would a bank considering a large loan to a business want to see several years of accounts, not just the most recent one? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### FA1.1 The primary users of final accounts: existing and potential investors, lenders, and other creditors, and how each uses the accounts
The **primary users** of a business's final accounts (per the IFRS Conceptual Framework): **existing and potential investors** (want to assess the return they are getting or might get, and the risk involved, to decide whether to hold, buy or sell their investment), **lenders** (want to assess whether the business can repay a loan with interest, i.e. its liquidity and solvency, before lending or continuing to lend) and **other creditors** (e.g. trade suppliers -- want to assess whether they will be paid on time for goods/services already supplied on credit). All three use the same general purpose financial statements to make resource-allocation decisions -- unlike internal management, who can request bespoke internal reports, these external users rely on what the business chooses (or is required) to publish.

#### FA1.2 The framework of accounting: the accruals, going concern, business entity, materiality, consistency, prudence and money measurement principles
The **framework of accounting principles** underlying final accounts preparation: the **accruals** principle -- income and expenses are recognised when earned/incurred, not when cash is received/paid; **going concern** -- accounts are prepared on the assumption the business will continue operating for the foreseeable future (unless there is evidence otherwise, which would require a different basis); **business entity** -- the business's transactions are recorded separately from the owner's personal transactions, even for a sole trader with unlimited liability; **materiality** -- an item is material if its omission or misstatement could influence a user's decisions, so what counts as material depends on its size and nature relative to the business; **consistency** -- the same accounting policies should be applied period to period, so figures are comparable; **prudence** -- exercising caution when making judgements under uncertainty, so that assets/income are not overstated and liabilities/expenses are not understated; **money measurement** -- only items that can be reliably measured in monetary terms are recorded (so, e.g., staff morale is not recorded, however important).

#### FA1.3 Qualities of useful financial information: the fundamental characteristics (relevance, faithful representation) and enhancing characteristics (comparability, verifiability, timeliness, understandability)
Two **fundamental** qualitative characteristics make information useful: **relevance** (capable of making a difference to a user's decisions -- has predictive and/or confirmatory value) and **faithful representation** (complete, neutral and free from error -- it depicts what it purports to depict). Four **enhancing** characteristics increase the usefulness of information that is already relevant and faithfully represented: **comparability** (users can identify similarities/differences between periods or businesses), **verifiability** (different knowledgeable, independent observers could reach consensus that the information is faithfully represented), **timeliness** (available to users in time to influence their decisions) and **understandability** (clearly and concisely presented). Financial statements must also be **free from material misstatement** -- and preparing them relies on the accountant's **fundamental ethical principles** and **professional scepticism**, since figures involving judgement (e.g. depreciation estimates, allowances) can otherwise be manipulated to mislead users.
