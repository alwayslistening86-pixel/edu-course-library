# S26_TAX_VAT_Registration_and_Deregistration - Test: VAT registration and deregistration

Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain the difference between the VAT registration threshold and the deregistration threshold, and why they are set at different figures. [3 marks]
2. Explain why a business's taxable turnover, rather than its total turnover, is what is tested against the VAT registration threshold. [2 marks]
3. A business deregisters from VAT. What is a direct implication of this? Choose every correct option.
   A. It can no longer reclaim input VAT on its purchases
   B. It must now charge VAT at a higher rate
   C. It becomes exempt from all UK taxes
   D. It must immediately cease trading
4. A café's taxable turnover for the 12 months to the end of last month was £91,500, having been below £90,000 the month before. Explain what the café must now do, and by when. [3 marks]
5. A small consultancy has taxable turnover of £60,000, well below the registration threshold, but most of its clients are VAT-registered businesses. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of voluntarily registering for VAT. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 registration threshold is £90,000 -- taxable turnover exceeding this in a rolling 12-month period (or expected to in the next 30 days) triggers mandatory registration; B1 deregistration threshold is £88,000 -- a lower figure, below which a business may deregister; B1 the £2,000 gap is deliberate, to prevent a business 'yo-yoing' in and out of registration as turnover fluctuates narrowly around a single figure.
2. [2] B1 taxable turnover means the value of taxable (standard/reduced/zero-rated) supplies only; B1 it excludes exempt and outside-the-scope supplies, which are never subject to VAT in the first place, so including them would overstate the turnover relevant to the registration decision.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] B1 the café has exceeded the VAT registration threshold of £90,000 in a rolling 12-month period, so it must register for VAT; B1 it must notify HMRC within 30 days of the end of the month in which the threshold was exceeded; B1 HMRC will then set an effective registration date, from which output VAT must be charged.
5. [2] B1 advantage, e.g. it can reclaim input VAT on its own costs, and can issue VAT invoices its VAT-registered clients expect/prefer; B1 disadvantage, e.g. it must now charge VAT on its fees, which could make it more expensive to any non-VAT-registered clients, plus the extra administrative burden of VAT returns.
