# S26_TAX_VAT_Registration_and_Deregistration - Lesson: VAT registration and deregistration

## Goal
The learner explains VAT registration and deregistration, including the thresholds, mandatory vs voluntary registration, and the implications of deregistering.

## Syllabus items taught here
- TAX2.2 - VAT registration and deregistration: the registration threshold, mandatory vs voluntary registration, the deregistration threshold and its VAT implications

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: a business's taxable turnover for the past 12 months has just reached £91,000. What must it now do, and by when? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### TAX2.2 VAT registration and deregistration: the registration threshold, mandatory vs voluntary registration, the deregistration threshold and its VAT implications
A business must **register for VAT** (mandatory registration) if its **taxable turnover** (the total value of taxable supplies, excluding exempt/outside-the-scope items) exceeds the **registration threshold of £90,000** in any rolling 12-month period (not a fixed tax year -- checked at the end of every month, looking back over the previous 12 months), or if it expects taxable turnover to exceed £90,000 in the *next* 30 days alone. **Voluntary registration** (turnover below the threshold) may still be beneficial -- e.g. to reclaim input VAT on costs, or to appear more credible to VAT-registered customers who expect a VAT invoice -- though it also means charging VAT on sales, which can make prices less competitive to non-VAT-registered customers. The **registration process** requires notifying HMRC within 30 days of exceeding the threshold (or by the date it becomes clear the 30-day test is met), with registration then effective from a set date HMRC specifies. **Deregistration** is possible where taxable turnover falls below the **deregistration threshold of £88,000** (a deliberately lower figure than the £90,000 registration threshold, to avoid businesses "yo-yoing" in and out of registration around a single figure), and may also be required where the business stops making taxable supplies altogether (e.g. ceases trading). The main **implication of deregistering** is that the business can no longer charge output VAT on its sales (which may make it more price-competitive to non-VAT-registered customers) but also can no longer reclaim input VAT on its purchases -- and it must account for VAT on the value of any business assets still held on which input VAT was previously reclaimed, above a de minimis amount.
