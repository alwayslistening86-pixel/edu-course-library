# S09_FA_Depreciation - Test: Calculating and recording depreciation: straight-line and diminishing balance

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry and financial-statement questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A machine costs £54,000 with no residual value, useful life 9 years, straight-line. It is bought on 1 October, with a 31 March year end (6 months owned in year 1). Calculate the depreciation charge for year 1. [3 marks]
2. An asset costs £32,000 and is depreciated at 20% reducing balance. Calculate the depreciation charge for each of years 1 and 2, and the carrying amount at the end of year 2. [4 marks]
3. Which method of depreciation charges the same amount every full year an asset is owned? Choose every correct option.
   A. Straight-line
   B. Diminishing (reducing) balance
   C. Both charge the same amount every year
   D. Neither
4. State the journal entry (account debited and account credited) to record a period's depreciation charge. [2 marks]
5. Explain why the non-current asset register should be reconciled to the general ledger, giving one example of a discrepancy this might catch. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 annual depreciation = 54,000/9 = £6,000; M1 pro-rata for 6 months = 6,000 x 6/12; A1 = £3,000.
2. [4] M1 year 1 depreciation = 32,000 x 20% = £6,400 (carrying amount £25,600); M1 year 2 depreciation = 25,600 x 20% = £5,120; A1 carrying amount at end of year 2 = 25,600 - 5,120 = £20,480; B1 correctly using the reduced (not original) carrying amount as the base in year 2.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 debit depreciation expense (statement of profit or loss); B1 credit accumulated depreciation.
5. [2] B1 to check the two sources agree and catch errors, since each is maintained/updated separately; B1 example, e.g. an asset disposed of but not removed from the register, or a depreciation journal entry not posted to the general ledger.
