# S09_FA_Depreciation - Lesson: Calculating and recording depreciation: straight-line and diminishing balance

## Goal
The learner calculates depreciation using the straight-line method (with/without residual value, full year or pro-rata) and the diminishing balance method, and records it in the non-current asset register and general ledger.

## Syllabus items taught here

#### FA4.1 Calculating depreciation: the straight-line method (with/without residual value, full year or pro-rata) and the diminishing (reducing) balance method
Depreciation applies the **accruals** principle -- spreading an asset's **depreciable amount** (cost less residual value) over its useful life, matching the cost of using the asset against the income it helps generate, rather than expensing the whole cost when purchased. **Straight-line depreciation** charges an equal amount each full year: Depreciable amount / Useful life -- or, if given as a percentage, Cost (or depreciable amount) x the percentage. If an asset is owned for only part of a year (e.g. bought partway through the accounting period), depreciation is charged **pro-rata** for the number of months owned. *Worked example (with residual value):* cost £30,000, residual value £2,000, useful life 4 years: annual depreciation = (£30,000 - £2,000) / 4 = **£7,000**. *Worked example (pro-rata):* an asset with an annual straight-line charge of £8,000 is bought on 1 July, with a 31 December year end (6 months owned that year): depreciation for that year = £8,000 x 6/12 = **£4,000**. **Diminishing (reducing) balance depreciation** charges a fixed percentage of the asset's **carrying amount** (not its original cost) each year, so the charge falls over time. *Worked example:* cost £25,000, 20% reducing balance: Year 1 depreciation = £25,000 x 20% = **£5,000** (carrying amount now £20,000); Year 2 depreciation = £20,000 x 20% = **£4,000** (carrying amount now £16,000). Accounting software or a spreadsheet often calculates depreciation automatically, with the figure then journaled into the general ledger.

#### FA4.2 Recording depreciation: in the non-current asset register and the general ledger (journal entries), and reconciling the two
Depreciation is recorded in the **non-current asset register** (updating each asset's accumulated depreciation and carrying amount) and in the **general ledger** via a **journal entry**: debit **depreciation expense** (statement of profit or loss) and credit **accumulated depreciation** (a contra-asset account reducing the non-current asset's carrying amount on the statement of financial position; the original cost account is left unchanged). The non-current asset register should periodically be **reconciled** to the corresponding general ledger balances (cost and accumulated depreciation) to catch discrepancies, such as an asset disposed of but not removed from the register, or a depreciation journal not posted.
