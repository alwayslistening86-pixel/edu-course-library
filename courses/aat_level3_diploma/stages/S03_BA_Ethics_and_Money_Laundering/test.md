## Test items
1. An accountant is asked by a colleague to certify a set of accounts they have not had time to properly check, because a filing deadline is close. Identify the ethical principle at risk and explain why. [2 marks]
2. Explain why 'tipping off' is a criminal offence for an accountant who has reported a money-laundering suspicion. [2 marks]
3. Which of these is an example of the 'layering' stage of money laundering? Choose every correct option.
   A. Moving illegally obtained cash through a complex series of transactions to obscure its origin
   B. Paying illegally obtained cash into a bank account for the first time
   C. Using now-'clean' funds to buy a legitimate business
   D. Reporting a suspicion to the National Crime Agency
4. State two steps in resolving an ethical conflict, in order. [2 marks]
5. Explain the difference between reporting unethical behaviour through internal procedures and whistle-blowing. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 professional competence and due care; B1 because the accountant must act diligently and in accordance with applicable technical and professional standards, not skip proper checking to meet a deadline.
2. [2] B1 tipping off means disclosing to the client (or another party) that a suspicion has been raised/reported; B1 this could prejudice an investigation by allowing evidence to be destroyed or the suspect to be warned, so it is made a specific offence.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 gather the relevant facts; B1 identify the ethical issue(s) and who is affected; B1 consider established internal procedures; B1 evaluate alternative courses of action; B1 seek advice from a professional body or independent adviser if unresolved internally -- any 2, in a sensible order, for full marks.
5. [3] B1 internal reporting uses the organisation's own established procedures (e.g. to a line manager, compliance officer); B1 whistle-blowing is used when internal routes fail or are inappropriate, disclosing to an external body or regulator; B1 a genuine, reasonable whistle-blowing disclosure made in the public interest is a protected disclosure, giving the discloser legal protection.
