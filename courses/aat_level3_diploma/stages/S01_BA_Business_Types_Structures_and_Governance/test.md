# S01_BA_Business_Types_Structures_and_Governance - Test: Business types, the legal framework, stakeholders, structure and governance

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry and financial-statement questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain the difference in liability between a sole trader and a limited liability partnership (LLP). [2 marks]
2. State three of AAT's eight stakeholder categories and, for each, one thing that stakeholder wants from the business. [3 marks]
3. A company with many management layers and a narrow span of control is best described as having which structure? Choose every correct option.
   A. a tall structure
   B. a flat structure
   C. a matrix structure
   D. a divisional structure
4. Explain the difference between centralised and decentralised decision-making, giving one advantage of decentralisation. [3 marks]
5. A business identifies a risk that a key supplier might fail, and decides to take out a contract with a second, backup supplier. State which risk response this is, and name one other risk response. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 a sole trader has unlimited liability -- personally liable for all business debts; B1 an LLP gives its partners limited liability, capped (broadly) at what they have invested/agreed to contribute.
2. [3] B1 customers (quality/fair price); B1 suppliers (timely payment); B1 finance providers (return/repayment); B1 owners (return on investment); B1 government (tax paid, compliance); B1 employees (fair pay, security); B1 regulatory bodies (compliance); B1 public (responsible behaviour) -- any 3 for full marks.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] B1 centralised: decision-making concentrated at the top of the organisation; B1 decentralised: decision-making pushed down to local/divisional managers; B1 advantage of decentralisation, e.g. faster response to local conditions, or better local knowledge informing decisions.
5. [2] B1 this is 'reduce' (or 'transfer', if argued as spreading dependency) the risk -- reasonable either way if justified; B1 one other response named: avoid, transfer or accept.
