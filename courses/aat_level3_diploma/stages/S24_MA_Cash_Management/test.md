# S24_MA_Cash_Management - Test: Cash flow forecasting and managing cash surpluses and deficits

Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why a profitable business can still run out of cash, giving one specific reason. [2 marks]
2. A month's opening cash balance is £(2,400) (overdrawn). Receipts are £51,000 and payments are £46,200. Calculate the closing balance, stating whether it remains overdrawn. [3 marks]
3. Receivables are £36,000 and credit sales are £324,000 for the year. Calculate receivables days. [2 marks]
4. A cash flow forecast shows a large deficit in three months' time. Which of these is an appropriate response? Choose every correct option.
   A. Arrange a short-term overdraft or loan in good time
   B. Ignore it, since profit is positive
   C. Record it as a non-cash item like depreciation
   D. Wait until the deficit occurs before taking any action
5. Explain why depreciation is never included as a cash outflow in a cash flow forecast. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 profit is recognised under the accruals principle (when income is earned/expenses incurred), which can differ from when cash is actually received/paid; B1 reason, e.g. customers being slow to pay (rising receivables days), or the business investing heavily in inventory or non-current assets.
2. [3] M1 closing balance = -2,400 + 51,000 - 46,200; M1 = £2,400; A1 the account moves from overdrawn to a positive balance of £2,400 (no longer overdrawn).
3. [2] M1 (36,000/324,000) x 365; A1 = 40.6 days (to 1 decimal place).
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [2] B1 depreciation is a non-cash accounting adjustment, spreading an asset's cost over its useful life for profit-measurement purposes; B1 no actual cash moves when depreciation is charged, so it has no place in a forecast of cash inflows and outflows.
