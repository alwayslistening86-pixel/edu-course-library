## Test items
1. A trial balance's credit column totals £280 more than its debit column. Explain what this tells the accountant, and what it does not tell them. [3 marks]
2. A purchase invoice for £420 was correctly entered in the purchases daybook and posted to the purchases account, but was never posted to the payables ledger control account. State whether this would be disclosed by the trial balance, and explain your reasoning. [3 marks]
3. On the ETB, which columns does a non-current liability (e.g. a bank loan) get extended into? Choose every correct option.
   A. The statement of financial position columns
   B. The statement of profit or loss columns
   C. Both sets of columns equally
   D. Neither -- it does not appear on the ETB
4. A trial balance is found to include a compensating error: rent was overstated by £200 and, separately and unrelated, sales were overstated by £200. Explain why this trial balance still balances despite containing two errors. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 it tells them there is at least one error that has caused an imbalance of £280 (e.g. a one-sided posting, or a figure entered on only one side); B1 it does not identify which account(s) are affected, or the cause of the error; B1 it also does not confirm the accounts are otherwise free of error, since errors that affect both sides equally (e.g. omission, commission, compensating errors) would not be picked up by an imbalance at all.
2. [3] B1 yes, it would be disclosed -- the trial balance would fail to balance; B1 because only one side of the double entry (the debit to purchases) was completed, so the debit column will be £420 too high (or the credit column £420 too low) relative to what it should be; B1 a suspense account of £420 would be opened, then cleared once the missing credit to payables is posted.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [2] B1 both errors happen to be of equal amount (£200) and, although unrelated, one is a debit-side overstatement (rent, an expense) and the other a credit-side overstatement (sales, income), so they cancel out and the trial balance still balances; B1 this is why a trial balance balancing is not proof the accounts are free of error.
