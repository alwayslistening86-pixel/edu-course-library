# S12_FA_Trial_Balance_and_Extended_Trial_Balance - Lesson: The initial trial balance, the adjusted trial balance and the extended trial balance

## Goal
The learner prepares an initial trial balance and corrects errors (with and without a suspense account), places adjustments onto an adjusted trial balance, and completes an extended trial balance (ETB).

## Syllabus items taught here
- FA6.1 - The initial trial balance: normal debit/credit balances, its purpose and limitations, and correcting errors with and without a suspense account
- FA6.2 - The adjusted trial balance: placing closing inventory, accruals/prepayments, error corrections, depreciation, irrecoverable debts/allowances and disposals
- FA6.3 - The extended trial balance (ETB): completing the profit-or-loss and financial-position columns and balancing off to find the profit or loss for the period

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: a trial balance's debit column totals £150 more than its credit column. What does this tell you, and what does it not tell you? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### FA6.1 The initial trial balance: normal debit/credit balances, its purpose and limitations, and correcting errors with and without a suspense account
The **initial trial balance** lists every general ledger account balance in a debit or credit column, checking total debits equal total credits before adjustments and financial statements are prepared. Certain accounts normally carry a particular type of balance -- e.g. **VAT** (usually credit, if output VAT exceeds input VAT, i.e. VAT is owed to HMRC), **disposals** (a debit or credit balance depending on whether there is a loss or profit on disposal), **bank** (usually debit, but can be credit if overdrawn) and **irrecoverable debts expense** (debit, an expense). A trial balance that balances gives some assurance double entry was applied correctly, but has real **limitations**: it cannot detect errors that affect both sides equally (e.g. a transaction omitted completely, a transaction posted to the wrong account of the correct type, or a transaction posted with the correct amount to the wrong side but of an account that still happens to balance by coincidence) -- so a balancing trial balance is not proof the accounts are error-free. Accounting software usually completes the transfer of ledger balances into the trial balance automatically. **Errors not disclosed by the trial balance** (it still balances) include: an error of **omission** (transaction left out entirely), an error of **commission** (posted to the wrong account, but of the correct type, e.g. one supplier's account instead of another), an error of **principle** (posted to the wrong *type* of account, e.g. an expense treated as a non-current asset), a **compensating error** (two unrelated errors of equal and opposite amounts cancel out), an error of **original entry** (the wrong figure was used consistently on both sides) and a **complete reversal of entries** (debit and credit swapped) -- these are corrected with a journal entry across the two affected accounts, with no need for a suspense account. **Errors disclosed by the trial balance** (it fails to balance) are corrected using a **suspense account** -- opened with a balance equal to the discrepancy, then cleared as each underlying error is found and corrected. *Worked example:* a trial balance's debit column totals £360 more than its credit column, because a cheque payment of £150 for insurance was incorrectly posted to the insurance account as £510 (an error of original entry that, unusually, did create an imbalance, since only one side was mis-keyed). A suspense account is opened with a **credit** balance of £360 to make the trial balance balance. Correcting journal: debit Suspense £360, credit Insurance £360 (reducing the overstated insurance expense back down by £360), clearing the suspense account to zero.

#### FA6.2 The adjusted trial balance: placing closing inventory, accruals/prepayments, error corrections, depreciation, irrecoverable debts/allowances and disposals
The **adjusted trial balance** takes the initial trial balance and places the period end adjustments in additional columns: **closing inventory** (debit to inventory, credit to the cost of sales adjustment), **accruals and prepayments** (each an extra debit or credit as calculated), **error corrections** (from any suspense-account or journal corrections), **depreciation** (debit expense, credit accumulated depreciation), **irrecoverable debts and allowances for doubtful receivables** (debit expense, credit receivables/allowance), and **disposals** (the profit or loss transferred to the statement of profit or loss). Accounting software automatically recalculates account balances once these adjustments are entered, rather than requiring them to be re-added by hand.

#### FA6.3 The extended trial balance (ETB): completing the profit-or-loss and financial-position columns and balancing off to find the profit or loss for the period
The **extended trial balance (ETB)** takes the adjusted trial balance and extends every account's final balance into either the **statement of profit or loss** columns (income and expense accounts) or the **statement of financial position** columns (asset, liability and equity accounts) -- becoming the working paper the financial statements are prepared directly from. For a **sole trader**, the ETB is completed in full, including balancing off the statement of profit or loss columns to derive the profit or loss for the period, which is then carried into the statement of financial position columns (added to capital). For a **partnership**, completing the ETB in full (including the appropriation of profit between partners) is more complex and is *not* required at this level -- the appropriation is instead calculated separately (S14) once the overall net profit figure is known. To **balance off** the ETB: total the statement of profit or loss debit column (expenses) and credit column (income); the difference is the profit (if credit exceeds debit) or loss (if debit exceeds credit) for the period, entered as the balancing figure in both the statement of profit or loss columns (to make them agree) and the statement of financial position columns (representing the profit/loss added to, or the loss deducted from, capital). *Worked example:* after all adjustments, the statement of profit or loss debit (expense) column on the ETB totals £84,000 and the credit (income) column totals £102,000. The difference, £102,000 - £84,000 = **£18,000**, is the profit for the period -- entered as a debit balancing figure in the statement of profit or loss columns (to make both sides total £102,000) and as a credit balancing figure in the statement of financial position columns.
