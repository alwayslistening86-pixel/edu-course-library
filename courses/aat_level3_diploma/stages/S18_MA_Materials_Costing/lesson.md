#### MA2.2 Materials costing: calculating the cost of issues and closing inventory using FIFO, LIFO and AVCO, and their effect on profit and inventory valuation
**FIFO** (First In, First Out) assumes the *oldest* inventory is issued/sold first, so issues are costed at the oldest prices and closing inventory is valued at the *most recent* prices. **LIFO** (Last In, First Out) assumes the *most recently received* inventory is issued first, so issues are costed at the newest prices and closing inventory is valued at the *oldest* prices (LIFO is not permitted under UK/international financial reporting standards for external financial statements, but AAT still requires it as a costing technique). **AVCO** (Average Cost) recalculates a weighted average cost per unit each time new inventory is received, and issues are costed at that running average. In a period of **rising prices**, FIFO gives the *highest* closing inventory value and *lowest* cost of sales (so the highest reported profit), LIFO gives the *lowest* closing inventory value and *highest* cost of sales (so the lowest reported profit), and AVCO falls in between. *Worked example:* opening inventory 100 units @ £5.00 = £500; receipt of 200 units @ £6.50 = £1,300 (300 units, £1,800 total, average cost £6.00/unit); 250 units are then issued; a further 150 units are received @ £7.00 = £1,050. **FIFO** issue cost = (100 @ £5.00) + (150 @ £6.50) = 500 + 975 = **£1,475**; closing inventory = (50 @ £6.50) + (150 @ £7.00) = 325 + 1,050 = **£1,375**. **LIFO** issue cost = (200 @ £6.50) + (50 @ £5.00) = 1,300 + 250 = **£1,550**; closing inventory = (50 @ £5.00) + (150 @ £7.00) = 250 + 1,050 = **£1,300**. **AVCO** issue cost = 250 x £6.00 (the average just before issue) = **£1,500**, leaving 50 units @ £6.00 = £300; adding the new receipt gives 200 units at £1,350 total, a new average of £6.75/unit, so closing inventory = **£1,350**. As expected in a rising-price period: FIFO's closing inventory (£1,375) is highest, LIFO's (£1,300) is lowest, AVCO's (£1,350) is in between -- and all three are consistent with total cost received of £500 + £1,300 + £1,050 = £2,850 (issue cost plus closing inventory always sums to this).
