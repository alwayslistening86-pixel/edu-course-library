# S08_FA_Non_Current_Asset_Acquisition_and_Disposal - Test: Authorising capital expenditure, capital vs revenue expenditure, and recording acquisitions/disposals

## Test items
1. A machine has a list price of £24,000 with a 10% trade discount, plus £1,200 delivery costs and £800 installation costs. Calculate the total cost to be capitalised. [3 marks]
2. An asset cost £35,000, with accumulated depreciation of £22,000. It is sold for £15,000 cash. Calculate the profit or loss on disposal, stating which it is. [3 marks]
3. Why is prior authorisation normally required before capital expenditure is committed? Choose every correct option.
   A. Because it is typically large, long-term and harder to reverse than routine spending
   B. Because capital expenditure never appears in the financial statements
   C. Because it is always revenue expenditure in disguise
   D. Authorisation is never required for capital expenditure
4. Explain why classifying a repair as capital expenditure, when it should be revenue expenditure, would overstate profit for the period. [3 marks]
5. Explain what a part-exchange allowance represents when a business trades in an old van against a new one, and how it is treated for the old van's disposal. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 discounted price = 24,000 x 0.90 = £21,600; M1 add delivery and installation = 21,600 + 1,200 + 800; A1 = £23,600.
2. [3] M1 carrying amount = 35,000 - 22,000 = £13,000; M1 proceeds £15,000 exceed carrying amount £13,000; A1 profit on disposal of £2,000.
3. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
4. [3] B1 revenue expenditure should be expensed in full to the statement of profit or loss in the period incurred; B1 if wrongly capitalised, it is instead added to the non-current asset's cost and only a small depreciation charge hits profit that period; B1 so the period's expenses are understated and profit is overstated (the non-current asset and equity on the statement of financial position are also overstated).
5. [2] B1 the part-exchange allowance is the value the supplier gives for the old van, reducing the amount owed/paid for the new one -- a form of funding the purchase, not a cash sale; B1 it is treated as the 'proceeds' figure for calculating the profit or loss on disposal of the old van.
