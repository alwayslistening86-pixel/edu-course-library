# S22_MA_Spreadsheet_Techniques - Lesson: Spreadsheet techniques for management accounting information

## Goal
The learner uses spreadsheet formulas/functions, sorting/filtering, pivot tables and charts, and checks data accuracy, to produce management accounting information.

## Syllabus items taught here
- MA5.1 - Spreadsheet techniques for management accounting: formulas and functions, sorting/filtering, pivot tables, charts and validating data

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if you needed to find the total cost of only the products in one cost centre, out of a spreadsheet listing hundreds of products across many centres, which spreadsheet tool would help most? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### MA5.1 Spreadsheet techniques for management accounting: formulas and functions, sorting/filtering, pivot tables, charts and validating data
Spreadsheets are widely used to prepare management accounting information. Common **formulas and functions**: `SUM` (adds a range of values, e.g. total costs), `IF` (returns one value if a condition is true, another if false, e.g. flagging a variance as 'adverse'/'favourable'), `SUMIF`/`SUMIFS` (totals values meeting one or more criteria, e.g. total cost for one cost centre), `VLOOKUP`/`XLOOKUP` (looks up a value, e.g. a standard cost, from a reference table), and basic arithmetic formulas for calculations like variances or absorption rates. **Sorting and filtering** lets a large dataset be reordered (e.g. by cost centre, or highest-to-lowest cost) or narrowed down to only the rows meeting a chosen condition -- useful for isolating a single cost centre's data, or the largest variances, from a much bigger dataset. A **pivot table** summarises and cross-tabulates large amounts of data quickly (e.g. total cost by cost centre and by month, without writing a formula for every combination), and can be easily reconfigured to answer a different question. **Charts** turn the resulting figures into a visual form (as in BA5.2) -- a bar chart for comparing cost centres, a line chart for a cost trend over time. Before relying on any spreadsheet output, its data should be **checked and validated** -- e.g. that formulas reference the correct cells and ranges, that totals reconcile to an independent check, and that no rows/data were accidentally excluded from a filter or a formula range (a very common source of spreadsheet error).
