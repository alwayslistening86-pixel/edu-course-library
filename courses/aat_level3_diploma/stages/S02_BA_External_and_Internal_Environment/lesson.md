# S02_BA_External_and_Internal_Environment - Lesson: PESTLE, the micro-economic environment and sustainability

## Goal
The learner applies PESTLE to the external/macro environment, explains the micro-economic environment (supply, demand, competition), and explains sustainability and the triple bottom line.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BA2.1 - PESTLE analysis of the external/macro-economic environment: political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors
- BA2.2 - The micro-economic environment: supply and demand, the price mechanism, effects of price changes, and factors affecting competition
- BA2.3 - Sustainability: the triple bottom line (social, ecological, economic/financial), long-term stakeholder perspectives, responsible resource management and the accountant's public interest duty

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if the Bank of England raises interest rates, which PESTLE category does that sit in, and how might it affect a business that has borrowed heavily? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### BA2.1 PESTLE analysis of the external/macro-economic environment: political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental factors
**PESTLE** analyses the external, **macro-economic** environment a business cannot control but must respond to: **P**olitical (government policy, taxation, trade policy, public spending -- e.g. a change of government changing business tax rates); **E**conomic (interest rates, exchange rates, disposable income, the business cycle -- boom, recession, recovery -- and inflation, whether demand-pull, cost-push or built-in); **S**ocial (demographic change, e.g. an ageing population; changing consumer trends; unemployment levels affecting spending power); **T**echnological (new technology changing products, processes or how customers are reached, and the risk of falling behind); **L**egal (changes in trade regulation and other law affecting how the business must operate); **E**nvironmental (sustainability expectations, climate-related regulation, resource scarcity). PESTLE is used to identify which of these factors are relevant to a given business and assess their likely impact -- e.g. a UK exporter is highly exposed to the Economic factor of exchange-rate movements, while a food manufacturer is highly exposed to Legal food-safety regulation.

#### BA2.2 The micro-economic environment: supply and demand, the price mechanism, effects of price changes, and factors affecting competition
The **micro-economic environment** looks at a business's immediate market. **Supply and demand**: demand is how much customers want to buy at a given price (usually falls as price rises); supply is how much producers are willing to sell at a given price (usually rises as price rises); the **price mechanism** is where supply and demand meet to set the market price. A **price change** affects: **volume** sold (higher price usually reduces volume, and vice versa -- how much depends on price elasticity), **revenue** (price x volume -- may rise or fall depending on elasticity), **costs** (higher volume can raise total variable costs) and ultimately **profitability**. **Competition** is shaped by: the distinctiveness of **product features** (a unique product faces less direct competition), the **number of buyers and sellers** in the market, **barriers to entry** (how easy it is for new competitors to enter -- high capital costs, regulation, brand loyalty all raise barriers), **location** (local monopolies vs a crowded high street) and the **availability of information** (how easily customers can compare prices/quality across sellers).

#### BA2.3 Sustainability: the triple bottom line (social, ecological, economic/financial), long-term stakeholder perspectives, responsible resource management and the accountant's public interest duty
**Sustainability** means meeting present needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own -- taking a **long-term perspective** that balances current and future needs, and considering a **wider range of stakeholders** than just shareholders. The **triple bottom line** measures performance across three dimensions: **social** (people -- fair treatment of employees, the community, customers), **ecological/environmental** (planet -- resource use, emissions, waste) and **economic/financial** (profit -- the business must still be financially viable to survive and keep delivering the other two). **Responsible resource management** spans products, services, customers, employees, the workplace, the supply chain and processes -- e.g. reducing waste, sourcing materials responsibly, minimising energy use. Accountants have a **public interest duty**: professional accountancy bodies expect members to act in a way that serves the wider public interest, not just their employer's or client's narrow short-term interest, which increasingly includes considering sustainability in the advice and information they provide.

## Explicitly not here
Sustainability as a specific reporting framework (e.g. triple bottom line reporting formats) beyond the concept itself is out of scope at this level.
