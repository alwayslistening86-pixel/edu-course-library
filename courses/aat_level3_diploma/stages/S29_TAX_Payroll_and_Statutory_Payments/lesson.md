# S29_TAX_Payroll_and_Statutory_Payments - Lesson: Payroll, statutory payments and RTI

## Goal
The learner calculates gross to net pay (income tax via PAYE, employee NIC, voluntary deductions), and explains statutory payments (SSP, SMP, SPP), RTI and payment deadlines to HMRC.

## Syllabus items taught here
- TAX6.1 - Payroll: the documents used, the components of gross pay, statutory and voluntary deductions (income tax via PAYE, employee NIC), and calculating net pay
- TAX6.2 - Statutory payments (SSP, SMP, SPP), Real Time Information (RTI) submission, and payment deadlines to HMRC

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: an employee's payslip shows income tax and National Insurance both deducted from the same gross pay. Are these the same deduction, or two separate ones? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. AAT's assessments mix objective-test items (single- and multiple-select, numeric gap-fill, drag-and-drop/matching) with multi-task, human-marked written/journal-entry questions on Business Awareness; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### TAX6.1 Payroll: the documents used, the components of gross pay, statutory and voluntary deductions (income tax via PAYE, employee NIC), and calculating net pay
**Payroll** documents include the **payslip** (given to each employee, showing gross pay, deductions and net pay), the **P60** (an annual summary of pay and deductions, given after the tax year ends) and the **starter checklist** (replacing form P45 information for a new employee with no P45, used to determine their tax code). **Gross pay** is made up of: basic pay, plus any overtime, bonus or commission. From gross pay, **statutory deductions** are taken: **income tax**, calculated via **PAYE (Pay As You Earn)** using the employee's tax code (which reflects their annual personal allowance -- £12,570 for 2026/27, i.e. £1,047.50/month -- tax-free before the basic rate of 20% applies), and **employee Class 1 National Insurance contributions (NIC)** -- for 2026/27, 8% of pay between the monthly primary threshold (£1,047.50) and the monthly upper earnings limit (£4,189.17), and 2% above that. **Voluntary deductions** (e.g. pension contributions, union subscriptions, a season ticket loan repayment) are then also taken, if applicable, before arriving at **net pay** -- the amount actually paid to the employee. *Worked example:* an employee's gross monthly pay is £2,200, with a voluntary pension deduction of 5% of gross pay. Taxable pay = 2,200 - 1,047.50 (monthly personal allowance) = **£1,152.50**; income tax at 20% = 1,152.50 x 20% = **£230.50**. Employee NIC = (2,200 - 1,047.50, all within the 8% band) x 8% = 1,152.50 x 8% = **£92.20**. Pension deduction = 2,200 x 5% = **£110.00**. Net pay = 2,200 - 230.50 - 92.20 - 110.00 = **£1,767.30**.

#### TAX6.2 Statutory payments (SSP, SMP, SPP), Real Time Information (RTI) submission, and payment deadlines to HMRC
**Statutory Sick Pay (SSP)** for 2026/27 is paid at **£123.25/week** (or 80% of average weekly earnings, if lower), payable from the **first** qualifying sick day (both the previous 3 waiting days and the lower earnings limit eligibility test were removed from 6 April 2026, so SSP is now payable from day one, to all eligible employees regardless of earnings level). **Statutory Maternity Pay (SMP)** is paid for up to 39 weeks: the **first 6 weeks** at **90% of average weekly earnings** (uncapped), and the remaining weeks at the **lower of £194.32/week or 90% of average weekly earnings**; **Statutory Paternity Pay (SPP)** uses the same standard rate (lower of £194.32/week or 90% of average weekly earnings) throughout. Employers must submit a **Full Payment Submission (FPS)** to HMRC under **Real Time Information (RTI)** on or before each **payday**, reporting pay and deductions for that period -- rather than only at the tax year end. PAYE and NIC deducted from employees, along with employer NIC, must then be **paid over to HMRC** by set **deadlines** -- normally by the 22nd of the following month if paying electronically (the 19th if paying by cheque/post). *Worked example -- SSP:* an employee normally works 5 qualifying days a week and is off sick for 3 of them. Daily SSP rate = £123.25 / 5 = £24.65; SSP due = 3 x £24.65 = **£73.95**. *Worked example -- SMP:* an employee's average weekly earnings are £420. For weeks 1-6, SMP = £420 x 90% = **£378.00/week**; from week 7, SMP = the lower of £194.32 and (£420 x 90% = £378.00), so **£194.32/week**.

## Explicitly not here
The employer's own NIC and its administration is out of scope at this level of detail; the mechanics of VAT are S26-S27.
