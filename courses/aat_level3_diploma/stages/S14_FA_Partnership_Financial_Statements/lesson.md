# S14_FA_Partnership_Financial_Statements - Lesson: Preparing the partnership's appropriation account, statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position

## Goal
The learner prepares a partnership's appropriation account and statement of profit or loss, and its statement of financial position using partners' capital and current accounts.

## Syllabus items taught here

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: two partners share profit 3:2 after being paid interest on capital and a salary. Why might a partnership agreement include interest on capital at all, rather than just splitting the whole profit by the ratio? Have the learner attempt every calculation (depreciation, accruals/prepayments, irrecoverable debts, inventory valuation, trial balance/ETB adjustments, sole trader and partnership financial statements, profitability ratios, incomplete records, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, overhead absorption, marginal vs absorption costing, budgets, variances, breakeven, limiting factors, cash flow forecasts, VAT returns, PAYE/NIC and statutory payments) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry and multi-task items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% (reduced rate 5%, zero rate 0%) and the VAT registration threshold at £90,000 (deregistration £88,000) throughout, and payroll figures use the 2026/27 tax year -- check gov.uk for the current rates if they may have changed. #### FA7.3 The partnership's statement of profit or loss and appropriation account: interest on capital/drawings, salaries and the residual profit share by profit-sharing ratio
A partnership's statement of profit or loss is prepared exactly as for a sole trader down to the net profit figure, then an **appropriation account** shows how that net profit is shared between the partners, following the partnership agreement -- since, unlike a sole trader, there is more than one owner to share it with. The appropriation account typically deducts, in order: **interest on capital** (a return to each partner for the capital they have invested, calculated as a percentage of their fixed capital balance, regardless of how profitable the year was), **salaries** (a fixed amount paid to a partner for extra time/responsibility, again regardless of overall profit) and sometimes **interest on drawings** (a charge to a partner for drawing out cash early in the year, added back into the amount available to share) -- what remains, the **residual profit**, is then shared between the partners according to the agreed **profit-sharing ratio (PSR)**. *Worked example:* partners A and B have fixed capital of £40,000 and £30,000; interest on capital is 5% p.a.; A also receives an £8,000 salary; the PSR is 3:2; net profit for appropriation is £50,000. Interest on capital: A = 40,000 x 5% = **£2,000**; B = 30,000 x 5% = **£1,500** (total £3,500). Residual profit = 50,000 - 3,500 - 8,000 = **£38,500**, shared 3:2: A's share = 38,500 x 3/5 = **£23,100**; B's share = 38,500 x 2/5 = **£15,400**. Total allocated to A = 2,000 + 8,000 + 23,100 = **£33,100**; total allocated to B = 1,500 + 15,400 = **£16,900**; check: 33,100 + 16,900 = £50,000, agreeing with net profit.

#### FA7.4 The partnership's statement of financial position: partners' capital and current accounts, and preparing both
A partnership's statement of financial position differs from a sole trader's in showing **two accounts per partner**: a **capital account** (normally fixed, holding each partner's originally invested/agreed capital, changed only by a formal capital injection or withdrawal) and a **current account** (a running account of each partner's share of profit, less drawings, changing every period) -- unlike the appropriation account, which is a period statement of *how much* each partner is entitled to, the current account is the *cumulative running balance* of what is actually still owed to (or by) each partner. Each partner's current account: starts with the **opening balance** brought forward, adds their **interest on capital**, **salary** and **share of residual profit** from the appropriation account, and deducts their **drawings** (cash, goods or services taken during the period) to give the **closing balance**, carried to the statement of financial position. *Worked example (continuing from FA7.3):* Partner A's current account opens at £3,000 (credit); adds interest on capital £2,000, salary £8,000 and residual profit share £23,100; deducts drawings £15,000: closing balance = 3,000 + 2,000 + 8,000 + 23,100 - 15,000 = **£21,100**. Partner B's current account opens at £1,500 (credit); adds interest on capital £1,500 and residual profit share £15,400; deducts drawings £12,000: closing balance = 1,500 + 1,500 + 15,400 - 12,000 = **£6,400**. The statement of financial position shows each partner's fixed capital account (£40,000 and £30,000) and closing current account (£21,100 and £6,400) separately, within the total equity section.

## Explicitly not here
Completing a full ETB for a partnership (including the appropriation within it) is out of scope at this level -- the appropriation is calculated separately, as here.
