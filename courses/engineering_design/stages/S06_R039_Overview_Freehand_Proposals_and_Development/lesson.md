# S06_R039_Overview_Freehand_Proposals_and_Development - Lesson: R039 overview, freehand design proposals and developing a proposal further

## Goal
The learner knows how R039 is assessed and produces a range of annotated freehand design proposals, then develops one proposal further.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R039-A - R039 assessment overview: four tasks and mark bands: know the four practical tasks, the 60 marks, and how the three OCR mark bands are applied per task
- R039-T1 - Task 1: freehand design proposals: produce a range of creative freehand 2D/3D sketches with rendering techniques and full annotation
- R039-T2 - Task 2: developing one design proposal: develop one proposal further with additional rendering, detail and annotation against the specification

## How to teach this
Show the learner a rough, unannotated sketch next to a rendered, fully labelled one of the same idea. 'Which one actually tells a manufacturer or a client what they need to know?' Use this to introduce R039 as the unit where the sketching skills from R038's Topic Area 3 become graded evidence.

#### R039-A R039 assessment overview: four tasks and mark bands
R039 is a 36 GLH, OCR-set and OCR-moderated practical assignment worth 60 marks (60 UMS), completed over roughly 10-12 hours of supervised, independent centre-assessed time. It has four tasks: Task 1 freehand design proposals, Task 2 developing one proposal further, Task 3 orthographic and assembly drawings, Task 4 CAD formal presentation drawings. Each task is marked using OCR's three mark bands (MB1 limited/basic, MB2 adequate/sound, MB3 comprehensive/effective); no MB1 evidence in a task scores zero for that task. As with any OCR-set assignment, learners must work independently once the assignment starts, with teachers restricted to general guidance rather than telling learners what to draw. Activity: name R039's four tasks in order and state roughly how the marks are spread across a 60-mark unit.

#### R039-T1 Task 1: freehand design proposals
Task 1 asks for a genuine range of creative freehand design proposals responding to a set brief and specification, drawn in 2D and/or 3D, using rendering techniques (thick and thin line weight, texture, tone and shading to show form and material) and fully annotated (labelling materials, approximate dimensions and how the idea meets specific specification points). MB3 evidence shows a wide, genuinely varied range with confident rendering and detailed, specification-linked annotation; MB1 evidence shows very few ideas, weak rendering or little to no annotation. Activity: sketch two visually different proposals for a simple product (for example a phone stand) and annotate each with one material choice and one specification point it meets.

#### R039-T2 Task 2: developing one design proposal
Task 2 takes one proposal from Task 1 and develops it further: additional or more refined sketches showing the idea in more detail, further rendering, and more detailed annotation explaining design decisions against the specification. This shows the learner can take a promising idea and improve it with purpose, rather than simply redrawing it unchanged. MB3 evidence shows clear, purposeful development (changes explained and linked to specification points); MB1 evidence shows little real change from the original proposal. Activity: take one of the two phone-stand proposals sketched above and develop it further, explaining in an annotation what was changed and why.

## Explicitly not here
Orthographic/assembly drawings and CAD presentation (S07), R040's tasks (S08-S09).
