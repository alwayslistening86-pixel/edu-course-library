# S08_R040_Product_Analysis_Disassembly_and_CAD_Modelling - Lesson: Product analysis with ACCESS FM, product disassembly and virtual 3D CAD modelling

## Goal
The learner analyses and compares products using ACCESS FM and a comparison matrix, records a product disassembly with hazards and safety controls, and produces a 3D CAD virtual model from a specification.

## Syllabus items taught here
- R040-T1 - Task 1: product analysis using ACCESS FM and an engineering comparison matrix: analyse a specified product against ACCESS FM and compare products using a ranking/comparison matrix
- R040-T2 - Task 2: product disassembly and analysis: disassemble a product, analyse components, materials and manufacturing methods, and record hazards and safety controls
- R040-T3 - Task 3: virtual 3D CAD modelling: produce a 3D CAD virtual model from a specification, with multiple views and simulated operation

## How to teach this
Bring in (or describe) a simple product that can be safely taken apart, such as a retractable pen. Ask the learner: 'What could you learn from taking it apart that you couldn't learn from just looking at it?' Use this to bridge from ACCESS FM analysis (looking at a product) to disassembly (looking inside it) as two connected but distinct analysis skills.

#### R040-T1 Task 1: product analysis using ACCESS FM and an engineering comparison matrix
Task 1 applies ACCESS FM (S01) to a specified existing product in detail, then compares that product against one or more rival products using an engineering comparison/ranking matrix, scoring each against shared criteria to reach an evidenced conclusion about relative strengths and weaknesses. This differs from S01's introductory ACCESS FM practice by requiring full, evidenced Task 1 depth: specific, product-grounded comments on every factor, plus a genuinely comparative matrix rather than analysing one product alone. MB3 evidence is detailed and fully evidenced across every factor and comparison point; MB1 evidence is generic or incomplete. Activity: build a comparison matrix scoring two rival versions of the same everyday product against three ACCESS FM-based criteria.

#### R040-T2 Task 2: product disassembly and analysis
Task 2 involves observing (or carrying out) a product disassembly, analysing the variety, function and materials of its components and the production, assembly and manufacturing methods used to make and join them, and recording a commentary on hazards and the safety controls needed during disassembly. This connects directly to the manufacturing considerations taught in S03 (materials, wasting/shaping/forming/joining/finishing) by requiring the learner to identify these processes in a real, physical product rather than describe them abstractly. MB3 evidence is thorough, correctly identifies components/materials/processes, and includes specific, well-controlled hazard and safety points; MB1 evidence is superficial or missing hazard/safety commentary. Activity: list three components of a simple product, the material and manufacturing process used for each, and one hazard from disassembling it.

#### R040-T3 Task 3: virtual 3D CAD modelling
Task 3 asks the learner to produce a 3D CAD virtual model from a design specification, including multiple views of the model and evidence of simulated or animated operation where relevant (for example a moving part shown working). This builds on the CAD skills from S04 and R039 Task 4, but here the model is produced from a specification for a new or modified design rather than as a presentation drawing of an already-decided proposal, and it feeds directly into the physical prototype built in Task 5. MB3 evidence shows an accurate, detailed model from multiple views with convincing simulation; MB1 evidence shows a basic or single-view model. Activity: list the views (for example front, isometric, exploded) a strong 3D CAD model of the desk organiser should include as evidence.

## Explicitly not here
Production planning and prototype manufacture (S09), prototype evaluation and qualification-level content (S09-S10).
