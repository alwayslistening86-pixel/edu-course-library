# S10_Paper_1_Question_Types - Lesson: Paper 1 question types: Christianity, Religion and Ethics

## Goal
The learner can answer every Paper 1 question part correctly: the 1-mark recognition question, the 2-mark 'give two', the 4-mark 'explain two', the 5-mark 'explain two contrasting beliefs' with a source of authority, and the extended 12(+3 SPaG)-mark discuss/evaluate question.

## Syllabus items taught here
- Q-P1-a - Paper 1 part (a): 1-mark recognise/identify question: Answer the 1-mark AO1 question correctly
- Q-P1-b - Paper 1 part (b): 2-mark 'give two' question: Answer the 2-mark AO1 'give two' question with two accurate points
- Q-P1-c - Paper 1 part (c): 4-mark 'explain two' question: Answer the 4-mark AO1 'explain two reasons/ways' question
- Q-P1-d5 - Paper 1 part (d)(i): 5-mark 'explain two contrasting religious beliefs' question: Answer the 5-mark AO1 question citing a source of wisdom and authority
- Q-P1-d12 - Paper 1 part (d)(ii): 12(+3 SPaG)-mark 'discuss/evaluate' question: Answer the extended AO2 evaluate question with religious and non-religious arguments and a justified conclusion

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Paper 1 has four questions, and every single one is built the same way: parts (a) to (d), from 1 mark up to 15. Today we drill exactly what each part wants, using Christianity content you already know.'

#### Q-P1-a Paper 1 part (a): 1-mark recognise/identify question
Part (a) is a 1-mark AO1 question, often multiple choice or a very short factual recall, for example 'Which of the following is one of the Christian sacraments?' Technique: answer briefly and directly with no explanation; explanation earns no extra credit here and wastes time. This part exists purely to check basic factual recognition before the question moves to more demanding parts.

#### Q-P1-b Paper 1 part (b): 2-mark 'give two' question
Part (b) is a 2-mark AO1 question asking the learner to 'give two' examples, reasons or features (for example 'Give two Christian festivals'), worth one mark per accurate point. Technique: state each point briefly and clearly; no explanation or justification is required or rewarded, so time is better spent making sure both points are accurate and distinct from each other rather than developing either one.

#### Q-P1-c Paper 1 part (c): 4-mark 'explain two' question
Part (c) is a 4-mark AO1 question asking the learner to 'explain two' reasons, ways or beliefs (for example 'Explain two reasons why Christians go on pilgrimage'), worth up to two marks per point: one mark for the point itself and one for its development or explanation. Technique: state each point, then add a linked reason, example or brief explanation ('...because...' or '...this shows...'), so that each of the two points is genuinely developed, not just listed; a bare list of two facts with no development will not reach full marks.

#### Q-P1-d5 Paper 1 part (d)(i): 5-mark 'explain two contrasting religious beliefs' question
Part (d)(i) is a 5-mark AO1 question asking the learner to 'explain two contrasting religious beliefs about X. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority' (for example 'Explain two contrasting Christian beliefs about the Eucharist'). Technique: choose two beliefs that genuinely contrast (for example, from different denominations, such as a Catholic and a Protestant view), explain each fully, and support at least one, ideally both, with a specific, accurately named source of wisdom and authority (a Bible reference, a creed, a named Church document such as Humanae Vitae, or a named tradition); failing to cite any source of authority at all will cap the mark significantly below full marks.

#### Q-P1-d12 Paper 1 part (d)(ii): 12(+3 SPaG)-mark 'discuss/evaluate' question
Part (d)(ii) is the extended AO2 question, presenting a statement (for example ''The most important Christian festival is Easter, not Christmas.' Discuss two contrasting views on this statement. In your response you should: refer to religious arguments, which may draw on the views of more than one Christian tradition; refer to non-religious arguments; reach a justified conclusion.'), worth 12 marks (15 on the two questions per paper carrying SPaG). Technique: plan first; present at least one religious argument supporting the statement and one against (or from contrasting Christian traditions), present at least one non-religious argument, weigh the arguments against each other explicitly rather than just listing them, and end with a clear, justified personal conclusion stating which side is more convincing and why. Use full sentences, paragraphs and accurate specialist terminology throughout for the SPaG marks where they apply.
