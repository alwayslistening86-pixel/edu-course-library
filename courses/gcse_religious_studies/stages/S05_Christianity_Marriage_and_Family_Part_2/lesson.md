# S05_Christianity_Marriage_and_Family_Part_2 - Lesson: Christianity: contraception, divorce, and equality between men and women

## Goal
The learner can explain divergent Christian and non-religious attitudes to contraception, explain divergent Christian and non-religious attitudes to divorce and remarriage, explain Christian teaching on gender roles in the family, and explain Christian opposition to gender prejudice and discrimination.

## Syllabus items taught here
- C-MAF.5 - Christianity: family planning and contraception: Explain divergent Christian and non-religious attitudes to contraception
- C-MAF.6 - Christianity: divorce and remarriage: Explain divergent Christian and non-religious attitudes to divorce and remarriage
- C-MAF.7 - Christianity: equality of men and women in the family: Explain Christian teaching and divergent attitudes on gender roles in the family
- C-MAF.8 - Christianity: opposing gender prejudice and discrimination: Explain Christian opposition to gender prejudice, including the role of women in the Church

## How to teach this
Open with: 'On some ethical questions, all Christians broadly agree. On others, denominations and individual Christians disagree sharply. Which do you think contraception and divorce are?'

#### C-MAF.5 Christianity: family planning and contraception
Christian attitudes to contraception (deliberately preventing pregnancy) diverge. The Catholic Church, in the encyclical Humanae Vitae (1968), teaches that only natural methods of family planning (such as tracking fertility cycles) are acceptable, because artificial contraception is held to separate the unitive (bonding) and procreative (life-giving) purposes of sex that Catholic teaching holds should stay together. Many Protestant Churches take a more permissive view, accepting artificial contraception as a responsible way for couples to plan family size within marriage. Non-religious, including atheist and Humanist, attitudes generally support the free use of contraception as a matter of personal choice and responsible family planning, sometimes applying situation ethics (judging each situation on whether the most loving outcome results) rather than a fixed rule, a reasoning some more liberal Christians also draw on.

#### C-MAF.6 Christianity: divorce and remarriage
Jesus' teaching in Matthew 19:1-12 discourages divorce ('what God has joined together, let no one separate'), and some Christians and denominations, holding marriage to be an unbreakable, lifelong covenant, oppose divorce and remarriage in church except in very limited circumstances. Other Christians take a more permissive, pastoral approach, accepting that marriages can genuinely break down and permitting divorce and remarriage, particularly where the alternative (remaining trapped in a harmful relationship) seems clearly less loving; this more flexible reasoning sometimes draws on situation ethics. Non-religious, including atheist and Humanist, attitudes generally treat divorce as a legitimate personal and legal option when a relationship has broken down, without attaching religious significance to the marriage bond itself.

#### C-MAF.7 Christianity: equality of men and women in the family
Genesis 1-3 teaches that men and women are both made in the image of God, implying fundamental equality, while Ephesians 5:21-30 instructs mutual submission and love between husband and wife, though its call for wives to 'submit' to husbands has been read very differently by different Christians. More traditional ('complementarian') Christians hold that men and women have distinct, complementary roles within marriage and family, often with the husband as head of the household. More liberal ('egalitarian') Christians argue Genesis' shared 'image of God' and the New Testament's overall trajectory point to full equality of role and authority between men and women in marriage and family life, treating older hierarchical readings as culturally, not divinely, determined.

#### C-MAF.8 Christianity: opposing gender prejudice and discrimination
Galatians 3:28 ('there is neither... male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus') is widely used by Christians to oppose gender prejudice and discrimination, teaching fundamental spiritual equality regardless of sex. In practice, Christian opposition to gender prejudice includes campaigning for equal pay and opportunity, supporting women's access to education and leadership, and, within the Church itself, the ordination of women as priests and bishops in many, though not all, denominations (the Church of England ordained its first women priests in 1994 and its first female bishop in 2015). Divergence remains: the Catholic Church and Orthodox Churches do not ordain women as priests, holding to a male-only priesthood on grounds of tradition and Christ's choice of male apostles, while many Protestant denominations do, seeing this as consistent with Galatians' teaching on equality.
