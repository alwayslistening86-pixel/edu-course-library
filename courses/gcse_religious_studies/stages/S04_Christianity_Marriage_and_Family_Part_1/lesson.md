# S04_Christianity_Marriage_and_Family_Part_1 - Lesson: Christianity: marriage, sexual relationships, family and parish support

## Goal
The learner can explain Christian teaching on marriage and contrast it with non-religious attitudes, explain divergent Christian teachings on sexual relationships, explain the purpose of the family and divergent responses to family types, and explain how the local parish supports families.

## Syllabus items taught here
- C-MAF.1 - Christianity: the importance and purpose of marriage: Explain Christian teaching on marriage and contrast it with non-religious attitudes
- C-MAF.2 - Christianity: sexual relationships: Explain divergent Christian teachings on sex outside marriage and same-sex relationships
- C-MAF.3 - Christianity: the purpose and importance of the family: Explain Christian teaching on the family's purpose and divergent responses to family types
- C-MAF.4 - Christianity: support for families in the local parish: Explain how and why the local church supports families

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Most religions treat marriage as far more than a legal contract. What do you think Christians believe marriage is actually for?' Build the answer from the learner's guesses before giving the specification content.

#### C-MAF.1 Christianity: the importance and purpose of marriage
Christian teaching, drawing on Mark 10:6-9 ('what God has joined together, let no one separate'), holds marriage to be a sacred, lifelong union between two people, instituted by God, providing companionship, a stable context for sexual intimacy, and, for many Christians, the proper setting for raising children. Marriage is treated as a public commitment, usually made before God and a worshipping community, not simply a private arrangement. Non-religious, including atheist and Humanist, attitudes vary: some regard marriage as lacking special sanctity, seeing it as simply a legal and social contract with no religious meaning, and are more accepting of cohabitation (living together without marriage) as an equally valid choice. Christians generally respond that marriage's religious and lifelong commitment gives it a significance and stability that cohabitation, in their view, does not guarantee.

#### C-MAF.2 Christianity: sexual relationships
Christian teaching has traditionally held that sexual relationships belong within marriage, drawing on passages such as 1 Corinthians 6:7-20, which teaches respect for the body as, for Christians, 'a temple of the Holy Spirit'. Teaching diverges on relationships outside this traditional view: some Christians and denominations maintain that sex outside marriage and same-sex sexual relationships are not in keeping with biblical teaching, while other, more liberal Christians and denominations affirm same-sex relationships and are more accepting of sex outside marriage, especially within a committed relationship, arguing that love, faithfulness and mutual respect matter more than the precise legal or traditional form a relationship takes. This divergence reflects wider disagreement within Christianity about how far biblical teaching, rooted in a different historical context, should be applied literally today.

#### C-MAF.3 Christianity: the purpose and importance of the family
Christian teaching sees the family as providing procreation (bringing new life into being), security (emotional and material support) and education, including moral and religious upbringing of children, drawing on Ephesians 6:1-4 ('children, obey your parents... fathers, do not exasperate your children'). Christians respond in divergent ways to the range of modern family types: nuclear families (two parents and children), single-parent families, families with same-sex parents, extended families (including wider relatives) and blended families (formed through remarriage). More traditional Christians may hold up the nuclear family as an ideal rooted in biblical teaching, while more liberal Christians emphasise love, care and commitment as the defining features of a good family, whatever its particular structure, and are more accepting of the full range of family types found in society today.

#### C-MAF.4 Christianity: support for families in the local parish
Local churches support families in practical and spiritual ways. Family worship, drawing on Matthew 19:13-14 ('let the little children come to me'), welcomes children into services, sometimes through dedicated all-age or family services. Rites of passage mark key family moments: infant baptism or dedication, confirmation, marriage and funerals, each supported pastorally by clergy. Many parishes run classes for parents (preparing for baptism or marriage), counselling for couples or families in difficulty, and groups specifically for children and young people, including Sunday schools, which provide religious education and a sense of community alongside the main service. This support reflects the church's understanding of itself as a community responsible for nurturing both individual believers and family life from birth through to death.
