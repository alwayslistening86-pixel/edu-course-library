# S17_Peace_and_Conflict_Victims_Pacifism_and_Interfaith - Lesson: Peace and conflict: victims of war, terrorism, pacifism, reconciliation and interfaith relations

## Goal
The learner can explain Muslim teaching on protecting victims of war and responses to terrorism, explain divergent Muslim views on pacifism and the importance of reconciliation, and explain Muslim approaches to resolving conflict with people of other religions.

## Syllabus items taught here
- I-PC.5 - Islam: victims of war and responses to terrorism: Explain Muslim teaching on protecting civilians and prisoners, and condemnation of terrorism
- I-PC.6 - Islam: pacifism, reconciliation and forgiveness: Explain divergent Muslim views on pacifism and the importance of reconciliation
- I-PC.7 - Islam: resolving conflict with people of other religions: Explain Muslim approaches to interfaith dialogue and cooperation
- Q-P2-a - Paper 2 part (a): 1-mark recognise/identify question: Answer the 1-mark AO1 question correctly

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Islamic teaching sets rules for how war should be fought and who must be protected. Where does terrorism, deliberately targeting civilians, fit against those rules?' Build the item on victims and terrorism from the answer.

#### I-PC.5 Islam: victims of war and responses to terrorism
Traditional Islamic teaching on the conduct of war requires the protection of non-combatants: civilians, women, children, the elderly, religious figures and prisoners of war must not be harmed, and property such as places of worship, crops and civilian infrastructure should not be needlessly destroyed. This reflects the requirement that war be proportionate and limited to genuine military necessity. Terrorism, defined broadly as the deliberate use of violence against civilians to create fear for political or ideological ends, is understood by the overwhelming majority of Muslims and Muslim religious authorities to violate these core Islamic principles directly, since it deliberately targets exactly those the tradition requires be protected; major Muslim organisations and scholars worldwide have repeatedly and explicitly condemned terrorism as contrary to Islamic teaching, rejecting attempts by extremist groups to justify it religiously.

#### I-PC.6 Islam: pacifism, reconciliation and forgiveness
Pacifism, the belief that violence is never justified and peaceful means should always be pursued instead, is not the dominant position in mainstream Islamic teaching, which permits defensive fighting under strict conditions; however, some Muslims argue that Islam's deeper values of mercy, patience and peace should be understood as pointing towards pacifism as an ideal, or at least towards exhausting every peaceful alternative before ever considering force, giving genuinely divergent views within the Muslim community. Reconciliation and forgiveness, resolving conflict and restoring right relationships, are highly valued in Islamic teaching, seen as spiritually superior to prolonging conflict or revenge; Muslims are encouraged to forgive when possible and to work actively towards ending hostility and rebuilding trust, treating peacemaking as a religious virtue in its own right, not merely the absence of fighting.

#### I-PC.7 Islam: resolving conflict with people of other religions
Islamic teaching encourages dialogue and cooperation with people of other faiths as a means of resolving and preventing conflict, recognising Jews and Christians in particular as 'People of the Book' who share belief in one God and revealed scripture, deserving particular respect within Islamic tradition. Many Muslim organisations and leaders actively participate in interfaith dialogue, working alongside other religious communities on shared concerns such as poverty, peacebuilding and social justice, and Muslim peacemaking initiatives include local and international interfaith projects that bring together Muslims, Christians, Jews and others to build mutual understanding and cooperate practically. This reflects a broader Islamic teaching that Allah created humanity in diverse nations and communities so that people would come to know one another, not to remain permanently divided or in conflict.

#### Q-P2-a Paper 2 part (a): 1-mark recognise/identify question
Part (a) on Paper 2 is a 1-mark AO1 question, following the same format as Paper 1's part (a): often multiple choice or a very short factual recall about Islam, for example 'Which of the following is one of the Five Pillars?' Technique: answer briefly and directly with no explanation, since none is required or rewarded at this tariff; the aim is simply to confirm accurate basic recognition before the question develops into more demanding parts.
