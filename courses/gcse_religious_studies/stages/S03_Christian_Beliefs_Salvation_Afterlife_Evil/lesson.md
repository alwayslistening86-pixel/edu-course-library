# S03_Christian_Beliefs_Salvation_Afterlife_Evil - Lesson: Christian beliefs: salvation, life after death, and the problem of evil

## Goal
The learner can explain law, sin, grace and salvation, explain divergent Christian teachings on life after death, explain the problem of evil and suffering, and explain and assess proposed solutions to it.

## Syllabus items taught here
- C-BEL.5 - Christianity: salvation: Explain law, sin, grace and the role of Christ in salvation and atonement
- C-BEL.6 - Christianity: divergent Christian beliefs about life after death: Explain divergent Christian teachings on resurrection, judgement, heaven, hell and purgatory
- C-BEL.7 - Christianity: the problem of evil and suffering: Explain the problem evil and suffering raises for belief in an omnipotent, benevolent God
- C-BEL.8 - Christianity: solutions to the problem of evil and suffering: Explain and assess biblical, theoretical and practical solutions to the problem of evil

## How to teach this
Open with: 'If God is all-powerful and completely good, why does suffering exist at all?' Hold this question open through the whole stage; it is answered, provisionally, only at the very end.

#### C-BEL.5 Christianity: salvation
Salvation is being saved from sin and its consequences and restored to a right relationship with God. Christian teaching holds that all humans sin (fall short of God's law and standard) and so are separated from God; salvation is made possible not by human effort ('law') alone but by God's grace, God's free, unearned gift of love and forgiveness, working through the Holy Spirit ('Spirit') in a believer's life. Christ's role is central: his death is understood as atonement, repairing the broken relationship between humanity and God caused by sin. John 3:16 ('For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son...') and Acts 4:12 stress that salvation comes specifically through Christ, making this belief foundational to Christian identity and practice.

#### C-BEL.6 Christianity: divergent Christian beliefs about life after death
Christians broadly agree that Jesus' resurrection guarantees some form of life after death, referenced in 2 Corinthians 5:1-10, but teachings diverge on the detail. Most Christians believe in a final judgement, when God assesses each person's life, followed by eternal reward (heaven, full presence with God) or punishment (hell, separation from God); some understand these literally as places, others more symbolically as states of being. The Catholic Church additionally teaches purgatory, a temporary state of purification for those who die in God's grace but are not yet fully ready for heaven, a belief rejected by most Protestant traditions (reflected in the 39 Articles of Religion), who hold that judgement leads directly to heaven or hell with no intermediate state.

#### C-BEL.7 Christianity: the problem of evil and suffering
The problem of evil is a challenge to Christian belief: if God is omnipotent (all-powerful) and benevolent (completely good), why does evil and suffering exist, since an all-powerful, all-good God should be both able and willing to prevent it? The specification distinguishes natural suffering (caused by nature, such as disease, earthquakes and famine) from moral suffering (caused by human choices, such as cruelty, war and crime). Psalm 103 reflects on God's mercy and compassion despite suffering. The problem is significant because it directly questions two of God's core attributes at once, and Christian theology has developed a range of responses, both biblical and philosophical, to try to reconcile God's power and goodness with the reality of suffering in the world.

#### C-BEL.8 Christianity: solutions to the problem of evil and suffering
Christians offer several types of response. Biblical: the Book of Job explores a righteous man's suffering without a full explanation, teaching trust in God's wisdom even when suffering seems unjust or unexplained. Theoretical: the 'free will' defence argues moral evil results from God giving humans genuine freedom to choose, which some misuse, while the 'vale of soul-making' idea (drawing on Psalm 119's theme of learning through hardship) argues suffering can build character, courage and compassion that could not develop otherwise. Practical: prayer and charity are offered as Christian responses that do not fully explain suffering but show faith in action, comforting sufferers and reducing suffering where possible. None of these solutions is universally accepted as fully resolving the problem, and Christians differ on which they find most convincing.
