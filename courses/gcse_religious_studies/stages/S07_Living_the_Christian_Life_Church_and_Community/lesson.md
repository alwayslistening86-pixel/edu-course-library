# S07_Living_the_Christian_Life_Church_and_Community - Lesson: Living the Christian life: festivals, mission, and the church in the local and worldwide community

## Goal
The learner can explain the significance of Christmas and Easter, explain the purpose of mission and evangelism, explain the local church's role in its community, and explain the Church's worldwide role including reconciliation, the persecuted Church and Christian Aid.

## Syllabus items taught here
- C-LIV.5 - Christianity: Christmas and Easter: Explain the significance of Advent/Christmas and Holy Week/Easter
- C-LIV.6 - Christianity: church growth, mission and evangelism: Explain the purpose of missionary and evangelistic work
- C-LIV.7 - Christianity: the role of the local church in the local community: Explain how and why the local church supports its local area
- C-LIV.8 - Christianity: the role of the Church in the worldwide community: Explain reconciliation work, the persecuted Church, and the work of Christian Aid

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Christmas and Easter are the two biggest Christian festivals. If you had to say which one matters more theologically, which would you pick, and why?' Use disagreement to open the stage.

#### C-LIV.5 Christianity: Christmas and Easter
Advent, the weeks leading up to Christmas, is a season of preparation and anticipation for the birth of Christ (the incarnation), while Christmas itself celebrates that birth, God becoming human, central to Christian belief. Holy Week, the week before Easter, commemorates Jesus' final days (Palm Sunday's entry into Jerusalem, the Last Supper on Maundy Thursday, the crucifixion on Good Friday), culminating in Easter Sunday, celebrating the resurrection; 1 Corinthians 15:12-34 stresses that the resurrection is foundational to Christian faith ('if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile'). Many Christians consider Easter theologically the more significant festival, since the resurrection, not the birth alone, proves Jesus' divine identity and guarantees salvation and eternal life, though Christmas remains hugely important culturally and devotionally.

#### C-LIV.6 Christianity: church growth, mission and evangelism
Mission and evangelism mean actively spreading the Christian message and inviting others to faith, rooted in Jesus' final instructions to his disciples, the 'Great Commission' (Mark 16:9-20, 'go into all the world and preach the gospel') and the sending of the Holy Spirit to empower witness (John 20:21-22). Churches put this into practice in divergent ways: some emphasise direct preaching, evangelistic events or door-to-door outreach; others emphasise 'living witness', quietly modelling Christian values through service and relationships, or organised social action and charity as a form of evangelism. Missionary work has historically included sending Christians to other countries to establish churches, translate scripture and provide education or medical care, though modern mission increasingly happens locally, nationally and globally at the same time, reflecting shifts in where churches are growing fastest.

#### C-LIV.7 Christianity: the role of the local church in the local community
The local church supports its area in many practical ways, reflecting the New Testament model of shared leadership and mutual care (1 Peter 5:1-4 instructs church elders to shepherd their community willingly). Local parish activities can include running food banks and support for those in poverty, youth groups and toddler groups, visiting the sick, elderly and housebound, and offering a building used by wider community groups. Ecumenism, cooperation between different Christian denominations (for example joint services or shared projects), strengthens this local presence, while outreach work, activities specifically aimed at engaging people outside the regular congregation, extends it further. The local church often functions as a hub of Christian identity and worship for its area, as well as a practical community resource open to believers and non-believers alike.

#### C-LIV.8 Christianity: the role of the Church in the worldwide community
Globally, the Church works for reconciliation, helping to heal conflict and division, drawing on 2 Corinthians and Matthew 25:31-46's teaching that caring for 'the least of these' is caring for Christ himself, and 1 Corinthians 13's teaching on the priority of love. The Church also supports the persecuted Church, Christians in parts of the world who face discrimination, violence or legal restriction for their faith, through advocacy, prayer and practical aid. Charitable work is a major expression of this worldwide role: Christian Aid, a major Christian development and relief charity, works to relieve poverty, respond to disasters and campaign for global justice, funded and supported by Christians who see such charity as a direct expression of Christian teaching on love for neighbour, wherever that neighbour lives.
