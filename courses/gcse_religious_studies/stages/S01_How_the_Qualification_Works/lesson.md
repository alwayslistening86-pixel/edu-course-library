# S01_How_the_Qualification_Works - Lesson: How GCSE Religious Studies B works: papers, assessment objectives and technique

## Goal
The learner can describe the two-paper structure and marks, distinguish AO1 from AO2, explain where SPaG marks sit, place non-religious worldviews correctly, and recognise what each command word requires.

## Syllabus items taught here
- H-STRUCT - Qualification structure: two 1h45 papers, 102 marks each, 204 total
- H-AO1 - AO1: knowledge and understanding of religion and belief: Demonstrate knowledge and understanding of religions and beliefs, including practices, sources of authority and their influence and similarities/differences
- H-AO2 - AO2: analyse and evaluate religion and belief: Analyse and evaluate aspects of religion and belief, including significance and influence, using evidence and reasoned argument
- H-SPAG - Spelling, punctuation, grammar and specialist terminology (5% of marks, on the 15-mark evaluate questions): Earn SPaG marks by using accurate religious vocabulary and correct written English on the extended evaluate answers
- H-NRW - Religion and belief in Great Britain today, including non-religious worldviews: Recognise that religion and belief in Great Britain today includes Christianity and other principal religions, and non-religious worldviews such as atheism and Humanism
- H-CMD - Command words and question technique: give/outline, explain two, explain two contrasting (with a source of authority), discuss/evaluate: Recognise what each command word requires and structure an answer of the right length and type for its marks

## How to teach this
Open with: 'This course studies two religions through two big themes: Christianity through ethics, and Islam through peace and conflict. Every single exam question will ask you to do one of two things. What do you think they are?' Build towards AO1 (know it) and AO2 (argue about it).

#### H-STRUCT Qualification structure: two 1h45 papers, 102 marks each, 204 total
GCSE Religious Studies B (1RB0) is assessed by two written exam papers, each 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 102 marks (204 marks in total), with no coursework or non-exam assessment. Paper 1 assesses Area of Study 1 (in this course, Religion and Ethics studied through Christianity); Paper 2 assesses Area of Study 2 (Religion, Peace and Conflict studied through Islam). Each paper has four questions, one per content section of that area of study, so Paper 1's four questions cover Christian Beliefs, Marriage and the Family, Living the Christian Life, and Matters of Life and Death, and Paper 2's four questions cover Muslim Beliefs, Crime and Punishment, Living the Muslim Life, and Peace and Conflict. There is no tiering (Foundation/Higher): every candidate sits the same papers.

#### H-AO1 AO1: knowledge and understanding of religion and belief
AO1 asks the learner to demonstrate knowledge and understanding of religions and beliefs: their beliefs, practices and sources of wisdom and authority (scripture, tradition, key teachings), how beliefs and practices influence individuals, communities and societies, and similarities and differences within and between religions and beliefs. AO1 is tested through the shorter parts of each question: (a) 1-mark recognition, (b) 2-mark 'give two', (c) 4-mark 'explain two', and (d)(i) 5-mark 'explain two contrasting beliefs', which specifically requires reference to a source of wisdom and authority (usually scripture) to earn full marks. AO1 rewards accurate, specific, well-selected factual content rather than opinion.

#### H-AO2 AO2: analyse and evaluate religion and belief
AO2 asks the learner to analyse and evaluate aspects of religion and belief, including their significance and influence, using evidence and reasoned argument. AO2 is tested through the final, extended part of every question, (d)(ii), worth 12 marks (plus SPaG on two of the four questions per paper), which presents a statement and asks the learner to discuss it, referring to religious arguments (which may draw on more than one viewpoint within the religion) and non-religious arguments, and to reach a justified conclusion. A strong AO2 answer weighs both sides using specific evidence rather than simply asserting an opinion, and states clearly, with reasons, which side it finds more convincing overall.

#### H-SPAG Spelling, punctuation, grammar and use of specialist terminology (5% of marks, on the 15-mark evaluate questions)
Five per cent of the qualification's marks are awarded for spelling, punctuation, grammar and the accurate use of specialist religious terminology, added on top of the 12-mark AO2 evaluate question on two of the four questions in each paper (making those two questions worth 15 marks rather than 12). To earn these marks, the learner should write in full sentences and paragraphs, spell key terms correctly (for example, 'Eucharist', 'Shahadah', 'Akhirah'), and use specialist vocabulary accurately and appropriately rather than avoiding it.

#### H-NRW Religion and belief in Great Britain today, including non-religious worldviews
The specification expects the learner to recognise that religion and belief in Great Britain today is diverse, including the principal religions (Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism, Buddhism, Sikhism) and also non-religious worldviews such as atheism (the position that God or gods do not exist) and Humanism (a non-religious ethical worldview holding that people can live good, meaningful lives through reason, empathy and evidence, without belief in the supernatural). Several topics across both papers, especially in ethics (marriage, contraception, divorce, abortion, euthanasia) and Islam's Crime and Punishment and Peace and Conflict sections, specifically require the learner to set out and compare a named non-religious, typically atheist or Humanist, viewpoint alongside religious teaching.

#### H-CMD Command words and question technique: give/outline, explain two, explain two contrasting (with a source of authority), discuss/evaluate
Each command word signals a different length and type of answer. 'Give' or 'name' (1-2 marks) wants a brief, accurate fact with no explanation. 'Explain two ways/reasons' (4 marks) wants two developed points, each with a reason or example, not just a list. 'Explain two contrasting religious beliefs about X. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority' (5 marks) wants two genuinely different beliefs (for example, from different denominations or traditions) each supported by scripture, tradition or a named teaching. 'Discuss two contrasting views on the statement... refer to religious arguments... refer to non-religious arguments... reach a justified conclusion' (12+3 marks) wants a balanced, evidenced argument ending in a clear, supported personal judgement, never a one-sided rant or an answer that avoids taking a position.

## Explicitly not here
The content of Christianity and Islam themselves, covered from the next stage onward.
