# S08_Matters_of_Life_and_Death_Part_1 - Lesson: Matters of life and death: the universe, sanctity of life, science, and abortion

## Goal
The learner can explain scientific and Christian accounts of the universe's origin and value, explain why Christians hold life to be sacred, explain Christian responses to evolution, and explain divergent Christian and non-religious positions on abortion.

## Syllabus items taught here
- C-MLD.1 - Christianity: the origins and value of the universe: Explain scientific and Christian accounts of the universe's origin and its value
- C-MLD.2 - Christianity: the sanctity of life: Explain why Christians hold human life to be sacred
- C-MLD.3 - Christianity: scientific and non-religious explanations for life: Explain Christian responses to evolution and non-religious accounts of life
- C-MLD.4 - Christianity: abortion: Explain divergent Christian pro-life/pro-choice teachings and non-religious arguments

## How to teach this
Open with: 'Science and religion are sometimes presented as enemies. On the origin of the universe and of life, do Christians and scientists have to disagree?' Keep returning to this across the stage.

#### C-MLD.1 Christianity: the origins and value of the universe
The Big Bang theory, the scientific account that the universe began around 13.8 billion years ago from an extremely dense, hot state and has been expanding ever since, was significantly developed by Georges Lemaître, a Catholic priest and physicist, showing science and Christian faith need not conflict for many believers. Christians commonly hold that Genesis 1-2's creation account and scientific explanations answer different kinds of question: science explains how the universe developed physically, while Genesis explains why it exists and its meaning, as God's deliberate, valuable creation. Christians generally respond that treating the universe merely as a resource or commodity to exploit denies its God-given value and humanity's proper responsibility, discussed further under stewardship, to care for creation rather than simply use it up.

#### C-MLD.2 Christianity: the sanctity of life
Sanctity of life is the belief that human life is sacred, holy and of unique worth because it is created and given by God, not simply valuable for what a person can do or achieve. This is grounded in Genesis 1-3's teaching that humans are made 'in the image of God' (imago Dei), giving every person inherent dignity regardless of ability, age or circumstance. This belief underpins Christian positions across many life-and-death issues: it is a central reason many Christians oppose or place strict limits on abortion and euthanasia, since both involve ending a human life that Christians believe belongs, ultimately, to God rather than to the individual alone. Sanctity of life remains foundational to Christian ethics today, shaping how Christians think about the beginning and end of life.

#### C-MLD.3 Christianity: scientific and non-religious explanations for life
Evolution by natural selection, Charles Darwin's scientific explanation for how species, including humans, developed gradually over immense time through variation and survival of the fittest, initially seemed to many nineteenth-century Christians to challenge Genesis' account of a direct, instantaneous creation. Christian responses today vary: some Christians accept evolution as the scientific 'how' alongside Genesis as the theological 'why', seeing no real conflict; a minority hold a more literal reading of Genesis and reject evolution; others take intermediate positions. Church statements, including motions passed by bodies such as the Diocese of Manchester's synod, have affirmed that evolutionary science and Christian faith in God as creator can be held together, reflecting the now-common view among mainstream Christian denominations that these are complementary rather than competing explanations.

#### C-MLD.4 Christianity: abortion
Abortion is the deliberate ending of a pregnancy. Christian teaching diverges sharply: pro-life Christians, drawing on sanctity of life and Genesis' 'image of God' teaching, and echoed in the Catholic Humanae Vitae's teaching on the sacredness of life, generally oppose abortion, holding that human life and its value begin at conception, so ending a pregnancy takes an innocent human life. Pro-choice Christians place more weight on the wellbeing, circumstances and conscience of the pregnant woman, sometimes applying situation ethics to argue abortion can, in some circumstances, be the most loving available option. Non-religious, including atheist and Humanist, arguments generally emphasise bodily autonomy (a person's right to control decisions about their own body) and quality of life considerations; Christians who oppose abortion respond that this does not outweigh the sanctity of the life of the unborn child.
