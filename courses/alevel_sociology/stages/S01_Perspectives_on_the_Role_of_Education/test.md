## Test items
1. Outline two differences between the functionalist and Marxist views of the role of education. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section A style] Applying material from the Item below and your knowledge, evaluate the Marxist view of the role of education in capitalist society.

Item: One study found that pupils' experience of school -- being told what to do, working for external rewards like grades rather than intrinsic interest, and having little control over the curriculum -- closely resembled what many of the same pupils later described experiencing as employees. [30 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B2 functionalists see education as serving society as a whole/promoting value consensus and meritocracy genuinely (e.g. Durkheim/Parsons), developed; B2 Marxists see education as serving the ruling class/capitalism and meritocracy as a myth disguising reproduced class inequality (e.g. Bowles and Gintis), developed.

Level 3 (13-18) some effective application of the item, but only in parts of the answer; generally accurate knowledge, though with gaps or occasional inaccuracy; analysis and evaluation are present but uneven, one-sided in places, or asserted rather than argued. Credit: for the Marxist view -- explicit use of the item as evidence for the correspondence principle (Bowles and Gintis), the myth of meritocracy, Althusser's ideological state apparatus; against/evaluation -- functionalist alternative (education genuinely does teach useful skills and social solidarity), the New Right's marketisation critique of state-run education, criticism that Bowles and Gintis's US-based study is dated and correlational (does not prove causation), postmodernist/late-modern critiques that education is now too diverse/fragmented for one 'correspondence' to hold, feminist critique that Marxism ignores gender inequality in education; balanced judgement.
