# S01_Perspectives_on_the_Role_of_Education - Lesson: Functionalist, Marxist and New Right perspectives on education

## Goal
The learner explains the functionalist, Marxist and New Right views of the role and functions of education, and how each links education to the economy and the class structure.

## Syllabus items taught here
- EDU.1 - Functionalist, Marxist and New Right perspectives on the role and functions of education

## How to teach this
Ask: is school mainly there to teach you things, to sort you into a job, or to make you a certain kind of citizen -- and would a functionalist, a Marxist and a New Right thinker each answer differently? Every named theorist and study in this course (e.g. Durkheim, Parsons, Bowles and Gintis, Althusser, Bourdieu, Bernstein, Rosenthal and Jacobson, Ball, Willis, Mac an Ghaill, Gillborn, Merton, Cohen, Cloward and Ohlin, Becker, Lemert, Chambliss, Taylor Walton and Young, Lea and Young, Wilson and Kelling, Murray, Heidensohn, Pollak, Messerschmidt, Situ and Emmons, Green and Ward, Foucault, Miers, Murdock, Engels, Zaretsky, Delphy and Leonard, Young and Willmott, Oakley, Duncombe and Marsden, Bott, Pahl, Aries, Postman, Palmer, Smart, Giddens, Stacey, Weber, Troeltsch, Niebuhr, Wallis, Heelas, Davie, Bruce, Wilson, Berger, Stark and Bainbridge, Comte, Popper, Kuhn, Mead, Blumer, Lyotard, Baudrillard, Beck, Gouldner) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of sociology, checked against real published sociological knowledge and AQA's own specification content when this course was built -- present it as real scholarship, never as illustrative fiction, and correct the learner explicitly if they misattribute a theory or misstate a finding. Theory and Methods (3.1.3/3.3.2) is genuinely common content examined on both Paper 1 (10 marks) and Paper 3 (30 marks) -- teach it once (S07-S11) and remind the learner it will reappear, at a different tariff, on both papers. AQA's extended-writing items (4/6-mark 'Outline...' items are point-based, scored per developed reason; 10/20/30-mark items are marked by best-fit levels of response, not summed points) -- teach the learner to write in continuous, structured prose for 10-mark-and-above items, always explicitly using the given Item's own detail where a question asks the learner to apply material from it, and to build toward a substantiated evaluative conclusion on 20/30-mark essays rather than a simple list of points. Some content in this course (e.g. current UK divorce-rate or migration figures) is genuinely time-bound; where flagged, check the latest ONS release rather than relying on a fixed number, since the sociological explanation/theory matters far more for the exam than any single current statistic.

#### EDU.1 Functionalist, Marxist and New Right perspectives on the role and functions of education
**Perspectives on the role and functions of education.** Functionalism (Durkheim, Parsons): education performs positive functions for society as a whole. Durkheim argued education creates social solidarity (a shared sense of belonging, e.g. through subjects like history) and teaches specialist skills needed for the increasingly complex division of labour in industrial society. Parsons described school as a 'bridge' between the particularistic standards of the family (judged by who you are) and the universalistic, meritocratic standards of wider society (judged by what you achieve); school is a 'focal socialising agency', instilling the shared values of achievement and equality of opportunity. Davis and Moore's role allocation theory (part of the functionalist account) argues education sifts and sorts individuals by ability into the most functionally important jobs, justifying unequal rewards. Marxism (Bowles and Gintis, Althusser): education instead serves the interests of capitalism and reproduces class inequality. Bowles and Gintis's 'correspondence principle' argues the social relationships of school (hierarchy, external rewards, fragmented subjects, lack of control over one's own learning) directly mirror the social relationships of the capitalist workplace, preparing working-class pupils psychologically to accept exploitation; their 'myth of meritocracy' argues education legitimates inequality by making it look as if success is due to ability and effort, disguising the real role of class background. Althusser described education as part of the 'ideological state apparatus', reproducing capitalist ideology (rather than using force, like the 'repressive state apparatus') so the working class accept the system as fair. The New Right (Chubb and Moe): shares the functionalist view that education should serve economy and society, but argues state education has failed to do so efficiently; favours marketisation -- competition between schools, parental choice and reduced state control -- as the way to raise standards, on the assumption that schools run like businesses respond better to consumer (parent/pupil) demand than a centrally planned system.
