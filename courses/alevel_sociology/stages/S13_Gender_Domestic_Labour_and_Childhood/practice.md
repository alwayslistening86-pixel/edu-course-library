# S13_Gender_Domestic_Labour_and_Childhood - Practice: Gender roles, domestic labour and power; the social construction of childhood

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two ways in which Oakley's research challenged the 'march of progress' view of the symmetrical family. [4 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by childhood as a social construction. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 found housework and childcare remained mainly women's responsibility even when women also had paid jobs outside the home, developed; B2 introduced the concept of the 'dual burden' (women carrying paid work and the bulk of domestic labour), directly contradicting the claim of genuinely joint/equal roles, developed.
2. [3] B1 the idea that childhood, though grounded in a real biological period of immaturity, is defined/understood differently across different times and cultures rather than being fixed; B1 example: Aries's argument that medieval children were seen as 'mini adults' with no distinct concept of childhood; B1 shows today's child-centred, protected view of childhood is a specific, historically recent social construction, not a universal fact.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
