## Test items
1. Outline three consequences of an ageing population for family life in the UK. [6 marks]
2. [Paper 2, Section A style, no item] Outline and explain two reasons for the fall in the death rate in the UK since 1900. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B2 rising demand for adult children (the 'sandwich generation') to care for elderly parents while still supporting their own children, developed; B2 more multi-generational households or elderly people living alone (rising single-person households among the very elderly), developed; B2 increased pressure on pensions/health and social care that can affect family finances and decisions (e.g. delaying retirement, grandparents providing childcare to support working-age children), developed; credit any three genuine consequences.
2. [10] Mark out of 10 using AQA's band descriptors for a 10-mark 'Outline and explain two ...' question (no item; AO1 knowledge + AO2 explanation of each reason; no evaluation required): Band 2 (6-10) both reasons are accurate and explicit, each explained with developed sociological reasoning and correctly used concepts/theory/evidence; a partial Band 2 answer develops one reason fully and the other more thinly. Band 1 (1-5) knowledge of relevant material is present but explanation is limited, one reason is missing or barely developed, or the answer is largely a list of undeveloped assertions. 0 marks: no relevant content, or knowledge only with no attempt at explanation. Credit reasons such as: improvements in medicine and public health (vaccination, antibiotics, sanitation) reducing deaths from infectious disease, and improved nutrition/living standards and safer working conditions reducing mortality; each reason must be explained with developed sociological reasoning, not just named.
