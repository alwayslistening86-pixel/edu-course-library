# S09_Choosing_Methods_Theoretical_Practical_and_Ethical - Practice: Theoretical, practical and ethical factors in choosing a method

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two ethical issues a sociologist must consider before carrying out research. [4 marks]
2. Explain the difference between validity and reliability in sociological research. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 informed consent (participants understanding and freely agreeing to take part), developed; B2 protection from harm/confidentiality (avoiding physical, psychological or reputational harm, and safeguarding identifiable data), developed; credit any two genuine ethical issues.
2. [3] B1 validity is whether a method genuinely captures the true, authentic picture of what it claims to study; B1 reliability is whether a method, repeated under the same conditions, would produce the same results; B1 correctly notes the trade-off -- methods that raise one (e.g. unstructured interviews for validity) often lower the other (reliability).

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
