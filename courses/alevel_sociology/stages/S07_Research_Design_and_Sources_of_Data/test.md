## Test items
1. Outline two strengths of stratified sampling compared with a simple opportunity sample. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 1, Section C style, no item] Outline and explain two reasons why some sociologists choose to use unstructured interviews in their research. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B2 stratified sampling divides the population into relevant strata (e.g. age, class, gender) and samples proportionately within each, improving representativeness of the whole population, developed; B2 an opportunity sample (whoever is available/convenient) risks systematic bias (e.g. over-representing people in a particular place/time), which stratified sampling is designed to reduce, developed.
2. [10] Mark out of 10 using AQA's band descriptors for a 10-mark 'Outline and explain two ...' question (no item; AO1 knowledge + AO2 explanation of each reason; no evaluation required): Band 2 (6-10) both reasons are accurate and explicit, each explained with developed sociological reasoning and correctly used concepts/theory/evidence; a partial Band 2 answer develops one reason fully and the other more thinly. Band 1 (1-5) knowledge of relevant material is present but explanation is limited, one reason is missing or barely developed, or the answer is largely a list of undeveloped assertions. 0 marks: no relevant content, or knowledge only with no attempt at explanation. Credit reasons such as: higher validity/richer, more authentic data reflecting the respondent's own meanings (favoured by interpretivists), and the ability to build rapport and probe/follow up unexpected answers, particularly useful for sensitive or complex topics; each reason must be explained with developed sociological reasoning.
