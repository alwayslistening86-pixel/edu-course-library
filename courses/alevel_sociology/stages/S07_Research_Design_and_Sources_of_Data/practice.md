# S07_Research_Design_and_Sources_of_Data - Practice: Quantitative/qualitative methods, research design and sampling; questionnaires and interviews

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two differences between structured and unstructured interviews. [4 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by operationalising a concept. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 structured interviews use fixed questions in a fixed order/resemble a questionnaire read aloud, giving high reliability/comparability but limited depth, developed; B2 unstructured interviews are a guided conversation allowing probing/follow-up, giving higher validity/depth but lower reliability and harder generalisability, developed.
2. [3] B1 turning an abstract sociological concept (e.g. social class, deviance) into something that can be measured/observed in research; B1 example: operationalising social class via occupation, income or postcode; B1 interpretivists criticise this as potentially imposing the researcher's own definition rather than capturing respondents' own meanings.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
