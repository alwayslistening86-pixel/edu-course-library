# S23_Victims_and_Measuring_Crime - Practice: Victims of crime, and measuring crime

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two criticisms of positivist victimology. [4 marks]
2. Explain why victim surveys generally produce higher estimates of crime than official police-recorded statistics. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 risks 'victim blaming' by focusing on victim proneness/lifestyle factors rather than the offender's responsibility, developed; B2 ignores wider structural factors (power, poverty, inequality) that critical victimology argues better explain patterns of victimisation, developed.
2. [3] B1 victim surveys ask a randomly sampled group directly about their experience of crime, regardless of whether it was reported to police; B1 this captures crimes never reported to or recorded by police (part of the 'dark figure'); B1 official statistics only count crimes both reported to and then recorded by the police, systematically undercounting the real total.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
