## Test items
1. Outline two problems in measuring the true extent of green crime. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 3, Section A style] Applying material from the Item below and your knowledge, analyse two reasons why self-report studies may reveal a different pattern of offending from official statistics.

Item: Self-report studies ask individuals, usually anonymously, to disclose their own offending directly to researchers, rather than relying on what has come to police attention. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)

[4] B2 much environmentally harmful activity is not defined as illegal in the jurisdiction where it occurs, so a strictly legal definition/official statistics will miss it entirely (Situ and Emmons's harm-based critique), developed; B2 powerful state and corporate perpetrators are often under-investigated/under-prosecuted relative to the real harm caused, similar to white-collar crime generally, developed.

Credit reasons grounded in the item: capturing offending that never came to police attention at all (part of the 'dark figure'), and potentially revealing offending among groups (e.g. higher-status individuals, or particular age groups) under-represented in official statistics because they are less likely to be caught/labelled (linking to S19-S20); each analysed, with a caveat about self-report studies' own reliability/validity limits (honesty, sample representativeness).
