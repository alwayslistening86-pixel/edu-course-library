# S19_Labelling_Marxist_Criminology_and_Realism - Practice: Labelling theory, Marxist criminology, and left/right realism

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two features of Becker's labelling theory of deviance. [4 marks]
2. Explain the difference between left realism and right realism as explanations of crime. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 no act is inherently deviant; it becomes deviant through being successfully labelled so by others (moral entrepreneurs), developed; B2 who gets labelled depends on social factors (class, ethnicity, appearance, prior record) as much as the act itself, developed.
2. [3] B1 left realism explains crime through relative deprivation, subculture and marginalisation, taking working-class victims' experience of crime seriously (Lea and Young); B1 right realism emphasises individual rational choice and visible disorder/broken windows inviting further crime (Wilson and Kelling), favouring zero-tolerance policing; B1 correctly contrasts their different views of the underlying cause (structural/relative deprivation versus individual choice/opportunity).

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
