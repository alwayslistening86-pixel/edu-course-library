# S10_Positivism_Interpretivism_and_Sociological_Theory - Practice: Positivism, interpretivism, sociology as science, and consensus/conflict/structural/social action theories

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two features of a positivist approach to sociological research. [4 marks]
2. Explain what Weber meant by Verstehen. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 favours quantitative, large-scale, reliable and representative methods (e.g. statistics, structured questionnaires), developed; B2 aims to identify objective social facts/causal laws by treating society similarly to the natural sciences, developed.
2. [3] B1 empathetic understanding of the subjective meaning an individual gives to their own action; B1 contrasted with positivism's focus on external, observable, measurable behaviour alone; B1 requires qualitative methods (e.g. unstructured interviews, participant observation) capable of accessing meaning, not just counting behaviour.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
