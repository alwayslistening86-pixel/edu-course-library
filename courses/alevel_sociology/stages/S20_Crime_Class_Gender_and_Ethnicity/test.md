## Test items
1. Outline two reasons why official statistics may overstate the extent of working-class crime relative to white-collar/corporate crime. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 3, Section A style] Applying material from the Item below and your knowledge, analyse two reasons for the over-representation of some minority ethnic groups at various stages of the criminal justice system.

Item: Statistics on stop and search, arrest and sentencing have shown some minority ethnic groups over-represented relative to their share of the population, prompting debate over how far this reflects real differences in offending and how far it reflects how different groups are treated by the criminal justice system. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B2 policing resources and public/media attention are disproportionately directed at visible 'street' crime associated with working-class areas, developed; B2 white-collar/corporate crime is harder to detect, is investigated by different (often less resourced or more lenient) regulatory bodies, and is comparatively under-prosecuted relative to its real harm, developed.

Credit reasons grounded in the item's own framing (offending versus treatment): relative deprivation/marginalisation as a genuine cause of higher offending for some groups (left realism); disproportionate stop-and-search and possible institutional bias in policing/charging/sentencing (echoing the Macpherson Report finding); each analysed, not just asserted.
