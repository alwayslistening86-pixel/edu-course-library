# S20_Crime_Class_Gender_and_Ethnicity - Practice: Crime and social class, gender and ethnicity

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two explanations for why women are convicted of crime at much lower rates than men. [4 marks]
2. Explain what is meant by the chivalry thesis. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 differential socialisation: girls traditionally socialised toward conformity and given less opportunity/expectation for risk-taking, developed; B2 control theory (Heidensohn): women's behaviour is more closely controlled at home, in public and at work, reducing opportunity to offend, developed; (or) the chivalry thesis -- credit any two genuine explanations, developed.
2. [3] B1 the argument (Pollak) that the criminal justice system treats female offenders more leniently than male offenders for comparable offences; B1 this would mean official statistics understate the real amount of female offending; B1 the thesis is contested -- some research suggests women can instead be treated more harshly for offences seen as breaking gender-role expectations ('doubly deviant').

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
