# S03_Ethnicity_Differences_and_School_Processes - Lesson: Ethnicity differences in achievement, and teacher/pupil relationships and labelling

## Goal
The learner explains external and internal explanations for ethnic differences in educational achievement, and how labelling and teacher/pupil interaction can produce a self-fulfilling prophecy.

## Syllabus items taught here
- EDU.4 - Ethnicity differences in achievement: external and internal factors, including institutional racism and the ethnocentric curriculum
- EDU.5 - Teacher/pupil relationships, labelling and the self-fulfilling prophecy

## How to teach this
Ask: if two pupils get identical grades on a piece of work, could a teacher's unconscious assumptions about who they are still change how that pupil is treated afterwards? Every named theorist and study in this course (e.g. Durkheim, Parsons, Bowles and Gintis, Althusser, Bourdieu, Bernstein, Rosenthal and Jacobson, Ball, Willis, Mac an Ghaill, Gillborn, Merton, Cohen, Cloward and Ohlin, Becker, Lemert, Chambliss, Taylor Walton and Young, Lea and Young, Wilson and Kelling, Murray, Heidensohn, Pollak, Messerschmidt, Situ and Emmons, Green and Ward, Foucault, Miers, Murdock, Engels, Zaretsky, Delphy and Leonard, Young and Willmott, Oakley, Duncombe and Marsden, Bott, Pahl, Aries, Postman, Palmer, Smart, Giddens, Stacey, Weber, Troeltsch, Niebuhr, Wallis, Heelas, Davie, Bruce, Wilson, Berger, Stark and Bainbridge, Comte, Popper, Kuhn, Mead, Blumer, Lyotard, Baudrillard, Beck, Gouldner) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of sociology, checked against real published sociological knowledge and AQA's own specification content when this course was built -- present it as real scholarship, never as illustrative fiction, and correct the learner explicitly if they misattribute a theory or misstate a finding. #### EDU.4 Ethnicity differences in achievement: external and internal factors, including institutional racism and the ethnocentric curriculum
**Ethnicity differences in achievement.** The pattern is complex and varies by specific ethnic group, not uniform across a single category: some minority ethnic groups (e.g. Chinese and Indian pupils) have, on average, outperformed the White British average, while others (e.g. Black Caribbean and Gypsy/Roma/Traveller pupils) have on average underperformed relative to it -- so explanations must avoid treating 'ethnic minorities' as one group. External factors: material deprivation -- some minority ethnic groups are disproportionately represented in poverty and disadvantaged housing, intersecting with the material-deprivation explanations in S02; cultural factors -- Driver and Ballard highlighted variation in family structure and attitudes to education across ethnic groups, though such 'cultural deficit' explanations are criticised for stereotyping and ignoring structural racism; racism in wider society limiting parents' job opportunities and therefore family income and resources. Internal factors: labelling and teacher racism -- Gillborn and Youdell found some teachers held stereotypical, often unconscious, expectations of Black pupils' behaviour and ability that could produce a self-fulfilling prophecy and lead to disproportionate discipline/exclusion; institutional racism -- Gillborn's concept of an 'A-to-C economy' (schools under pressure from league tables prioritising pupils likely to get grade C/borderline pupils) can disadvantage pupils already labelled as lower-ability, disproportionately from some minority groups; the ethnocentric curriculum -- a National Curriculum historically centred on White British/Eurocentric culture and history can alienate some minority ethnic pupils and fail to reflect their heritage; setting/streaming can compound labelling, similar to the class-based mechanism in S02. Wright's classroom observation research found some teachers held stereotypical assumptions about Asian pupils' language ability (assuming poor English even when fluent), showing labelling can misfire even where the stereotype is intended as sympathetic.

#### EDU.5 Teacher/pupil relationships, labelling and the self-fulfilling prophecy
**Teacher/pupil relationships and labelling.** Labelling theory (drawn from interactionist sociology, applied to education by Hargreaves, Becker and others) argues teachers do not treat pupils as blank slates but classify them using typifications -- often based on class, gender, ethnicity, accent, appearance or behaviour in early encounters -- forming an 'ideal pupil' image against which real pupils are judged. Becker's early research on US high-school teachers found teachers judged pupils against an image of the 'ideal pupil' shaped by middle-class characteristics of work, conduct and appearance, disadvantaging working-class pupils who did not fit it. Once a label is applied, it can shape a teacher's expectations and behaviour toward that pupil (e.g. more encouragement, harder questions, more patience for a pupil labelled bright; less attention, lower expectations, quicker discipline for one labelled difficult), which the pupil may then internalise, changing their own self-concept and effort -- a self-fulfilling prophecy. Rosenthal and Jacobson's Pygmalion in the Classroom (1968) is the classic, if methodologically contested, demonstration: pupils randomly identified to teachers as likely 'intellectual spurters' (with no real basis) showed measurably greater IQ gains a year later than the control group, suggesting teacher expectation alone can affect real outcomes -- criticised for ethical concerns (deceiving teachers) and for the difficulty of proving expectation, rather than other factors, caused the gain. Labelling is not necessarily accurate or fixed: pupils can resist labels, and later research (e.g. Woods) shows pupils actively negotiate and sometimes reject the identities teachers assign them, rather than passively accepting a self-fulfilling prophecy -- a key qualification interactionists themselves make.
