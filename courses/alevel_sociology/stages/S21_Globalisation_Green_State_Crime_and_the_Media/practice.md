# S21_Globalisation_Green_State_Crime_and_the_Media - Practice: Globalisation and crime, green crime, state crime, and crime and the media

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two features of green crime, using Situ and Emmons's approach. [4 marks]
2. Explain why state crime is considered uniquely difficult to detect, prosecute or hold accountable. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 defines crime by real environmental harm caused, not only by whether an act is technically illegal in a given jurisdiction (a 'harm-based' rather than strictly legal definition), developed; B2 distinguishes primary green crimes (direct environmental harm, e.g. pollution) from secondary green crimes (harm from breaking rules meant to regulate/prevent environmental damage, e.g. illegal dumping), developed.
2. [3] B1 the state is normally the body expected to define and enforce criminal law itself; B1 when the state (or its agencies) is the perpetrator, ordinary domestic criminal justice mechanisms are typically controlled by that same state, creating a conflict of interest; B1 accountability often instead requires international law/institutions (e.g. the International Criminal Court) or investigative journalism/whistleblowing to expose.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
