## How to teach this
Ask: is prison mainly meant to punish, to deter, to protect the public, to reform the offender, or to make a symbolic statement about right and wrong -- and can one system really do all of these at once? Every named theorist and study in this course (e.g. Durkheim, Parsons, Bowles and Gintis, Althusser, Bourdieu, Bernstein, Rosenthal and Jacobson, Ball, Willis, Mac an Ghaill, Gillborn, Merton, Cohen, Cloward and Ohlin, Becker, Lemert, Chambliss, Taylor Walton and Young, Lea and Young, Wilson and Kelling, Murray, Heidensohn, Pollak, Messerschmidt, Situ and Emmons, Green and Ward, Foucault, Miers, Murdock, Engels, Zaretsky, Delphy and Leonard, Young and Willmott, Oakley, Duncombe and Marsden, Bott, Pahl, Aries, Postman, Palmer, Smart, Giddens, Stacey, Weber, Troeltsch, Niebuhr, Wallis, Heelas, Davie, Bruce, Wilson, Berger, Stark and Bainbridge, Comte, Popper, Kuhn, Mead, Blumer, Lyotard, Baudrillard, Beck, Gouldner) is a genuine, correctly attributed piece of sociology, checked against real published sociological knowledge and AQA's own specification content when this course was built -- present it as real scholarship, never as illustrative fiction, and correct the learner explicitly if they misattribute a theory or misstate a finding. Theory and Methods (3.1.3/3.3.2) is genuinely common content examined on both Paper 1 (10 marks) and Paper 3 (30 marks) -- teach it once (S07-S11) and remind the learner it will reappear, at a different tariff, on both papers. #### CD.9 Crime control, surveillance, prevention and punishment
**Crime control, surveillance, prevention and punishment.** Situational crime prevention (Clarke) focuses on reducing the opportunity for a specific crime in a specific setting (e.g. target-hardening -- better locks, CCTV, lighting) rather than addressing offenders' underlying motivation, drawing on rational choice theory's assumption that offenders weigh up risk/reward before acting (linking to right realism, S19); criticised for potentially displacing rather than reducing crime overall (crime simply moves to a softer target) and for not addressing root causes. Environmental crime prevention (Wilson and Kelling's broken windows thesis, S19) argues visibly controlling minor disorder/signs of decay prevents escalation to more serious crime, underpinning zero-tolerance policing strategies. Social/community crime prevention instead targets the root socio-economic causes of offending (e.g. poverty, poor housing, lack of opportunity), consistent with left realist policy recommendations (S19). Surveillance: Foucault's concept of the panopticon (a prison design allowing constant potential observation of inmates who can never be sure if they are being watched, producing self-discipline) is used more broadly by sociologists as a metaphor for how modern societies increasingly govern behaviour through the mere possibility of being watched (CCTV, digital surveillance, data tracking) rather than only through direct, physical punishment -- part of a wider shift Foucault termed from sovereign power (spectacular, physical punishment of the body, e.g. historical public execution) to disciplinary power (subtler, internalised self-regulation). Punishment: sociologists distinguish several justifications/functions of punishment -- retribution (punishing wrongdoing as deserved, backward-looking), deterrence (discouraging future offending, by the individual or others, forward-looking), rehabilitation (reforming the offender), public protection (incapacitation, e.g. imprisonment physically preventing further offending while detained), and, per Durkheim, punishment's function of reaffirming society's shared moral boundaries (linking to CD.1's boundary-maintenance argument) regardless of its practical effect on the individual offender; Marxists (Rusche and Kirchheimer's classic historical argument) note that the form punishment takes has often tracked the labour needs of the prevailing economic system (e.g. historical use of transportation/forced labour), suggesting punishment serves ruling-class economic interests as much as any purely moral or purely reformist purpose.
