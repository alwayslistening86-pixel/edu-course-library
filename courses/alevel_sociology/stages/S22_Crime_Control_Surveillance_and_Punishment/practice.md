# S22_Crime_Control_Surveillance_and_Punishment - Practice: Crime control, surveillance, prevention and punishment

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two problems with using CCTV and target-hardening as ways of preventing crime. [4 marks]
2. Explain what Foucault meant by the panopticon. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 crime may simply be displaced to a softer, unprotected target rather than genuinely reduced overall, developed; B2 does not address the underlying causes/motivations for offending (e.g. poverty, relative deprivation), developed.
2. [3] B1 a prison design allowing constant potential (not necessarily actual) observation of inmates; B1 inmates internalise the possibility of being watched and self-discipline their behaviour as a result; B1 used more broadly as a metaphor for modern surveillance-based social control (e.g. CCTV, digital tracking) governing behaviour through the possibility of being watched rather than direct physical force.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
