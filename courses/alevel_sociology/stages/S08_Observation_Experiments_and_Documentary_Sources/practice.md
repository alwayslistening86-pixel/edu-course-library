# S08_Observation_Experiments_and_Documentary_Sources - Practice: Observation, experiments, documents and official statistics

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two strengths of covert participant observation. [4 marks]
2. Explain one criticism of using official statistics on crime as a valid measure of the real amount of crime. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 very high validity: the researcher sees genuine, unaffected behaviour since the group does not know it is being studied (avoids the observer effect), developed; B2 can access secretive or hard-to-reach groups (e.g. gangs, cults) who would refuse overt research or a formal interview, developed.
2. [3] B1 official crime statistics only record crimes that are reported to and recorded by the police; B1 this leaves a substantial 'dark figure' of unrecorded crime, meaning statistics reflect recording practices/priorities as much as actual offending; B1 example (e.g. under-reporting of domestic violence, or police recording priorities shifting statistics without any real change in offending).

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
