## Test items
1. Outline two practical problems of using laboratory experiments to study social behaviour. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 3, Section B style, no item] Outline and explain two reasons why some sociologists prefer to use documents rather than conducting primary research. [10 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B2 many forms of real social behaviour cannot ethically or practically be manipulated/created within a lab setting, developed; B2 the artificial lab setting risks low ecological validity/the Hawthorne effect, so behaviour observed may not reflect how people act in real life, developed.
2. [10] Mark out of 10 using AQA's band descriptors for a 10-mark 'Outline and explain two ...' question (no item; AO1 knowledge + AO2 explanation of each reason; no evaluation required): Band 2 (6-10) both reasons are accurate and explicit, each explained with developed sociological reasoning and correctly used concepts/theory/evidence; a partial Band 2 answer develops one reason fully and the other more thinly. Band 1 (1-5) knowledge of relevant material is present but explanation is limited, one reason is missing or barely developed, or the answer is largely a list of undeveloped assertions. 0 marks: no relevant content, or knowledge only with no attempt at explanation. Credit reasons such as: access to the past/otherwise inaccessible settings where primary research is impossible, and being unobtrusive (no risk of the researcher's presence affecting the data, unlike interviews/observation); each must be explained with developed sociological reasoning, e.g. referencing Scott's criteria of authenticity/credibility/representativeness/meaning where relevant.
