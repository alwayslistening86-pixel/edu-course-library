# S18_Functionalist_Strain_and_Subcultural_Theories - Practice: Functionalist and strain theories, and subcultural theories of crime

## Goal
Low-stakes practice: the learner attempts each question with a full, developed answer before seeing the model. Mistakes are expected and corrected here, before any grading.

## Practice items
1. Outline two functions of crime according to Durkheim. [4 marks]
2. Explain Merton's concept of 'innovation' as a response to strain. [3 marks]

## Answers (for the tutor; reveal only after a genuine attempt)
1. [4] B2 boundary maintenance: society's collective reaction against crime reaffirms and reinforces shared values/the boundary of acceptable behaviour, developed; B2 crime can act as a safety valve for built-up social tension, or as a source of social change (today's deviant becomes tomorrow's reformer), developed.
2. [3] B1 an individual accepts society's culturally approved goal (e.g. material success) but lacks legitimate/institutionalised means to achieve it; B1 they turn to illegitimate means instead (e.g. theft, fraud) to pursue the same goal; B1 Merton used this to explain much utilitarian, financially-motivated working-class crime.

## How to run it
One question at a time. Let the learner finish a genuine attempt before offering a hint; then walk through the model answer and have them redo any step they missed, especially explicit use of a given Item where the question requires it.

## When to move to test
When the learner gets a new item of each type right without prompting.
