# S29_Ethical_Legal_Environmental_Impacts - Test: Ethical, legal and environmental impacts of digital technology

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: a mix of multiple-choice, short-answer and longer written or code-tracing questions, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and no running of code until every answer is in. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain one legal issue and one ethical issue raised by storing personal data in cloud storage. [4 marks]
2. Discuss the ethical and legal issues raised by hacking, distinguishing authorised ("ethical") hacking from unauthorised, malicious hacking. [6 marks]
3. Explain the privacy tension between citizens and governments/security services, giving an example. [3 marks]
4. Autonomous vehicles raise both ethical and legal questions. Give one example of each. [2 marks]
5. Which is an example of a legal (rather than purely ethical or environmental) impact of digital technology? Choose every correct option.
   A. unauthorised hacking being an offence under the Computer Misuse Act
   B. the energy used by data centres supporting cloud storage
   C. a debate about whether monitoring is an invasion of privacy
   D. e-waste generated by frequently replaced wearable devices

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 legal, e.g. which country's laws apply to data stored on servers that may be located abroad, or who can be compelled to hand the data over; B1 explained; B1 ethical, e.g. how much a user can trust a third-party provider with their personal data, given they have little direct control over it; B1 explained.
2. [6] Level 2 (4-6): clear, balanced discussion covering: unauthorised access to computer systems is illegal (e.g. under the Computer Misuse Act); authorised/ethical hacking (e.g. by penetration testers or security researchers, with permission) aims to find and report weaknesses so they can be fixed, and is generally viewed positively; malicious/unauthorised hacking causes harm (data theft, disruption, financial/reputational damage) and is both illegal and widely viewed as unethical; a reasoned point on why the distinction (permission and intent) matters more than the technical act itself. Level 1 (1-3): some relevant but limited/one-sided discussion. 0: no relevant discussion.
3. [3] B1 governments/security services may want access to communications/data to prevent crime or protect national security; B1 this can conflict with individuals' right to privacy over their own communications/data; B1 example, e.g. debate over government access to encrypted messaging, or mass surveillance versus targeted, warranted access.
4. [2] B1 ethical, e.g. how the vehicle's software should be programmed to behave in an unavoidable accident; B1 legal, e.g. who is liable (manufacturer, software developer, owner) when an autonomous vehicle is involved in a collision.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
