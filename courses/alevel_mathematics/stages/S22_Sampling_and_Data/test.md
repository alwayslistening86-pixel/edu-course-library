# S22_Sampling_and_Data - Test: Sampling and data presentation

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of OCR's papers: structured questions with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows full working and may use a calculator. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A council wants residents' views on a new park. (a) Explain why an opportunity sample outside the park entrance might be biased. (b) Suggest a better method and one practical difficulty with it. [4 marks]
2. A histogram shows a class 20 ≤ x < 30 as a bar of height 3.2 and a class 30 ≤ x < 50 as a bar of height 1.5 (frequency density). Find the frequencies, and estimate how many values lie between 25 and 40. [4 marks]
3. For the data [12, 15, 15, 18, 21, 22, 25, 29, 31, 60]: find the quartiles, the IQR, and show that 60 is an outlier using the 1.5 × IQR rule. State one reason why it might be kept. [5 marks]
4. For grouped data with midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35 and frequencies 4, 9, 12, 5, estimate the mean and standard deviation, and explain why they are estimates. [4 marks]
5. A study finds strong positive correlation between the number of fire engines at a fire and the damage caused. Explain why this does not show that fire engines cause damage. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 people at the park are likely to use it and favour it; B1 non-users are under-represented; B1 e.g. simple random sample from the electoral roll; B1 difficulty, e.g. non-response or the roll being out of date.
2. [4] B1 32; B1 30; M1 half of 32 plus half of 30 (assuming even spread); A1 31.
3. [5] B1 Q1 = 15; B1 Q3 = 29; B1 IQR 14; B1 limit 50 < 60, so outlier; B1 it might be a genuine value (e.g. a real extreme), not an error.
4. [4] M1 uses midpoints; A1 mean 21.00; A1 sd 9.17; B1 the raw values are not known, so midpoints are used.
5. [2] B1 correlation does not imply causation; B1 a third variable (the size of the fire) causes both.
