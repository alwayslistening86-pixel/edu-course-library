# L02_Verbs_Present_System - Lesson: Verbs — present, imperfect, future and irregular verbs

## Goal
The learner conjugates present, imperfect and future indicative active across the four conjugations, and the six required irregular verbs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LAT-GR-pres-ind - Present indicative active, four conjugations: conjugate present indicative active verbs across all four conjugations
- LAT-GR-impf-ind - Imperfect indicative active: form and translate the imperfect indicative active for ongoing or repeated past action
- LAT-GR-fut-ind - Future indicative active: form and translate the future indicative active across the four conjugations
- LAT-GR-irregular-verbs - Irregular verbs: sum, possum, eo, fero, volo, nolo: conjugate and translate the six required irregular verbs across their specified forms

## How to teach this
Ask: "Puella cantat" — the girl sings. Now: "Puella cantābat." "Puella cantābit." Same subject, same verb stem, three different time frames carried entirely by the ending. Use this minimal triple to introduce the present system before moving to irregulars.

#### LAT-GR-pres-ind Present indicative active, four conjugations
Latin verbs conjugate by person and number, and the ending itself tells you who is acting — a subject pronoun is usually unnecessary. The four conjugations, grouped by their infinitive ending (-āre, -ēre, -ere, -īre), each have a distinct present-tense pattern: 1st amō, amās, amat, amāmus, amātis, amant (I love...); 2nd moneō, monēs, monet, monēmus, monētis, monent (I warn...); 3rd regō, regis, regit, regimus, regitis, regunt (I rule...); 4th audiō, audīs, audit, audīmus, audītis, audiunt (I hear...). A third-conjugation "-iō" subgroup (e.g. capiō, "I take") mixes 3rd and 4th patterns and needs separate drilling: capiō, capis, capit, capimus, capitis, capiunt.

#### LAT-GR-impf-ind Imperfect indicative active
The imperfect describes an ongoing or repeated past action — "was doing" or "used to do" — built from the present stem plus -bam, -bās, -bat, -bāmus, -bātis, -bant for 1st/2nd conjugations (amābam, "I was loving/used to love") and -ēbam etc. for 3rd/4th (regēbam, audiēbam). Getting the aspect right matters for translation marks: "I was walking" (imperfect, ongoing) is a genuinely different claim from "I walked" (perfect, completed, taught next stage), and mark schemes specifically penalise conflating the two.

#### LAT-GR-fut-ind Future indicative active
The future for 1st/2nd conjugations uses -bō, -bis, -bit, -bimus, -bitis, -bunt (amābō, "I will love"; monēbō); for 3rd/4th conjugations it instead uses -am, -ēs, -et, -ēmus, -ētis, -ent (regam, "I will rule"; audiam) — a genuinely different pattern that catches learners who assume all futures work like -bō. Distinguishing 1st/2nd future forms from 3rd/4th future forms by conjugation, not by guesswork, is worth explicit drilling.

#### LAT-GR-irregular-verbs Irregular verbs: sum, possum, eo, fero, volo, nolo
Six verbs are irregular enough to need learning as fixed patterns rather than derived from a rule: sum (to be: sum, es, est, sumus, estis, sunt), possum (to be able: possum, potes, potest, possumus, potestis, possunt — literally pot- + sum), eo (to go: eō, īs, it, īmus, ītis, eunt), ferō (to carry/bear: ferō, fers, fert, ferimus, fertis, ferunt), volō (to want: volō, vīs, vult, volumus, vultis, volunt), nōlō (to not want: nōlō, nōn vīs, nōn vult, nōlumus, nōn vultis, nōlunt). These appear constantly in unseen passages, so treat them as vocabulary to over-learn rather than grammar to work out each time.

## Explicitly not here
Perfect, pluperfect, the passive voice and deponent verbs follow in the next stage.
