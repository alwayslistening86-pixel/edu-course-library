# L04_Nonfinite_Forms_and_Subjunctive - Lesson: Non-finite forms and the subjunctive

## Goal
The learner forms the present infinitive, present and perfect participles, the imperative, and the imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive active.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LAT-GR-infin-part-imper - Present infinitive, participles and imperatives: form the present infinitive, present and perfect participles, and imperative mood
- LAT-GR-subjunctive - Imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive active: form the imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive active and recognise their use in subordinate clauses

## How to teach this
Ask: "Puella cantāre vult" (the girl wants to sing). What is doing the work of the second verb here — is it conjugated normally? It is not: it is an infinitive. Use this to introduce every non-finite form the exam requires.

#### LAT-GR-infin-part-imper Present infinitive, participles and imperatives
The present infinitive ("to __") is used after verbs like possum, volō and dēbeō ("puella cantāre vult," the girl wants to sing) and is also how a verb is identified in a dictionary (amāre, monēre, regere, audīre reveal the conjugation by their exact ending pattern — note the short vs. long vowel distinguishing 2nd from 3rd, though this is not always marked in texts). The present participle ("-ing," e.g. amāns, "loving") declines like a third-declension adjective and describes an action happening at the same time as the main verb. The perfect passive participle ("having been -ed," e.g. amātus, -a, -um) declines like bonus and is the form used to build the perfect/pluperfect passive taught in the previous stage. The imperative gives a direct command: 2nd person singular drops the ending to the bare stem (amā!, "love!"; regē! — note 3rd conjugation shortens differently: rege!); plural adds -te (amāte!, regite!).

#### LAT-GR-subjunctive Imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive active
The subjunctive mood signals that a clause is not simply stating a fact but expressing purpose, indirect command, result or another subordinate function — its forms must be recognised even though full independent use is not required at GCSE. Imperfect subjunctive: formed from the present active infinitive + personal endings (amārem, amārēs, amāret, amārēmus, amārētis, amārent; regerem, regerēs...). Pluperfect subjunctive: perfect stem + -issem, -issēs, -isset, -issēmus, -issētis, -issent (amāvissem, rexissem). Recognising these forms is essential for the indirect command, purpose clause and result clause constructions taught in the Syntax stages that follow — a subjunctive verb inside a subordinate clause is the single clearest signal that a literal "so that" or "because" translation is probably wrong.
