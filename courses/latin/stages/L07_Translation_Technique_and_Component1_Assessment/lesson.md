# L07_Translation_Technique_and_Component1_Assessment - Lesson: Translation technique and Component 1 assessment

## Goal
The learner applies an effective reading strategy to unseen Latin, identifies English derivatives, understands how comprehension and translation are marked, and knows the structure of Component 1's three assessed tasks.

## Syllabus items taught here
- LAT-GR-translation-method - Reading strategy for unseen passages: identify the main verb first, then its subject, then work outward through phrases and subordinate clauses, rather than translating strictly left to right
- LAT-GR-derivation - Derivation: English words from Latin roots: identify English derivatives of set Latin vocabulary as a directly assessed skill
- LAT-GR-comprehension-technique - How comprehension and translation are marked: apply exam technique for comprehension (reasonable paraphrase earns credit) and translation (finite verbs/comparatives weighted more heavily; an established word is not re-penalised)
- LAT-C1-comprehension - Component 1 Section A: comprehension: answer comprehension questions on a Latin passage, worth around 55% of Component 1's marks
- LAT-C1-translation-le - Component 1 Section A: Latin-to-English translation: translate an unseen Latin passage into English, worth around 35% of Component 1's marks
- LAT-C1-section-b - Component 1 Section B: English-to-Latin translation or grammar analysis: complete the Section B choice (short English-to-Latin translation or grammar/syntax analysis), using only first-conjugation verbs and first/second-declension nouns, worth around 10% of Component 1's marks

## How to teach this
Ask: "Do you translate strictly left to right, word by word?" If yes, demonstrate why that produces ungrammatical, low-scoring English on a genuinely inflected language, and teach the alternative method directly.

#### LAT-GR-translation-method Reading strategy for unseen passages
Component 1's real approach rewards a specific method: identify the main verb first, find its subject, then work outward through subordinate clauses and phrases — rather than translating strictly left to right, which invites the literal, ungrammatical rendering the mark scheme penalises. Comprehension questions do not require an exact word-for-word match to a model answer; a reasonable paraphrase conveying the same meaning earns full credit, so learners should be taught to answer in natural English rather than searching for one "correct" phrase.

#### LAT-GR-derivation Derivation: English words from Latin roots
Component 1 also tests derivation — identifying English words that descend from a given Latin word in the passage or vocabulary list (e.g. videō → vision, visible, evident; portō → port, portable, transport). This is a directly assessed skill in its own right, not just a vocabulary-retention aid, so practise it explicitly with set-list words rather than assuming it will happen automatically alongside translation.

#### LAT-GR-comprehension-technique How comprehension and translation are marked
The real Component 1 approach weights words by difficulty: finite verbs and comparatives/superlatives are worth more than simple nouns or uninflected words, because they carry more grammatical information to get right. Once a word's meaning has been correctly established earlier in a passage, later repeats of that same word are not re-penalised if mistranslated the second time — examiners are testing whether the word was understood once, not whether the learner stayed perfectly consistent under exam pressure. Teach learners to prioritise verbs and comparative/superlative forms when time is short, since these carry more marks per word.

#### LAT-C1-comprehension Component 1 Section A: comprehension
Comprehension questions on a Latin passage make up around 55% of Component 1's 100 marks. Questions are answered in English, testing understanding of content, not translation word-for-word — apply the paraphrase-is-fine principle from LAT-GR-comprehension-technique directly here.

#### LAT-C1-translation-le Component 1 Section A: Latin-to-English translation
A further unseen Latin-to-English translation makes up around 35% of Component 1's marks, marked element by element as described above (verbs and comparatives/superlatives weighted more heavily). Apply the main-verb-first reading strategy and prioritise getting tense, mood and voice correct over polishing English style.

#### LAT-C1-section-b Component 1 Section B: English-to-Latin translation or grammar analysis
Section B (around 10% of Component 1's marks) offers a choice between a short English-to-Latin translation or a grammar/syntax analysis task, and — importantly — is restricted to first-conjugation verbs and first/second-declension nouns only, a deliberately narrower scope than Section A's unseen passage. This makes Section B a good diagnostic: a learner who cannot reliably produce first-conjugation, first/second-declension Latin accurately here has a foundational gap that will affect every other section.
