# S22_Spoken_Language_Skills_and_the_Speaking_and_Listening_AOs - Lesson: Presenting, responding and using spoken Standard English; the speaking and listening assessment objectives

## Goal
The learner can plan and deliver a spoken presentation, respond appropriately to questions and feedback, and use spoken Standard English where the context calls for it, and can explain what AO7-AO9 each assess.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.1.3.1 - Spoken language: presenting information and ideas: select, organise and deliver ideas persuasively in a prepared talk
- 3.1.3.2 - Spoken language: responding to spoken language, questions and feedback: listen and answer questions appropriately
- 3.1.3.3 - Spoken language: using spoken Standard English: use Standard English when and where appropriate in speech
- AO7 - AO7: demonstrate presentation skills in a formal setting: deliver a formal, organised presentation
- AO8 - AO8: listen and respond appropriately to spoken language, including questions and feedback: respond to questions and feedback well
- AO9 - AO9: use spoken Standard English effectively in speeches and presentations: use spoken Standard English effectively

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to explain, out loud and unscripted for one minute, why a rule at their school exists. Note afterwards: did they organise it (a beginning, a reason, an example, a conclusion), or did it ramble? Use this to introduce the idea that spoken presentation, like writing, needs a plan.

#### 3.1.3.1 Spoken language: presenting information and ideas
This content statement covers the skill of organising a talk before delivering it: choosing a clear main idea, ordering supporting points logically (often strongest point last, or chronologically for a narrative-style talk), and selecting a register and pace suited to a listening (not reading) audience, who cannot re-read a sentence they missed. Teach the 'tell them three times' structure for a short talk: say what you are going to say (a one-line opener), say it (two or three developed points), say what you said (a short summing-up). Visual or written prompts (a small card of headings, never a full script to read from) support delivery without replacing it.

#### 3.1.3.2 Spoken language: responding to spoken language, questions and feedback
Beyond delivering a talk, the learner must show they can listen actively and respond appropriately: answering a question directly rather than restarting the whole talk, acknowledging and building on a point someone else makes, and adjusting an answer if the first attempt did not address what was actually asked. Teach active listening as a specific skill: pause before answering, repeat the question briefly in your own words to confirm understanding, then answer it directly before adding extra detail. This matters because a talk that goes well but collapses under questioning does not meet the full requirement.

#### 3.1.3.3 Spoken language: using spoken Standard English
Spoken Standard English is grammatically standard speech (avoiding non-standard forms such as 'we was' or 'ain't') delivered where the context is formal or public (a presentation, a formal discussion), while recognising that spoken language legitimately varies by region and situation and that Standard English is a register choice, not a claim that other forms of spoken English are wrong. Teach the learner to notice their own habitual non-standard forms in casual speech and to practise switching them off specifically for the presentation context, in the same way written Standard English is a register choice for formal written tasks.

#### AO7 AO7: demonstrate presentation skills in a formal setting
This is the assessment objective behind 3.1.3.1: examiners (the class teacher, for the endorsement) are judging whether the talk is organised, appropriately paced, uses supporting detail effectively, and is delivered with confidence and clarity suited to the formal setting of standing up in front of an audience. Nerves are expected and not penalised in themselves; what is assessed is whether the content and delivery are controlled despite them. Teach rehearsal out loud (not just silent reading of notes) as the single most effective preparation, since timing and phrasing problems only reveal themselves when spoken.

#### AO8 AO8: listen and respond appropriately to spoken language, including questions and feedback
This is the assessment objective behind 3.1.3.2: it is marked through the question-and-answer section that follows a prepared talk, where the teacher-assessor and often peers ask genuine follow-up questions. Being assessed here means genuinely listening in the moment, not just waiting to speak again; a strong response shows understanding of the specific question asked, not a generic restatement of the original talk. Teach the learner to practise fielding unplanned questions from a partner as part of rehearsal, since this is the part of the endorsement that cannot be scripted in advance.

#### AO9 AO9: use spoken Standard English effectively in speeches and presentations
This is the assessment objective behind 3.1.3.3, assessed across the whole spoken activity (talk and follow-up discussion), not just the prepared opening. 'Effectively' means Standard English used with control and confidence throughout, including under the pressure of unplanned questions, not just in a rehearsed opening line. Teach the learner that this is often the descriptor that slips first under pressure, so it is worth deliberately practising answering unscripted questions in full, grammatically standard sentences during rehearsal, not only reciting the prepared script accurately.

## Explicitly not here
The formal grading bands (Pass/Merit/Distinction) and the endorsement's separate, unmarked reporting (S23).
