# S19_Writing_for_Impact_Argument_and_Rhetorical_Devices - Practice: Writing for impact: selecting and emphasising ideas, citing evidence, rhetorical devices and argument craft

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Rewrite 'Plastic bags are bad for the environment' three times using three different rhetorical devices.
2. Write a persuasive paragraph arguing for a school policy of your choice, ending on a deliberately emotional final sentence.
3. Identify one place in a persuasive paragraph of your own where evidence is vague, and rewrite it more specifically.
4. Write a short counter-argument-and-rebuttal pair ('Some argue that... However...') for a viewpoint of your choosing.
5. List two rhetorical devices you have overused in the past and explain how you would ration them in future.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
