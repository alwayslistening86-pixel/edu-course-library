# S05_Terminology_Figurative_and_Sound_Language - Lesson: Terminology III: imagery, sound devices and figurative language

## Goal
The learner can identify the main figurative and sound devices and explain what each does in context.

## Syllabus items taught here
- T.6 - Figurative and sound language: identify and explain imagery and sound devices

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to describe the sound of a storm without using the word 'loud'. Collect their images and then name them: similes, metaphors, onomatopoeia. Terminology follows use.

#### T.6 Figurative and sound language
Imagery is language that creates sensory pictures. Simile compares using 'like' or 'as' ('clouds like a flock of crows'); metaphor states one thing is another ('the sky was a bruise'); personification gives human qualities to non-human things ('the wind prowled the street, waiting for a door to open'); symbolism uses an object or setting to stand for an idea (a locked door for secrecy). Sound devices: onomatopoeia (words that sound like what they name: 'crash', 'hiss'), alliteration (repeated consonants at the start of words), sibilance (repeated s sounds, hissing or softness), assonance (repeated vowels), plosives (hard b, p, t, d, k sounds, violence or scorn). Also hyperbole (exaggeration), oxymoron (a phrase combining opposites: 'deafening silence'), juxtaposition (contrasting things placed side by side) and pathetic fallacy (weather or setting mirroring mood). The rule for every device: name it, quote it (short), and say what it makes the reader see, feel or think and why. 'The metaphor "a bruise" suggests damage and pain in the sky' scores; 'the writer uses a metaphor' does not.
