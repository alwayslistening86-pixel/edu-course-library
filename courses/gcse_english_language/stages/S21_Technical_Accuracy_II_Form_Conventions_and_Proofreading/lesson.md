# S21_Technical_Accuracy_II_Form_Conventions_and_Proofreading - Lesson: Standard English, spelling and vocabulary range; conventions of Paper 2 forms; proofreading; managing the exam room

## Goal
The learner can write with grammatical Standard English, accurate ambitious spelling and wide vocabulary, follow the conventions of common non-fiction forms, proofread effectively under time pressure, and manage the timing of a whole Paper 1 or Paper 2 sitting.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AO6.4 - AO6 descriptor: Standard English and grammatical control: use Standard English and secure agreement
- AO6.5 - AO6 descriptor: spelling accuracy including ambitious vocabulary: spell complex and irregular words accurately
- AO6.6 - AO6 descriptor: range and ambition of vocabulary: choose precise and ambitious vocabulary
- W.2 - Conventions of Paper 2 forms: article, letter, speech, leaflet, essay, review, guide: apply the conventions of each form
- W.3 - Proofreading and editing under time pressure: find and fix errors in the last minutes
- X.1 - Paper 1 in the room: timings, order and marks per minute: manage a whole Paper 1 sitting
- X.2 - Paper 2 in the room: timings, order, two sources and marks per minute: manage a whole Paper 2 sitting

## How to teach this
Give the learner a deliberately error-strewn short paragraph (subject-verb disagreement, a misspelled ambitious word, a form mismatch such as 'Dear Sir' followed by 'see ya later') and ask them to find and fix every problem in ninety seconds, naming what kind of error each one is.

#### AO6.4 AO6 descriptor: Standard English and grammatical control
Standard English means grammatically standard forms throughout: subject-verb agreement ('they were', not 'they was'), correct pronoun and tense use, no double negatives, and consistent tense within a passage unless a shift is deliberate and signalled. It does not forbid informal register where the task genuinely calls for it (a text to a friend within a transformation task), but even informal writing should be grammatically controlled rather than genuinely ungrammatical. Teach the learner to check verb agreement and tense consistency as a specific, separate proofreading pass, since these errors are easy to miss when checking only for spelling.

#### AO6.5 AO6 descriptor: spelling accuracy including ambitious vocabulary
Basic spelling accuracy is expected throughout for a Level 2+ response, but Level 4 specifically rewards ambitious vocabulary spelled correctly, not just safe, simple words spelled correctly. Teach the learner to build a personal bank of 15-20 ambitious, topic-flexible words they can spell with total confidence (meticulous, inevitable, profound, resilience, catastrophic) and to use two or three per response rather than reaching for an unfamiliar word mid-sentence under exam pressure and risking a spelling slip. A correctly spelled simple word beats an ambitiously misspelled one, but an ambitious, correctly spelled word beats both.

#### AO6.6 AO6 descriptor: range and ambition of vocabulary
Distinct from spelling: this rewards precise, varied word choice rather than repetition of the same safe adjectives (nice, good, bad, sad) or verbs (said, went, looked). Teach synonym substitution as a proofreading step: circle any word used three or more times in a response and replace at least two occurrences with a more precise alternative (said becomes murmured, insisted, admitted depending on the actual meaning intended -- never swap for a fancier word that changes the meaning). Precision matters more than obscurity: a well-chosen everyday word beats a showy but slightly wrong one.

#### W.2 Conventions of Paper 2 forms: article, letter, speech, leaflet, essay, review, guide
Each form has recognisable conventions worth signalling briefly: a letter needs an address/date and sign-off appropriate to formality (Dear Sir/Madam...Yours faithfully, or Dear Mr Khan...Yours sincerely); a speech uses direct address, rhetorical questions and sometimes a greeting/thanks to the audience; an article needs a headline and can use subheadings, with a more impersonal, broadsheet or tabloid register depending on context; a leaflet uses headings, bullet points and a call to action; a review states a clear verdict; a guide uses imperative, instructional language. Teach the learner that convention markers (an address block, a headline) should be brief -- a few words -- since content marks come from the body, not the formatting.

#### W.3 Proofreading and editing under time pressure
With only a few minutes left, proofreading should be targeted, not a vague reread. Teach a fixed checklist run in order: (1) sentence demarcation -- every sentence capitalised and stopped; (2) tense and agreement consistency; (3) spelling of any ambitious words used; (4) one clarity check -- does every sentence make sense as written. Teach the learner to fix errors by neat single-line crossing-out and a clear correction above or alongside, never scribbling out whole passages, since messy corrections cost time and readability without earning extra marks.

#### X.1 Paper 1 in the room: timings, order and marks per minute
Paper 1 is 1h45 for 80 marks, roughly one minute per mark as a rule of thumb, but sections need different pacing: read the source first (about 5 minutes), Q1 (4 marks, about 5 minutes), Q2 (8 marks, about 10 minutes), Q3 (8 marks, about 10 minutes), Q4 (20 marks, about 25 minutes including a short plan), Q5 (40 marks, about 45 minutes including a 5-minute plan). Teach the learner to write the target finish time for each question at the top of their answer booklet before starting, and to move on even if a question feels unfinished, since an incomplete Q5 costs far more than a slightly thin Q1.

#### X.2 Paper 2 in the room: timings, order, two sources and marks per minute
Paper 2 is also 1h45 for 80 marks with two linked sources to read (about 8-10 minutes for both, since they must be cross-referenced): Q1 (4 marks, about 5 minutes), Q2 (8 marks, about 10 minutes, needs both sources), Q3 (12 marks, about 12-15 minutes, one source), Q4 (16 marks, about 18-20 minutes, comparing both sources), Q5 (40 marks, about 40-45 minutes). Teach the learner that Paper 2's extra reading overhead (two sources, cross-referencing) means time discipline matters even more than on Paper 1, and that Q2 and Q4 specifically require flicking between both sources, which should be planned for rather than discovered mid-answer.
