# S15_Comparison_Paper_2_Q4 - Lesson: Comparing perspectives: Paper 2 Question 4 (16 marks)

## Goal
The learner can compare two writers' viewpoints and the methods used to convey them, in a sustained 16-mark answer.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AO3 - AO3: compare writers' ideas and perspectives, and how these are conveyed, across two or more texts: compare what writers think and how they show it
- 3.1.1.12 - Comparing two or more texts critically: compare texts on ideas, perspective and method
- P2.Q4 - Paper 2 Question 4: compare two sources' perspectives and methods (16 marks, AO3): write a comparison of perspective and method across both sources

## How to teach this
Ask the learner to describe how two people they know feel about the same topic (school uniform) and how each makes their view clear. Then tie it to sources: different writers, different viewpoints, different methods.

#### AO3 AO3: compare writers' ideas and perspectives, and how these are conveyed, across two or more texts
AO3 reads: 'Compare writers' ideas and perspectives, as well as how these are conveyed, across two or more texts.' It is 10% of the GCSE (Paper 2 Q4, 16 marks). The two parts to compare are what the writers think or feel (ideas and perspectives) and how they show it (methods: language, structure, tone, form). Levels: L4 (13-16) compares ideas and perspectives in a perceptive way, analyses how writers' methods are used, selects judicious detail from both texts; L3 (9-12) compares clearly and explains how methods are used; L2 (5-8) attempts to compare and comments on how methods are used; L1 (1-4) makes simple cross-reference. Both texts must be addressed in each level. A perceptive answer notices complexity, for example a writer whose feelings change through the source.

#### 3.1.1.12 Comparing two or more texts critically
'Comparing texts: comparing two or more texts critically.' Compare thoughts and feelings, attitudes, and methods. Use comparative connectives: 'whereas', 'in contrast', 'similarly', 'both writers', 'unlike', 'on the other hand'. Give each point a two-sided structure: 'Source A calls camping "hell" (quotation), suggesting dread, whereas Source B begins by praising it (quotation), suggesting hope.' Compare methods: structure of feeling (does the writer's attitude change?), tone (ironic, admiring), sentence forms, imagery, use of the first person, anecdote versus facts. Critically means weighing: which writer is more convincing, which more extreme, which more nuanced, and what might explain the difference (for example, the period each was writing in).

#### P2.Q4 Paper 2 Question 4: compare two sources' perspectives and methods (16 marks, AO3) -- 2026 wording
The question refers to the whole of Source A and the whole of Source B: 'Compare how the writers convey their different thoughts and feelings about X.' with prompts to compare their thoughts and feelings, the methods they use, and support with references to both texts. From 2026, AQA reworded the second prompt bullet about methods to point more directly at finding and using relevant quotations from each text (rather than a more general instruction to comment on method), to help learners identify which evidence to select; the marks (16), AO (AO3) and the comparison skill itself are unchanged, so this stage's 'thread' method above still applies. Spend about 15-18 minutes. Plan a 'thread' approach: choose three or four contrasts or similarities (for example the beginning attitude, how the attitude shifts, the ending) and for each write about both texts in the same paragraph. Pattern for each point: (1) comparative topic sentence naming both writers' perspective; (2) a short quotation from A with method and effect; (3) a short quotation from B with method and effect; (4) link back to the perspective. Do not write all of A and then all of B: that limits the comparison. Leave one minute for a concluding sentence.
