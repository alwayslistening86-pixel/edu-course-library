# S20_Paper_2_Section_B_Viewpoint_Task_and_Technical_Accuracy_I - Lesson: The Paper 2 viewpoint writing task, Question 5, and the technical-accuracy assessment objective

## Goal
The learner can identify the Paper 2 Section B task's audience, purpose and form and write a controlled viewpoint response, and can explain what AO6 rewards at sentence, punctuation and clause level.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.3.2 - Paper 2 Section B: a single viewpoint writing task with specified audience, purpose and form: know what the Paper 2 writing task offers and demands
- P2.Q5 - Paper 2 Question 5: viewpoint writing to a specified form (24 + 16 marks, AO5 and AO6): write one 40-mark argument piece under time
- AO6 - AO6: range of vocabulary and sentence structures with accurate spelling and punctuation (20% of marks): meet the technical-accuracy requirement
- AO6.1 - AO6 descriptor: sentence demarcation: mark sentence boundaries accurately
- AO6.2 - AO6 descriptor: range and accuracy of punctuation: use a wide range of punctuation with control
- AO6.3 - AO6 descriptor: variety of sentence forms for effect: vary sentence forms deliberately

## How to teach this
Show the learner a bare task instruction: 'Write an article for a broadsheet newspaper in which you explain your point of view on whether social media does more harm than good.' Ask them to identify, before planning content, three things: the form (article), the audience (broadsheet readers), the purpose (argue/explain a viewpoint) -- because every mark depends on getting these right first.

#### 4.3.2 Paper 2 Section B: a single viewpoint writing task with specified audience, purpose and form
Unlike Paper 1's open image prompt, Paper 2 Section B always specifies a real-world form (letter, article, speech, blog, leaflet are the recurring set), a named or implied audience, and an explicit purpose connected to the theme of the two Section A sources (though the learner writes their own content, not a response to the sources). It is worth 40 marks on the same 24/16 AO5/AO6 split as Paper 1 Question 5. Teach the learner to write the form, audience and purpose in the margin before planning, because a well-argued piece in the wrong form (an essay when a speech was asked for) loses marks for form regardless of content quality.

#### P2.Q5 Paper 2 Question 5: viewpoint writing to a specified form (24 + 16 marks, AO5 and AO6)
The task is usually phrased 'Write [a letter/an article/a speech] in which you explain your point of view on this statement: [a statement linked to the two sources' theme]', sometimes with 'you could write about' prompts suggesting angles. Teach a reliable four-part shape for 40 minutes: opening hook and clear position, two or three developed points each with evidence and a device, a considered counter-view, closing restatement with force -- essentially W.A's argument shape delivered inside the specified form's conventions (a letter's address and sign-off, a speech's direct address to an audience). Time budget mirrors Question 5 on Paper 1: about 5 minutes planning, 30-35 minutes drafting, a few minutes checking.

#### AO6 AO6: range of vocabulary and sentence structures with accurate spelling and punctuation (20% of marks)
AO6 is marked on both writing questions (16 of the 40 marks each, so 20% of the total 160-mark qualification) using a separate four-level grid from AO5, meaning content can be strong while technical accuracy is weak, and vice versa -- the two are assessed independently on the same script. AO6 rewards, at Level 4 (13-16): a wide range of vocabulary including ambitious choices used correctly; a full range of punctuation used with control and for deliberate effect; a variety of sentence structures used for effect; consistently accurate spelling, including of complex/irregular words; Standard English used consistently and appropriately. Teach the learner that AO6 is not just 'no mistakes' -- it is range and control used purposefully.

#### AO6.1 AO6 descriptor: sentence demarcation
The most basic technical requirement: every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with the correct end punctuation (full stop, question mark, exclamation mark), and there are no comma splices (two full sentences joined only by a comma) or sentence fragments used by accident. Teach the 'read backwards' check: read the last sentence of a paragraph first, then the one before it, in isolation, to catch missing capitals or fused sentences that get missed reading forwards at speed. Accurate demarcation is assumed at every level from Level 2 upward; its absence caps a response at Level 1 regardless of vocabulary or content quality.

#### AO6.2 AO6 descriptor: range and accuracy of punctuation
Beyond full stops and commas, Level 3-4 responses use semi-colons to join related independent clauses, colons to introduce a list or explanation, dashes or brackets for parenthetical asides, and apostrophes correctly for both contraction (don't) and possession (the writer's choice, the writers' choices). Teach one new punctuation mark at a time, with a model sentence, rather than asking the learner to add 'more punctuation' vaguely -- for example: 'The fairground was empty; not a single light remained on.' Common error: comma overuse where a full stop or semi-colon is needed, producing a long run-on sentence.

#### AO6.3 AO6 descriptor: variety of sentence forms for effect
Range means mixing simple, compound and complex sentences, plus occasional minor sentences (a fragment used deliberately, such as 'Silence.'), and varying sentence openings (not always starting with the subject: 'Slowly, the crowd dispersed' or 'Exhausted, she sat down'). Teach the learner to scan a paragraph for sentence-length variety: three short sentences in a row for pace, then one longer sentence that builds detail, is a deliberate and rewarded pattern, whereas uniform medium-length sentences throughout reads as flat. 'For effect' is the key phrase in the descriptor: variety must serve meaning, not be random.

## Explicitly not here
The remaining AO6 descriptors on Standard English, spelling and vocabulary range, plus form conventions and proofreading (S21).
