# S02_Texts_Periods_and_What_Sources_Look_Like - Practice: What the sources are: text types, time periods and substantial texts

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Classify these by text type and century, giving your evidence: (a) 'We set out at dawn, the carriage lamps still guttering, and by noon had reached the inn.' (b) 'Honestly, the new café on the corner is worth the queue.' (c) 'Chapter One. The house had been empty for eleven years when the letter arrived.'
2. Explain in your own words why a live news ticker would not be used as a source.
3. Why might a Paper 2 pair always involve a 19th-century text? What can that allow the exam to ask about?
4. Read any published 40-line extract from a novel. Write down its likely century, genre and three words on its tone.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
