# S23_Spoken_Language_Endorsement_Criteria_and_Reporting - Lesson: The Spoken Language endorsement's Pass, Merit and Distinction criteria and how it is reported

## Goal
The learner can state exactly what the prepared presentation task requires, what the general and grade-specific criteria demand at each level, and how the endorsement is reported separately from the GCSE grade.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.4.1 - Spoken Language: requirements of the prepared presentation: meet the topic, planning, format, length and audience requirements
- 4.4.2 - Spoken Language general criteria: audible and spoken Standard English: meet the two general criteria needed for any grade
- 4.4.3 - Spoken Language: Pass criteria: meet all four Pass criteria
- 4.4.4 - Spoken Language: Merit criteria: meet all Merit criteria
- 4.4.5 - Spoken Language: Distinction criteria: meet all Distinction criteria
- 5.0 - Spoken Language reporting: competency-based grade, no marks, Not Classified: understand how the endorsement is graded and reported

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: 'If this doesn't count towards your GCSE grade, why does it matter?' Let them attempt an answer, then explain that universities, colleges and employers do see the separate Pass/Merit/Distinction result, and that presenting confidently is a life skill the endorsement exists to certify honestly.

#### 4.4.1 Spoken Language: requirements of the prepared presentation
The task requires one individual extended contribution (a presentation, talk or speech) on a topic of the learner's choice or the centre's choice, planned in advance, of a length and format the centre specifies within AQA's guidance (typically several minutes), followed by responses to questions and feedback from the audience. Teach the learner that unlike the written exams, this is centre-marked (their own teacher assesses it, following AQA's criteria, sometimes moderated), and that preparation (choosing a topic they can speak on with genuine interest and knowledge) matters as much as delivery skill.

#### 4.4.2 Spoken Language general criteria: audible and spoken Standard English
Two baseline requirements apply to every grade, including a bare Pass: the learner must be audible and clear to the audience throughout (a Distinction-level talk delivered inaudibly still fails these general criteria), and must use spoken Standard English appropriately for the task. These are gateway conditions: teach the learner that no amount of sophisticated content rescues a talk that cannot be heard or that consistently uses non-standard grammar where formal register was required, because both general criteria must be met before grade-specific criteria are even considered.

#### 4.4.3 Spoken Language: Pass criteria
At Pass level, the learner communicates clearly using a range of vocabulary appropriate to purpose, organises their talk so it is understandable to a listener, listens and responds to questions and feedback in a relevant way, and uses some Standard English features appropriately. Teach this as the minimum honest bar: a talk that is understandable, organised, and answers questions on-topic, even without polish, clears Pass. Use it as a checkpoint the learner should be confident of clearing well before attempting Merit-level ambition.

#### 4.4.4 Spoken Language: Merit criteria
Merit adds precision and adaptability to the Pass baseline: communicating with confidence and increasing engagement of the audience, structuring the talk to guide the listener clearly through developing ideas, listening and responding perceptively (not just relevantly) to questions and feedback, adapting spoken language to context, and showing a secure command of Standard English. Teach the learner that the jump from Pass to Merit is mostly about depth of response to questions and audience engagement, not just a longer or more detailed prepared talk.

#### 4.4.5 Spoken Language: Distinction criteria
Distinction requires communicating with sophistication and fluency, using linguistic and rhetorical techniques to add impact and to engage the audience (the same devices taught for persuasive writing, applied live), responding to questions and feedback with sustained and perceptive engagement (developing an answer, not just answering it), and demonstrating a confident command of Standard English throughout, including under unplanned questioning. Teach the learner that Distinction is earned through control under the unpredictable part (questions), not just a well-rehearsed opening, since anyone can polish a script but sustaining fluency in live response is harder to fake.

#### 5.0 Spoken Language reporting: competency-based grade, no marks, Not Classified
The endorsement is graded Pass, Merit, Distinction or Not Classified -- it carries no numerical mark and is not combined with, or scaled into, the written GCSE grade in any way; the GCSE 9-1 grade comes entirely from Papers 1 and 2. The result is reported separately on the learner's certificate alongside (not inside) the overall grade. Teach the learner that this means the endorsement is neither a safety net nor a risk to the written grade: it stands alone as evidence of spoken communication skill, assessed once, by their own teacher-examiner, against AQA's criteria.

## Explicitly not here
Centre-side administration of moderation and sampling (declared exclusion 5.5); the written papers' content, covered throughout earlier stages; the cumulative exam.
