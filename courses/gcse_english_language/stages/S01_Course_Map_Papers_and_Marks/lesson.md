# S01_Course_Map_Papers_and_Marks - Lesson: The course map: two papers, the marks and how a mark is decided

## Goal
The learner can describe both AQA English Language papers, where every mark comes from, how marks are scaled, and how levels of response decide a mark.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.0.1 - Linear, untiered qualification: all assessments in one series, graded 9 to 1: know the qualification is untiered, linear, graded 9-1 and that Q4 on each paper gives the extended-response opportunity
- 2.P1 - Paper 1 Explorations in creative reading and writing: structure: know Paper 1 is 1h45, 80 marks, 50% with Section A reading (40) and Section B writing (40)
- 2.P2 - Paper 2 Writers' viewpoints and perspectives: structure: know Paper 2 is 1h45, 80 marks, 50% with two linked sources and one viewpoint writing task
- 2.NEA - Spoken Language endorsement: what it is: know the endorsement is separately reported, unmarked and does not count towards the GCSE grade
- 4.6.1 - Assessment objective weightings across the papers: recall the approximate weighting of AO1-AO6 on each paper and overall
- 4.6.2 - Raw marks, scaling and the 160-mark total: understand that each paper is 80 raw marks scaled x1 to a 160 total and that grade boundaries are set per series
- MS.1 - Levels of response: how a mark is decided: use the four-level ladder and the level-then-mark process to self-assess

## How to teach this
Open with: 'If I told you an exam is worth 160 marks and half of them are for writing, what would you do differently in your revision?' Let the learner guess before showing the structure. Then draw the two-paper map on paper together (a table of question number, marks, skill) and keep it as a reference for the whole course. Everything else in this course hangs off it. Do not teach any reading or writing skill yet; this stage is about knowing the target.

#### 4.0.1 Linear, untiered qualification: all assessments in one series, graded 9 to 1
GCSE English Language is linear: both exam papers and the Spoken Language endorsement are taken at the end of the course, all in the same exam series (May/June or, for older resitters, November). It is untiered, so every candidate sits the same papers, which is why questions run from short and accessible to demanding and extended. Results use the 1 to 9 scale (9 is highest); below the minimum for grade 1 is U. AQA states that the final reading question on each paper (Question 4 on Paper 1 and Question 4 on Paper 2) is where students give an extended response and draw together skills from across the whole course. All materials are in English and all texts in the exam are unseen, so no text can be learnt in advance. Ask the learner what 'unseen' means for revision: it means practising methods, not memorising content.

#### 2.P1 Paper 1 Explorations in creative reading and writing: structure
Paper 1 lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is out of 80 marks and is 50% of the GCSE. Section A (Reading, 40 marks) uses one literature fiction extract with four questions: Q1 (4 marks, AO1) shade the four true statements (from 2026; previously a free-response 'list four things' question), Q2 (8 marks, AO2) language, Q3 (8 marks, AO2) structure, now naming a single effect such as suspense to track (from 2026), Q4 (20 marks, AO4) evaluation of a statement. Section B (Writing, 40 marks) has one task, Q5, chosen from a description or narrative option, marked as 24 marks for content and organisation (AO5) and 16 marks for technical accuracy (AO6). The paper advises about 15 minutes reading, about 45 minutes on Section A and about 45 minutes on Section B. Have the learner say the question numbers, marks and AOs from memory as a rehearsal.

#### 2.P2 Paper 2 Writers' viewpoints and perspectives: structure
Paper 2 is also 1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks and 50% of the GCSE. Section A (40 marks) uses two linked, unseen non-fiction or literary non-fiction sources, Source A and Source B, printed in a separate insert: Q1 (4 marks, AO1) choose four true statements, Q2 (8 marks, AO1) a summary of the differences between the sources (synthesis), Q3 (12 marks, AO2) language in one source, Q4 (16 marks, AO3) a comparison of the writers' perspectives and methods across both sources. Section B (40 marks) is one viewpoint writing task with a stated form, audience and purpose (Q5, again 24 AO5 plus 16 AO6). The sources share a theme, and the Q5 statement relates to that theme. Note the Q4 is 16 marks here but the extended reading question on Paper 1 is 20.

#### 2.NEA Spoken Language endorsement: what it is
Spoken Language is a non-exam assessment: a prepared presentation followed by questions, assessed by the teacher. It is worth no marks and 0% of the GCSE grade, but every candidate must offer it and it is reported on the certificate as a separate result: Pass, Merit, Distinction or Not Classified. Failing to attempt it results in Not Classified. It assesses AO7, AO8 and AO9 and is covered later in this course (stages S22 and S23). Ask the learner why a task that carries no marks still matters: employers and colleges see it, and the skills of organising an argument transfer to the writing paper.

#### 4.6.1 Assessment objective weightings across the papers
Approximate weightings (% of the whole GCSE): AO1 10 (2.5 on Paper 1, 7.5 on Paper 2); AO2 17.5 (10 and 7.5); AO3 10 (Paper 2 only); AO4 12.5 (Paper 1 only); AO5 30 (15 on each paper); AO6 20 (10 on each paper); AO7 to AO9 endorsement only. Convert this into a plain message: reading is 50% and writing 50%; within reading the biggest single item is the 20-mark evaluation (12.5%); within writing, content and organisation is worth more than technical accuracy but accuracy is a fifth of the whole qualification. One mark on a paper is worth 0.625% of the GCSE, so a full Q4 (20 marks) is 12.5%. AO6 is set by Ofqual at 20% of the marks, which is why accuracy cannot be ignored.

#### 4.6.2 Raw marks, scaling and the 160-mark total
Each paper is out of 80 raw marks with scaling factor x1, so the total scaled mark is 160. Grade boundaries are set on this total after each series, so AQA does not publish a fixed marks-to-grade table and boundaries change from year to year (they have generally sat in a broadly similar region, but that is history, not a promise). What can be said is that the papers are equally weighted, so a weak paper cannot be rescued by a strong one beyond its share. Tell the learner to track progress with percentage marks and levels rather than promised grades, and to treat any single mock grade as a snapshot.

#### MS.1 Levels of response: how a mark is decided
Reading questions worth 8 or more and both writing grids are marked with levels of response. The four reading levels are key-worded Level 1 simple, limited; Level 2 some, attempts; Level 3 clear, relevant; Level 4 perceptive, detailed. Examiners annotate, then start at the lowest level and climb the ladder until the answer no longer fits, then fine-tune the mark inside the level. A student only has to meet a skills descriptor at a level once to reach it, and quality of comment, not the number of references, decides the level. The writing content and organisation grid has four levels (1-6, 7-12, 13-18, 19-24) each split into upper and lower marks; the technical accuracy grid has four levels (1-4, 5-8, 9-12, 13-16). Practise by comparing three short answers to one prompt (simple, clear and perceptive) and asking the learner to rank them and say what changed.

## Explicitly not here
Any reading or writing skill, terminology or the Spoken Language criteria in detail. Those start in the next stages.
