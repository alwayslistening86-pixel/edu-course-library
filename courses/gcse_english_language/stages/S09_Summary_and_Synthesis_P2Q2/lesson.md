# S09_Summary_and_Synthesis_P2Q2 - Lesson: Summary and synthesis: main ideas and Paper 2 Question 2

## Goal
The learner can identify the main themes of a text, summarise it accurately, and synthesise two sources into a clear, inferential summary of similarities or differences.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.1.1.8 - Summary: identifying main themes and summarising a single text: condense a text's main ideas accurately
- 3.1.1.9 - Synthesis from more than one text: combine information from two texts into one coherent account
- AO1b - AO1 (second strand): select and synthesise evidence from different texts: choose the right detail from each text and combine it
- P2.Q2 - Paper 2 Question 2: summary of differences across two sources (8 marks, AO1): write a synthesised summary with inference

## How to teach this
Give the learner two short descriptions of the same place from different writers (for example a campsite described by a holidaymaker and by a ranger) and ask: 'In one sentence, how are they different?' Then build up to a paragraph. Insist on 'both texts in one sentence' as the core habit.

#### 3.1.1.8 Summary: identifying main themes and summarising a single text
Summary means reducing a text to its essential points in your own words, keeping the ideas and dropping detail. Steps: identify the main theme(s); mark the two or three key ideas in each paragraph; combine them into short statements; check that you have not added your own opinion. A good summary is shorter than the source, accurate, and organised (not just chronological). Distinguish a summary (what it says) from analysis (how it says it): on Paper 2 Q2 you summarise a focus given in the question, not the whole text. Practise by summarising a 150-word paragraph in 30 words, then in 15, noting what you keep.

#### 3.1.1.9 Synthesis from more than one text
Synthesis is combining information from two or more texts into one coherent account. It differs from summary because you must draw a link between them: similarities, differences or a shared idea. On Paper 2 Q2 the focus is usually differences (for example between two places, experiences or attitudes). Method: pick the question's focus and list points for each source under matching headings (say, facilities, condition, surroundings, other people); pair them; write in comparative sentences using connectives ('whereas', 'in contrast', 'however', 'both'). Every point must be inferred and supported: not 'Source A has a pool' but 'Source A's site provides on-site entertainment, such as a pool, which suggests a developed commercial site, whereas Source B's campers have to build a shelter themselves'.

#### AO1b AO1 (second strand): select and synthesise evidence from different texts
This second bullet of AO1 is 'select and synthesise evidence from different texts'. Selecting means choosing textual detail that is relevant to the focus; synthesising means bringing those details together. The 8-mark question is marked on both bullets. Levels: Level 1 offers paraphrase and simple difference; Level 2 attempts some inference and some difference; Level 3 makes clear inferences, selects clear references and shows clear differences; Level 4 makes perceptive inferences, uses judicious detail and shows perceptive differences. Crucial cap from the mark scheme: dealing with only one text limits the mark to 1 (if Level 1) or 3 (if Level 2); you must deal with both texts and the correct focus to reach Level 3 or above.

#### P2.Q2 Paper 2 Question 2: summary of differences across two sources (8 marks, AO1) -- 2026 wording
The question tells you what to summarise ('the differences between the two camping sites', for example) and asks you to use details from both sources. From 2026, AQA reworded the question to make explicit that the answer must be inferred, not just lifted ('use details from both sources to write a synthesis, based on inference, of...'), which is AQA's own stated aim in tightening the wording; the marks (8), AO (AO1, both bullets) and the skill itself (this stage's inference-heavy method, above) are unchanged -- this course already teaches inferred, not copied, points, so no change of method is needed here, only awareness of the sharper wording. Timing: about 8 to 10 minutes. Structure: a short opening that states the overall difference; then three or four paired points, each naming Source A and Source B and inferring what the detail suggests; a concluding line if time permits. Do not write about language techniques or the writers' feelings here, and do not summarise the whole text, only the focus. Do not write about the writers' methods; that belongs to Q3 and Q4. Use short quotations, not long ones. Check you have used both sources equally.

## Explicitly not here
