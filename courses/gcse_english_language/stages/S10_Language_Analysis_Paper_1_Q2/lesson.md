# S10_Language_Analysis_Paper_1_Q2 - Lesson: Analysing language: Paper 1 Question 2 (8 marks)

## Goal
The learner can write a focused 8-mark analysis of a writer's language choices in a given extract, at Level 3 or 4.

## Syllabus items taught here
- AO2 - AO2: explain, comment on and analyse how writers use language and structure to achieve effects and influence readers: analyse effect with accurate subject terminology
- 3.1.1.10 - Evaluating vocabulary and grammar: effectiveness and impact: explain how word and grammar choices create effects, using accurate terminology
- P1.Q2 - Paper 1 Question 2: language analysis of an extract (8 marks, AO2): write a Level 4 language paragraph set in eight marks

## How to teach this
Give the learner a two-sentence description and ask them to change three words, then say what changed in the reader's mind. This shows that word choice is effect. Then move on to the fixed formula for a language paragraph.

#### AO2 AO2: explain, comment on and analyse how writers use language and structure to achieve effects and influence readers
AO2 reads: 'Explain, comment on and analyse how writers use language and structure to achieve effects and influence readers, using relevant subject terminology to support their views.' It is worth 17.5% of the GCSE across Paper 1 Q2 and Q3 (8 marks each) and Paper 2 Q3 (12 marks). Three elements: how (method), effect on the reader, and terminology. Marking descriptors are consistent: Level 4 analyses effects with judicious detail and sophisticated terminology; Level 3 explains clearly with relevant detail and accurate terminology; Level 2 attempts to comment; Level 1 offers simple comment. A response that lists techniques without effect stays at Level 1 or 2. The mark scheme's note also says that writing only about language outside the given lines caps the answer at Level 1 or 2 on Paper 1 Q2.

#### 3.1.1.10 Evaluating vocabulary and grammar: effectiveness and impact
The spec says students should explain and illustrate how vocabulary and grammar contribute to effectiveness and impact, using linguistic and literary terminology accurately and paying attention to detail. In practice: choose a strong word, phrase or sentence form; zoom in on one word within it; discuss connotations (what a word suggests, not just means); then link to the reader's response. 'The verb "crept" suggests slow, secret movement, making the fog seem alive and menacing.' Consider several layers of effect (the writer's message, the atmosphere, the character's feelings). Detail means precision: a single word explored in depth beats three broad comments. Compare choices: what would another word have meant? Practise with a substitution test ('slid' or 'crept' or 'stormed').

#### P1.Q2 Paper 1 Question 2: language analysis of an extract (8 marks, AO2)
The question asks: 'Look in detail at this extract, from lines 5 to 15 of the source. How does the writer use language here to describe X?' with the prompt to consider words and phrases, language features and techniques, and sentence forms. Aim for about ten minutes and about three developed points. Formula per point (PEE/TEA style, but flexible): make a point about what the language does; give a short quotation; zoom in on a word or feature (with terminology); explain the effect on the reader; and, if time, link to another quotation. Stay inside the lines. Cover the whole extract (beginning, middle, end) rather than the first few lines. Focus on the question stem (for example 'describe the city') and keep returning to it. Model paragraph (original extract: 'The market glittered with sun-struck brass and shouting'): 'The writer sets brightness against noise in "sun-struck brass and shouting". The compound adjective "sun-struck" personifies the light as violent, as if the sun hits the market, which makes the scene overwhelming for a newcomer. The abstract noun "shouting" placed next to shining metal blends sight and sound, suggesting sensory overload.'

## Explicitly not here
