# S04_Terminology_Sentences_and_Punctuation - Practice: Terminology II: sentence forms, punctuation and effects

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Label each as simple, compound or complex and say if it is a statement, question, command or exclamation: 'Open the door.' / 'What was that noise?' / 'The storm broke, and the lights went out.' / 'Although she was tired, she walked on.'
2. Write a minor sentence that would end a paragraph of description, and explain the effect.
3. Punctuate this in two ways to change its tone: 'He waited she knew he would'.
4. Rewrite a paragraph of three long sentences so the tension increases, using one short sentence for emphasis.
5. Find three examples of a dash, colon or semi-colon in a book you are reading and explain each effect.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
