# S13_Reading_Fiction_Openings_Voice_and_Perspective - Lesson: Reading modern fiction: openings, endings, narrative perspective, character and atmosphere

## Goal
The learner can read a 20th or 21st-century fiction extract for what the writer focuses on and distinguish tone, register and narrative viewpoint.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 4.2.1 - Paper 1 Section A source: 20th/21st-century literature fiction (openings, endings, perspectives, description, character, atmosphere): know what kinds of fiction extract Paper 1 uses and what they focus on
- T.7 - Tone, voice, register and narrative perspective: distinguish tone, register and narrative viewpoint

## How to teach this
Give three opening sentences of three different stories (write them: one first-person, one third-person close, one omniscient). Ask 'Who is telling this and how much do they know?' Then ask what each opening makes them want to find out.

#### 4.2.1 Paper 1 Section A source: 20th/21st-century literature fiction
The spec says the Paper 1 source is a literature fiction text drawn from the 20th or 21st century, in the genre of prose fiction: extracts from novels and short stories focusing on openings, endings, narrative perspectives and points of view, narrative or descriptive passages, character, atmospheric descriptions and other appropriate narrative and descriptive approaches. So expect a passage with a clear focus: a boy arriving in a city, a character remembering, a dark house. Reading routine for 15 minutes: read the whole passage once quickly; read the questions; re-read with a pencil, marking the three or four places where the mood, focus or pace shifts; write two-word margin notes ('excitement', 'unease', 'zoom out'). Q4's statement names the focus, so the notes will help there too. Ask: what is the writer trying to make me feel, and by what stages?

#### T.7 Tone, voice, register and narrative perspective
Narrative perspective: first person ('I', immediate and personal but limited), third person limited (close to one character's thoughts), omniscient third person (knows everything), and, rarely, second person ('you'). An unreliable narrator misreports or misleads. Voice is the personality of the narration (chatty, formal, sardonic). Tone is the writer's or narrator's attitude (wry, tender, chilling, detached, nostalgic). Register is the level of formality (colloquial, neutral, formal, technical). Tense affects immediacy: present tense feels ongoing. In analysis, connect perspective to effect: 'The close third person keeps us inside Ugwu's excitement, so we notice only what he notices' is the type of reasoning to make (the example is generic). Identify shifts in tone as the passage develops: hope turning to doubt is a common pattern that sets up a Q4 statement.

## Explicitly not here
Writing your own description or story (S18-S19) and the evaluation question (S14).
