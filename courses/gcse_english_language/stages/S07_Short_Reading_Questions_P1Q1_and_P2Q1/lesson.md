# S07_Short_Reading_Questions_P1Q1_and_P2Q1 - Lesson: The short questions: Paper 1 Q1 and Paper 2 Q1

## Goal
The learner can answer both 4-mark retrieval questions accurately and quickly, and avoid the traps each sets.

## Syllabus items taught here
- P1.Q1 - Paper 1 Question 1: shade the four true statements, updated multiple-choice format from 2026 (4 marks, AO1): answer the short retrieval question accurately
- P2.Q1 - Paper 2 Question 1: choose four true statements (4 marks, AO1): decide truth from a source carefully

## How to teach this
Give the learner a five-line passage and eight statements about it (A-H), and ask them to work out which four are true before you say anything about marking. Then reveal the mark-scheme rules: choose no more than four, only from the given lines, only about the named subject or focus. Note for the tutor: from the 2026 exam series, AQA changed Paper 1 Question 1 from a 'list four things' free-response format to this same shade-the-boxes multiple-choice format that Paper 2 Question 1 has always used -- so both short retrieval questions are now the same format, and this stage now teaches one method for both.

#### P1.Q1 Paper 1 Question 1: shade the four true statements (4 marks, AO1) -- 2026 format
From the 2026 exam series, the question reads 'Read again the first part of the source, from lines 1 to N. Choose four statements below which are true.', with eight statements (A to H) given and boxes to shade, exactly like Paper 2 Question 1's format (see below). This replaces the older 'list four things' free-response format, in which the learner wrote out four points of their own; AQA's stated aim was to remove ambiguity about what counts as a valid point by giving fixed statements to judge instead. Rules from the mark scheme: choose a maximum of four; selecting more than four risks losing marks; each correct choice earns a mark; distractors are built the same way as Paper 2 Q1's (a wrong number, a wrong person, a plausible but unmentioned idea, or an exaggeration such as 'always'/'never'/'everyone'). Method: read each statement, find its line in the text, shade only if every part of the statement is fully supported there. Spend no more than about five minutes; over-spending here costs the time needed for Q4. Common losses: shading a statement that is only partly true, shading more than four, and being misled by a statement that sounds plausible but is not actually stated or supported in the given lines.

#### P2.Q1 Paper 2 Question 1: choose four true statements (4 marks, AO1)
Eight statements (A to H) are given about a stated line range; choose up to four which are true, by shading the boxes. The rubric says choose a maximum of four; selecting more than four risks losing marks. Each correct choice earns a mark. Distractors are built from small changes: a wrong number, a wrong person, a plausible but unmentioned idea or an exaggeration ('always', 'never', 'everyone'). Method: read the statement, find its line in the text, tick only if every part of the statement is supported. Statements about feelings or ideas may be true as implicit meaning (for example, that a writer thinks holidays are a trick) so read the surrounding sentence. Cross out any error as the instructions say. Practise by writing your own distractors for a passage; this teaches how test-writers think. This format is unchanged for 2026; it is Paper 1 Q1 (above) that has been brought into line with it.
