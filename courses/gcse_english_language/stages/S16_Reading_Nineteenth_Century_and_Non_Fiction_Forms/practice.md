# S16_Reading_Nineteenth_Century_and_Non_Fiction_Forms - Practice: Reading Paper 2 sources: 19th-century prose and non-fiction forms

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. Identify the form and two conventions: (a) 'Dear Mr Hobbs, I write regarding the state of the road...' (b) 'Tuesday. The rain has not stopped since dawn.'
2. Paraphrase: 'It was with no small reluctance that the party, having survived the crossing, proceeded to the inn, which promised little and delivered less.'
3. State two features that suggest a passage is from the 19th century.
4. Read a short Victorian essay or letter extract and note the writer's attitude in two words with a quotation.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
