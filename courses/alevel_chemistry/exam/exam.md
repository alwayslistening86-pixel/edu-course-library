# A-level Chemistry (AQA 7405) - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
Three papers under timed conditions: Paper 1 covers physical chemistry 3.1.1-3.1.4, 3.1.6-3.1.8, 3.1.10-3.1.12 and all inorganic chemistry (S01-S04, S06-S08, S13, S15-S19); Paper 2 covers physical chemistry 3.1.2-3.1.6 and 3.1.9 with all organic chemistry (S02-S06, S09-S12, S14, S20-S24); Paper 3 covers any content, with 40 marks on practical techniques and data analysis (S25 and the required practicals), 20 marks across the specification and 30 multiple-choice. Questions are original; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests. The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest.

## 15 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. [Paper 1] Calculate the lattice enthalpy of formation of potassium bromide from: ΔfH(KBr) -394; atomisation K +89; first IE K +419; atomisation Br +112; electron affinity Br -325 kJ mol^-1. Explain whether the perfect ionic model value is likely to agree closely. [4 marks]
2. [Paper 1] 0.500 mol of H2 and 0.500 mol of I2 react in a sealed flask: H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI. At equilibrium 0.780 mol of HI is present at a total pressure of 150 kPa. Calculate Kp. [4 marks]
3. [Paper 1] Calculate the pH of the solution formed when 30.0 cm^3 of 0.100 mol dm^-3 NaOH is added to 20.0 cm^3 of 0.100 mol dm^-3 HCl. [3 marks]
4. [Paper 1] Describe what is seen when aqueous ammonia is added dropwise and then in excess to separate solutions of Cu^2+ and Fe^3+ ions, giving formulae of the species formed. [5 marks]
5. [Paper 1] Explain the trend in melting points from sodium to aluminium. [3 marks]
6. [Paper 2] 0.1658 g of an unknown monoprotic carboxylic acid requires 22.40 cm^3 of 0.100 mol dm^-3 NaOH for neutralisation. Calculate M_r and suggest the acid. [3 marks]
7. [Paper 2] Outline the mechanism for the nitration of benzene, including the formation of the electrophile. [5 marks]
8. [Paper 2] Compound X, C4H8O, gives no silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Its 1H NMR spectrum shows a quartet (2H), a singlet (3H) and a triplet (3H). Identify X and explain the splitting. [4 marks]
9. [Paper 2] For 2N2O5 → 4NO2 + O2 the rate equation is rate = k[N2O5]. At 318 K, k = 4.98 x 10^-4 s^-1. Calculate the initial rate when [N2O5] = 0.0150 mol dm^-3, and give the units of k. [3 marks]
10. [Paper 2] Name the type of reaction and the conditions to convert 1-bromopropane into propene, and explain why a mixture of products may form. [3 marks]
11. [Paper 3] Describe how to make up 250 cm^3 of a standard solution of sodium carbonate from the solid, and calculate the mass needed for 0.0500 mol dm^-3. [5 marks]
12. [Paper 3] A student measures the emf of a Cu(s)|Cu^2+(aq)||Ag+(aq)|Ag(s) cell as 0.42 V. The data-book value is 0.46 V. Suggest two reasons for the difference. [2 marks]
13. [Paper 3] Plan how to show that the reaction between propanone and iodine in acid is zero order with respect to iodine, using a continuous monitoring method. [4 marks]
14. [Paper 3] Which of these would give a white precipitate with acidified barium chloride solution? Choose every correct option.
   A. sodium sulfate
   B. sodium carbonate
   C. magnesium sulfate
   D. sodium chloride
15. [Paper 3] Which species are Brønsted-Lowry bases in this reaction: NH3 + H2O ⇌ NH4+ + OH-? Choose every correct option.
   A. NH3
   B. H2O
   C. NH4+
   D. OH-

## Answer key for the ready-made items (tutor only)
1. [4] M1 -394 - (89 + 419 + 112 - 325); A1 -689 kJ mol^-1; B1 yes, close agreement; B1 K+ is large with low charge, so little polarisation of Br- (bonding almost purely ionic).
2. [4] M1 H2 = I2 = 0.110 mol, total 1.000 mol; M1 partial pressures 16.5, 16.5 and 117 kPa; M1 Kp = 117^2/16.5^2; A1 50.3 (no units).
3. [3] M1 excess OH- = 1.00 x 10^-3 mol in 50.0 cm^3; M1 [OH-] = 0.0200, [H+] = 5.00 x 10^-13; A1 pH 12.30.
4. [5] B1 Cu: blue precipitate Cu(OH)2(H2O)4; B1 dissolves in excess to a deep blue solution; B1 [Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+; B1 Fe^3+: brown precipitate Fe(OH)3(H2O)3; B1 no change in excess.
5. [3] B1 metallic bonding in all three; B1 ions have increasing charge (1+, 2+, 3+) and decreasing size, with more delocalised electrons; B1 stronger attraction between ions and delocalised electrons, so more energy is needed.
6. [3] M1 n(NaOH) = 0.00224 mol = n(acid); A1 M_r = 74.0; B1 propanoic acid, C2H5COOH (M_r 74.0).
7. [5] B1 HNO3 + 2H2SO4 → NO2+ + H3O+ + 2HSO4-; B1 arrow from the delocalised ring to N of NO2+; B1 intermediate with a broken delocalised system and positive charge (horseshoe) and the H and NO2 on one carbon; B1 arrow from C-H bond into the ring; B1 H+ lost, forming nitrobenzene and regenerating the ring.
8. [4] B1 butanone, CH3COCH2CH3; B1 singlet: the CH3 next to C=O has no H on the adjacent carbon; B1 quartet: CH2 next to CH3 (3 + 1); B1 triplet: CH3 next to CH2 (2 + 1); a ketone, so no reaction with Tollens'.
9. [3] M1 rate = 4.98 x 10^-4 x 0.0150; A1 7.47 x 10^-6 mol dm^-3 s^-1; B1 s^-1 (first order overall).
10. [3] B1 elimination; B1 hot KOH in ethanol (reflux); B1 hydroxide can also act as a nucleophile, giving propan-1-ol by substitution.
11. [5] M1 n = 0.0500 x 0.250 = 0.0125 mol; A1 mass = 0.0125 x 106.0 = 1.325 g; B1 weigh by difference, dissolve in a beaker of distilled water; B1 transfer with washings to a 250 cm^3 volumetric flask; B1 make up to the mark (meniscus at eye level) and invert to mix.
12. [2] B1 concentrations not 1.00 mol dm^-3 (or temperature not 298 K); B1 electrodes not cleaned/impure metals, or current drawn by a low-resistance voltmeter.
13. [4] B1 mix with a large excess of propanone and acid; B1 at timed intervals withdraw samples and quench (NaHCO3); B1 titrate the remaining iodine with sodium thiosulfate (or use a colorimeter); B1 a straight-line graph of [I2] against time shows zero order.
14. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
15. Correct: A, D (exactly these options, no others)
