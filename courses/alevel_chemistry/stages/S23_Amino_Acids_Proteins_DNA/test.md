# S23_Amino_Acids_Proteins_DNA - Test: Amino acids, proteins, enzymes, DNA and anticancer drugs

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why amino acids have high melting points. [2 marks]
2. Describe the bonds that maintain the tertiary structure of a protein. [3 marks]
3. Describe how the amino acids in a protein can be identified after hydrolysis. [4 marks]
4. Explain how cisplatin prevents cancer cells dividing, and give one reason it must be used carefully. [3 marks]
5. Explain why enzymes are stereospecific and how this is used in drug design. [3 marks]
6. Which statements about DNA are correct? Choose every correct option.
   A. Adenine pairs with thymine
   B. Guanine pairs with cytosine by three hydrogen bonds
   C. The sugar is ribose
   D. The backbone consists of alternating sugar and phosphate groups

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 they exist as zwitterions; B1 strong ionic attractions between them.
2. [3] B1 hydrogen bonds; B1 ionic interactions (between charged side chains); B1 disulfide bridges (covalent S-S).
3. [4] B1 hydrolyse with 6 mol dm^-3 HCl, heat under reflux; B1 thin-layer chromatography; B1 spray with ninhydrin or use UV to locate spots; B1 compare Rf values with known amino acids.
4. [3] B1 a chloride ligand is replaced by the nitrogen of a guanine base (and it binds to two); B1 distorting DNA and preventing replication; B1 it also damages healthy cells (side effects such as hair loss).
5. [3] B1 active site has a specific 3D shape; B1 only one enantiomer of the substrate fits; B1 computers model the active site to design inhibitors that block it.
6. Correct: A, B, D (exactly these options, no others)
