# S07_Redox - Lesson: Oxidation states and redox equations

## Goal
The learner defines oxidation and reduction, oxidising and reducing agents, assigns oxidation states, and writes and balances half-equations and overall redox equations, including in acidic solution.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.1.7 - Oxidation, reduction and redox equations

## How to teach this
Ask what rusting, respiration and a battery have in common. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.1.7 Oxidation, reduction and redox equations
Oxidation is loss of electrons, reduction is gain (OIL RIG). An oxidising agent accepts electrons (and is reduced); a reducing agent donates electrons (and is oxidised). Oxidation state rules: elements 0; the sum equals the charge; O is -2 (except in peroxides, -1, and with F); H is +1 (except in metal hydrides, -1); F is -1; Group 1 +1, Group 2 +2. *Example:* Mn in MnO4- is +7; S in SO4^2- is +6; Cr in Cr2O7^2- is +6. Half-equations: balance the atoms, then O with H2O, H with H+, then charge with electrons: MnO4- + 8H+ + 5e- → Mn^2+ + 4H2O. Combine half-equations so that the electrons cancel: MnO4- + 8H+ + 5Fe^2+ → Mn^2+ + 4H2O + 5Fe^3+.
