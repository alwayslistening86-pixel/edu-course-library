# S03_Bonding - Test: Bonding, structure, shapes and intermolecular forces

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why the boiling point of water is much higher than that of hydrogen sulfide. [3 marks]
2. State the shape and bond angle of SF6, and explain why the H-N-H angle in NH4+ is larger than in NH3. [4 marks]
3. Graphite and diamond are both forms of carbon. Explain why graphite conducts electricity and is soft, while diamond does not conduct and is very hard. [4 marks]
4. Explain why pentane (bp 36 °C) boils at a higher temperature than its isomer 2,2-dimethylpropane (bp 10 °C). [2 marks]
5. Describe the formation of a dative covalent bond in the ammonium ion. [2 marks]
6. Which substances conduct electricity when solid? Choose every correct option.
   A. magnesium
   B. sodium chloride
   C. graphite
   D. iodine

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 water forms hydrogen bonds between molecules; B1 O is highly electronegative (S is not enough for hydrogen bonding), so H2S has only dipole-dipole and induced dipole forces; B1 more energy is needed to overcome hydrogen bonds.
2. [4] B1 SF6 octahedral; B1 90°; B1 NH4+ has four bonding pairs and no lone pair (tetrahedral, 109.5°); B1 NH3's lone pair repels more strongly, closing the angle to 107°.
3. [4] B1 graphite: each C bonded to 3 others, leaving one delocalised electron per atom; B1 delocalised electrons move and carry charge; B1 weak forces between layers let them slide; B1 diamond: each C bonded to 4 others in a rigid giant lattice with no delocalised electrons.
4. [2] B1 pentane's straight chains have a larger surface area of contact; B1 so stronger induced dipole-dipole (van der Waals) forces.
5. [2] B1 the nitrogen of NH3 donates its lone pair; B1 to H+, which has no electrons, forming a shared pair from one atom.
6. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
