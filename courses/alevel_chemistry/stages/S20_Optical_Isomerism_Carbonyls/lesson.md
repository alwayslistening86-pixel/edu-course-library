# S20_Optical_Isomerism_Carbonyls - Lesson: Optical isomerism, aldehydes and ketones

## Goal
The learner explains optical isomerism, chiral centres and racemates, describes reactions of aldehydes and ketones including reduction and nucleophilic addition of cyanide, explains why a racemic mixture forms, and uses symmetry in 3D shapes.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.7 - Optical isomerism (A-level only)
- 3.3.8 - Aldehydes and ketones (A-level only)
- MS 4.3 - Understand the symmetry of 2D and 3D shapes

## How to teach this
Hold up both hands and ask why a left glove doesn't fit a right hand, then connect it to a carbon with four different groups. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.3.7 Optical isomerism (A-level only)
Optical isomerism: a carbon with four different groups (a chiral centre, or asymmetric carbon) gives two non-superimposable mirror images, the enantiomers. Enantiomers rotate plane-polarised light by equal amounts in opposite directions. A racemic mixture (racemate) has equal amounts of each and is optically inactive (rotations cancel). Draw enantiomers in 3D with wedges and dashes as mirror images. *Example:* butan-2-ol and 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid) are chiral; propan-2-ol is not.

#### 3.3.8 Aldehydes and ketones (A-level only)
Aldehydes are readily oxidised (S11: Tollens', Fehling's); ketones are not. Reduction with NaBH4 (in aqueous or alcoholic solution) gives alcohols: aldehydes → primary, ketones → secondary; [H] in equations: CH3COCH3 + 2[H] → CH3CH(OH)CH3. Mechanism: nucleophilic addition by the hydride ion :H- to the δ+ carbonyl carbon, then protonation of the O-. Nucleophilic addition of KCN followed by dilute acid gives hydroxynitriles: CH3CHO + HCN → CH3CH(OH)CN (2-hydroxypropanenitrile); the :CN- attacks the δ+ carbon, and the O- takes an H+. KCN is used rather than HCN (a very toxic gas), and both are hazardous. The carbonyl group is planar (trigonal), so CN- attacks equally from either side, forming a racemic mixture when the product has a chiral centre.

#### MS 4.3 Understand the symmetry of 2D and 3D shapes
Symmetry in 3D: a molecule with a plane of symmetry is superimposable on its mirror image (not chiral). The planar C=O group means attack from above and below is equally likely: the two faces are symmetry-related.

## Explicitly not here
