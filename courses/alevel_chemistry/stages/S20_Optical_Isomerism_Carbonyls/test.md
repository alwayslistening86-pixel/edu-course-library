# S20_Optical_Isomerism_Carbonyls - Test: Optical isomerism, aldehydes and ketones

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe the mechanism for the reaction of propanone with KCN followed by dilute acid, name the product, and explain whether it is optically active. [6 marks]
2. Explain why the reaction of propanal with KCN/H+ gives a product that does not rotate plane-polarised light, even though it has a chiral centre. [3 marks]
3. Name the reagent to reduce butanone and the product, and write an equation using [H]. [3 marks]
4. Give one hazard of using KCN in this reaction and how it is controlled. [2 marks]
5. Which molecules have a chiral centre? Choose every correct option.
   A. butan-2-ol
   B. propan-2-ol
   C. 2-hydroxypropanoic acid
   D. 2-methylpropan-2-ol

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [6] B1 lone pair on :CN- attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon; B1 C=O π electrons move to O; B1 O- is protonated (by H+ or HCN); B1 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile; B1 not optically active; B1 the product has two identical CH3 groups on that carbon, so no chiral centre.
2. [3] B1 the carbonyl group is planar; B1 the cyanide ion attacks equally from either side; B1 so a racemic mixture (equal amounts of both enantiomers) forms and the rotations cancel.
3. [3] B1 NaBH4; B1 butan-2-ol; B1 CH3COCH2CH3 + 2[H] → CH3CH(OH)CH2CH3.
4. [2] B1 KCN (and HCN) are highly toxic; B1 use in a fume cupboard, avoid acidifying to form HCN gas/gloves (any sensible control).
5. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
