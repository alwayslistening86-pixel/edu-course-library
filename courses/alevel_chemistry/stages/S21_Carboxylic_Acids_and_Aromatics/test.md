# S21_Carboxylic_Acids_and_Aromatics - Test: Carboxylic acids, esters, acylation and aromatic chemistry

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe the mechanism for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with methylamine, naming the product. [5 marks]
2. Use enthalpy data to explain why benzene is more stable than a hypothetical cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene. (ΔH hydrogenation: cyclohexene -120, benzene -208 kJ mol^-1) [3 marks]
3. Give two reasons why ethanoic anhydride is preferred to ethanoyl chloride for manufacturing aspirin. [2 marks]
4. Give the reagents and conditions for Friedel-Crafts acylation of benzene with ethanoyl chloride, name the product, and write an equation showing how the catalyst is regenerated. [4 marks]
5. Write an equation for the alkaline hydrolysis of ethyl ethanoate and explain why it goes to completion while acid hydrolysis does not. [3 marks]
6. What is biodiesel, and how is it made? [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] B1 name: nucleophilic addition-elimination; B1 N lone pair of CH3NH2 attacks δ+ carbonyl carbon, C=O electrons move to O; B1 C=O reforms and Cl- leaves; B1 loss of H+ from N (to Cl- or another amine); B1 N-methylethanamide.
2. [3] M1 expected 3 x (-120) = -360 kJ mol^-1; A1 benzene is 152 kJ mol^-1 less exothermic, so more stable; B1 due to delocalisation of π electrons.
3. [2] B1 cheaper (and less corrosive/less vulnerable to hydrolysis); B1 reaction less violent/does not produce toxic, corrosive HCl gas.
4. [4] B1 CH3COCl with AlCl3 catalyst; B1 heat under reflux (anhydrous); B1 phenylethanone; B1 AlCl4- + H+ → AlCl3 + HCl.
5. [3] B1 CH3COOC2H5 + NaOH → CH3COONa + C2H5OH; B1 the carboxylate ion is not attacked by the alcohol; B1 so the reverse reaction does not occur (acid hydrolysis is reversible).
6. [2] B1 a mixture of methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids; B1 vegetable oils react with methanol with a catalyst.
