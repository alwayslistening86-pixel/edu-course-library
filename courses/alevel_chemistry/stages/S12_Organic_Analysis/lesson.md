# S12_Organic_Analysis - Lesson: Test-tube tests, mass spectrometry and infrared spectroscopy

## Goal
The learner identifies functional groups by test-tube reactions, uses high-resolution mass spectrometry to find molecular formulae, and interprets infrared spectra, including the fingerprint region and links to global warming.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.3.6.1 - Identification of functional groups by test-tube reactions
- 3.3.6.2 - Mass spectrometry
- 3.3.6.3 - Infrared spectroscopy

## How to teach this
Give the learner an 'unknown' with a C=O stretch in its IR spectrum and ask what else they would need to know. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.3.6.1 Identification of functional groups by test-tube reactions
Test-tube reactions (see RP6): bromine water for alkenes, acidified dichromate for primary/secondary alcohols and aldehydes, Tollens'/Fehling's for aldehydes, NaHCO3 for carboxylic acids (and acidified AgNO3 after hydrolysis for halogenoalkanes).

#### 3.3.6.2 Mass spectrometry
High-resolution mass spectrometry gives M_r to 4 or more decimal places, which distinguishes molecular formulae with the same nominal mass. Precise masses: 1H 1.0078, 12C 12.0000, 14N 14.0031, 16O 15.9949. *Example:* M = 44: C3H8 = 44.0624, CO2 = 43.9898, N2O = 44.0011, C2H4O = 44.0261.

#### 3.3.6.3 Infrared spectroscopy
Bonds absorb infrared radiation at characteristic wavenumbers (cm^-1), causing them to vibrate (stretch or bend). Key absorptions (data sheet): O-H alcohols 3230-3550 (broad); O-H acids 2500-3000 (very broad); N-H 3300-3500; C-H 2850-3300; C=O 1680-1750; C=C 1620-1680; C-O 1000-1300. The fingerprint region (below 1500 cm^-1) is unique to each compound: compare with a database to identify it, and extra peaks show impurities. CO2, methane and water vapour absorb IR radiation emitted by the Earth, so they are greenhouse gases that contribute to global warming.
