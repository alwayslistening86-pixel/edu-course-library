# S12_Organic_Analysis - Test: Test-tube tests, mass spectrometry and infrared spectroscopy

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. A compound has a precise M_r of 58.0419. Using 12C 12.0000, 1H 1.0078, 16O 15.9949, decide whether it is C3H6O or C4H10. [2 marks]
2. Explain how IR spectroscopy can show that ethanol has been fully oxidised to ethanoic acid, not ethanal. [3 marks]
3. Explain why methane contributes to global warming. [2 marks]
4. Describe how the fingerprint region is used to identify a compound. [2 marks]
5. Give a test to distinguish hex-1-ene from hexane, with results. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 C3H6O = 58.0417; C4H10 = 58.0780; A1 C3H6O.
2. [3] B1 C=O absorption at 1680-1750 cm^-1 in both products; B1 ethanoic acid shows a very broad O-H at 2500-3000 cm^-1; B1 ethanal has no O-H absorption (and the alcohol's O-H at 3230-3550 has gone).
3. [2] B1 its C-H bonds absorb infrared radiation (emitted by the Earth); B1 re-emitting energy that would otherwise escape, warming the atmosphere.
4. [2] B1 the region below 1500 cm^-1 has a pattern unique to each compound; B1 match it with a known spectrum in a database.
5. [2] B1 bromine water; B1 hex-1-ene decolourises it (orange to colourless), hexane does not.
