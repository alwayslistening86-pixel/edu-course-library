# S10_Halogenoalkanes_and_Alkenes - Test: Halogenoalkanes, ozone depletion and alkenes

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe, with curly arrows in words, the mechanism for the reaction of propene with hydrogen bromide to form the major product, and explain why it is major. [5 marks]
2. 2-bromo-2-methylpropane reacts with hot KOH in ethanol. Name the type of reaction and the organic product, and state the role of the hydroxide ion. [3 marks]
3. Write equations showing how chlorine radicals catalyse the decomposition of ozone. [3 marks]
4. Explain why iodoalkanes react faster than chloroalkanes in nucleophilic substitution, even though the C-Cl bond is more polar. [2 marks]
5. State the reagent and conditions to convert bromoethane into propanenitrile, and name the mechanism. [3 marks]
6. Explain how a plasticiser makes PVC flexible. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] B1 π electrons (arrow from C=C) to Hδ+ of HBr; B1 H-Br bond electrons move to Br; B1 secondary carbocation CH3C+HCH3 and Br-; B1 lone pair on Br- attacks C+ giving 2-bromopropane; B1 the secondary carbocation is more stable than the primary (more electron-releasing alkyl groups).
2. [3] B1 elimination; B1 methylpropene; B1 base (accepts H+).
3. [3] B1 Cl• + O3 → ClO• + O2; B1 ClO• + O3 → 2O2 + Cl•; B1 the Cl• is regenerated (overall 2O3 → 3O2).
4. [2] B1 the C-I bond enthalpy is lower (weaker bond); B1 bond enthalpy, not polarity, determines the rate: less energy to break.
5. [3] B1 KCN; B1 in ethanol (and water), heat under reflux; B1 nucleophilic substitution.
6. [2] B1 plasticiser molecules get between the polymer chains; B1 weakening the intermolecular forces so chains slide past each other.
