# S25_Practical_Skills_and_Data_Analysis - Lesson: Practical skills, graphs and data analysis

## Goal
The learner plans and evaluates experiments, identifies variables, presents and processes data, plots and interprets graphs, handles uncertainties and reaches justified conclusions, and uses a range of apparatus and techniques as Paper 3 requires.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PS1.1 - Solve problems set in practical contexts
- PS1.2 - Apply scientific knowledge to practical contexts
- PS2.1 - Comment on experimental design and evaluate scientific methods
- PS2.2 - Present data in appropriate ways

- PS2.4 - Identify variables including those that must be controlled

## How to teach this
Give the learner a table of titres with one rough and one anomalous value and ask which to use. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### PS1.1 Solve problems set in practical contexts
Solve problems set in practical contexts: e.g. from a description of an unfamiliar preparation, identify the reaction type, calculate the theoretical yield and suggest the purification steps.

#### PS1.2 Apply scientific knowledge to practical contexts
Apply knowledge to practical contexts: e.g. explain why a reaction mixture is heated under reflux (to heat for a long time without losing volatile substances), why anti-bumping granules are used, or why a burette is rinsed with the solution.

#### PS2.1 Comment on experimental design and evaluate scientific methods
Comment on experimental design and evaluate methods: suggest improvements that reduce uncertainty (larger volumes or masses, a more precise balance, repeat readings), or avoid systematic errors (heat loss: insulate; gas loss: use a gas syringe or bung).

#### PS2.2 Present data in appropriate ways
Present data in tables with headings of quantity and unit (volume / cm^3) and consistent precision (burette readings to 2 decimal places, e.g. 23.45).

#### PS2.3 Evaluate results and draw conclusions with reference to measurement uncertainties and errors
Evaluate results with reference to uncertainty: compare the percentage error with the total percentage uncertainty; if the error is larger, there must be systematic errors (e.g. heat loss in calorimetry).

#### PS2.4 Identify variables including those that must be controlled
Identify variables: independent (changed), dependent (measured) and control variables (kept constant), e.g. in a rate experiment: temperature (independent), time (dependent), concentrations, volumes and surface area (controlled).

#### PS3.1 Plot and interpret graphs
Plot and interpret graphs: sensible scales covering at least half the grid, labelled axes with units, best-fit lines or curves, extrapolation (e.g. calorimetry cooling curves), tangents for rates.

#### PS3.2 Process and analyse data using appropriate mathematical skills
Process data: means of concordant titres, moles from masses and volumes, yields, and gradients (e.g. Ea from ln k against 1/T).

#### PS3.3 Consider margins of error, accuracy and precision of data
Margins of error: percentage uncertainty = uncertainty/value x 100%. *Example:* a 2-decimal balance (±0.005 g per reading, so ±0.01 g by difference) weighing 0.50 g gives 2.0%; a 25.0 cm^3 pipette ±0.06 cm^3 gives 0.24%; a titre of 22.50 ± 0.10 cm^3 gives 0.44%. Distinguish accuracy (closeness to the true value) from precision (closeness of repeat results).

#### PS4.1 Know and understand how to use a wide range of experimental and practical instruments, equipment and techniques
Instruments and techniques (theory for the written papers): balances, burettes, pipettes, volumetric flasks, gas syringes, thermometers and data loggers, pH meters (calibrate with buffers), colorimeters, melting-point apparatus, reflux and distillation apparatus, Büchner filtration, separating funnels, chromatography, and electrochemical cells; know the purpose and correct use of each.

#### MS 3.1 Translate information between graphical, numerical and algebraic forms
Translate between graphical, numerical and algebraic forms: e.g. from a rate-concentration graph, state the order and write the rate equation; from pV = nRT, predict a straight V-T graph at constant p.

#### MS 3.2 Plot two variables from experimental or other data
Plot two variables from experimental data with the independent variable on the x-axis and a line of best fit; identify and omit anomalies with a reason.
