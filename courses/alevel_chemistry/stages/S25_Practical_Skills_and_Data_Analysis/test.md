# S25_Practical_Skills_and_Data_Analysis - Test: Practical skills, graphs and data analysis

Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. In a calorimetry experiment the temperature rose from 21.0 °C to 34.5 °C, measured with a thermometer reading to ±0.5 °C. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the temperature change, and suggest an improvement. [3 marks]
2. A student's experimental enthalpy of neutralisation is -51.0 kJ mol^-1; the accepted value is -57.1 kJ mol^-1, and the total uncertainty is 4%. Is the difference due to random error alone? Explain. [3 marks]
3. For a study of how temperature affects the rate of the thiosulfate-acid reaction, identify the independent and dependent variables and two control variables. [4 marks]
4. Explain why a pH meter should be calibrated before use, and how. [2 marks]
5. Give three features of a well-plotted graph from experimental data. [3 marks]
6. Explain the difference between accuracy and precision, using titration results as an example. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 ΔT = 13.5 °C, uncertainty ±1.0 °C (two readings); A1 7.4%; B1 use a thermometer with better resolution (e.g. ±0.1 °C) or a larger ΔT (more reactant).
2. [3] M1 percentage difference = 10.7%; B1 larger than the 4% uncertainty; B1 so systematic error (heat loss to the surroundings) also contributes.
3. [4] B1 independent: temperature; B1 dependent: time for the cross to disappear (rate); B1 B1 control: concentrations and volumes of both solutions, total volume, the same cross/flask.
4. [2] B1 the electrode's response drifts, so readings may be inaccurate; B1 measure buffer solutions of known pH (e.g. 4, 7, 10) and adjust.
5. [3] B1 labelled axes with units; B1 scales using at least half the grid; B1 points plotted accurately; B1 line or curve of best fit (not dot-to-dot); B1 anomalies identified -- any 3 for full marks.
6. [2] B1 precision: repeat titres close to each other (concordant); B1 accuracy: the mean titre close to the true value (precise results can be inaccurate because of systematic error, e.g. a wrongly made standard solution).
