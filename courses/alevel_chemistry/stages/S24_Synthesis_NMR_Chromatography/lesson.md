# S24_Synthesis_NMR_Chromatography - Lesson: Organic synthesis, NMR spectroscopy and chromatography

## Goal
The learner plans multistep organic syntheses, interprets 13C and 1H NMR spectra (chemical shift, integration, splitting), explains chromatography (TLC, column, gas), and carries out required practicals 10 and 12.

## How to teach this
Give the learner a 1H NMR spectrum with a triplet at 1.2 ppm and a quartet at 4.1 ppm, and ask what fragment it suggests. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.3.14 Organic synthesis (A-level only)
Plan syntheses of up to four steps using the reactions of this course, giving reagents and conditions (e.g. ethene → bromoethane (HBr) → propanenitrile (KCN, ethanol, reflux) → propylamine (H2/Ni or LiAlH4)). Chemists aim for processes that don't use hazardous solvents, have fewer steps and a high atom economy, and use renewable feedstocks. Know a map of functional group conversions in both directions.

#### 3.3.15 Nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy (A-level only)
NMR: nuclei with spin (1H, 13C) in a magnetic field absorb radio waves. Tetramethylsilane, Si(CH3)4 (TMS), is the reference (δ = 0): it gives a single strong signal upfield, is inert, volatile and non-toxic. Samples are dissolved in deuterated solvents (CDCl3) or CCl4 so the solvent gives no 1H signal. Chemical shift δ (ppm) depends on the environment (electronegative neighbours move the signal downfield). **13C NMR:** one peak per carbon environment; e.g. C=O 160-220, C-O 50-90, alkyl C-C 5-40 ppm. **1H NMR:** number of peaks = number of proton environments; integration (relative peak areas) = ratio of protons; splitting by the n + 1 rule (n = number of H on adjacent carbons): singlet, doublet, triplet, quartet. OH and NH protons usually give singlets. *Example:* ethyl ethanoate CH3COOCH2CH3: singlet (3H, ~2.0), quartet (2H, ~4.1), triplet (3H, ~1.2).

#### 3.3.16 Chromatography (A-level only)
Chromatography separates by distribution between a mobile phase and a stationary phase: components with greater affinity for the stationary phase move more slowly. TLC: a plate coated with silica or alumina; Rf = distance moved by spot/distance moved by solvent. Column chromatography: a column packed with silica or alumina; the solvent carries components out at different times (retention time). Gas chromatography (GC): a gas mobile phase through a column with a liquid or solid stationary phase; components are identified by retention time, and GC can be coupled with mass spectrometry (GC-MS) to identify each component.

#### RP10 Required practical 10: preparation of a pure organic solid (with a purity test) and a pure organic liquid
**Pure organic solid (e.g. aspirin):** react 2-hydroxybenzoic acid with ethanoic anhydride; collect the crude product by filtration under reduced pressure (Büchner funnel); recrystallise: dissolve in the minimum volume of hot solvent, filter hot to remove insoluble impurities, cool to crystallise, filter under reduced pressure, wash with a little cold solvent and dry. Test purity: a sharp melting point close to the data-book value (impure solids melt lower and over a range). **Pure organic liquid (e.g. an ester or cyclohexene):** after distillation, wash in a separating funnel (e.g. with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution to remove acid; release pressure), run off the aqueous layer, dry with anhydrous MgSO4 or CaCl2, filter or decant, and redistil, collecting the fraction at the boiling point.

#### RP12 Required practical 12: separation of species by thin-layer chromatography
**TLC:** draw a pencil baseline (ink would run), spot the samples with a capillary tube, place in a beaker with solvent below the baseline and a lid (a saturated atmosphere), remove when the solvent front is near the top and mark it, dry and locate spots (UV lamp or iodine/ninhydrin), measure distances and calculate Rf. Hazards: flammable solvents (no flames), handle plates by the edges.
