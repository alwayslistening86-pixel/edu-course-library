# S17_Acids_and_Bases - Test: Brønsted-Lowry acids, pH, Ka, titration curves and buffers

A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. 25.0 cm^3 of 0.200 mol dm^-3 NaOH is added to 40.0 cm^3 of 0.150 mol dm^-3 propanoic acid (Ka = 1.35 x 10^-5 mol dm^-3). Calculate the pH of the resulting buffer. [4 marks]
2. Calculate the pH of 0.250 mol dm^-3 methanoic acid (Ka = 1.78 x 10^-4 mol dm^-3), stating one assumption. [3 marks]
3. Explain how an ethanoic acid/sodium ethanoate buffer resists a change in pH when a small amount of NaOH is added. [3 marks]
4. Describe the pH curve for adding 0.1 mol dm^-3 ammonia to 25 cm^3 of 0.1 mol dm^-3 HCl, and choose a suitable indicator. [3 marks]
5. The ionic product of water at 40 °C is 2.92 x 10^-14 mol^2 dm^-6. Calculate the pH of pure water at 40 °C and explain why it is neutral. [3 marks]
6. 0.0500 mol dm^-3 sulfuric acid is diluted 10 times. Assuming both protons fully dissociate, give the pH before and after dilution. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 n(OH-) = 0.00500 mol, n(HA) = 0.00600 mol; M1 HA left 1.00 x 10^-3 mol, A- formed 0.00500 mol; M1 [H+] = Ka x 1.00 x 10^-3/0.00500 = 2.70 x 10^-6; A1 pH 5.57.
2. [3] M1 [H+] = root(1.78 x 10^-4 x 0.250) = 0.00667; A1 pH 2.18; B1 [H+] = [HCOO-] (water's dissociation ignored), or [HA] at equilibrium ≈ initial.
3. [3] B1 OH- reacts with CH3COOH (H+): CH3COOH + OH- → CH3COO- + H2O; B1 large reservoir of CH3COOH; B1 so the ratio [HA]/[A-] changes little, and [H+] (pH) stays nearly constant.
4. [3] B1 starts at pH 1; B1 vertical section roughly pH 3-7 at 25 cm^3, then levels off around pH 9-10; B1 methyl orange (changes within the vertical section).
5. [3] M1 [H+] = root(2.92 x 10^-14); A1 pH 6.77; B1 [H+] = [OH-], so it is neutral.
6. [2] B1 [H+] = 0.100: pH 1.00; B1 after: pH 2.00.
