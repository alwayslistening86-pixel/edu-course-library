# S06_Equilibria_and_Kc - Lesson: Equilibria, Le Chatelier's principle and Kc

## Goal
The learner describes dynamic equilibrium, applies Le Chatelier's principle to changes of concentration, pressure and temperature and to industrial compromises, writes and calculates Kc (including from equilibrium amounts), and explains which factors change Kc.

## How to teach this
Ask what is happening, at the particle level, in a sealed bottle of fizzy drink that looks unchanging. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.1.6.1 Chemical equilibria and Le Chatelier's principle
A reversible reaction in a closed system reaches dynamic equilibrium: forward and reverse rates are equal, so concentrations stay constant. Le Chatelier's principle: if a factor is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change. Concentration: adding a reactant shifts right. Pressure (gases): higher pressure shifts to the side with fewer gas molecules. Temperature: a rise shifts in the endothermic direction. A catalyst does not change the position, only how quickly equilibrium is reached. Industrial compromise, e.g. the Haber process N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 (ΔH = -92 kJ mol^-1): low temperature gives a higher yield but too slow a rate, so about 450 °C; high pressure gives a higher yield but is expensive and hazardous, so about 200 atm; an iron catalyst. Similarly for the production of methanol and ethanol.

#### 3.1.6.2 Equilibrium constant Kc for homogeneous systems
For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD (all in the same phase): Kc = [C]^c[D]^d/([A]^a[B]^b); its units follow from the expression. Find equilibrium amounts from initial amounts and the equation, then divide by volume. *Example:* H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI: 1.00 mol H2 and 1.00 mol I2 in 1.00 dm^3 give 1.56 mol HI at equilibrium, leaving 0.22 mol each of H2 and I2: Kc = 1.56^2/0.22^2 = 50.3 (no units). Only temperature changes Kc. For an exothermic forward reaction, raising the temperature decreases Kc. Changing concentration or pressure changes the position, but Kc stays the same; a catalyst changes neither.

#### MS 2.1 Understand and use the symbols =, <, <<, >>, >, ∝, ~ and the equilibrium sign
Symbols: ⇌ (equilibrium), ∝, ~, >>, <<: e.g. if Kc >> 1, the equilibrium lies well to the right.

#### MS 2.4 Solve algebraic equations
Solve equations for unknown equilibrium amounts, including quadratics. *Example:* for Kc = 4.0 in CH3COOH + C2H5OH ⇌ CH3COOC2H5 + H2O starting with 1 mol of each reactant: x^2/(1 - x)^2 = 4, so x/(1 - x) = 2 and x = 0.67 mol of ester.
