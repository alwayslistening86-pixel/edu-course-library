## Test items
1. Burning 0.500 g of propan-1-ol raised the temperature of 150 g of water by 21.5 K. Calculate the enthalpy of combustion of propan-1-ol and suggest two reasons it is less exothermic than the data-book value (-2021 kJ mol^-1). [5 marks]
2. Calculate ΔH for C2H4 + H2 → C2H6 using mean bond enthalpies: C=C 612, H-H 436, C-C 348, C-H 413 kJ mol^-1. [3 marks]
3. Define standard enthalpy of formation and write the equation it refers to for ethanol. [3 marks]
4. Use Hess's law to find ΔH for C(s) + 2H2(g) → CH4(g) from ΔcH values C -394, H2 -286, CH4 -890 kJ mol^-1. [2 marks]
5. Explain why an enthalpy change calculated from mean bond enthalpies differs from one found by Hess's law from formation data. [1 mark]
6. In required practical 2, explain why the temperature-time graph is extrapolated back to the time of mixing. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [5] M1 q = 150 x 4.18 x 21.5 = 13500 J; M1 n = 0.500/60.0 = 0.00833 mol; A1 ΔH = -1620 kJ mol^-1 (negative sign); B1 heat lost to the surroundings; B1 incomplete combustion (or evaporation of the alcohol, non-standard conditions).
2. [3] M1 broken 612 + 436 = 1048; M1 made 348 + 2 x 413 = 1174; A1 -126 kJ mol^-1.
3. [3] B1 enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed; B1 from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions; B1 2C(s) + 3H2(g) + (1/2)O2(g) → C2H5OH(l).
4. [2] M1 -394 + 2(-286) - (-890); A1 -76 kJ mol^-1.
5. [1] B1 mean bond enthalpies are averages over many compounds, not the values for the specific molecules (and assume gaseous substances).
6. [2] B1 the solution is losing heat to the surroundings throughout; B1 extrapolation estimates the maximum temperature if the reaction were instantaneous with no heat loss.
