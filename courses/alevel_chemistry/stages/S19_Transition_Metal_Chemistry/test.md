# S19_Transition_Metal_Chemistry - Test: Colour, variable oxidation states, catalysts and aqueous ions

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. An iron tablet (1.20 g) is dissolved in acid and made up to 250 cm^3. 25.0 cm^3 portions need 18.60 cm^3 of 0.0100 mol dm^-3 KMnO4. Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in the tablet. [4 marks]
2. Explain why [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ is coloured but [Zn(H2O)6]^2+ is not. [3 marks]
3. Explain, with equations, how Fe^2+ ions catalyse the reaction between peroxodisulfate and iodide ions, and why the uncatalysed reaction is slow. [4 marks]
4. Explain why [Fe(H2O)6]^3+ is more acidic than [Fe(H2O)6]^2+. [3 marks]
5. Describe what is seen, and write an equation, when sodium carbonate solution is added to a solution containing [Fe(H2O)6]^3+. [3 marks]
6. Explain why a heterogeneous catalyst is often spread on a support medium, and what catalyst poisoning is. [2 marks]
7. Which precipitates dissolve in excess aqueous ammonia? Choose every correct option.
   A. Cu(OH)2(H2O)4
   B. Al(OH)3(H2O)3
   C. Fe(OH)3(H2O)3
   D. Fe(OH)2(H2O)4

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 n(MnO4-) = 1.86 x 10^-4; M1 n(Fe^2+) in 25 cm^3 = 9.30 x 10^-4, x 10 = 0.00930 mol; M1 mass = x 55.8 = 0.519 g; A1 43.2%.
2. [3] B1 Cu^2+ has an incomplete 3d sub-level (3d^9); B1 d orbitals split, and electrons absorb visible light to move between levels (the rest is transmitted, giving the colour); B1 Zn^2+ is 3d^10: no d-d transitions possible.
3. [4] B1 S2O8^2- + 2Fe^2+ → 2SO4^2- + 2Fe^3+; B1 2Fe^3+ + 2I- → 2Fe^2+ + I2; B1 Fe^2+ is regenerated (variable oxidation state); B1 uncatalysed: both ions are negative and repel (high Ea).
4. [3] B1 Fe^3+ has a higher charge-to-size ratio (charge density); B1 polarises the coordinated water more, weakening the O-H bond; B1 so H+ is released more readily.
5. [3] B1 brown precipitate; B1 effervescence (CO2); B1 2[Fe(H2O)6]^3+ + 3CO3^2- → 2Fe(OH)3(H2O)3 + 3CO2 + 3H2O.
6. [2] B1 support maximises surface area and minimises the mass of (expensive) catalyst needed; B1 poisoning: impurities adsorb onto/block active sites, reducing efficiency.
7. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
