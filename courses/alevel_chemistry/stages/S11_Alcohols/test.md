# S11_Alcohols - Test: Alcohols: production, oxidation and elimination

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe how to prepare a sample of propanoic acid from propan-1-ol, giving the reagents, conditions and an equation using [O]. [4 marks]
2. Give the conditions for the industrial hydration of ethene and one advantage and one disadvantage compared with fermentation. [4 marks]
3. Butan-2-ol is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid. Name the three alkenes formed and the type of reaction. [4 marks]
4. Give a chemical test to distinguish propanal from propanone, with the results. [3 marks]
5. Explain why bioethanol is not truly carbon-neutral. [2 marks]
6. In required practical 5, explain why water enters a Liebig condenser at the bottom. [1 mark]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 acidified potassium dichromate, K2Cr2O7/H2SO4; B1 excess oxidising agent; B1 heat under reflux; B1 CH3CH2CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3CH2COOH + H2O.
2. [4] B1 steam, phosphoric acid catalyst; B1 300 °C and 60-70 atm; B1 advantage: fast, continuous, pure product (high atom economy); B1 disadvantage: uses a non-renewable feedstock (crude oil) (or high energy cost).
3. [4] B1 but-1-ene; B1 E-but-2-ene; B1 Z-but-2-ene; B1 elimination (dehydration).
4. [3] B1 Tollens' reagent, warm (or Fehling's); B1 propanal: silver mirror (red precipitate); B1 propanone: no change.
5. [2] B1 CO2 from fermentation and combustion equals that absorbed in photosynthesis; B1 but fossil fuels are burned in farming, transport and distillation, releasing additional CO2.
6. [1] B1 so the condenser jacket fills completely with cold water, making cooling efficient.
