# S11_Alcohols - Lesson: Alcohols: production, oxidation and elimination

## Goal
The learner describes the production of ethanol by fermentation and by hydration, the oxidation of primary, secondary and tertiary alcohols and tests to distinguish the products, the elimination of alcohols to alkenes, and carries out required practicals 5 and 6.

## Syllabus items taught here

- RP5 - Required practical 5: distillation of a product from a reaction

## How to teach this
Ask whether ethanol made from sugar cane is truly 'carbon-neutral'. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.3.5.1 Alcohol production
Alcohols are primary, secondary or tertiary by the number of carbons bonded to the C-OH carbon. Industrial hydration of ethene: C2H4 + H2O ⇌ C2H5OH, with steam, phosphoric acid catalyst, about 300 °C, 60-70 atm. The mechanism is electrophilic addition, with H+ from the acid. Fermentation of glucose: C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2, yeast, 30-40 °C, anaerobic. Bioethanol is claimed as carbon-neutral because CO2 released on combustion was absorbed in photosynthesis (6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2), but fuel for farming, transport and distillation releases more CO2, so it is not fully carbon-neutral.

#### 3.3.5.2 Oxidation of alcohols
Acidified potassium dichromate(VI), K2Cr2O7/H+ (orange → green Cr^3+), oxidises: primary alcohols → aldehydes (distil off as they form, so they are not oxidised further) → carboxylic acids (heat under reflux with excess oxidant); secondary → ketones; tertiary alcohols are not oxidised easily. Use [O] in equations: CH3CH2OH + [O] → CH3CHO + H2O; CH3CHO + [O] → CH3COOH. Distinguish aldehydes from ketones: Tollens' reagent ([Ag(NH3)2]+) gives a silver mirror with aldehydes; Fehling's solution goes from blue to a red precipitate (Cu2O) with aldehydes; ketones give no change.

#### 3.3.5.3 Elimination
Alcohols dehydrate to alkenes with concentrated H2SO4 or H3PO4 (heat), by acid-catalysed elimination: the OH is protonated, water leaves to give a carbocation, and H+ is lost from an adjacent carbon. This provides a route to polymers from renewable ethanol rather than crude oil. Unsymmetrical alcohols can give more than one alkene (including E/Z isomers): butan-2-ol gives but-1-ene, E-but-2-ene and Z-but-2-ene.

#### RP5 Required practical 5: distillation of a product from a reaction
**Distillation:** heat the reaction mixture in a round-bottomed or pear-shaped flask (anti-bumping granules), with a still head and thermometer bulb at the side-arm; water enters the Liebig condenser at the bottom and leaves at the top; collect the product in a cooled receiver. Example: preparing ethanal by adding acidified dichromate dropwise to warm ethanol and distilling immediately. Never heat a closed system. Reflux: a vertical condenser returns vapour to the flask so that the reaction can proceed with heating and without losing volatile substances.

#### RP6 Required practical 6: tests for alcohol, aldehyde, alkene and carboxylic acid
**Tests:** alcohol (primary/secondary): warm with acidified K2Cr2O7: orange to green; alkene: shake with bromine water: orange to colourless; aldehyde: warm with Tollens' (silver mirror) or Fehling's (blue to red precipitate); carboxylic acid: add NaHCO3 or Na2CO3: effervescence, CO2 turns limewater milky. Use a water bath for warming (flammable organics).
