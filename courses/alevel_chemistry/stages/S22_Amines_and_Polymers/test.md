# S22_Amines_and_Polymers - Test: Amines and condensation polymers

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why ethylamine is a stronger base than ammonia, and phenylamine a weaker base. [4 marks]
2. Give the repeating unit of the polymer formed from hexane-1,6-diamine and hexanedioyl dichloride, name the type of polymer and the small molecule lost. [3 marks]
3. Explain why polyesters are biodegradable while poly(ethene) is not. [2 marks]
4. Describe how excess bromoethane reacts with ammonia to give a quaternary ammonium salt, and state a use of quaternary ammonium salts. [3 marks]
5. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of disposing of waste polymers by incineration. [2 marks]
6. Explain why Kevlar is very strong. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 base strength depends on the availability of the N lone pair to accept H+; B1 the ethyl group releases electrons, increasing electron density on N; B1 in phenylamine the lone pair is delocalised into the ring; B1 so less available.
2. [3] B1 -NH(CH2)6NHCO(CH2)4CO-; B1 polyamide (condensation polymer; nylon-6,6); B1 HCl.
3. [2] B1 the ester links are polar and can be hydrolysed; B1 poly(ethene) has only non-polar C-C and C-H bonds, which are unreactive.
4. [3] B1 successive nucleophilic substitutions (ethylamine, diethylamine, triethylamine); B1 finally (C2H5)4N+ Br-; B1 cationic surfactants (fabric softeners, hair conditioners).
5. [2] B1 energy recovered for electricity/reduces landfill; B1 produces CO2 (greenhouse gas) or toxic gases such as HCl.
6. [2] B1 rigid, planar chains pack closely; B1 many strong hydrogen bonds between chains (N-H···O=C).
