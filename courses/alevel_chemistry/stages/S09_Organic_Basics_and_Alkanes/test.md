# S09_Organic_Basics_and_Alkanes - Test: Nomenclature, mechanisms, isomerism and alkanes

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Explain why pent-2-ene has E/Z isomers but pent-1-ene does not. [2 marks]
2. Give the conditions and a use of the products of catalytic cracking, and write an equation for cracking C12H26 into octane and ethene. [3 marks]
3. Describe how a catalytic converter reduces pollution, giving one equation. [3 marks]
4. Write the mechanism steps (initiation, two propagation and one termination) for the formation of chloromethane from methane. [4 marks]
5. Describe the skeletal formula of 2-methylpentane and give its molecular formula. [2 marks]
6. Name the type of structural isomerism between propanal and propanone. [1 mark]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 restricted rotation about C=C; B1 in pent-2-ene each carbon of the double bond has two different groups, while in pent-1-ene one carbon has two H atoms.
2. [3] B1 zeolite catalyst, about 450 °C, slight pressure; B1 motor fuels (aromatics, branched alkanes); B1 C12H26 → C8H18 + 2C2H4.
3. [3] B1 Pt/Rh (or Pd) catalyst on a ceramic honeycomb (large surface area); B1 converts CO, NO and unburned hydrocarbons into CO2, N2 and H2O; B1 2CO + 2NO → 2CO2 + N2.
4. [4] B1 Cl2 → 2Cl• (UV); B1 Cl• + CH4 → •CH3 + HCl; B1 •CH3 + Cl2 → CH3Cl + Cl•; B1 •CH3 + Cl• → CH3Cl (or another valid termination).
5. [2] B1 a five-carbon zig-zag with a branch on carbon 2; B1 C6H14.
6. [1] B1 functional group isomerism.
