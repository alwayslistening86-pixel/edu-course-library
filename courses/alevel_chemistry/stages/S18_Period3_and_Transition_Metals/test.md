# S18_Period3_and_Transition_Metals - Test: Period 3 oxides and transition metal complexes

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Write equations to show that aluminium oxide is amphoteric. [2 marks]
2. Explain why sodium oxide has a high melting point and phosphorus(V) oxide a low one. [3 marks]
3. Explain, in terms of enthalpy and entropy, why [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ + 3en → [Cu(en)3]^2+ + 6H2O is feasible. [3 marks]
4. Explain why zinc is not a transition metal although it is in the d block. [1 mark]
5. Explain why carbon monoxide is toxic, referring to haemoglobin. [2 marks]
6. Name the type of isomerism shown by [Ni(en)3]^2+ and by [Co(NH3)4Cl2]+. [2 marks]
7. Write an equation for the reaction of excess concentrated HCl with [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ and explain why the coordination number changes. [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] B1 Al2O3 + 6HCl → 2AlCl3 + 3H2O (or with H2SO4); B1 Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O → 2NaAl(OH)4.
2. [3] B1 Na2O is ionic: strong attraction between oppositely charged ions in a giant lattice; B1 P4O10 is simple molecular; B1 only weak intermolecular forces (van der Waals, dipole-dipole) are overcome.
3. [3] B1 ΔH ≈ 0: six Cu-N bonds replace six similar Cu-O bonds; B1 4 particles become 7, so ΔS is positive; B1 ΔG = ΔH - TΔS is negative.
4. [1] B1 its only ion, Zn^2+, has a full 3d^10 sub-level (no incomplete d sub-level).
5. [2] B1 CO forms a stronger coordinate bond to the Fe(II) of haem than O2; B1 so it replaces oxygen, and haemoglobin can no longer carry oxygen.
6. [2] B1 optical isomerism; B1 cis-trans isomerism.
7. [2] B1 [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ + 4Cl- → [CuCl4]^2- + 6H2O; B1 Cl- ligands are larger than water molecules, so fewer fit around the ion.
