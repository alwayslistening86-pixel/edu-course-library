# S18_Period3_and_Transition_Metals - Lesson: Period 3 oxides and transition metal complexes

## Goal
The learner describes the reactions of Period 3 elements with oxygen and of their oxides with water, acids and bases, relating them to structure and bonding; defines transition metals and their characteristic properties; explains ligands, coordination number, the chelate effect, ligand substitution, the shapes and isomerism of complexes, and the role of haem.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.2.4 - Properties of Period 3 elements and their oxides (A-level only)
- 3.2.5.1 - General properties of transition metals (A-level only)
- 3.2.5.2 - Substitution reactions (A-level only)
- 3.2.5.3 - Shapes of complex ions (A-level only)

## How to teach this
Ask why sodium oxide dissolves to give an alkali while sulfur trioxide gives an acid. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.2.4 Properties of Period 3 elements and their oxides (A-level only)
Reactions with oxygen: Na (yellow flame, Na2O), Mg (white flame, MgO), Al (white, Al2O3), Si (SiO2), P (white flame, P4O10), S (blue flame, SO2). Oxides with water: Na2O + H2O → 2NaOH (pH 12-14); MgO + H2O → Mg(OH)2 (pH about 9, slightly soluble); Al2O3 and SiO2 insoluble; P4O10 + 6H2O → 4H3PO4 (pH 0-1); SO2 + H2O → H2SO3 (pH 2-3); SO3 + H2O → H2SO4 (pH 0-1). Trend: ionic basic oxides (Na2O, MgO), amphoteric Al2O3, giant covalent acidic SiO2, simple molecular acidic P4O10, SO2, SO3. Melting points reflect structure (ionic and giant covalent high; molecular low). Neutralisation: MgO + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2O; SiO2 + 2NaOH → Na2SiO3 + H2O; Al2O3 + 6HCl → 2AlCl3 + 3H2O and Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O → 2NaAl(OH)4; P4O10 + 12NaOH → 4Na3PO4 + 6H2O; SO2 + 2NaOH → Na2SO3 + H2O.

#### 3.2.5.1 General properties of transition metals (A-level only)
Transition metals (Ti to Cu) have an incomplete d sub-level in their atoms or ions (Sc and Zn are not transition metals: Sc^3+ has no d electrons and Zn^2+ a full 3d^10). Characteristic properties: formation of complexes, coloured ions, variable oxidation states and catalytic activity. A complex is a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands; a ligand donates a lone pair to the metal to form a coordinate bond; the coordination number is the number of coordinate bonds. Ligands: monodentate (H2O, NH3, Cl-), bidentate (H2NCH2CH2NH2 'en', ethanedioate C2O4^2-), multidentate (EDTA^4-, hexadentate). Haem is an iron(II) complex with a multidentate ligand: oxygen forms a coordinate bond to Fe(II) in haemoglobin, carrying oxygen in the blood; carbon monoxide binds more strongly and replaces it, which is why CO is toxic.

#### 3.2.5.2 Substitution reactions (A-level only)
Ligand substitution: H2O and NH3 are similar in size and uncharged, so exchange happens without a change in coordination number: [Co(H2O)6]^2+ + 6NH3 → [Co(NH3)6]^2+ + 6H2O (and partial substitution for Cu: [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ + 4NH3 → [Cu(NH3)4(H2O)2]^2+ + 4H2O, deep blue). Cl- is larger, so substitution changes the coordination number from 6 to 4: [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ + 4Cl- → [CuCl4]^2- + 6H2O (yellow-green). The chelate effect: substitution of a monodentate by a bidentate or multidentate ligand is favoured by an entropy increase: [Cu(H2O)6]^2+ + 3en → [Cu(en)3]^2+ + 6H2O (4 → 7 particles; ΔH ≈ 0 as similar bonds break and form; ΔS positive, so ΔG negative); with EDTA^4- 2 particles become 7.

#### 3.2.5.3 Shapes of complex ions (A-level only)
Shapes: octahedral with small ligands (H2O, NH3), 90° angles; tetrahedral with larger Cl- ([CuCl4]^2-), 109.5°; square planar (cisplatin, Pt(NH3)2Cl2), 90°; linear ([Ag(NH3)2]+, Tollens' reagent), 180°. Isomerism: cis-trans in square planar and octahedral complexes (cisplatin is the cis isomer); optical isomerism in octahedral complexes with three bidentate ligands ([Ni(en)3]^2+).
