# S05_Kinetics - Test: Collision theory, Maxwell-Boltzmann distributions and catalysts

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Using the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution, explain why a small increase in temperature causes a large increase in reaction rate. [3 marks]
2. Explain how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction, referring to the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution. [3 marks]
3. State and explain the effect of doubling the pressure on the rate of a gaseous reaction. [2 marks]
4. In a reaction producing a gas, the tangent drawn to the volume-time curve at 30 s passes through (0 s, 12 cm^3) and (80 s, 60 cm^3). Calculate the rate at 30 s. [2 marks]
5. In required practical 3, give one control variable and explain why the reactants are warmed separately before mixing. [2 marks]
6. Which change increases the proportion of collisions with energy above E_a? Choose every correct option.
   A. raising the temperature
   B. raising the concentration
   C. adding a catalyst
   D. increasing the surface area

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 the peak moves to higher energy and lower height; B1 many more molecules have energy greater than or equal to E_a; B1 so the frequency of successful collisions increases greatly.
2. [3] B1 provides an alternative route; B1 with a lower activation energy; B1 so a greater proportion of molecules (area to the right of E_a) have enough energy.
3. [2] B1 rate increases; B1 more molecules per unit volume, so more frequent collisions (and successful collisions) per unit time.
4. [2] M1 gradient = 48/80; A1 0.60 cm^3 s^-1.
5. [2] B1 e.g. concentrations/volumes of both solutions, depth of solution, the same cross; B1 so the reaction starts at the measured temperature (it would otherwise begin before the temperature is reached).
6. Correct: A, C (exactly these options, no others)
