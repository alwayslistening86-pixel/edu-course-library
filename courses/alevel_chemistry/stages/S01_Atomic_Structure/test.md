# S01_Atomic_Structure - Test: Atomic structure, isotopes, mass spectrometry and electron configuration

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Describe how electrospray ionisation produces ions in a time-of-flight mass spectrometer, and write an equation for a molecule X. [3 marks]
2. Explain why the first ionisation energy of aluminium is lower than that of magnesium. [2 marks]
3. The successive ionisation energies (kJ mol^-1) of an element in Period 3 are 578, 1817, 2745, 11 577, 14 842. Identify the element and explain. [2 marks]
4. A sample of boron gives peaks at m/z 10 (relative abundance 23) and 11 (relative abundance 100). Calculate A_r to 1 decimal place. [2 marks]
5. In a TOF mass spectrometer, explain why ions of different mass reach the detector at different times. [2 marks]
6. Which electron configurations are correct? Choose every correct option.
   A. Cr: [Ar]3d^5 4s^1
   B. Cu^2+: [Ar]3d^9
   C. Ca: [Ar]3d^2
   D. S^2-: 1s^2 2s^2 2p^6 3s^2 3p^6

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 sample dissolved in a volatile solvent; B1 injected through a needle at high voltage (positive), each molecule gains a proton; B1 X(g) + H+ → XH+(g).
2. [2] B1 aluminium's outer electron is in a 3p sub-level (magnesium's in 3s); B1 3p is higher in energy/slightly further from the nucleus with more shielding, so less energy is needed.
3. [2] B1 aluminium (Group 3); B1 large jump after the third electron: the fourth is removed from an inner (second) shell.
4. [2] M1 (10 x 23 + 11 x 100)/123; A1 10.8.
5. [2] B1 all ions are given the same kinetic energy; B1 KE = (1/2)mv^2, so lighter ions travel faster and arrive first.
6. Correct: A, B, D (exactly these options, no others)
