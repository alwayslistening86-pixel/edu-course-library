# S16_Electrode_Potentials - Test: Electrode potentials and electrochemical cells

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. Use E° values (Fe^3+/Fe^2+ +0.77 V, I2/I- +0.54 V, Br2/Br- +1.07 V) to predict whether Fe^3+ ions will oxidise I- ions and Br- ions. Write an equation for any reaction. [4 marks]
2. Describe the standard hydrogen electrode, giving all standard conditions. [4 marks]
3. Write the half-equations at each electrode of an alkaline hydrogen-oxygen fuel cell, and give one disadvantage of fuel cells. [3 marks]
4. Write the conventional representation for a cell made from a Fe^3+/Fe^2+ half-cell and a Zn^2+/Zn half-cell (Zn^2+/Zn -0.76 V), and calculate E°cell. [3 marks]
5. In required practical 8, explain why a salt bridge made with KNO3 is used rather than a wire. [2 marks]
6. Explain why a lithium-ion cell can be recharged but an alkaline cell cannot (easily). [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 Fe^3+ with I-: E°cell = 0.77 - 0.54 = +0.23 V, feasible; A1 2Fe^3+ + 2I- → 2Fe^2+ + I2; M1 with Br-: 0.77 - 1.07 = -0.30 V; A1 not feasible.
2. [4] B1 hydrogen gas at 100 kPa; B1 bubbled over a platinum electrode; B1 in 1.00 mol dm^-3 H+ (e.g. HCl); B1 at 298 K.
3. [3] B1 H2 + 2OH- → 2H2O + 2e-; B1 O2 + 2H2O + 4e- → 4OH-; B1 hydrogen is flammable/hard to store, or is usually made using fossil fuels.
4. [3] B1 Zn(s)|Zn^2+(aq)||Fe^3+(aq),Fe^2+(aq)|Pt(s); B1 platinum electrode for the iron half-cell; B1 E° = 0.77 + 0.76 = 1.53 V.
5. [2] B1 it allows ions to move to complete the circuit (balancing charge); B1 without a metal/metal junction that would create extra potential differences (KNO3 doesn't react with the ions or form precipitates).
6. [2] B1 in a rechargeable cell the electrode reactions can be reversed by applying a current; B1 in non-rechargeable cells the products cannot be converted back (e.g. they don't remain on the electrode).
