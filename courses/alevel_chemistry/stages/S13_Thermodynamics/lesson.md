# S13_Thermodynamics - Lesson: Born-Haber cycles, entropy and Gibbs free energy

## Goal
The learner defines the enthalpy terms in Born-Haber cycles, constructs cycles to find lattice enthalpies, compares them with perfect ionic model values, calculates enthalpies of solution, explains entropy changes, and uses ΔG = ΔH - TΔS to decide feasibility and the temperature at which a reaction becomes feasible.

## Syllabus items taught here
- 3.1.8.1 - Born–Haber cycles (A-level only)
- 3.1.8.2 - Gibbs free-energy change, ∆G, and entropy change, ∆S (A-level only)

## How to teach this
Ask why ice melts spontaneously at room temperature even though melting is endothermic. Teach from the particles outwards: what the electrons and bonds are doing, then the equation or mechanism, then a worked example with units and sensible significant figures. Use relative atomic masses from AQA's Periodic Table (e.g. H 1.0, C 12.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5) and its data sheet values (N_A = 6.022 x 10^23 mol^-1, R = 8.31 J K^-1 mol^-1). Mechanisms are described in words: say where each curly arrow starts (a lone pair or a bond) and where it ends. Every numerical answer here was computed when the course was built.

#### 3.1.8.1 Born–Haber cycles (A-level only)
Definitions (one mole, standard conditions): enthalpy of formation; ionisation energy (gaseous atoms → gaseous 1+ ions); enthalpy of atomisation (elements → one mole of gaseous atoms); bond enthalpy; electron affinity (gaseous atoms gain one electron each: first EA exothermic, second EA endothermic because an electron is added to a negative ion); lattice enthalpy (either lattice dissociation, solid → gaseous ions, endothermic, or lattice formation, gaseous ions → solid, exothermic; state which); enthalpy of hydration (gaseous ions → aqueous ions); enthalpy of solution (solid → aqueous ions). Born-Haber cycle for NaCl (kJ mol^-1): ΔfH -411, atomisation of Na +107, first IE +496, atomisation of Cl +122, EA of Cl -349: lattice formation enthalpy = -411 - (107 + 496 + 122 - 349) = -787 kJ mol^-1. The perfect ionic model (spherical ions, purely ionic) calculates lattice enthalpies from charges and sizes; if the Born-Haber value is more exothermic than the model's (e.g. AgCl, MgI2), the bonding has some covalent character (the cation polarises the anion). Enthalpy of solution = lattice dissociation enthalpy + Σ hydration enthalpies. *Example:* NaCl: +787 + (-406) + (-364) = +17 kJ mol^-1. Hydration enthalpies are more exothermic for small, highly charged ions.

#### 3.1.8.2 Gibbs free-energy change, ∆G, and entropy change, ∆S (A-level only)
Entropy S is a measure of disorder: the number of ways particles and their energy can be arranged. S(gas) >> S(liquid) > S(solid); S increases with temperature and on dissolving. ΔS = ΣS(products) - ΣS(reactants) (J K^-1 mol^-1). Gibbs free energy: ΔG = ΔH - TΔS (convert ΔS to kJ K^-1 mol^-1); a reaction is feasible when ΔG ≤ 0. When ΔG = 0, T = ΔH/ΔS: the temperature at which the reaction becomes feasible. A plot of ΔG against T is a straight line with gradient -ΔS and intercept ΔH. *Example:* CaCO3 → CaO + CO2, ΔH = +178 kJ mol^-1, ΔS = +161 J K^-1 mol^-1: feasible above T = 178 000/161 = 1110 K. A feasible reaction may still be very slow (high E_a): kinetics is separate from thermodynamics.
