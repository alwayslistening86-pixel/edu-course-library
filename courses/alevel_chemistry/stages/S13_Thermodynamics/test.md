# S13_Thermodynamics - Test: Born-Haber cycles, entropy and Gibbs free energy

## Test items
1. Calculate the lattice enthalpy of formation of MgCl2 from: ΔfH(MgCl2) -642; atomisation of Mg +148; first and second IE of Mg +738, +1451; atomisation of Cl (per mole of Cl atoms) +122; electron affinity of Cl -349 kJ mol^-1. [3 marks]
2. The Born-Haber lattice enthalpy of silver chloride is more exothermic than the value from the perfect ionic model. Explain. [2 marks]
3. The lattice dissociation enthalpy of KCl is +711 kJ mol^-1; hydration enthalpies are K+ -322 and Cl- -364 kJ mol^-1. Calculate the enthalpy of solution and explain why KCl still dissolves. [3 marks]
4. For N2O4(g) → 2NO2(g), ΔH = +57.2 kJ mol^-1. S values: N2O4 304, NO2 240 J K^-1 mol^-1. Calculate ΔS, ΔG at 298 K, and the temperature above which the reaction is feasible. [5 marks]
5. Explain why a reaction with a negative ΔG may not occur at room temperature. [1 mark]
6. Which processes have a positive entropy change? Choose every correct option.
   A. melting ice
   B. 2H2(g) + O2(g) → 2H2O(l)
   C. dissolving NaCl in water
   D. CaCO3(s) → CaO(s) + CO2(g)

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 sum of steps from elements to gaseous ions: 148 + 738 + 1451 + 2(122) + 2(-349) = 1883; M1 lattice = -642 - that sum; A1 -2525 kJ mol^-1.
2. [2] B1 the bonding in AgCl has some covalent character; B1 because the Ag+ ion polarises the Cl- ion (distorts its electron cloud), adding extra attraction.
3. [3] M1 711 - 322 - 364; A1 +25 kJ mol^-1 (endothermic); B1 entropy increases on dissolving, so ΔG is negative (TΔS > ΔH).
4. [5] M1 ΔS = 2(240) - 304; A1 +176 J K^-1 mol^-1; M1 ΔG = 57.2 - 298 x 0.176; A1 +4.8 kJ mol^-1 (not feasible); A1 T = 57 200/176 = 325 K.
5. [1] B1 the activation energy may be very high, so the rate is negligible.
6. Correct: A, C, D (exactly these options, no others)
