# S02_Amount_of_Substance - Test: Amount of substance, the ideal gas equation and titration

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in the style of AQA's papers: structured questions with marks shown (and some multiple choice). Give the whole test at once, with no hints; the learner shows working and may use a calculator, the Periodic Table and the data sheet. Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. 25.0 cm^3 of sodium hydroxide solution is neutralised by 21.40 cm^3 of 0.150 mol dm^-3 sulfuric acid. Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide. [3 marks]
2. A 0.120 g sample of a volatile liquid vaporises to give 45.0 cm^3 of gas at 373 K and 101 kPa. Calculate its M_r. [3 marks]
3. Iron is made by Fe2O3 + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO2. Calculate the atom economy for iron and the percentage yield if 1.00 tonne of Fe2O3 gives 0.600 tonnes of iron. [4 marks]
4. Hydrated magnesium sulfate MgSO4.xH2O (2.46 g) is heated to constant mass, leaving 1.20 g of anhydrous MgSO4. Find x. [3 marks]
5. In required practical 1, explain why the burette is rinsed with the titrant rather than with water before filling. [2 marks]
6. How many chloride ions are in 2.22 g of calcium chloride? [2 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] M1 n(H2SO4) = 0.003210 mol; M1 x 2 for NaOH (2NaOH + H2SO4 → Na2SO4 + 2H2O); A1 0.257 mol dm^-3.
2. [3] M1 n = pV/RT = 0.001466 mol; M1 M_r = m/n; A1 81.8.
3. [4] M1 2 x 55.8/(159.6 + 3 x 28.0); A1 45.8%; M1 theoretical Fe = 1.00 x 111.6/159.6 = 0.699 t; A1 85.8%.
4. [3] M1 n(MgSO4) = 1.20/120.4 = 0.0100; M1 n(H2O) = 1.26/18.0 = 0.0700; A1 x = 7.0, so 7.
5. [2] B1 water would dilute the titrant; B1 lowering its concentration and increasing the titre (making the calculated result wrong).
6. [2] M1 n(CaCl2) = 2.22/111.1 = 0.0200 mol; A1 x 2 x N_A = 2.41 x 10^22.
