# S21_BE_Business_Entities - Lesson: Business entities and legal administration

## Goal
The learner explains the different models of business ownership, their legal administration requirements, and how business entities are formed.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BE4.1 - Models of business ownership: sole trader, partnership (including LLP), company, not-for-profit; limited liability; taxes applicable to each
- BE4.2 - The legal administration of a business: statutory books, accounting records, tax records, annual filing requirements
- BE4.3 - Business formation: how entities are formed, off-the-shelf companies, pre-incorporation contracts, business names

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if a sole trader's business fails owing money, can the owner's personal house be at risk? What about a shareholder in a limited company? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### BE4.1 Models of business ownership: sole trader, partnership (including LLP), company, not-for-profit; limited liability; taxes applicable to each
**Sole trader**: one individual owns and runs the business; simple and cheap to set up; the owner keeps all profit but has **unlimited liability** -- personally liable for all business debts, putting personal assets at risk. **Partnership**: two or more people share ownership, profit and (in an ordinary partnership) unlimited liability; a **limited liability partnership (LLP)** gives partners limited liability (liability capped at what they've invested) while keeping partnership-style flexibility, and must be registered at Companies House. **Company**: a separate legal entity from its owners (shareholders), who have **limited liability** (liability capped at the amount unpaid on their shares); more complex and costly to set up and run (e.g. filing requirements), but protects owners' personal assets. **Not-for-profit** organisations (e.g. charities) exist to pursue a social/charitable purpose rather than to generate profit for owners; any surplus is reinvested in the organisation's purpose rather than distributed. **Taxes applicable** differ by entity: sole traders and partners pay Income Tax and National Insurance on profits; companies pay Corporation Tax on profits, and may also need to register for VAT and operate PAYE for employees, as can sole traders/partnerships with employees.

#### BE4.2 The legal administration of a business: statutory books, accounting records, tax records, annual filing requirements
Legal administration requirements differ by entity type, but generally include: **statutory books** (formal records a company must keep, e.g. register of members, register of directors); **accounting records** (records supporting the preparation of accurate financial statements, required of all entities though in more prescribed form for companies); **tax records** (records supporting tax returns, which HMRC can require to be produced); and **annual filing requirements** (e.g. companies must file annual accounts and a confirmation statement at Companies House, and file a Corporation Tax return with HMRC; sole traders and partners file Self Assessment tax returns).

#### BE4.3 Business formation: how entities are formed, off-the-shelf companies, pre-incorporation contracts, business names
**How entities are formed**: a sole trader simply starts trading (with some registration requirements, e.g. for tax); a partnership is formed by agreement between the partners (often a formal partnership agreement, though not legally required for an ordinary partnership); a company is formed by **incorporation** -- registering with Companies House, submitting required documents (e.g. memorandum and articles of association) and paying a fee. An **off-the-shelf company** is a company already incorporated by a formation agent, ready to be bought and have its details (name, directors, etc.) changed by the new owner, saving time versus incorporating from scratch. A **pre-incorporation contract** is one entered into on behalf of a company before it is legally formed; because the company does not yet legally exist, the person who signed it is generally personally liable on it unless the contract states otherwise. **Business names** must not be identical or too similar to an existing registered name, must not be offensive, and (for companies) certain sensitive words require permission to use.
