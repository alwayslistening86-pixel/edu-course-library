## Test items
1. Explain the difference between a sole trader and a limited company in terms of legal status and liability. [4 marks]
2. Explain what an off-the-shelf company is and why a business owner might choose to buy one rather than incorporate a new company from scratch. [2 marks]
3. Explain who is generally liable on a pre-incorporation contract, and why. [2 marks]
4. Which business entity gives its partners limited liability while keeping partnership-style flexibility, and must be registered at Companies House? Choose every correct option.
   A. a limited liability partnership (LLP)
   B. an ordinary partnership
   C. a sole trader
   D. a not-for-profit organisation

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 a sole trader is not a separate legal entity from its owner, so the owner and the business are legally the same; B1 a sole trader has unlimited liability, personally liable for all business debts; B1 a limited company is a separate legal entity from its shareholders; B1 shareholders have limited liability, capped at the amount unpaid on their shares.
2. [2] B1 a company already incorporated by a formation agent, ready to be bought and have its details changed by the new owner; B1 this saves time compared with going through the full incorporation process from scratch.
3. [2] B1 the person who signed the contract on the company's behalf is generally personally liable; B1 because the company does not yet legally exist at the time the contract is made, so it cannot itself be a party to the contract.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
