## Test items
1. Explain the difference between criminal law and civil law, giving the standard of proof used in each. [4 marks]
2. Explain the difference between an offer and an invitation to treat, giving an example of each. [3 marks]
3. A supplier fails to deliver a unique piece of machinery as contractually agreed. Explain why the buyer might seek specific performance rather than damages, and why the court might refuse it. [3 marks]
4. Which of these is a source of law developed through court decisions rather than created directly by Parliament? Choose every correct option.
   A. common law (precedent)
   B. an Act of Parliament
   C. delegated legislation
   D. a statutory instrument

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] B1 criminal law deals with conduct treated as an offence against society, prosecuted by the state; B1 standard of proof is 'beyond reasonable doubt'; B1 civil law deals with disputes between private parties, brought by the wronged party; B1 standard of proof is 'on the balance of probabilities'.
2. [3] B1 an offer is a clear proposal to be bound on stated terms if accepted, e.g. a specific price quoted directly to a buyer; B1 an invitation to treat merely invites others to make an offer, e.g. goods displayed in a shop window with a price tag; B1 correct examples given.
3. [3] B1 specific performance orders the supplier to actually deliver the machinery, appropriate because the machinery is unique and money (damages) may not adequately compensate the buyer; B1 specific performance is an equitable remedy, so it is entirely at the court's discretion, not available as of right; B1 the court could refuse it and award damages instead if it judged damages to be an adequate remedy after all.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
