# S24_BE_Information_and_Security - Lesson: Information and data security

## Goal
The learner explains the role of information in the finance function, including the characteristics of useful information and digital technologies, and the importance of data and information security.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BE7.1 - The role of information in the work of the finance function: characteristics of useful information, digital technologies, documents received and produced
- BE7.2 - The importance of data and information security: privacy, confidentiality, passwords, archiving, backups, cybersecurity

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: what makes financial information actually useful to the person receiving it, rather than just accurate? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### BE7.1 The role of information in the work of the finance function: characteristics of useful information, digital technologies, documents received and produced
Useful information in the finance function should have certain **characteristics**: it should be **accurate** (correct, free from material error), **relevant** (fit for the purpose it's needed for), **timely** (available when needed to be useful for a decision), **complete** (containing everything needed, not omitting material facts), and presented in a way that's **understandable** to its intended audience. **Digital technologies** (accounting software, spreadsheets, cloud storage, automated bank feeds) increasingly support the finance function by speeding up processing, reducing manual error, and enabling real-time reporting. The finance function both **receives** documents (e.g. supplier invoices, bank statements, expense claims) and **produces** documents (e.g. financial statements, management reports, customer invoices) as part of its daily operation.

#### BE7.2 The importance of data and information security: privacy, confidentiality, passwords, archiving, backups, cybersecurity
**Data and information security** matters because the finance function handles highly sensitive information (financial records, personal data about employees and customers). Key aspects: **privacy** and **confidentiality** (only sharing information with those authorised to see it, in line with data protection law and the confidentiality principle of ethics); **passwords** (using strong, regularly-changed passwords and not sharing them, to prevent unauthorised access); **archiving** (retaining records for as long as legally/organisationally required, in a way that keeps them retrievable and secure); **backups** (keeping copies of data so it can be recovered if lost, corrupted or destroyed); and **cybersecurity** (protecting systems from threats such as phishing, malware and unauthorised access, e.g. through firewalls, staff training and access controls).
