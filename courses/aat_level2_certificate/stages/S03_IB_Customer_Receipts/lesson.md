# S03_IB_Customer_Receipts - Lesson: Processing receipts from customers

## Goal
The learner identifies discrepancies in customer receipts, calculates amounts due (including PPD), and allocates receipts correctly.

## Syllabus items taught here
- IB2.3 - Process receipts from customers: discrepancies, calculating amounts due including PPD, allocating receipts

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if a customer owes £1,200 across two invoices and pays only £700, how should that £700 be allocated? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### IB2.3 Process receipts from customers: discrepancies, calculating amounts due including PPD, allocating receipts
Records/documents used to process customer receipts: the **customer account**, **sales invoice**, **sales credit note**, **remittance advice** and the **discount policy**. Discrepancies to check for when a receipt arrives: **underpayment**, **overpayment**, **incorrect discount taken** (e.g. PPD claimed after the deadline), **incorrect amounts**, **incorrect details**, **timing differences**, **missing transactions** and **duplicated transactions**. Amounts due from customers are calculated taking any PPD into account (see IB2.2 for the credit-note method). Receipts are **allocated** correctly against the customer's account: **in full** (paying exactly what is owed), **in part** (paying less than the full balance, leaving a remainder owed), **against opening balances**, **against specific invoices**, and **against credit notes** (reducing what is owed before applying a payment). *Worked example -- part payment:* a customer owes £1,200 in total; they pay £700. The receipt is allocated against the oldest/agreed invoice(s) first (or as the remittance advice specifies); the remaining balance owed is £1,200 - £700 = **£500**.
