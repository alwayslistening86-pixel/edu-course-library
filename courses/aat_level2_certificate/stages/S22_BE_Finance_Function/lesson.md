# S22_BE_Finance_Function - Lesson: The finance function

## Goal
The learner explains the different functions of a business, the role of the finance function, and how the finance team contributes to organisational success.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BE5.1 - The different functions of a business: operations, sales and marketing, finance, HR, IT, distribution and logistics
- BE5.2 - The role of the finance function: statutory financial statements, service to internal and external stakeholders, outsourcing
- BE5.3 - How the finance team contributes to the success of an organisation: effective communication, solvency, policies and procedures

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: besides producing the annual accounts, what else might a finance department be asked to do for other departments in the business? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### BE5.1 The different functions of a business: operations, sales and marketing, finance, HR, IT, distribution and logistics
A business typically has several distinct functions working together: **operations** (making the product or delivering the service); **sales and marketing** (generating and winning customer demand); **finance** (managing money, records and reporting -- see BE5.2); **HR** (recruiting, managing and supporting employees); **IT** (systems and technology supporting every other function); **distribution and logistics** (getting goods/services to customers). Understanding these functions helps the finance function serve the whole organisation effectively, since it interacts with and supports all of them (e.g. paying suppliers for operations, processing payroll for HR, budgeting for sales and marketing).

#### BE5.2 The role of the finance function: statutory financial statements, service to internal and external stakeholders, outsourcing
The **finance function**'s role includes producing the **statutory financial statements** required by law (e.g. for companies: filing accounts at Companies House and a Corporation Tax return with HMRC). It also provides a **service to internal stakeholders** (e.g. management information and budgets to support decision-making by other departments) and **external stakeholders** (e.g. accurate accounts for investors, lenders, HMRC). Some organisations choose to **outsource** parts of the finance function (e.g. payroll, bookkeeping) to external providers, which can reduce cost and access specialist expertise, but reduces direct control and may raise data-security/confidentiality concerns.

#### BE5.3 How the finance team contributes to the success of an organisation: effective communication, solvency, policies and procedures
The finance team contributes to organisational success through: **effective communication** (clearly explaining financial information to non-finance colleagues, so it can actually be used in decisions); maintaining organisational **solvency** (ensuring the business can pay its debts as they fall due, e.g. through cash flow monitoring and credit control); and having clear **policies and procedures** (e.g. authorisation limits, expense policies, segregation of duties) that support accuracy, consistency and control over financial transactions.
