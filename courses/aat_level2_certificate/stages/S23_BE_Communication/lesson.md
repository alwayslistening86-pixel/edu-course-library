# S23_BE_Communication - Lesson: Communication and workload planning

## Goal
The learner selects appropriate sources of information, communicates effectively in business formats, and plans workload to meet organisational needs.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BE6.1 - Sources of information: valid vs invalid, primary vs secondary, choosing information for a purpose
- BE6.2 - Communicate information: business letters and emails, features of business communications, risks of social media and inappropriate sharing
- BE6.3 - Plan workload to meet the needs of the organisation: prioritising, monitoring and reviewing

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: why might information found on an unverified social media post be a poor source to rely on for a business decision, compared with an official government statistic? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### BE6.1 Sources of information: valid vs invalid, primary vs secondary, choosing information for a purpose
Sources of information should be assessed for validity. **Valid** sources are accurate, reliable and from a credible origin (e.g. official statistics, audited accounts, a verified industry report); **invalid** sources may be inaccurate, biased, outdated or unverifiable (e.g. an unverified social media post, a source with an undisclosed conflict of interest). **Primary sources** are original/first-hand (e.g. data the business collects itself through a customer survey); **secondary sources** are compiled or reported by someone else from existing data (e.g. a published industry report summarising market data). Choosing information for a purpose means matching the source's reliability, relevance and level of detail to what the task actually requires.

#### BE6.2 Communicate information: business letters and emails, features of business communications, risks of social media and inappropriate sharing
Effective business communication uses the right format for the audience and purpose. **Business letters** are formal, used for official correspondence (e.g. to external parties); **emails** are faster and more common for day-to-day internal and external communication but still require a professional tone and clear structure. Good business communications share features: clarity, appropriate tone and formality for the audience, correct grammar/spelling, and a clear purpose/call to action. **Risks of social media and inappropriate sharing** include: reputational damage to the business from an ill-judged post, breach of client/employer confidentiality, and legal risk (e.g. defamation, data protection breaches) -- accounting technicians should be especially careful given their access to sensitive financial information.

#### BE6.3 Plan workload to meet the needs of the organisation: prioritising, monitoring and reviewing
Planning workload to meet organisational needs involves: **prioritising** tasks (e.g. by deadline, importance, or dependency on other tasks/people); **monitoring** progress against the plan as work proceeds; and **reviewing** and adjusting the plan when circumstances change (e.g. an urgent request arrives, a deadline moves). Effective workload planning helps meet deadlines, maintain quality, and manage the competing demands typical of a finance role.
