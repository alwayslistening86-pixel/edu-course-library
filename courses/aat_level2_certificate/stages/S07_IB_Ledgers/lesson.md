# S07_IB_Ledgers - Lesson: The ledgers: transferring data and balancing accounts

## Goal
The learner transfers data from the books of prime entry to the receivables, payables and general ledgers, and totals and balances ledger accounts.

## Syllabus items taught here
- IB5.1 - Transfer data from the books of prime entry to the ledgers: receivables, payables and general ledgers
- IB5.2 - Total and balance ledger accounts: balance carried/brought down, debit and credit balances

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: once the sales daybook has been totalled for the month, where do those totals go next? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### IB5.1 Transfer data from the books of prime entry to the ledgers: receivables, payables and general ledgers
Totals from the books of prime entry (daybooks, cash book, petty cash book) are **transferred** to the relevant accounts in the **receivables ledger** (individual customer accounts), **payables ledger** (individual supplier accounts) and **general ledger** (every other account, including the receivables and payables *control* accounts). The receivables and payables ledger *control accounts* (in the general ledger) are part of the double-entry system and should, in total, agree with the sum of the individual balances in the receivables/payables ledgers (checked by reconciliation, covered in Principles of Bookkeeping Controls).

#### IB5.2 Total and balance ledger accounts: balance carried/brought down, debit and credit balances
Ledger accounts are **totalled and balanced** the same way as the cash book (IB4.3): find the **balance carried down** so both sides total the same, then bring that figure down as the **balance brought down** for the next period, on the opposite side from where it was carried down. An account with a debit balance brought down (assets, expenses) means it has a net debit position; a **credit balance** brought down (liabilities, income, capital) means a net credit position.
