# S06_IB_Cash_Book_and_Petty_Cash - Lesson: The cash book, petty cash book and recurring entries

## Goal
The learner enters receipts and payments into an analysed cash book and petty cash book, totals and balances both, and processes recurring entries.

## Syllabus items taught here
- IB4.1 - Enter receipts and payments into an analysed cash book
- IB4.2 - Enter receipts and payments into an analysed petty cash book, including the imprest and non-imprest systems
- IB4.3 - Total and balance the cash book and petty cash book
- IB4.4 - Process recurring receipts and payments

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: what's the difference between money coming into a till (cash) and money moving through the business bank account, and why might a cash book track both side by side? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### IB4.1 Enter receipts and payments into an analysed cash book
The **analysed cash book** records money received and paid through the bank (and cash), with columns for **date**, **details**, **cash**, **bank** and **analysis columns** (including VAT), so amounts can be summarised by type as well as in total. Documents used to make entries: **direct debit/standing order schedules**, **remittance advice (including BACS)**, **paying-in slips**, **cheque stubs**, **cash receipts** and **receipts and payments listings**. Entries are made from net and total figures already calculated on these documents.

#### IB4.2 Enter receipts and payments into an analysed petty cash book, including the imprest and non-imprest systems
The **analysed petty cash book** records small cash payments (and any cash received into the float), with columns for **date**, **details**, **cash** and **analysis columns** (including VAT). Documents used: **cash receipts** and **petty cash vouchers**. It is reimbursed under the **imprest system** (the float is topped back up to a fixed amount at the end of each period, by the exact amount spent) or the **non-imprest system** (a variable amount is added as needed, with no fixed float total). *Worked example (imprest system):* the float (imprest amount) is £100.00; during the period, vouchers total £23.50 + £14.20 + £8.00 = **£45.70** spent. Under the imprest system, exactly £45.70 is reimbursed at the end of the period, restoring the float to £100.00.

#### IB4.3 Total and balance the cash book and petty cash book
The cash book and petty cash book are **totalled and balanced** the same way as any ledger account: sum each column, then find the **balance carried down** (the difference between total receipts and total payments) so the account balances, and bring that figure down as the **balance brought down** for the next period. *Worked example:* a cash book's receipts total £845.50 (£450.00 + £275.50 + £120.00) and its payments total £390.25 (£300.00 + £90.25); the balance carried down (and brought down) = £845.50 - £390.25 = **£455.25**.

#### IB4.4 Process recurring receipts and payments
A **recurring entry** is a transaction that repeats regularly with the same (or predictable) amount, e.g. monthly rent, a loan repayment or a subscription. To set one up, the business needs to know: the amount (or how it varies), the frequency, the start/end dates and the accounts affected. In a digital bookkeeping system, once set up, a recurring entry is generated and posted automatically each period, saving time -- but it can also create errors if the amount or frequency of the underlying payment changes and the recurring entry is not updated to match, so it should be reviewed regularly rather than left unchecked indefinitely.
