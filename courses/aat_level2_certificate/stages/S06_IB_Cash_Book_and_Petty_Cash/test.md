## Test items
1. Explain the difference between the imprest and non-imprest systems for petty cash. [3 marks]
2. A cash book's receipts total £2,180.60 and its payments total £1,905.15. Calculate the balance carried down, and state what this figure becomes at the start of the next period. [3 marks]
3. Explain one benefit and one risk of setting up a recurring entry in a digital bookkeeping system. [2 marks]
4. Which documents are used to make entries in the analysed cash book? Choose every correct option.
   A. direct debit/standing order schedules, remittance advice, paying-in slips, cheque stubs
   B. petty cash vouchers only
   C. purchase orders and delivery notes
   D. statements of account only

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 imprest: the float is topped back up to a fixed amount at the end of each period, by exactly the amount spent; B1 non-imprest: a variable amount is added as needed, with no fixed total the float is restored to; B1 the imprest system makes it easier to spot discrepancies, since the float should always return to the same fixed total.
2. [3] M1 2,180.60 - 1,905.15; A1 £275.45; B1 this becomes the balance brought down at the start of the next period.
3. [2] B1 benefit, e.g. saves time/reduces errors from repeated manual entry, since it posts automatically each period; B1 risk, e.g. if the amount or frequency of the real payment changes and the recurring entry is not updated, it will keep posting the wrong figure.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
