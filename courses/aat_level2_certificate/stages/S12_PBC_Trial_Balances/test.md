# S12_PBC_Trial_Balances - Test: Trial balances

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. An Insurance expense account had a debit balance of £1,800 before adjustment. A journal entry adds a further £150 of insurance not yet recorded (debit Insurance Expense £150, credit accrued expenses £150). Calculate the redrafted Insurance Expense balance. [2 marks]
2. Explain why a trial balance that balances does not prove the bookkeeping is completely correct. [3 marks]
3. A Rent Receivable (income) account had a credit balance of £6,200 before adjustment. A journal entry corrects an error, adding £300 of rent income that had been omitted (debit Receivables £300, credit Rent Receivable £300). Calculate the redrafted Rent Receivable balance. [2 marks]
4. When redrafting a trial balance following adjustments, what must still be true once every adjustment is posted? Choose every correct option.
   A. total debits still equal total credits
   B. every account balance must increase
   C. the suspense account must show a balance
   D. no further journal entries can ever be made

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [2] M1 1,800 + 150; A1 £1,950.
2. [3] B1 it only confirms that total debits equal total credits; B1 several types of error still leave the trial balance balancing (errors of commission, omission, original entry, principle, reversal of entries, and compensating errors); B1 so a balancing trial balance is necessary but not sufficient evidence of accuracy.
3. [2] M1 6,200 + 300; A1 £6,500 (credit balance).
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
