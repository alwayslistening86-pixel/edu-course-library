# S01_IB_Systems_and_Documents - Test: Business documents, prime-entry books, coding and bookkeeping systems

## How to run this
A real checkpoint in AAT's own style: numeric gap-fill calculations, journal/ledger-entry questions and multiple-choice items, with marks shown. Give the whole test at once, with no hints and a calculator allowed (as in the real assessment). Then mark against the mark schemes below and this stage's entry in `rubric.json`.

## Test items
1. State the purpose of a remittance advice, and explain how it differs from a statement of account. [3 marks]
2. Explain the dual effect of bookkeeping transactions, using the example of a business paying £500 cash for a new item of equipment. [3 marks]
3. A business has liabilities of £5,400 and equity (capital) of £21,300. Calculate its total assets. [2 marks]
4. Which type of coding system combines letters and numbers, e.g. 'SM102'? Choose every correct option.
   A. alphanumerical
   B. numerical
   C. alphabetical
   D. sequential
5. Classify each of the following: (a) a bank loan; (b) rent paid; (c) inventory held for sale. [3 marks]

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [3] B1 a remittance advice is sent with/before a payment, stating which invoice(s) it covers; B1 a statement of account is sent periodically by the supplier, listing all invoices/credit notes/payments and the balance; B1 a remittance advice comes from the payer about one payment; a statement of account comes from the supplier and summarises the whole account.
2. [3] B1 every transaction affects two accounts, with equal and opposite debit and credit entries; B1 Equipment (an asset) increases, so it is debited £500; B1 Bank/Cash (an asset) decreases, so it is credited £500.
3. [2] M1 assets = liabilities + equity = 5,400 + 21,300; A1 £26,700.
4. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
5. [3] B1 (a) liability; B1 (b) expense; B1 (c) asset.
