# S10_PBC_Journal_Transactions - Lesson: The journal: opening entries, write-offs and payroll

## Goal
The learner uses the journal to record opening entries, write off irrecoverable debts (including the VAT adjustment), and record payroll transactions.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PBC3.1 - Produce journal entries to record bookkeeping transactions: opening entries, irrecoverable debts written off, payroll transactions

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: a customer who owes £240 (including VAT) has gone out of business and will never pay -- what happens to that debt, and to the VAT already charged on it? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### PBC3.1 Produce journal entries to record bookkeeping transactions: opening entries, irrecoverable debts written off, payroll transactions
The **journal** is a book of prime entry (manual and digital) used for transactions that don't fit the other daybooks: recording **opening entries** (setting up a new set of books, or a new accounting period, with opening balances), writing off **irrecoverable debts** and recording **payroll transactions**. *Worked example -- irrecoverable debt write-off:* a customer owes £240.00 including VAT, and the debt is now irrecoverable. The gross amount includes VAT at 20%, so the net (irrecoverable expense) = £240.00 / 1.20 = **£200.00**, and the VAT element = £240.00 - £200.00 = **£40.00**. The journal entry is: debit Irrecoverable Debts Expense £200.00, debit VAT control account £40.00 (the VAT already charged can be reclaimed back from HMRC since it will never be collected from the customer), credit Receivables Control Account £240.00 (removing the debt entirely). **Payroll transactions** are recorded via the **wages control account**, covering: **gross pay** (before deductions), **income tax** and **employer's and employees' National Insurance Contributions (NICs)** (deducted from pay and/or paid on top by the employer), **employer's and employees' pensions**, and **voluntary deductions** (e.g. union fees) -- gross pay and employer's costs are a debit (an expense); amounts owed to HMRC, the pension provider and the employee (net pay) are credited (liabilities, until paid).
