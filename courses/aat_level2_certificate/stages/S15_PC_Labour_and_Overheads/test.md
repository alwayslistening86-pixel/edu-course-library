# S15_PC_Labour_and_Overheads - Test: Labour costing and overhead absorption

## Test items
1. An employee's standard week is 35 hours at £13.00/hour; they also work 4 hours of overtime at time-and-a-half. Calculate their total pay. [4 marks]
2. A piecework employee produces 320 units at £0.60/unit, with a guaranteed minimum of £180 for the period. Calculate their pay, showing your reasoning. [3 marks]
3. Budgeted overheads are £60,000 and budgeted labour hours are 12,000. Calculate the OAR per labour hour, and the overhead absorbed by a job taking 22 labour hours. [3 marks]
4. Explain why a business might choose to absorb overheads per machine hour rather than per labour hour. [2 marks]
5. What is the overhead absorption rate per unit if budgeted overheads are £20,000 and budgeted production is 4,000 units? Choose every correct option.
   A. £5.00 per unit
   B. £4.00 per unit
   C. £8.00 per unit
   D. £0.20 per unit

## Answer key (for the tutor only)
1. [4] M1 basic = 35 x 13.00 = £455.00; M1 overtime rate = 13.00 x 1.5 = £19.50; M1 overtime pay = 4 x 19.50 = £78.00; A1 total = £533.00.
2. [3] M1 piecework pay = 320 x 0.60 = £192.00; A1 this exceeds the guaranteed minimum of £180, so pay = £192.00; B1 correct reasoning (the higher of piecework pay and the guaranteed minimum is paid).
3. [3] M1 OAR = 60,000/12,000 = £5.00 per labour hour; A1 overhead absorbed = 22 x £5.00 = £110.00; B1 correct method (job hours x OAR).
4. [2] B1 if production is highly automated/machine-intensive, machine hours better reflect how the overhead (e.g. machine running costs, depreciation, power) is actually incurred than labour hours do; B1 using the basis that best reflects how the cost arises gives a more accurate/fair overhead charge to each job or unit.
5. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
