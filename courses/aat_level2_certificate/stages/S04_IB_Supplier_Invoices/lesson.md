# S04_IB_Supplier_Invoices - Lesson: Supplier invoices, credit notes and books of prime entry

## Goal
The learner checks the accuracy of supplier invoices and credit notes, calculates their amounts, and enters them into the purchases daybook, purchases returns daybook and discounts received daybook.

## Syllabus items taught here
- IB3.1 - Check the accuracy of supplier invoices and credit notes: documents used, discounts, discrepancies
- IB3.2 - Enter purchase supplier invoices and credit notes into books of prime entry: purchases daybook, purchases returns daybook, discounts received daybook

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: what should be checked when a supplier's invoice arrives, before it is paid? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### IB3.1 Check the accuracy of supplier invoices and credit notes: documents used, discounts, discrepancies
Documents used to check a supplier invoice: **quotations including discounts**, **purchase orders**, **goods received notes**, **delivery notes** and **goods returned notes**. As with customer invoices, three discount types apply -- **prompt payment**, **trade** and **bulk** -- shown the same way on a supplier's invoice (trade/bulk deducted before VAT; PPD adjusted later via a credit note, IB3.2). Discrepancies to check for: **non-delivery of goods**, **incorrect type or quantity of goods**, **incorrect calculations**, **incorrect discounts** (trade, bulk or prompt payment), and mismatches in the **date and terms of payment** against what was agreed.

#### IB3.2 Enter purchase supplier invoices and credit notes into books of prime entry: purchases daybook, purchases returns daybook, discounts received daybook
The books of prime entry for supplier transactions: the **purchases daybook** (every credit purchase invoice), **purchases returns daybook** (every credit note received for returned goods) and **discounts received daybook** (every credit note received for PPD taken). Each has columns for supplier name, supplier account code, total, VAT, net and an analysis column (e.g. product code), calculated the same way as for customer invoices (net and total figures already worked out on the document; entries made from those figures; the daybook totals are what get posted to the ledgers, S07).

## Explicitly not here
Actually paying the supplier (and identifying discrepancies at that stage) is S05.
