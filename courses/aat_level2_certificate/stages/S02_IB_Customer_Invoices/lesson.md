# S02_IB_Customer_Invoices - Lesson: Customer invoices, credit notes and books of prime entry

## Goal
The learner calculates invoice and credit note amounts (including trade discount, bulk discount and VAT), and enters customer invoices and credit notes into the sales daybook, sales returns daybook and discounts allowed daybook.

## Syllabus items taught here
- IB2.1 - Calculate invoice and credit note amounts: item price, net, VAT, total, trade discount, bulk discount
- IB2.2 - Enter customer invoices and credit notes into books of prime entry: sales daybook, sales returns daybook, discounts allowed daybook

## How to teach this
Ask the learner: if a £200 item has a 15% trade discount, is VAT charged on £200 or on the discounted price? Have the learner attempt every calculation (VAT, discounts, control-account reconciliations, bank reconciliations, FIFO/LIFO/AVCO, labour pay, overhead absorption, product costs, budget variances) with full workings before checking the model answer -- these are computer-marked numeric-entry items in the real AAT assessment, so exact figures matter. Every numeric example in this course was computed and verified in Python when the course was built. UK VAT is taken at the standard rate of 20% throughout unless an item states otherwise; check the current rate at gov.uk if it may have changed. AAT's assessments use a mix of multiple-choice, numeric gap-fill and journal/ledger-entry question tools; this course's items mirror the same calculation-and-entry style using clearly marked short-answer and multiple-choice items.

#### IB2.1 Calculate invoice and credit note amounts: item price, net, VAT, total, trade discount, bulk discount
Before invoicing, a customer typically receives a **quotation** (a price offer), sees the seller's **discount policy**, sends a **customer order**, and may receive a **delivery note** (confirming what was delivered) and see a **price list**. Three types of discount: **prompt payment discount (PPD)** -- a reduction offered for paying within an agreed time; **trade discount** -- a reduction given to a particular type of customer (e.g. a trade buyer), always deducted before VAT is calculated; **bulk discount** -- a reduction for buying in large quantities, also deducted before VAT. At AAT Level 2, PPD is *not* deducted on the face of the invoice: the invoice is raised for the full (trade/bulk-discounted) amount, and if the customer takes up the PPD, a separate **credit note** is issued afterwards for the discount plus its VAT (see IB2.2). *Worked example -- trade discount:* a customer orders goods with a list price of £250, with a 15% trade discount. Discounted net = £250 x (1 - 0.15) = **£212.50**; VAT at 20% = £212.50 x 0.20 = **£42.50**; invoice total = £212.50 + £42.50 = **£255.00**. *Worked example -- bulk discount:* 40 units at £12 each, with a 5% bulk discount for the order size. Gross = 40 x £12 = £480; discounted net = £480 x (1 - 0.05) = **£456.00**; VAT = £456.00 x 0.20 = **£91.20**; total = £456.00 + £91.20 = **£547.20**.

#### IB2.2 Enter customer invoices and credit notes into books of prime entry: sales daybook, sales returns daybook, discounts allowed daybook
The books of prime entry for customer transactions: the **sales daybook** (every credit sales invoice), the **sales returns daybook** (every credit note for returned goods) and the **discounts allowed daybook** (every credit note issued for PPD taken). Each has columns for: customer name, customer account code, total, VAT, net and an analysis column (e.g. by product code, for management information). Entries are made from the **net** and **total (gross)** figures already calculated on the invoice/credit note; each book is then totalled, and those totals (not the individual lines) are what get posted on to the ledgers (S07). *Worked example -- PPD via a credit note:* an invoice is raised for net £1,000, VAT at 20% = £200, total £1,200 -- with a note that a 2.5% PPD applies if paid within 10 days. The customer pays within 10 days, so a credit note is issued: discount = £1,000 x 0.025 = **£25.00**; VAT on the discount = £25.00 x 0.20 = **£5.00**; credit note total = £25.00 + £5.00 = **£30.00**. The customer therefore pays £1,200 - £30.00 = **£1,170.00**. The credit note is entered in the discounts allowed daybook, and posted as a debit to Discounts Allowed and the VAT control account, and a credit to the customer's account/receivables control account (reducing what is owed).
