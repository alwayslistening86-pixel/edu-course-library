# AAT Level 2 Certificate in Accounting - Cumulative Exam

## Unlock condition
Only available once every stage in `stage_ladder` has a passed test.

## Format
The exam follows AAT's 8 real assessment objectives for this qualification's synoptic assessment: business types and functions (S18-S24, 10%); the finance function, its information and sources (S22-S24, 13%); CSR, ethics and sustainability (S20, 14%); processing bookkeeping transactions and communicating information (S01-S07, S23, 22%); control accounts, bank reconciliation and journal error correction (S08-S12, 10%); contract law (S18, 7%); bookkeeping systems, receipts/payments and information/data security (S01-S07, S24, 10%); the global business environment (S19, 14%). Principles of Costing (S13-S17) is examined in its own standalone unit assessment in the real qualification and is not part of AAT's synoptic assessment; this course's own PC stage tests (S13-S17) are that unit's checkpoint. Questions are original; write fresh ones rather than reusing stage tests. The ready-made questions below are a starter bank; the tutor writes the rest, kept to the same objective weightings.

## 9 ready-made items (write the rest fresh, never reusing stage-test items)
1. A business has assets of £22,000 and liabilities of £7,500. What is its capital (equity)? Choose every correct option.
   A. £14,500
   B. £29,500
   C. £7,500
   D. £22,000
2. A sales invoice shows: net £600, trade discount 20%. Calculate the discounted net and the VAT at 20%. [3 marks]
3. A colleague asks you to share a client's financial figures with a friend who is 'just curious' and not connected to the client's business, and separately, to sign off a calculation you haven't had time to properly check because a deadline is close. Identify which fundamental ethical principle is at risk in each situation, and explain why. [5 marks]
4. A receivables ledger control account opens at £6,200. During the period: credit sales £18,500, receipts from customers £17,900, discounts allowed £250, sales returns £180. Calculate the closing balance. [3 marks]
5. State one advantage and one disadvantage of a sole trader as a business structure. [2 marks]
6. Explain why an organisation should keep information sources and data secure, giving two reasons. [2 marks]
7. Which of these is an example of the global business environment affecting a UK business? Choose every correct option.
   A. a fall in the value of the pound making imported materials more expensive
   B. a local competitor opening a new shop
   C. a change to the business's own opening hours
   D. an internal restructuring of the finance team
8. Describe the purpose of the receivables ledger control account and explain why it is reconciled with the receivables ledger. [3 marks]
9. A business's cash book shows a balance of £2,450. The bank statement shows £2,690. There is an unpresented cheque of £360 and an outstanding lodgement of £120. Prepare the bank reconciliation statement starting from the bank statement balance. [4 marks]

## Answer key for the ready-made items (tutor only)
1. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
2. [3] M1 discounted net = 600 x (1-0.20) = £480; A1 VAT = £480 x 0.20 = £96; B1 invoice total £576 (480+96).
3. [5] B1 sharing figures with an unconnected friend breaches confidentiality; B1 because information acquired professionally must not be disclosed without proper authority or used other than for its proper purpose; B1 signing off unchecked work under time pressure breaches professional competence and due care; B1 because the technician must act diligently and in line with applicable technical standards, not skip proper checking to meet a deadline; B1 both responses should be to decline, explaining the ethical principle involved.
4. [3] M1 6,200 + 18,500 - 17,900 - 250 - 180; A1 closing balance = £6,370; B1 correct treatment (sales added; receipts, discounts allowed and returns deducted).
5. [2] B1 advantage, e.g. full control, simple/cheap to set up, keeps all profit; B1 disadvantage, e.g. unlimited liability -- the owner is personally liable for business debts.
6. [2] B1 to maintain privacy/confidentiality of sensitive (customer, employee, financial) data; B1 to avoid the consequences of a breach, e.g. financial loss, reputational damage, legal/regulatory penalties.
7. Correct: A (exactly these options, no others)
8. [3] B1 it is a general ledger account summarising, in total, everything owed by all credit customers, and is part of the double-entry system; B1 the receivables ledger holds the individual customer accounts (memorandum, not part of double-entry); B1 reconciling the two checks that the total agrees with the sum of the individual balances, and helps identify errors.
9. [4] M1 bank statement balance £2,690; M1 less unpresented cheque £360; M1 add outstanding lodgement £120; A1 adjusted balance = 2,690 - 360 + 120 = £2,450 (agrees with cash book).

## Grading
Mark short answers, journal/ledger entries and multiple-choice against the mark schemes; mark calculations with method shown (a correct final answer with no valid method shown earns only what the mark scheme allows for a bare answer). A pass needs at least 70% of the marks overall, mirroring AAT's own published pass mark for the synoptic assessment. Report the total, the percentage and the weakest assessment objectives.
