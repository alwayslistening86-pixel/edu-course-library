# S02_BLP_Finance_Insolvency_and_Tax - Lesson: Raising finance, corporate insolvency, and the taxation of businesses

## Goal
The learner advises on equity and debt finance options with appropriate security, explains the corporate insolvency procedures and priority order, and applies the correct tax treatment to a sole trader, partnership or company.

## Syllabus items taught here
- BLP-6 - Raising equity finance: learn and apply: share capital, pre-emption rights, allotment and issue procedure
- BLP-7 - Raising debt finance and taking security: learn and apply: loan capital, fixed and floating charges, registration at Companies House
- BLP-8 - Corporate insolvency procedures: learn and apply: administration and liquidation, when each is used
- BLP-9 - Order of priority on liquidation: learn and apply: the statutory distribution order on winding up
- BLP-10 - Taxation of unincorporated businesses: learn and apply: income tax and CGT for sole traders and partners
- BLP-11 - Taxation of companies and VAT; reading business accounts: learn and apply: corporation tax, VAT basics, and reading a balance sheet/P&L

## How to teach this
Ask: "A company needs 500,000 pounds to expand. What are its two broad choices, and what does each cost the company in return?" Use the answer (equity dilutes control, debt must be repaid and is often secured) to introduce the finance items, then move to what happens when finance runs out (insolvency), and finally how the business and its owners are taxed throughout.

#### BLP-6 Raising equity finance
Issuing new shares raises equity finance without creating a repayment obligation, but dilutes existing shareholders' percentage ownership and control. Existing shareholders normally have statutory pre-emption rights (CA 2006 s.561): new shares must be offered to existing holders first, in proportion to their existing holding, before being offered to anyone else, unless pre-emption is validly disapplied by special resolution or excluded by the articles. The allotment and issue procedure requires the directors to have authority to allot (from the articles or an ordinary resolution) and, for most private companies with only one class of shares, no further shareholder approval is needed beyond that authority. Always check both the source of allotment authority and whether pre-emption rights apply or have been disapplied.

#### BLP-7 Raising debt finance and taking security
Debt (loan capital) must be repaid, usually with interest, and is commonly secured. A fixed charge attaches to a specific, identifiable, non-changing asset (freehold property, specific equipment); the company needs the lender's consent to deal with that asset while the charge subsists. A floating charge hovers over a class of changing assets, such as stock or receivables, letting the company trade and deal with those assets in the ordinary course of business until the charge crystallises, typically on default or insolvency, at which point it fixes over whatever assets are then held. Charges created by a company must be registered at Companies House within 21 days of creation (CA 2006 s.859A); an unregistered charge is void against a liquidator, administrator or other creditor of the company, though the underlying debt remains owed.

#### BLP-8 Corporate insolvency procedures
A company is insolvent if it cannot pay its debts as they fall due (the cash-flow test) or its liabilities exceed its assets (the balance-sheet test), under Insolvency Act 1986 s.123. Administration is a rescue-focused procedure: an administrator takes control of the company to try to rescue it as a going concern, or to achieve a better result for creditors than an immediate winding up, or, failing either, to realise assets to pay secured or preferential creditors. Liquidation (winding up) ends the company's life: a liquidator collects in the company's assets and distributes them to creditors and members in the strict statutory order of priority. Distinguish the two by purpose: administration tries to save the business or maximise the return before distribution; liquidation is the final distribution and dissolution.

#### BLP-9 Order of priority on liquidation
Learn this order, since it is tested directly and a common source of avoidable marks lost: (1) fixed charge holders, paid out of the proceeds of their specific secured asset; (2) the insolvency practitioner's expenses and costs of the liquidation; (3) preferential creditors, such as certain employee claims for unpaid wages and holiday pay; (4) the prescribed part, a ring-fenced slice of floating charge realisations set aside for unsecured creditors; (5) floating charge holders; (6) unsecured creditors generally; and (7) shareholders, who are paid only if anything remains after every class above has been satisfied in full. A worked example: if floating charge realisations are 100,000 pounds and the prescribed part takes 20,000 pounds for unsecured creditors, the floating charge holder receives only the remaining 80,000 pounds towards its debt.

#### BLP-10 Taxation of unincorporated businesses
Sole traders and partners are taxed as individuals on their trading profits through income tax, at the applicable rates and bands, with trading profit computed by adjusting accounting profit for tax purposes (adding back disallowable expenses, deducting capital allowances instead of depreciation). On disposal of a chargeable business asset, an individual sole trader or partner is potentially liable to capital gains tax (CGT) rather than corporation tax, since an unincorporated business has no separate tax-paying entity distinct from its owner. This single layer of taxation, income tax on profits as earned and CGT on capital disposals, is a key point of comparison against the position for a company, and BLP problem questions frequently ask you to use this comparison to advise on vehicle choice.

#### BLP-11 Taxation of companies and VAT; reading business accounts
A company pays corporation tax on its own profits, computed similarly to income tax (adjusted trading profit plus capital allowances) but at the corporation tax rate, and separately pays corporation tax (not CGT) on chargeable gains from disposing of assets. Because profits are taxed once in the company and then again as income tax on the shareholder when a dividend is paid out of already-taxed profits, this creates a "double taxation" consideration relevant to vehicle-choice advice. VAT is a separate transaction tax charged on the supply of most goods and services by a VAT-registered business, with standard-rated, zero-rated and exempt categories, and compulsory registration once turnover crosses the statutory threshold. Basic accounts literacy matters throughout BLP: a balance sheet shows assets, liabilities and equity at a point in time, and a profit and loss account shows income and expenditure over a period, both routinely used when advising on lending, insolvency risk or a sale.
