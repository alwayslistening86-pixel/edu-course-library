# S19_EMP_Redundancy_Discrimination_and_Tribunal_Procedure - Lesson: Elective, Employment Law: redundancy, discrimination, reasonable adjustments and tribunal procedure

## Goal
The learner applies the fair redundancy process, identifies the four types of discrimination and the reasonable adjustments duty, and explains Employment Tribunal procedure including time limits.

## Syllabus items taught here
- EMP-5 - Redundancy: learn and apply: genuine redundancy situation, fair process, statutory redundancy pay
- EMP-6 - Discrimination under the Equality Act 2010: learn and apply: protected characteristics and the four types of discrimination
- EMP-7 - Reasonable adjustments: learn and apply: the proactive duty to make reasonable adjustments for disabled employees
- EMP-8 - Employment Tribunal procedure: learn and apply: ACAS early conciliation, time limits, ET1 and ET3

## How to teach this
Ask: "A business genuinely needs to make one of three similar roles redundant. What must it do, beyond simply deciding who to let go, to make that process fair?" Build out the redundancy process from the answer, then move into discrimination, which can arise in a redundancy process or completely independently of it, and finish with the tribunal procedure that enforces all of these rights.

#### EMP-5 Redundancy
A genuine redundancy situation requires that the need for employees of a particular kind to do a particular kind of work has ceased or diminished, or that the business or workplace is closing altogether; simply wanting to replace someone with a cheaper alternative doing the same job is not a genuine redundancy. A fair redundancy process generally requires a fair selection pool, meaning the right group of comparable employees is considered together rather than artificially narrowed to target one individual, objective selection criteria fairly and consistently applied across that pool, meaningful consultation with the affected employees before a final decision is made, and genuine consideration of any suitable alternative employment within the business. Statutory redundancy pay is calculated by reference to the employee's age, length of continuous service, and weekly pay, subject to a statutory cap on the weekly pay figure used.

#### EMP-6 Discrimination under the Equality Act 2010
The Equality Act 2010 protects a defined list of protected characteristics, including age, disability, gender reassignment, marriage and civil partnership, pregnancy and maternity, race, religion or belief, sex, and sexual orientation. Distinguish the four types of discrimination clearly. Direct discrimination means treating someone less favourably because of a protected characteristic. Indirect discrimination means applying a provision, criterion or practice to everyone equally that in fact puts people sharing a protected characteristic at a particular disadvantage, and that cannot be justified as a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim. Harassment means unwanted conduct related to a protected characteristic that violates a person's dignity or creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or offensive environment for them. Victimisation means subjecting someone to a detriment because they have done a "protected act," such as raising a genuine discrimination complaint.

#### EMP-7 Reasonable adjustments
An employer must make reasonable adjustments where a disabled employee is placed at a substantial disadvantage compared to non-disabled colleagues by a provision, criterion or practice, or by a physical feature of the workplace. This is a distinctly proactive duty, not simply a duty not to discriminate: an employer that does nothing, rather than actively considering and making appropriate adjustments once it knows or ought reasonably to know of the disability and the disadvantage, can itself be in breach of the duty even without any deliberate less-favourable treatment. What counts as "reasonable" depends on factors such as the cost and practicality of the adjustment and the size and resources of the employer, so advice on this duty should always be fact-specific rather than assuming a fixed list of required steps.

#### EMP-8 Employment Tribunal procedure
Most employment claims require ACAS Early Conciliation to be attempted before a claim can be lodged with the Employment Tribunal at all, giving the parties a structured opportunity to settle before formal proceedings begin. Strict time limits apply to almost all employment claims, commonly a short window such as broadly three months less one day from the relevant act complained of, though this period is effectively extended to reflect time spent in the Early Conciliation process. Missing the relevant time limit is usually fatal to a claim, so it should always be checked and flagged to the client at the very first opportunity. Claims are brought on a standard ET1 form, setting out the claimant's case, and the employer responds using the corresponding ET3 form, setting out its defence.
