# S03_PLP_PreContract_to_Exchange - Lesson: Conveyancing procedure from instructions to exchange of contracts

## Goal
The learner takes instructions, investigates title, runs the standard searches and enquiries, drafts and negotiates the contract, and explains the legal effect of exchange.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PLP-1 - Taking instructions and investigating title: learn and apply: conflicts check, the three registers of a registered title
- PLP-2 - Pre-contract searches and enquiries: learn and apply: standard search bundle and TA6 pre-contract enquiries
- PLP-3 - Drafting and negotiating the contract: learn and apply: Standard Conditions, special conditions, deposit, freehold vs leasehold terms
- PLP-4 - Exchange of contracts: learn and apply: effect of exchange, passing of risk, insurance

## How to teach this
Open with: "You have just been handed a new file: a client wants to buy a house. What is the very first thing you check before you do anything else, and why?" Use the answer (who the client actually is, and any conflict) to move into the five-phase conveyancing checklist, treating the whole transaction as one continuous procedure rather than isolated topics.

#### PLP-1 Taking instructions and investigating title
Before acting, confirm who the client is, check for conflicts of interest, and identify the property and its title number, if registered, via HM Land Registry. For a registered title, obtain and check the official copies of the register, which have three parts. The property register describes the land itself and any rights that benefit it, such as a right of way benefiting the property. The proprietorship register states who owns the property and the class of title, such as absolute, possessory, good leasehold or qualified. The charges register lists burdens on the property, such as mortgages, restrictive covenants, and easements that burden (rather than benefit) the land. Always check all three registers, not just the proprietorship register, since a buyer's solicitor's job is to spot problems in any of them before exchange.

#### PLP-2 Pre-contract searches and enquiries
Before exchange, run the standard search bundle appropriate to the property: a local authority search, covering planning history, road adoption and enforcement notices; a water and drainage search; an environmental search, covering contamination risk; and any additional searches the location demands, such as mining, chancel repair liability, or flood risk. Separately, raise pre-contract enquiries of the seller, commonly using the Law Society's standard Property Information Form (TA6), which asks the seller to disclose matters such as boundaries, disputes, notices and alterations. Searches and enquiries serve different purposes: searches reveal matters of public record, while enquiries extract information only the seller is likely to know, and both must be reviewed and any issues resolved before advising the client to exchange.

#### PLP-3 Drafting and negotiating the contract
The Standard Conditions of Sale, or the Standard Commercial Property Conditions for commercial transactions, form the backbone of most contracts, with transaction-specific terms added as Special Conditions. Check the key terms: the purchase price, the deposit, usually 10% though sometimes negotiated down, the completion date, what is included as fixtures and fittings, and any special conditions, such as the sale being conditional on the buyer obtaining a mortgage offer. A leasehold contract must additionally deal with the term of years remaining, the ground rent, the service charge, and whether the landlord's consent to assignment is required under the lease terms, since failing to address this can delay or derail completion.

#### PLP-4 Exchange of contracts
Before exchange, the transaction is not legally binding, and either party can withdraw without legal consequence, subject to any costs already incurred. At exchange, the contracts become binding on both parties, a fixed completion date is set, and the deposit is normally paid by the buyer. After exchange, risk in the property generally passes to the buyer under the Standard Conditions, even though legal title has not yet passed, which is why a buyer should have buildings insurance in place from the moment of exchange, not merely from completion. Advise clients clearly that exchange, not the earlier agreement of terms, is the moment commitment becomes irreversible.
