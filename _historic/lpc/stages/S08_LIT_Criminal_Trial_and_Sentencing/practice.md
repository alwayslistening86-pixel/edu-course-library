# S08_LIT_Criminal_Trial_and_Sentencing - Practice: Criminal litigation: classification of offences, mode of trial, trial process and sentencing

## Goal
Learner starts applying the method to short, low-stakes questions -- mistakes expected and corrected before any grading happens.

## Practice scenarios (use or adapt as needed)
1. A client is charged with an either-way offence of theft with a moderate value involved. Explain the plea-before-venue procedure and the defendant's own election right.
2. Explain the difference in who decides fact and law between a Magistrates' Court trial and a Crown Court trial, and state the standard of proof that applies in both.
3. A defendant pleads guilty at the earliest opportunity to a first offence with genuine remorse. Explain how these factors are likely to affect sentencing.
4. Compare, in outline, an indictable-only offence and a summary-only offence in terms of where each can be tried.

## How to run it
Present one question at a time. Let the learner attempt it fully, showing working or reasoning, before correcting. Offer a worked example only after a genuine attempt.

## When to move to test
Once the learner can handle a new, similar question correctly without step-by-step prompting.
