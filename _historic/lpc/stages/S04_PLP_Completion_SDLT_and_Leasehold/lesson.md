# S04_PLP_Completion_SDLT_and_Leasehold - Lesson: Completion, Stamp Duty Land Tax, post-completion registration and leasehold extras

## Goal
The learner explains completion mechanics and when legal title passes, calculates SDLT and its filing deadline, completes post-completion registration steps, and identifies leasehold-specific procedural requirements.

## Syllabus items taught here
- PLP-5 - Completion procedure: learn and apply: completion mechanics and when legal title actually passes
- PLP-6 - Stamp Duty Land Tax: learn and apply: SDLT banded calculation, filing deadline, reliefs and surcharges
- PLP-7 - Post-completion registration: learn and apply: applying to HM Land Registry, the priority period and OS1/OS2 searches
- PLP-8 - Leasehold-specific conveyancing procedure: learn and apply: landlord's consent, service charge apportionment, notice of assignment

## How to teach this
Ask: "Contracts have exchanged and completion day has arrived. What actually needs to happen today, and does your client legally own the house by the end of it?" Use the surprising answer (not yet, on a registered title) to structure the rest of the transaction: completion, then SDLT, then registration, then any leasehold extras.

#### PLP-5 Completion procedure
On completion, the purchase money is transferred, usually via the buyer's solicitor to the seller's solicitor, keys are released to the buyer, and the seller vacates. On a registered title, legal title does not pass at the moment of completion itself; completion instead passes only an equitable interest to the buyer, with legal title passing only on registration at HM Land Registry. This gap between completion and registration is exactly why the post-completion steps covered next matter so much, and clients should be told plainly that they are not yet the registered legal owner on completion day itself.

#### PLP-6 Stamp Duty Land Tax
SDLT is a tax payable by the buyer, calculated on a slice or banded basis against the purchase price, or, for leases, potentially also against the net present value of the rent payable. A SDLT return must be filed and any tax due paid within 14 days of completion under the rules in force since 2019; earlier transactions sat under a 30-day deadline, so always check which period a given fact pattern falls under rather than assuming the current rule applies. Reliefs and surcharges are common exam wrinkles: first-time buyer relief may reduce or remove SDLT on a first home, while an additional-property surcharge typically applies where a buyer already owns another residential property, such as a buy-to-let or second home purchase.

#### PLP-7 Post-completion registration
After completion, the buyer's solicitor must, in order: pay any SDLT due and file the SDLT return within the statutory deadline; apply to HM Land Registry to register the buyer as the new proprietor, and register any new mortgage at the same time; and protect the buyer's priority in the meantime, since until registration the buyer holds only an equitable interest. A priority period, protected by an official search of the register (OS1 for a whole registered title, OS2 for part), gives protection against intervening registrations made by third parties during the gap between completion and the buyer's own registration, so this search should be run and relied on promptly rather than left until the application is submitted.

#### PLP-8 Leasehold-specific conveyancing procedure
Leasehold transactions carry extra procedural steps beyond the freehold checklist. Check the lease for any requirement of the landlord's licence to assign; proceeding without it, where required, can be a breach of covenant even if the sale otherwise completes normally. Service charge and ground rent must be apportioned fairly on completion, with the buyer typically paying a fair proportion from the completion date to the next service charge demand date. After completion, a notice of assignment must usually be served on the landlord or managing agent, sometimes together with a registration fee specified in the lease itself, and failing to serve this notice can leave the new owner unrecognised by the landlord for service charge and enforcement purposes.
