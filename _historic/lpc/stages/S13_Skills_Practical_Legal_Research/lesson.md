# S13_Skills_Practical_Legal_Research - Lesson: Course Skills: practical legal research methodology

## Goal
The learner frames a precise research question, selects the right source type, searches efficiently, verifies currency of the law, and produces a usable research trail with an honestly caveated answer.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SK-PLR-1 - Framing the research question: learn and apply: turning a vague instruction into a precise answerable question
- SK-PLR-2 - Selecting the right source type: learn and apply: choosing between legislation, case law and secondary sources
- SK-PLR-3 - Efficient search technique: learn and apply: precise legal-test search terms, widening and narrowing iteratively
- SK-PLR-4 - Verifying currency of the law: learn and apply: checking a provision is in force and a case has not been overturned
- SK-PLR-5 - Building a usable research trail: learn and apply: a running research note ending in a clear, honestly caveated answer

## How to teach this
Explain: "In practice you are rarely handed the answer; you are handed a messy client problem and expected to find, verify, and apply the current law under time pressure, then hand something usable to a supervisor or client. This is a methodology skill, not a memory test." Walk the learner through the five-step method below using a realistic but simple client question.

#### SK-PLR-1 Framing the research question
Turn a vague instruction, such as "can he get out of this contract?", into a precise, answerable legal question, such as "does a material breach of clause 4, the delivery timeline, entitle the Claimant to terminate the contract, or only to claim damages?" A badly framed question guarantees a wasted research session, since it is impossible to know when you have actually answered it or to search efficiently for the right authority. Spend real time on this framing step before touching a single source, and be willing to refine the question again once early research reveals the real legal issue is not quite what the initial instruction suggested.

#### SK-PLR-2 Selecting the right source type
Start with legislation where the question concerns a statutory scheme, such as Companies Act 2006 procedure or Equality Act 2010 discrimination categories, using legislation.gov.uk for the current text and always checking the "changes to legislation" or commencement information, since sections are sometimes not yet in force, or have since been amended or repealed. Start with case law for common-law tests, such as what counts as a material breach or the principles governing contractual interpretation, using case law databases such as the National Archives' Find Case Law service or BAILII, alongside a practitioner text to find a leading case as a curated starting point. Use secondary sources, such as practitioner texts, journal articles and professional body guidance, for orientation and to locate the primary sources that matter, but never treat a secondary source as the final authority; always trace back to the actual statute or case.

#### SK-PLR-3 Efficient search technique
Use precise search terms drawn from the legal test itself, not simply the client's own words, for example searching "repudiatory breach" rather than "get out of contract," since databases and indexes are built around legal terminology, not lay phrasing. Widen or narrow your search terms iteratively based on what comes back: if a search returns nothing useful, your terms are probably too narrow, or use the wrong legal vocabulary; if it returns far too much, add a jurisdiction or date filter, or use a more specific phrase. Treat the first search as a starting point to refine, not a final answer, and be prepared to run several iterations before you have located the right authority.

#### SK-PLR-4 Verifying currency of the law
Always check two things before relying on anything you find: is this the version of the statute currently in force, and has this case been overturned, distinguished, or followed on a later appeal. A citation that looked perfect an hour into your research can be worthless if the provision was later repealed, or the case was overruled by a higher court, so build in a dedicated currency-check step as a standard part of the method rather than skipping it under time pressure. This is especially important on a course, like this one, that also teaches historical material, where a fact pattern's era matters to which version of a rule actually applies.

#### SK-PLR-5 Building a usable research trail
Keep a running note as you go, recording the source, what it says, how relevant it is, and any caveat about its currency, rather than trying to reconstruct your reasoning from memory once the research is finished. This running trail is what turns research into something a supervising fee-earner can actually rely on and independently check, rather than simply trusting your final answer. End with a short, clear answer to the precise question you framed at the outset, plus the supporting authority, plus any genuine uncertainty flagged honestly; a good researcher says "the position is unclear because..." rather than guessing confidently when the law genuinely does not give a clean answer.
