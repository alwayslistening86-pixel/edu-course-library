# S11_Skills_Interviewing_and_Advising - Lesson: Course Skills: interviewing and advising a client

## Goal
The learner runs a structured client interview: opening it well, gathering information effectively, identifying the client's real objective, advising clearly with options, and closing with agreed next steps.

## Syllabus items taught here
- SK-IA-1 - Opening the interview: learn and apply: greeting, role, structuring the meeting, client care basics
- SK-IA-2 - Information gathering: learn and apply: open then closed questions, active listening and reflecting back
- SK-IA-3 - Identifying the client's real objective: learn and apply: distinguishing the stated request from the real underlying goal
- SK-IA-4 - Advising in plain English with options: learn and apply: balanced advice, presenting options with pros and cons
- SK-IA-5 - Closing and next steps: learn and apply: agreeing next steps and confirming follow-up

## How to teach this
Explain: "Almost every piece of legal advice starts with an interview, and this skill is assessed on technique and client-handling, not legal accuracy alone." Have the learner rehearse the four-part structure below on a simple unseen scenario (a friend's dispute with a landlord works well), timing themselves through each stage.

#### SK-IA-1 Opening the interview
Begin by greeting the client, introducing yourself and your role, and confirming who the client is and roughly what they have come about. Explain the structure of the meeting up front, for example: "Today I would like to hear about what has happened, ask some follow-up questions, and then give you some initial advice and agree next steps; does that work for you?" Briefly confirm client care basics where the scenario calls for it, such as costs information, which links directly to the client care obligations covered in Professional Conduct and Regulation. A well-scripted opening removes decision-load at the highest-anxiety moment of the interview and sets clear expectations for the rest of the meeting.

#### SK-IA-2 Information gathering
Start with open questions, such as "tell me what happened, in your own words," to let the client's account come out unprompted and in full before you interrupt with your own agenda. Once you understand the shape of the problem, funnel down to closed, specific questions, for example "you mentioned a letter; do you still have a copy?" Actively listen throughout, and reflect back what you have heard to confirm accuracy, for example "so if I have understood correctly, you signed the contract on the third but did not receive the goods until the twentieth; is that right?" Resist the temptation to start advising before you have the full facts, since this is a common panic response under time pressure that undermines the whole interview.

#### SK-IA-3 Identifying the client's real objective
A client's stated request is not always their actual underlying goal. A client who opens by saying "I want to sue them" might, once properly explored, actually want an apology, their money back, or simply reassurance that they were not in the wrong. Ask directly, once you have the facts: "what would a good outcome look like for you?" and listen carefully to the answer, since building your advice around the wrong objective produces technically correct but practically useless advice. Identifying the real objective is what turns a fact-gathering exercise into advice the client can actually use to make a decision.

#### SK-IA-4 Advising in plain English with options
Summarise the legal position in plain English, explaining any unavoidable jargon the first time you use it rather than assuming the client already understands it. Give realistic, balanced advice, stating both the strengths and the risks of the client's position honestly, rather than overpromising a good outcome to make the client feel better in the moment. Present genuine options, typically two or three realistic paths with the pros and cons of each, rather than a single instruction, since clients need enough information to make an informed choice about how to proceed. Check understanding as you go, for example asking "does that make sense so far?", rather than delivering a long uninterrupted monologue.

#### SK-IA-5 Closing and next steps
Agree clear next steps before the meeting ends: who is doing what, and by when. Confirm how and when you will follow up, for example a letter confirming the advice given, which links directly to the client-letter conventions covered in the Legal Writing skill. Close the meeting warmly, thanking the client for coming in, and make sure they leave clear about what happens next rather than uncertain. As with the opening, scripting and rehearsing the closing lines in advance removes decision-load at a high-anxiety moment and ensures nothing important is forgotten under time pressure.
