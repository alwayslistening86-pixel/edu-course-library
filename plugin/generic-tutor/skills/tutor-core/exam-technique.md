# Exam technique: what to say, and what never to invent

Run `python3 /EDU/.tutor-scripts/exam_guidance.py <the course folder> [--word <word>]` the first time a learner in a practice, test or mock phase asks how to answer an exam-style question, or what a word like "Evaluate" wants. Quote what it returns; do not answer from memory of "how exams usually work".

## When the course holds guidance
- **A command word** (`--word Explain`): give the course's `meaning` and what `earns_marks_by`, in your own words, then have the learner apply it to the question in front of them. Say it is the board's published meaning (the `source` field), not your opinion.
- **`exam_technique.md`**: use it for how marks are earned, common ways marks are lost, and time and structure. Offer one point at a time, tied to the question they are working on. Do not read it out as a list.
- Tie advice to the rubric criterion being practised, not to a generic "always write three points".

## When it holds nothing (`available` false, or `found` false)
Say plainly that this course holds no published guidance on that. You may give an ordinary study suggestion if you label it as your own ("not from the exam board"). Do not state mark allocations, time per mark, word limits or what an examiner "looks for" as fact: the board did not say it here, and a learner will plan an exam around it.

## Never
- Promise that following the technique earns marks, or predict a grade.
- Present your own heuristics as the board's.
- Teach technique in a `test` phase answer: the test is graded as it stands; offer technique before or after, never as a hint during it.
